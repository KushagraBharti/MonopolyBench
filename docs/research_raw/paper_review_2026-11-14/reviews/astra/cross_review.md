## Corrections accepted from Sol's completed cross-review

## Additional novelty search after the independent review

A more specific search found financial anchoring and financial-memory work not named in the feedback. This further narrows the proposed novelty claim; it does not establish a duplicate of the conditional-collateral experiment.

- [Human bias in AI models? Anchoring effects and mitigation strategies in large language models](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbef.2024.100971): the publisher's indexed abstract describes numerical anchoring in forecasts. Direct full text returned 403; no full-method claim is made.
- [Memory and Generative AI](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=192584), October 28, 2025 working paper: the original abstract and introduction describe financial choices affected by associative memory and knowledge injection. Its memory construct is parameter-level associations, not ten carried game notes. This is an adjacent precedent, not evidence against our exact proposed mechanism.
- [MemTrial](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.11732), v2 October 11, 2026: I read the primary setup and methods surrounding memory ablations and PlantedMem. It compares decisions with different retrieved experiences in portfolio tasks. Crucially, PlantedMem imposes experience effects through a synthetic tilt of real LLM drafts; it does not demonstrate that a planted textual reserve number causes false surrender. Its name must not be mistaken for that result.

The research question must therefore be more precise than either generic memory influence or financial anchoring. The candidate distinction remains updating conditional resource categories when eligibility changes, and its relationship to termination under an exact objective. The search is targeted, not exhaustive; no priority claim follows.

### Accepted findings and primary-source checks

1. **Correct standard-rule knowledge can produce a wrong prediction of this engine.** I directly read the official [Hasbro rulebook](https://www.hasbro.com/common/instruct/monins.pdf), printed pages 4–5. It requires selling buildings during bankruptcy settlement and charges the creditor interest on inherited mortgages. The engine instead transfers buildings intact and charges no inheritance interest. Gemini's G18 d344 interest motive therefore matches a published rule but predicts the implemented settlement incorrectly. The release rule used in the successful financing texts is standard; the settlement rules affecting the desirability of continuing are not. The final review must report both facts together. Calling the interest belief simply faulty recall would be wrong.
2. **Separate the false budget from the reason for surrendering.** Sol's $408 maximum and Gemini's $530 maximum exclude collateral released by selling buildings. Their financial statements are incomplete. That does not identify everything that caused the applied declarations: Sol's provider field also considers the transfer of assets, and the historical objective permits surrender when financing is judged undesirable. A strict diagnostic that requires payment whenever possible would change the objective. Payoff-relevant house rules should be disclosed when interpreting its stop decisions.
3. **Inclusion is weaker than an explicit dependency statement.** The original 13 + 1 + 2 count describes whether selected text includes subsequent mortgages or an inclusive budget. It cannot become thirteen verified dependency plans. Opus proposes five explicit statements, one tentative statement, three ordered routes, five inclusive budgets and two omissions. I reread the five proposed inclusive cases and agree that the distinction is useful. This remains a proposed model-coded rubric, not a validated replacement statistic.
4. **Payment is conditional on continuing valid financing in the sixteen.** It remains an outcome worth reporting, but is not an additional independent demonstration of competence once the conditional proof is stated. The scientific comparison should distinguish what is written, whether the applied action agrees, and whether financing continues or stops. The proof assumes valid cash-raising actions and uninterrupted execution; it does not cover invalid-call loops, exhausted call budgets, or all hotel states.
5. **The two failed totals are exactly U0.** They equal cash plus all nominal building salvage plus initially eligible mortgages, not arbitrary reserve numbers. Successful texts can also pass through this subtotal before including released collateral. This sharpens the descriptive inventory pattern and weakens a uniquely memory-based explanation. 6. **G20 d327 is exact valuation under a binding budget.** Its $1,414 capacity against a $1,400 debt requires the full liquidation end state, given the available asset increments. The sequence is useful for valuation and action accounting, not evidence that ten difficult planning choices were solved. G13 d195 is a useful complementary example of selecting a subset of assets while leaving other deeds unmortgaged. Neither demonstrates optimal liquidation.
7. **Multi-step financing is not unique to MonopolyBench.** I directly checked [FinBoardBench v1, Appendix C.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27896v1): it describes mortgaging property or selling houses when cash is insufficient, before bankruptcy. ### Qualifications and retained disagreements

- **Do not turn the proposed text tiers into a new strong headline.** The inclusive texts contain named assets and intended sales, not only unexplained totals. G4 d308 specifies starting with an Illinois sale; G6 d185 states Boardwalk first and then house sales; G17 d254 discusses even selling and subsequent mortgages. They are weaker evidence of the prerequisite than an explicit “sale unlocks mortgage” sentence, but “no planning content” would erase what is actually written. Conversely, counting explicit wording cannot reveal private internal reasoning. The artifact should retain the passages and explain the coding rule.
- **G13 is not established as the only case with route choice.** Many focal states have resources above the debt. Its selective plan makes it a good illustration; uniqueness would require an analysis not provided by Opus's statement. G20 has a forced full-liquidation end state, not a unique action order.
- **Statement–action agreement still needs the contrary cases.** The six above-bound financing starts and the payable Grok declaration must accompany the successes. Five of those six starts conflict with Grok's own text; the sixth expresses the standard-rule interest motive. They are not six false beliefs that payment was possible.
- **Counterfactual rules are one control, not the only possible control.** They are established and useful, but do not automatically separate retrieval from reasoning perfectly. Renaming, explicit rule comprehension, unfamiliar transition structures and matched representation controls target different alternatives. No single proposed arm settles all of them.
- **Independent assessment and execution have different estimands.** This agreement with Sol survives Opus's suggestions. Separate instances estimate distributions conditional on state; they cannot show that one particular instance held a correct belief and then ignored it.
- **A local optimum can support objective-relative regret.** Sol's net-cost correction makes the objective coherent under declared assumptions. “Regret under this liquidation-cost objective” can be accurate if defined and computed; it must not be presented as lost win probability or global strategic value. No optimization was run here.
- **Scope first, not another failure-only paper by default.** A focused financing account is the strongest current candidate. The bargaining census and contextual conduct remain useful observational evidence and should be retained in the full artifact. A short main text should select material by its question, not conceal examples of competence or assume that only mistakes count as science.

3. **The proposed total-elimination validation outcome is invariant.** Every completed four-player game has three eliminated players. Replacing a financing policy cannot change that total. A future study would need a defined focal-player outcome, payable-state declaration rate, survival time, winner distribution or another nonconstant target. Conversely, the three historical payable declarations are not an upper bound on an intervention's effects in new trajectories: changing a policy changes subsequent states.
4. **Provider text can end without a conclusion.** Gemini's G17 d234 field ends by considering additional options, and successful G8/G18 fields also end before their notes' conclusions. ### Claims narrowed or not adopted

- **179/188 is not a new accuracy score.** It describes concordance of first action with a nominal asset bound. The bound is insufficient for payability in G7, some declarations were the only menu option, and the historical objective permits strategic surrender. The useful result is the decomposition of the nine departures, not an apparently calibrated 95% decision-accuracy headline.
- **The named-action scan has unequal observability.** Fable reports nine conflicts over 338 decisions, all Grok, but only Grok's assistant content supplies an explicit parseable action name. The scan cannot establish that other systems lack text–action conflicts. Six of the nine first-action departures are verified Grok discrepancies; describing them as non-decisions or claiming the prose reveals the real intention would overstep. The tool call remains the applied action under the declared contract.
- **The bargaining association is weakened, not disproved.** “Does not survive composition” and “an honest null” are too strong if read as evidence of no effect. Adjustment changes the magnitude and leaves uncertainty spanning zero. The exploratory association and the negotiated conduct remain reportable; they do not identify an effect of completion.
- **Rulebook-compatible beliefs do not establish a universal recall mechanism.** Gemini's interest motive is a clear local example. The broader statement that agents simply play by the rulebook would overgeneralize. Describe the documented belief and the actual implementation separately.
- **The cost metric is neither established nor shown saturated.** The search-free sale summaries are descriptive under one ordering. They do not bound the optimal net penalty when mortgage selection, over-raising and released collateral interact. They cannot establish that an oracle would mostly report zero regret. This supports defining the objective carefully, not selecting a predicted result.
- **Counterfactual replay requires explicit context handling, not an absolute same-model-only rule.** Reusing original identity and memory with a different model introduces a condition that must be named. A standardized cross-model context is possible, but is a new diagnostic condition. No such study is authorized here.
- **Keep the original user questions visible without pretending all were answered.** The question map can remain in a concise appendix. External business leaderboards do not by themselves provide a matched general-intelligence measure. The paper's inability to establish learning, transfer, calibrated opponent modeling and a general capability axis is a substantive scope boundary, not a reason to invent answers or erase the questions.
