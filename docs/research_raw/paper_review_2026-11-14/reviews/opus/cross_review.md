## 1. Where the three completed assessments converge

These points are now three-way agreements, each reached independently before any peer reading:

1. **The paper's counts are correct.** Its scientific contribution is modest and observational. It is not a main-track planning or capability result as written, and another rewrite on the same data cannot supply the missing result.
2. **Payment in the sixteen necessity states is guaranteed for any continuing legal cash-raising policy under the frozen rules.** I derived this from the code and the entry states (review §3.1). Sol states it most cleanly, as a conditional proof:
   - Q = cash + remaining salvage + unmortgaged principal is invariant.
   - A finite measure decreases at every step.
   - Ownership and evenness were checked.
   - The single hotel entry has enough bank stock.

   I adopt Sol's formulation. My argument is the same in substance but less explicitly stated. It is code reasoning, not an executed baseline.
3. **The middle 53-case category is supported:**
   - 47 actual no-release routes;
   - five constructive source arguments for G3 d228, d356, d461, d482 and G18 d428;
   - the retained G12 d249 sale-only witness.

   I reached the same five routes independently (review §3.8). The correction is to state the justification, not to change the counts.
4. **Statement and first action are jointly generated.** The note is prospective relative to the menu, not to the decision. The sentence "before choosing whether to proceed" must go.
5. **Both channels must be shown for all sixteen.** The six above-bound starts and the three payable declarations belong in any statement–action analysis. Five of the six starts are Grok channel conflicts. G18 d344 is Gemini acting on an interest charge the engine does not levy.
6. **The carried $130/$30 figures fit a conditional-inventory hypothesis.**
   - The figures equal the currently eligible mortgage values, so "reuse to the dollar" is uninformative.
   - Counterexamples exist: G13, G20 d327, G5, and Opus's successes without notes.
   - There is no causal memory evidence.
   - The planted-number headline is unsupported.
7. **Inference limits.**
   - 16/6,989 is a units error.
   - Neither the Fisher p nor the iid binomial interval should be added as a rescue.
   - Five-block resampling is exact conditional enumeration (3,125 ordered draws, 126 weighted count vectors), not replication.
8. **The surviving bargaining error.** "Negative only when pooled" is false (Grok −0.245). The bargaining census is otherwise sound, composition-sensitive and non-causal.
9. **Novelty.** The broad constructs are established:
   - action reachability;
   - false impossibility;
   - interactive versus plan generation;
   - menu order;
   - verifiers;
   - counterfactual rules;
   - memory error propagation;
   - saved-state replay.
10. **Proposals.** None of the referees' proposals is authorised by this review. Cost figures are historical averages, not budgets.

## 3. Disagreements I retain, with evidence

### RD-1. Neither peer flags that the engine silently departs from the published bankruptcy rules. This matters for every stop-decision claim.

**Both peers frame the interest beliefs as agent errors:**
- Astra: Gemini G18 d344 "describes imposing a mortgage-transfer fee that this engine does not charge", a "mistaken consequence of liquidation".
- Sol: "the stated immediate-interest motive is wrong under this engine".

**What the record and rules show:**
- The Hasbro FAQ I fetched states that the new owner of a bankrupt player's mortgaged deeds must repay or pay the Bank 10% "now".
- `_handle_bankruptcy` transfers deeds and buildings with no charge.
- The system prompt states no rules: "Your role is not to explain rules or debug the system".
- Agents explicitly reason about which regime applies:
  - G9 d195 Grok: "Standard Monopoly rules say … must pay 10% interest";
  - G19 d224: "The engine might enforce different rules";
  - G18 d313 Grok instead expects its creditor to inherit built dark blues;
  - Sol's G12 d190 failure text adds "bankruptcy might transfer property anyway".

**Why I keep this:**
1. Gemini's G18 d344 belief is **correct recall of the published rule** that the engine silently changes. Calling it an agent error puts the benchmark's undisclosed variant on the model.
2. Whether a declaration on a payable debt is a feasibility error or a strategic choice depends on the agent's beliefs about the settlement payoff. Those beliefs are uncontrolled because the variant is undisclosed. Sol's own point that bankruptcy to preserve a reserve "is not unambiguously erroneous under the original strategic prompt" is the same issue from the other side.
3. The paper's framing, "application of a published game rule", is true of the release rule. It is not true of the payoffs that govern stopping.
4. Any future diagnostic must disclose the settlement rules in **every** arm, including control. Otherwise false-infeasibility errors are not identified.

Neither peer's recommendations include this. I regard it as the most consequential omission in both peer reviews.

### RD-2. "Thirteen plus one" should be decomposed by evidential strength before it is called anticipation.

Astra "agree[s] with the broad 13 + 1 + 2 recognition reading". Sol calls it "defensible as a coarse reading" and "a broad selected-field inclusion criterion". Both rightly refuse to make it an accuracy rate.

My objection is different: what the "13" is evidence *of*.

| Tier | Episodes | n | Evidential strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| States the dependency | G1, G2, G5, G8, G18 d309 | 5 | Discriminating |
| States the dependency tentatively | G20 d291 | 1 | Discriminating |
| Gives an ordered route through the released resource | G4 d270, G13, G18 d313 | 3 | Discriminating |
| Inclusive total only | G4 d308, G6, G11, G17 d254 (four Opus), G20 d327 | 5 | Weak |

An agent that ignored the release rule and summed every deed's mortgage value would write the inclusive totals. The phrasing of the Opus texts ("mortgaging those three properties" after selling their houses) and Sol's "sell all development and mortgage everything" suggest linkage. So I do not claim these five are naive; only that they cannot discriminate.

Sol's own per-row table supports the split: it describes the four Opus rows only by their budget arithmetic (for example, "Correct maximal budget" for G4 d308 and "Correct $556 aggregate" for G11), with no dependency statement. The abstract and introduction should attach "anticipation" to the nine discriminating texts and describe the five as inclusive budgets.

My own 12 November reading ("12 explicit") made the conflation I now criticise.

### RD-3. Both peers keep G20 d327 as the central budget illustration. It is the least informative success about planning.

Sol calls it "the strongest budget illustration", and Astra keeps the "positive G20 budget-to-payment sequence". I agree it is the strongest **valuation** example: an exact U\* of $1,414 over 19 assets, independently matched by Gemini's estimate.

But D = U\* − 14, so full liquidation is the only payable end state. The "$14 prediction" is U\* − D, and the two sale calls are bundles. G13 Gemini is the only necessity case with real route choice under slack (U\* = 1,088, D = 750). It plans a specific subset, keeps Boardwalk and Kentucky unmortgaged, and realises the plan exactly.

**Recommendation:** keep G20 d327 as an exact-valuation illustration, labelled as a forced route. Add G13 as the route-selection illustration. This does not reduce the incidents; it relabels one.

### RD-4. The peers' "separable accomplishments" framing should absorb the conditional proof.

**The peers' framings:**
- Astra's central observation lists four separable accomplishments: recognising the dependency, calculating a complete budget, issuing a matching action and reaching payment.
- Sol lists the analogous four observables, including "interactive menus can carry execution to payment".

**The implication not yet drawn.** Both peers accepted the proof. In the sixteen states, "reaching payment" is not separable from "not stopping": it follows from it. The informative structure is:
1. what the first text contains: dependency stated, ordered, inclusive or omitted, plus values and arithmetic;
2. whether the agent stops;
3. whether the text and the applied action agree.

Payment is a consequence, not a fourth measurement. Writing payment as a separate accomplishment re-invites the reading that both peers reject.

### RD-5. The U0 identity makes the conditional-inventory hypothesis exact. Peers should state it.

**The peers' wording:**
- Astra: "reusing a conditional resource inventory as if it were the total after the condition changes".
- Sol: "upgrading that partial resource category to a full liquidation maximum".

That is the hypothesis I framed as the menu-bounded total. **The conditional inventory is U0**, cash plus all salvage plus currently eligible mortgages:
- Both failed sums equal U0 to the dollar ($408 and $530).
- Most successful texts state a U0-type subtotal (G1, G2, G5, G11, G18 d309, G18 d313; G13 states M0) before adding released collateral.
- Several successful provider fields transiently conclude on it: G8, G13, G18 d309, G5, G18 d313.

Sol's row table records these same partial success fields without connecting them to U0. Stating the identity:
1. defines the necessity subset as exactly the payable states on which the U0 total gives the wrong verdict;
2. shows the failures stopped at the same intermediate quantity successful agents pass through;
3. weakens the memory account, because that intermediate appears without the carried reserve frame too.

It remains a description of texts, not a mechanism. It does not make conditional inventory "the next thesis", and I agree with Sol on that.

### RD-6. FinBoardBench also has multi-step liquidation; "liquidation is where you're ahead" is wrong against it.

Neither peer's matrix notes that FinBoardBench's Monopoly includes a fund-raising manager with MORTGAGE, SELL_BUILDING and bankruptcy "when recovery is impossible" (v1 HTML, methods). Both referees, and the peers implicitly, treat the debt-rescue phase as the differentiator against AgentMonopoly only.

The defensible differentiator is narrower: **voluntary declaration on payable debts**, together with the arithmetic certificate and legal witnesses. That is precisely what makes premature stopping observable. If FinBoardBench enforces its "only when recovery is impossible" wording, it cannot observe that error at all; I could not establish whether it does. Related work should say this.

### RD-7. Rules in the first pilot: a position between Astra and Sol.

- Astra would start a pilot "with explicit rules and primitive values".
- Sol would not mandate them, so as to keep the historical information condition.

I would separate two kinds of rule:
- **Payoff-relevant settlement variants** (inheritance interest, buildings passing intact): disclose in every arm. Otherwise stop errors are unidentified (RD-1).
- **The release rule:** this is the treatment if the question is application of an unstated published rule, so it should not be in the baseline.

Supplied values have weak prior motivation. Values are already correct in 15 of the 16 texts; G2 is the exception.

### RD-8. Incidents: compatible positions, one emphasis difference.

Astra and Sol would keep all eight in the release, with main-text selection by venue. I recommended moving most to an appendix. These positions are compatible: keep all eight in the release, choose a short main-text set, and leave the decision to the owner.

I still think both peers under-weight the opportunity cost. The incidents are where the paper's title is least supported by measurement. Their main-text share should be decided after the financing section is restructured, not before.

## 4. Challenges to the peers' overall judgments

- **Astra's whole judgment**: "preserve the present paper as a coherent discovery study and stop changing its central claim after each review". I support the procedural point. But the central claim *must* change once, because the three of us now agree on two things:
  - the payment rate is uninformative about route construction in the focal states;
  - "before choosing" is wrong.

  The abstract currently leads with "Agents pay fourteen" and with ten actions leaving $14. Freezing that headline would preserve exactly the overreading all three assessments reject. The defensible freeze point is the question Astra states in recommendations: "what do the recorded financial assessments contain, and where do those assessments agree or disagree with applied actions and outcomes". The headline numbers should follow it: the U0 stops, the text tiers and the channel conflicts.
- **Sol's whole judgment** is closest to mine. My challenge is RD-4: Sol still lists "interactive menus can carry execution to payment" as a contribution-level observable. After CR-SOL-01 it is a property of the engine, not of the agents, and belongs in the methods section.
- **Neither peer** addresses the undisclosed settlement rules (RD-1), and neither notes FinBoardBench's liquidation phase (RD-6). These two omissions bear directly on what the paper can claim as distinctive and on whether its two failures are judgment errors.
