## What I currently believe about the paper

1. **The paper is honest and descriptively careful, but its scientific yield is small.** The central claim is existential: in 16 episodes, agents' first texts usually include collateral that only a prior sale releases, and they then pay. A careful observational paper can carry a claim like that, but not a 21-page manuscript whose title promises "stated plans and economic actions" in general. Both external reports reach this conclusion from different directions, and I agree with them.

2. **I expect the strongest new critique to be the "never-quit" point, and I expect it to cut differently from how the 4/10 referee states it.** If the liquidation menu is monotone (sales and mortgages never destroy other options, apart from bank-stock effects), then any policy that keeps liquidating until it pays will pay every feasible debt. In that case, payment success is no evidence of sequencing difficulty, and the only decision that discriminates between agents is the stop/continue judgment at each menu. Both failures are exactly that kind of error: a false claim of infeasibility followed by an immediate declaration. I do **not** expect this to mean "the task needs no anticipation." It means the anticipation that matters is the feasibility judgment, not route construction. My earlier position was that quit-or-continue is the informative decision, and I expect this critique to strengthen it, not overturn it. The referee's "16/16 by construction" is an unexecuted counterfactual. Whether it follows from the actual menu semantics depends on the fallback code, on hotel/bank-stock constraints and on whether payment triggers automatically. I must check this by reading the code, not by running anything.

3. **The "13 explicit or aggregate" count is probably too generous as evidence of anticipation, and neither referee says this precisely.** An agent that ignores the release rule altogether and simply adds up every deed's mortgage value would write the same inclusive budget. Only texts that state the ordering or the dependency ("unlock", "after clearing", "must sell before") separate anticipation of a changed menu from naive asset totalling. I expect fewer than thirteen texts to state the dependency. I also expect the two failures to look like menu-literal inventories, not arithmetic errors. Referee A's matrix proposal is the right tool, but the key column is "states the dependency" against "includes the resource".

4. **The statement and the first action are jointly generated.** For the eleven private-note cases, the note and the first action are arguments of the same tool call. Later actions are conditioned on the carried note. "The budget survives ten actions" therefore partly measures self-consistency with a note shown back to the agent. The provider-returned text for Opus is not carried, so Opus's five successes were executed without a carried plan. This undermines "realization of a plan" more than it undermines "prospective statement".

5. **Carried-note number reuse in the failures ($130, $30) may be an artifact.** $130 looks like Baltic plus two light-blue mortgages ($30+$50+$50), and $30 like Baltic's mortgage. If so, the "planted reserve number" hypothesis may only reflect an earlier note computing the same menu-limited inventory. A stale inventory carried forward is a better-formed hypothesis than steering by a reserve figure. I need to read the carried notes.

6. **Bargaining remains the weaker half.** After the Grok correction it carries no finding beyond composition-sensitive description. I expect Referee B's specific bargaining points (small-cell inconsistency between Table 4 and Figure 2; resampling ranges as renamed percentile bootstrap; the n=1 cell) to be largely correct. I expect Referee A to be literally right that "negative only when pooled" is false because one within-recipient value is −0.2. If so, that sentence came out of my own earlier OFR-02 recommendation, and I need to own it.

7. **Referee A's necessity-certificate point is correct in logic.** U0 < D ≤ U* certifies a need for released mortgages, because U0 is an upper bound on what is reachable from the first menu. U0 ≥ D does not certify a route without released mortgages, because nominal salvage can be unrealisable (G7). I expect this to affect at most the five released-use cases in the middle category, and I may be able to settle them by checking hotels and bank stock in the stored entry states.

8. **Rule and recall issues may be a bigger validity problem than the referees frame them.** If the engine charges no interest on inherited mortgaged deeds while the published rules do, and the prompt does not disclose the variant, then agents relying on the published rule are being judged against an undisclosed house rule. That cuts against the paper's own framing that agents "apply a published rule", and against the repository's "no undocumented house rules" policy. I need to verify it in the engine.

## Where I expect to disagree with the reviewers

- **Referee B's "0.2% of the corpus"** compares episodes with decisions, so the units don't match. The right denominators are 16/188 episodes or 16/131 payable debts. The smallness is real, but the rhetoric overstates it.
- **The replay-probe proposal as stated does not hold "identical states" constant across models.** The saved requests contain one model's own first-person notes and history. The Opus standard requests were historically refused 100% after G4. A one-call replay measures only the first-menu decision. That is in fact the estimand that matters for false infeasibility, but it is not a "same-state" comparison of planning or execution.
- **Referee B's call to cite at least 40 references** is a quota, not a scientific requirement. The brief rejects quotas.
- **The minimum-restoration-cost regret is not a neutral optimum.** It encodes a particular objective (rebuild cost plus 10% unmortgage interest) that ignores strategic value, bank stock and the game objective. Naming it "regret" risks reintroducing the value judgement the paper deliberately avoids.
- **"No independent capability axis available"** may be wrong as stated if an external leaderboard lists these identifiers. Even so, an external leaderboard is not a capability axis measured within this design. To be checked.

## Novelty (prior, unchecked)

I expect the literature claims to be largely right in kind: action-reachability benchmarks (ACPBench), false-unsolvability reports in PlanBench-style evaluations of reasoning models, counterfactual-rule recitation tests (Wu et al.), and LLM-Modulo verifier framing. The paper's possible remaining distinctiveness is narrow: false infeasibility over an agent's *own* resources, in a naturally reached multi-agent state with a verifiable legal witness and an irreversible stop action. I won't claim novelty from absence in a few searches.

## What I expect to recommend for the present paper

Shrink and re-centre. Correct the abstract denominator and "reproducible". Annotate G20 d291. Replace "13 + 1" with a matrix that separates dependency statements from inclusive sums. Print both channels for the two failures. Treat the fallback / never-quit point honestly, as a statement about what payment success can and cannot show. Move most bargaining and incident material out of the centre. All new experiments (replays, ablations, reference policies, verifiers) need separate authorization and fresh states. None of them should be run under this brief.
