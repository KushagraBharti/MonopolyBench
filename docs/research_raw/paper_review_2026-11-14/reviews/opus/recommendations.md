## C. Human decisions

1. **Paper identity.** Choose one: a focused financing paper; a two-paper split (financing, bargaining/communication); or keep the current broad scope. The user's stated wish to keep eight incidents in the main text conflicts with focus. Both referees and I would move most of them to an appendix.
2. **Venue and length.** The main text runs about 8,100 words and 12 two-column pages, against a 9-page main-track limit. Target template, anonymity and authorship are unresolved.
3. **Human annotation.** Two human coders for the 16 texts and the 74 refusals. Only this would justify any "validated" language. It is not an evaluation, but it needs people.
4. **Authorise any spending and new runs in B.** Pre-register hypotheses before confirmation data, and keep discovery states separate from confirmation states.
5. **Benchmark versioning.** Decide whether to:
   - disclose the house rules to agents;
   - equalise tool choice;
   - address the Opus refusal regime.

   Each changes the benchmark and requires a new version label.
6. **Release.** Committing or publishing the manuscript package and analysis entry point. The paper currently cites files that the repository does not contain.
7. **Headline discipline.** Do not adopt "premature termination", "interface-induced false infeasibility" or "planted-number steering" as the thesis without authorised new data. The present paper should describe three stop decisions, not a phenomenon.
