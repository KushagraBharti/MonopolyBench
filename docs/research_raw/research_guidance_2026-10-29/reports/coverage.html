These columns describe evidence types, not a universal maturity ladder. A descriptive question can be answered observationally. “Not run” refers to a causal or transfer test of the broad question, not the paper's executable local diagnostics. No completion percentages are assigned.

What to improve for each question
Q1. Strategic reasoning and rationality

Partial descriptive answer

Current evidence. Concrete rival-sensitive choices and certified feasible alternatives.

Gap. No general optimality, regret or exploitability measure.

Writing. Answer the rival-gain trade example and distinguish local payment from eventual victory.

Existing logs. Code the complete eligible trade-response population for stated strategic considerations.

New evaluation. Use held-out states and repeated continuations against defined comparators.

Current manuscript pages: 8–13, 36. All improvements are proposals.

Q2. Opponent modeling and theory of mind

Case evidence only

Current evidence. Source-checked recipient interpretations and tactical forecasts.

Gap. No prediction accuracy, calibration or validated mental-state inference.

Writing. Separate public-state accounting, behavioral prediction and hidden-state inference.

Existing logs. Score genuine pre-outcome forecasts, recording unscorable and selectively expressed cases.

New evaluation. Elicit probabilities against varied known policies and a state-only baseline.

Current manuscript pages: 13–20, 36. All improvements are proposals.

Q3. Cooperation, negotiation and deception

Substantial description; effectiveness untested

Current evidence. 113 settlements, explicit initiation denominators and exact contextual transcripts.

Gap. No strategy-effectiveness, generosity-bias, surplus or fairness evaluation.

Writing. Separate negotiation activity, term quality, influence and instruction adherence.

Existing logs. Reconstruct negotiation threads and code tactics and responses over a defined population.

New evaluation. Randomize message variants while controlling economic terms and information.

Current manuscript pages: 8, 13–20, 37–40. All improvements are proposals.

Q4. Long-horizon planning and memory

Partial trajectory evidence

Current evidence. Acquisition-to-development and liquidation sequences with legal-opportunity accounting.

Gap. No plan-persistence measure or causal memory result.

Writing. Organize existing trajectories by prospective goal, opportunities, actions and revisions.

Existing logs. Track plan follow-through, reserve exposure and factual retention against delivered memory.

New evaluation. Vary relevant memory under controlled resolution policies and matched endpoints.

Current manuscript pages: 5–6, 9–13, 21–22. All improvements are proposals.

Q5. Adaptability and learning

Local corrections; learning untested

Current evidence. Local response corrections are visible and bounded.

Gap. Durable improvement and policy transfer remain unevaluated.

Writing. Do not equate response repair with learning or repeated games with memory carryover.

Existing logs. Track errors, delivered feedback and recurrence at comparable opportunities.

New evaluation. Compare feedback and no-feedback conditions, then test a held-out opponent shift.

Current manuscript pages: 10–11, 22, 36. All improvements are proposals.

Q6. Economic decisions and risk management

Strong local feasibility; partial overall

Current evidence. Complete financing cases and detailed event accounting.

Gap. Expected asset value, optimal reserves and risk-adjusted utility are unmeasured.

Writing. Distinguish certified feasibility and realized receipts from economic optimality.

Existing logs. Decompose ownership episodes, reserve buffers, forced sales and prospective numerical forecasts.

New evaluation. Define utility and horizon, then compare distributions under repeated continuations.

Current manuscript pages: 8–13, 33–35. All improvements are proposals.

Q7. Emergent behavior and collective dynamics

Partial descriptive evidence

Current evidence. Actual coordination attempts and aggregate transfer pathways.

Gap. Coordination incidence, coalition stability and causal emergence remain open.

Writing. Separate individual tactics, shared incentives and collective participation.

Existing logs. Code all appeals and uptake, build typed interaction networks and measure constrained opportunities.

New evaluation. Vary communication or incentive conditions for a prespecified collective hypothesis.

Current manuscript pages: 13, 17–18, 33–36. All improvements are proposals.

Q8. Evaluation validity and generalization

Strong bounded validity; transfer untested

Current evidence. Reproducible checks for specified records and claims.

Gap. Cross-opponent and cross-condition generalization is untested.

Writing. Separate fidelity, claim validity, reliability and external transfer.

Existing logs. Report game/block sensitivity and blind re-adjudication of a defined claim set.

New evaluation. Use independent repetitions, diverse opponents, held-out conditions and policy controls.

Current manuscript pages: 6–8, 21–24, 26. All improvements are proposals.

A. Strategic skill versus strategic-sounding text

Strong narrow answer; broad distinction unresolved

Current evidence. Defined populations establish that strategic text is insufficient evidence of executed policy.

Gap. Coherent behavior does not establish genuine or high-quality strategy.

Writing. Separate proposition truth, plan value, encoding validity, action origin and consequence.

Existing logs. Code prospective plans and score their quality separately from execution alignment.

New evaluation. Pair independent strategic-knowledge measures with matched held-out decisions.

Current manuscript pages: 6–8, 9–13, 22, 37–40. All improvements are proposals.

B. General intelligence versus multi-agent intelligence

Not evaluated

Current evidence. The paper identifies the design gap; it supplies no empirical answer.

Gap. No independent capability measure or controlled tiers.

Writing. Label this untested and distinguish it from capability versus alignment.

Existing logs. Define outcomes and feasibility only; these logs cannot supply missing variation.

New evaluation. Measure several family tiers independently and test multi-agent predictions out of sample.

Current manuscript pages: 23–24, 26. All improvements are proposals.

C. Skill versus luck and exploitation

Partial local attribution; outcome decomposition absent

Current evidence. Some invalid explanations of individual decisions can be ruled out.

Gap. Winning cannot yet be apportioned among strategy, randomness and opponent mistakes.

Writing. Distinguish local attribution from whole-game skill measurement.

Existing logs. Count objectively defined errors and realized opportunity/transfer mechanisms.

New evaluation. Use held-out tasks, repeated continuations and quality-checked diverse opponents.

Current manuscript pages: 4, 9–13, 23–26. All improvements are proposals.

Read-only assessment of the current 40-page draft. The manuscript and outline remain unchanged; no proposed analysis or experiment has begun.
