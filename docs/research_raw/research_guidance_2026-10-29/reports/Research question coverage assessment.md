# The paper answers narrower questions well

**No, the current paper does not yet answer all eight core questions or all three deeper questions.** It provides substantial evidence about economic execution, legal financing, observed negotiation, and the validity of specific behavioral claims. It has useful examples of strategic interaction, but little systematic measurement of opponent prediction, persistent adaptation, or planning quality. It does not test whether general intelligence predicts multi-agent ability, or whether the observed results transfer to unfamiliar opponents. Its strongest contribution is establishing when the records justify a particular interpretation, including when an apparent strategic choice was actually a default or a conflict between response channels. That contribution should remain, but it is not a substitute for the broader research agenda in the six screenshots. This assessment proposes future work only; the manuscript and outline have not been revised.

The three reviewers read the screenshots and current relevant manuscript sections, then challenged the initial coverage judgments. Two qualifications matter. Question A has a strong answer about the limits of strategic-sounding text, but not a general test of strategic intelligence. ## Coverage depends on the question actually tested

The categories below describe the evidence, not model scores or completion percentages. A complete census of a narrow event can be strong while the broader ability question remains open. A selected case can establish that something happened without estimating how often it happens. A proposed experiment, an implemented tool, or a limitations paragraph does not count as an empirical answer. The companion chart's columns describe evidence types, not a universal maturity ladder: a defined descriptive question can be answered observationally without a causal experiment.

## The eight core questions need different improvements

### Q1. Strategic reasoning and rationality

**Current coverage: Partial descriptive answer.** The screenshot asks whether agents understand the game, compare alternatives, and maximize their objectives. Its specific example is answered: the paper shows a player rejecting a trade that would complete its own set because it would also fund a dangerous opponent. In G11 d258, Grok rejects genuine pink completion while citing Sol's prospective greens and construction cash. Other examples show chosen cash reserves and successful or failed financing. These support rival-sensitive reasoning in particular decisions, not a general rationality score. See Section 4, pp. 8–13, and the [G11 chronology on p. 36](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/social_detail.tex).

The strongest systematic evidence concerns local feasibility. All sixty eliminations are classified against current-debt financing, and construction outcomes distinguish missing opportunities, defaults, conflicts and valid choices. Paying a current debt is a precise endpoint; maximizing eventual victory is a different objective. A legal alternative need not be the best strategy. [Economic results](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/economic_results.tex) and [certificate results](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/data/certificate_results.json).

**Writing improvement:** state the bounded answer directly and pair successful rival-sensitive reasoning with a failed feasibility forecast. **Existing-log improvement:** code all eligible trade responses for own completion, rival completion, rival funding, liquidity and timing, with source channels and denominators. This measures recorded considerations, not their causal influence. **New evaluation:** use held-out states, a declared objective and credible reference policies, then estimate uncertain action values through repeated continuations. Regret needs a specified comparator; exploitability needs a defined opponent class and solution concept. Neither can be obtained by renaming win totals or cash differences.

### Q2. Opponent modeling and theory of mind

**Current coverage: Case evidence only.** The paper records forecasts and interpretations of rivals, but does not score their accuracy or calibration. Public cash and holdings make some forecasts tactical accounting rather than inference about hidden beliefs. G20's cash-denial appeal and G11's rival-funding refusal show incentive awareness; they do not establish accurate theory of mind. Section 5, pp. 13–20, checks delivered information and recipient responses, while [setup](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/setup.tex) defines the information available.

A retained G7 audit exposes an especially useful distinction. Grok says Opus cannot afford three houses on each pink, but an actual management opportunity permits railroad mortgages and the construction. Opus nevertheless fails to build because of a default. Scoring only the observed non-build could reward the false inability forecast. That case is available in the [retained finance audit](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_phase2_2026-10-28/core_review/sol/g07_sample_finance_check.json), but is not a systematic opponent-modeling result in the paper. The screenshot's example of recognizing deliberate auction cash retention remains untested.

**Writing improvement:** distinguish prediction of feasible actions, prediction of actual choices, and inference about knowledge or intentions. **Existing-log improvement:** extract falsifiable pre-outcome forecasts with target, horizon and delivered information; score deterministic claims and subsequent behavior separately. Report unscorable statements and selective expression. Qualitative prose does not supply probabilities for calibration. **New evaluation:** elicit prospective probabilities against controlled, held-out opponent policies and compare with a state-only predictor. Known information asymmetries can test knowledge inference; another model's private note is not a gold-standard mental state.

### Q3. Cooperation, negotiation and deception

**Current coverage: Substantial description; effectiveness untested.** This is one of the most developed topics. Section 4.1, p. 8, reports 526 proposals and 113 settlements with initiation and response distinctions. Section 5, pp. 13–20, includes source-checked concealment, withdrawn assurances, coordination appeals, misleading obstacles and bargaining limits. The transcripts also preserve premise correction, rejection and execution conflicts. These establish observed conduct, not a rate of successful deception or a stable bias toward agreement. [Negotiation results](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/negotiation_table.tex) and [contextual transcripts](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/transcript_conduct.tex).

The paper does not show that G13's framing caused a higher price, that G18's assurance induced a costly action, or that G7's word "final" prevented another counteroffer. Nor does it define generous trades, joint surplus or fairness. An above-list payment can have strategic value, and a received proposal against an abort instruction cannot automatically reveal a coherent preference. Public deception is permitted by the task, so concerning play and instruction violation must remain distinct. [Conduct contract](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/social_contract.tex).

**Writing improvement:** separate negotiation activity, term quality, tactical influence and adherence. **Existing-log improvement:** reconstruct negotiation threads; report settlement per thread, acceptance per offer and activity per eligible turn; code a defined population of tactics and recipient responses. Add immediate economic changes and declared outcome windows without calling them effects. **New evaluation:** randomize disclosure or framing at fixed economic terms, with repeated recipient choices. Measure factual endorsement separately from acceptance. Assess generosity against an explicit outside option or valuation. Bilateral gains can harm third players, so joint surplus and fairness require a stated utility and norm.

### Q4. Long-horizon planning and memory

**Current coverage: Partial trajectory evidence.** Complete games let the paper connect acquisition, development, liquidation and inheritance. Among 63 trade-completed groups, 43 receive construction in the first actual management window. The paper distinguishes absent opportunities from failures or choices. It also preserves valid reserve decisions and later reversals of attempted inheritance denial. These are useful longitudinal findings, but no sustained-plan completion rate or planning-horizon measure is reported. See Sections 3.2 and 4.2–4.4, pp. 5–6 and 9–13, in [economic methods](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/economic_methods.tex) and [results](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/economic_results.tex).

Memory availability is measured more directly than memory ability. Section 6.2 reports missing or synthetic Opus private-memory windows and corrupted trade timelines. Those are information conditions, not randomized treatments. A long episode does not prove long-horizon planning, and final reward does not isolate memory. [Validation, pp. 21–22](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/validation.tex).

**Writing improvement:** organize an existing whole-game account around prospective goal, required opportunities, applied steps, revisions and endpoint. **Existing-log improvement:** trace explicit plans across later eligible opportunities; distinguish abandonment, justified revision, blocked execution and absent opportunity. Measure duration in own turns and opportunities. For the screenshot's liquidity example, report cash and reachable liquidation buffers before optional spending, subsequent forced sales and exposure duration. Check factual retention only where earlier information was actually delivered, accounting for corrupted memory. **New evaluation:** vary relevant history or memory representation under controlled resolution policies and compare matched plan endpoints and complete continuations. This is required for a causal memory claim, but not for a valuable descriptive plan-follow-through study.

### Q5. Adaptability and learning

**Current coverage: Local corrections; learning untested.** The paper shows revised proposals and recovery after invalid construction attempts. These are local corrections. It does not establish durable strategy improvement, adaptation to unfamiliar opponents, or learning across games. Section 4.2, pp. 10–11, measures whether some legal construction followed a rejection and whether the purchase multiset was preserved. G11's corrected offer on p. 36 illustrates a factual revision, not a learning curve. [Recovery data](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/data/t07_build_recovery.json) and [G11 account](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/social_detail.tex).

A forced liquidation after rent is a reaction to a changed state; it need not show a learned policy. Repeated games also do not imply knowledge carried between them. Unequal delivered feedback and missing private notes further complicate comparisons. The screenshot's example of becoming more conservative after repeated exploitation is not currently tested.

**Writing improvement:** explicitly call this an unanswered capability question, while retaining local correction examples. **Existing-log improvement:** build an error-feedback-repeat-opportunity record. Identify the feedback actually seen, its continued presence in memory, and the next comparable opportunity. Separate state-driven changes from documented revisions and keep cases without a later opportunity. **New evaluation:** vary feedback or persistent memory, repeat identifiable strategy challenges, and test a held-out opponent shift. Prespecify improvement and compare with no-feedback conditions. Cross-game adaptation requires an explicit memory-carryover condition; weight learning requires training. No such condition is supplied by the present cohort.

### Q6. Economic decision-making and risk management

**Current coverage: Strong local feasibility; partial overall.** The paper is unusually detailed about budgets that can execute, current-debt financing and realized flows. It reports 103 spending units, game-currency expenditure of $103,450, $93,000 whole-game developed paid rent and $92,550 after first own spending. Those totals are deliberately not profit or incremental return on investment. Inheritance, ownership changes, censoring and unpaid terminal demands prevent that shortcut. Section 4 and the [economic appendix, pp. 33–35](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/economic_detail.tex), supported by the [corpus audit](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_phase2_2026-10-28/core_review/sol/corpus_economics_audit.json).

The screenshot's $400 purchase question remains unanswered in its ex ante sense. A sound purchase can earn no rent because nobody lands there; a poor purchase can benefit from luck or inherited assets. The G20 comparison between preserving brown income and liquidating it to finance oranges demonstrates a feasible tradeoff, not which portfolio has higher expected value. Strong feasibility checks therefore do not justify a high overall economic-rationality rating.

**Writing improvement:** state the measured/unmeasured split beside the results. **Existing-log improvement:** decompose acquisition, interest, redemption, construction, receipts, salvage and estates by ownership episode and exposure. Report reserve buffers, forced-sale losses and prospective numerical forecasts with clear denominators. **New evaluation:** define utility, horizon and opponent distribution, then compare actions against credible policies through repeated stochastic continuations. Report distributions, bankruptcy risk and regret relative to that comparator. Reference policies already exist, but their use for continuation validation is not a matched economic-baseline evaluation. Such new evaluations, even unpaid ones, have not begun here.

### Q7. Emergent behavior and collective dynamics

**Current coverage: Partial descriptive evidence.** The G20 cash-denial appeal is an observed attempt to coordinate. Prior independent refusal and later incompatible cash offers prevent treating it as an established cartel. The paper also documents estate concentration: winners are creditors in 54 of 60 eliminations, including 35 of 40 nonterminal ones. These are collective trajectory facts, but they do not identify the effect of coordination or inheritance on winning. [Transcript 3, pp. 17–18](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/transcript_conduct.tex) and [economic detail](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/economic_detail.tex).

Retained house-stock traces identify zero houses in nine games, but stock exhaustion is not a count of strategically denied construction. Missing cash, incomplete ownership and legal workarounds matter. The current paper has no coalition-frequency or stability estimate. "Emergent" also needs a definition beyond striking conduct; supplier-style price wars do not directly map onto fixed Monopoly rents. [Retained stock report](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_phase3_2026-10-29/fable/exhibits/house_stock_report.json).

**Writing improvement:** distinguish individual tactics from collective patterns and define cooperation, coalition and collusion. **Existing-log improvement:** code coordination invitations, actual exposure, acknowledgment, compatible actions, dissent, reversals and duration. Construct typed trade, funding, rent and estate networks. Count supply-constrained opportunities separately from stock-zero observations and multi-step workarounds. **New evaluation:** vary communication, opponent composition or explicitly versioned rule conditions to test specified collective mechanisms. Common incentives can produce parallel behavior without communication causing it. A no-communication comparison is useful for a defined hypothesis, not a universal collusion detector.

### Q8. Evaluation validity and generalization

**Current coverage: Strong bounded validity; transfer untested.** The paper can establish that a particular label is unsupported or mechanically false. Its retained 126-case suite covers sixty eliminations and sixty-six defaults, and its reproducibility checks support the specified instrument. Section 6, pp. 21–23, carefully limits replay and branch claims. That is meaningful validation. It does not establish that the benchmark measures general intelligence or transfers beyond the studied cohort. [Claim protocol](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/claim_protocol.tex) and [validation](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/validation.tex).

The design has five seed blocks with four rotations, fixed opponent identities, unequal response policies and uneven channel availability. It does not test stronger or unfamiliar opponent pools, new games, or rule familiarity. Thousands of decisions are not thousands of independent trials. A disclaimer about those limits is not an empirical answer to them. [Cohort export](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/data/t01_cohort.json) and [limitations, p. 26](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/limitations.tex).

**Writing improvement:** separate record fidelity, claim validity, repeated-run reliability and external transfer. **Existing-log improvement:** show per-game and seed-block sensitivity and evaluate a fixed, blinded claim set against a defined baseline annotation procedure. Neither decision-level resampling nor fragile five-block intervals establish population rankings. **New evaluation:** add independently sampled repetitions, diverse opponents, held-out conditions and controlled response policies. Rule perturbations require checks that the changed instructions are understood. Complete-system comparison and base-model comparison are different targets; exclusions based on refusal cannot silently redefine the population.

## The three deeper questions expose the largest gap

### A. Genuine strategy versus strategic-sounding text

**Current coverage: Strong narrow answer; broad distinction unresolved.** This is the closest match to the paper's central contribution. Of 663 applied END actions on construction menus, sixty-six are defaults and twenty-four have detected selected content/tool-name conflicts. The remaining 573 are not automatically verified strategic holds. Reachable construction targets and the G12 bankruptcy/sale conflict show why text cannot simply be read as executed policy. Section 4.2, pp. 9–11, and [resolution data](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/data/resolution_conditions.json).

But consistency is not strategic quality. A model can execute a poor plan consistently; a correct recommendation can coexist with confused arithmetic. The paper does not determine how much genuine planning occurs internally or which participant has more strategic intelligence. This is why the reviewers narrowed an initially strong coverage label rather than endorsing the whole dichotomy.

**Writing improvement:** explicitly distinguish proposition correctness, target quality, encoding validity, action resolution and consequence. **Existing-log improvement:** code a declared plan-to-action population, score plan quality separately from execution and retain missing channels. **New evaluation:** pair independently scored strategic-knowledge tasks with matched held-out decision tasks under controlled resolution conditions. Compare explanation and action formats without treating either channel as privileged access to internal thought. Asking for more reasoning alone is not a validated knowledge test. [Method boundaries](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/methods.tex).

### B. General intelligence versus multi-agent intelligence

**Current coverage: Not evaluated.** The paper has no independently measured general-capability axis or controlled capability tiers. Four heterogeneous configurations at one time point cannot establish whether mathematics, coding or reasoning predicts negotiation, opponent modeling or multiplayer performance. The discussion proposes a related capability-alignment design, but multi-agent performance and honesty are different dependent variables. [Discussion, pp. 23–24](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/discussion.tex).

**Writing improvement:** label the relationship untested and distinguish it from capability versus alignment. **Existing-log improvement:** use the cohort to define outcome measures and estimate feasibility, not to infer a missing capability relationship. Adding external leaderboard scores to four names would not solve the confounding. **New evaluation:** sample multiple capability levels within several families, measure independent abilities under documented conditions, and evaluate a prespecified multi-agent battery with varied opponents and seeds. Account for family dependence, compute and response policies; test predictions on held-out models or conditions. The needed sample size follows the desired precision and effect, not an arbitrary game-count promise.

### C. Strategic skill versus luck and exploitation

**Current coverage: Partial local attribution; outcome decomposition absent.** The paper isolates specific execution failures and feasible alternatives, but does not estimate the contribution of skill, favorable movement or opponent mistakes to winning. One seed block is swept by Sol from every seat; another by seat 2 across all configurations. These patterns challenge simplistic rankings without establishing a seat or luck effect. Same seeds also do not preserve equivalent opportunities after actions change random-draw consumption. Section 3.1, p. 4, Figure 1, p. 9, and [limitations](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/limitations.tex).

**Writing improvement:** distinguish local error attribution from whole-game skill measurement. **Existing-log improvement:** count objectively checkable errors, stated-plan execution and realized transfer routes with fixed denominators. These identify mechanisms, not counterfactual winners. **New evaluation:** combine certified held-out local tasks, repeated action-value continuations and cross-play against varied, quality-checked opponents. Six reference policies and 130 scenario files exist, but neither their presence nor validation continuations constitute a capability tournament. Historical future draws cannot be assumed unchanged after a policy intervention. The endpoint must determine the strength of the conclusion. [Validation](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/validation.tex) and [implemented-versus-measured ledger](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_phase3_2026-10-29/opus/exhibits/affordance_ledger.md).

## The metrics and paper balance need an explicit plan

The screenshots' metrics are useful prompts, but several require definitions before use. Win rate and game score need a sampled population, independent unit and horizon. Prediction accuracy needs pre-outcome targets; calibration additionally needs probabilities. Agreement rate needs an offer or thread denominator. Individual payoff, joint surplus and fairness need a utility, outside option and norm. Final reward, resource efficiency and failure frequency need to distinguish opportunity, voluntary choice and resolution failure. Adaptation needs delivered feedback and later comparable opportunities. Profit, risk-adjusted return and reference-policy deviation need a complete accounting boundary and ex ante comparator. Cooperation frequency, coalition stability and collusion need episode, uptake, duration and norm criteria. Seed variance, confidence intervals and opponent-adjusted rankings need a design that actually varies those factors. The [validity review's metric table](../research_notes/Research%20question%20coverage%20assessment/validity.md) records these requirements in detail.

The current severe transcripts improve inspectability, but they now occupy roughly pp. 15–19, with controls on pp. 37–40. Opponent modeling, adaptation and sustained planning lack comparable question-led measurement. If these screenshots are the foremost agenda, future work should stop expanding only the alarming cases. Preserve those cases and their counterexamples, then connect them to complete populations, ordinary decisions and successful behavior. Otherwise the paper risks becoming a vivid conduct account while leaving several of the user's main questions largely untouched. [Economic/social assessment](../research_notes/Research%20question%20coverage%20assessment/economics_social.md).

A later revision should first make this question-to-evidence map explicit. The next analytical priority is a reusable temporal record connecting forecasts, plans, feedback, available opportunities and subsequent actions. Separate complete negotiation and coordination coding would strengthen Q3 and Q7. These existing-log projects can answer defined observational questions without claiming causation. The next experimental priority is held-out local ability tests and diverse-opponent evaluation, followed by targeted memory, framing and capability designs. This order reuses the current paper's source discipline while directing effort toward the weakest answers. It is a proposed program, not work begun by this assessment.

No single unrestricted question here is resolved by a stronger narrative or a more severe example. The useful standard for the next paper is that every central question has an explicit target, a suitable measurement, a result with a denominator, and a stated boundary. Where data are missing, the paper should give a clear non-answer and a testable design. That is more informative than either declaring all eleven complete or abandoning the substantial narrow results already established.
