## Q8: Are we measuring valid abilities that generalize?

### Takeaway

The paper provides substantial evidence for the validity of specified mechanical and source-attribution claims. It provides only limited evidence for the much broader screenshot question about genuine intelligence, familiarity, luck, opponent weaknesses, and generalization. The appropriate coverage label is **strong for bounded claim validity; unanswered for transfer to new opponents and conditions**. Treating those as one completed research question would overstate the paper.

### Inferences

**What we can answer now.** For this cohort, some common readings of logs are wrong: an END need not mean chosen restraint, a declaration need not mean payment was impossible, and acceptance need not mean endorsement of a false premise. The paper gives population-level mechanical counts and original-checked social counterexamples for these statements. That is a meaningful evaluation-validity answer, but it is narrower than asking whether a benchmark measures general intelligence. [Economic results](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/economic_results.tex), [social results, lines 16–20](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/social_results.tex:16)

**Writing-only improvement.** State this split explicitly in the future question-to-evidence map. Separate record/replay fidelity, validity of a particular label, reliability across repeated runs, and transfer to another population. A reader should never have to infer that a clean build is not evidence for opponent-general strategic skill. This would synthesize existing material rather than require a new experiment.

**Existing-log analysis that could help.** Report descriptive seed-block and per-game sensitivity for the finite cohort, keeping configuration and eligibility denominators visible. A leave-one-block-out summary could show whether a finding is concentrated in one block. It would be sensitivity analysis, not independent replication or a calibrated confidence interval for all future games. Define and code complete populations of objectively checkable statements, including ordinary cases, instead of adding more severe excerpts. A blinded re-adjudication of a fixed claim set could test the value of the audit procedure against an explicitly defined baseline labeler. That needs new annotation work, not necessarily new game or model calls.

**New experiments required for the screenshot's generalization claim.** Evaluate additional independently sampled seeds and repeated runs, varied opponent pools, held-out opponent policies, rules or boards, and deliberate configuration controls. Separate the comparison of complete systems from a model-only comparison. Crossed model-by-response-policy conditions can diagnose policy effects if executable and ethically/provider-compatible; simply dropping refused games would select a different population. If game familiarity matters, controlled rule perturbations or unfamiliar structurally similar games need reliable instruction checks and transfer tasks. These are proposed designs, not work performed here.

### Gaps

- No unfamiliar-opponent test, independent opponent-strength manipulation, alternative-game transfer, or general familiarity control is reported. The screenshot's example, whether a winner still wins against stronger or unfamiliar opponents, is unanswered. Ordinary decision-level bootstrap intervals would be pseudoreplication. Block-level resampling with only five blocks would still be a fragile population inference, especially for a selected cohort. This is a methodological judgment from the dependence described in Section 3.1, not a new statistical test. ## Deeper C: Can we separate strategic skill from luck and exploitation?

### Takeaway

Coverage is **partial for local error attribution, unanswered for a general skill-versus-luck decomposition**. The paper can remove certain invalid explanations of a particular decision. It cannot yet estimate how much winning comes from strategic competence, random movement, opponent mistakes, or interaction among them. Acknowledging the ambiguity is scientifically necessary, but is not an empirical answer to the decomposition.

### Inferences

**Writing-only improvement.** Give the current bounded answer directly: legal-sequence and provenance audits identify specific feasible choices, errors, and failures to execute; they do not assign a strategic-skill score. Distinguish beating a weak policy, exploiting a particular opponent, adapting to diverse opponents, and selecting high-value actions under uncertainty. These can all matter, but they are different capabilities.

**Existing-log extensions.** A defined census can count factual rule mistakes, infeasible budgets, received conflicts, missed payment routes, or consistency of an explicit plan through later opportunities. A descriptive event ledger can attribute realized cash and property transfers to purchase, rent, trade and inheritance. None requires new paid games. It can describe realized opportunities and mistakes without assigning a counterfactual winner. Code the criterion before examining the target cases, preserve channels, and report denominators rather than counting candidate labels as episodes.

**New experimental extensions.** Held-out fixed-state tasks can compare action selection and execution against certified local endpoints. For uncertain long-horizon choices, evaluate candidate actions under declared opponent continuation policies across a distribution of future random draws. Do not replay the historical future as if other agents' choices and draw consumption would remain fixed after the intervention. A replicated cross-play design with stronger, weaker and unfamiliar opponents can measure performance robustness and opponent-specific exploitation. The available scripted policies need quality checks before they become meaningful reference standards; a weak baseline is not an optimality oracle.

**The endpoint must govern the claim.** Paying the current debt can be measured exactly in a finite financing class. Maximizing eventual win probability requires a defined policy class, future randomness and opponent behavior. An action can be locally legal and still strategically poor; surrendering a payable current debt does not prove that continuing was optimal. This distinction already appears in the manuscript and should remain central to proposed improvements. [Local-sequence method, lines 44–54](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/economic_methods.tex:44)

### Inferences

#### The strongest objection to calling A fully answered

The evidence defeats a shortcut, not the whole alternative hypothesis. When a returned explanation says build but the tool says END, we learn that the explanation cannot be read as the executed plan. We do not learn whether the model formed a sound strategic policy internally. Likewise, executing a reordered purchase multiset proves reachability, not that the purchase maximizes expected wins. A cleanly chosen but suboptimal action may still involve real strategy; an incoherent explanation may accompany a good move by chance. Therefore A should be presented as a strong contribution to **how to test the distinction**, with a bounded empirical demonstration, rather than proof that the models are genuinely strategic or merely rhetorical. [Channel method](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/methods.tex:11), [certificate scope](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/claim_protocol.tex:25)

#### Which screenshot measurements fit, and which need definitions

| Suggested metric | What is needed before using it here |
|---|---|
| Win rate and game score | Define the sampled population, independent unit, horizon and truncation. Existing win counts describe this cohort. A new scoring rule cannot silently replace last-nonbankrupt-player utility. |
| Regret | Define the policy comparator, the agent's information, opponent policies and randomness. A locally inferior cash figure is not full-game regret. |
| Exploitability | Define the adversary or response class and solution concept for a four-player stochastic game. Opponent mistakes in one record are not an exploitability estimate. |
| Prediction accuracy and calibration | Extract genuine pre-outcome predictions with an identifiable target and horizon. Numerical calibration needs probabilities; do not invent confidence for qualitative prose or infer hidden belief. |
| Agreement rate | Choose offer-response, thread or completed negotiation as unit; distinguish accepted counters from the original proposals. State whether incomplete or expired threads count. |
| Individual payoff, joint surplus and fairness | Choose utility and a reference allocation or counterfactual. A bilateral gain in productive assets may harm third players, and a completed monopoly alone is not surplus. In a winner objective, joint cash growth and joint winning probability are different quantities. |
| Final reward, resource efficiency and failure frequency | Define failure type, legal opportunity and who authored the outcome. Code channels and defaults. Long episodes do not prove planning. |
| Improvement against repeated opponents | Define memory carryover, feedback, exposure and stable opportunities. The current twenty-game ordering is not an identified learning sequence. |
| Risk-adjusted return and deviation from a reference policy | Measure uncertain ex ante value and liquidation risk at the decision, with an explicit policy standard. Observed developed rent divided by spending fails because inheritance, censoring and unpaid terminal demands matter. |
| Cooperation frequency, coalition stability and collusion | Define observable episode criteria, recipients, follow-through, duration and the applicable norm. An invitation without uptake does not establish a coalition, and authorized bargaining is not automatically an instruction breach. |
| Seed variance, confidence intervals and opponent-adjusted ranking | Preserve seed-block/game dependence; add a sufficiently broad sampling design before claiming population ranking. Fixed opponents do not identify performance against unfamiliar policies. |

These are proposed operational definitions, not findings about any cited external paper. They follow the distinctions already required by the current method. [Methods](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/economic_methods.tex), [social method](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/methods.tex)

#### What can improve without new controlled games

Controlled experiments are not required for every extension. Population-defined observational questions can use the retained records: how often a stated prediction is correct; how many false public claims have conflicting private text; how often a specific error recurs after delivered correction; how frequently a stated plan is attempted; whether a promise is later fulfilled under its declared conditions; and how many denials, invitations or agreements meet a predeclared episode definition. Each needs a finite target population, explicit channels, missing-data accounting, source verification and a denominator. The result would describe observed behavior, not a causal effect or hidden mental state.

Some measures need additional elicitation but not full games. Fixed-state forecast questions and strategy-execution probes could create common observable targets, provided they are held out and use controlled information. That changes the task and must be versioned. Source-derived examples are useful to design such tasks but should not simultaneously be the only evaluation set.

New controlled continuations are needed for claims such as a message caused a better price, memory restoration improved survival, fixing a tool policy improved play, or an action increased eventual win probability. Broader sampling and independent capability measurements are needed for generalization and B. A controlled intervention is also not enough if the outcome or norm remains vague.

#### Suggested priority for a later revision

1. First make the question-to-evidence map explicit and give direct bounded answers. This is a writing change and can expose rather than conceal the largest gaps.
2. Define one manageable existing-log population each for opponent forecasts, plan follow-through, and adaptation after feedback. Feasibility should be checked before promising an exhaustive coding project; some predictions lack a testable target.
3. Retain the strongest current census results and give selected severe transcripts the proper role of cases, not prevalence or causal proof.
4. Plan new held-out fixed-state and diverse-opponent evaluation only after defining the intended constructs. Prioritize a credible measurement of B or transfer over adding more anecdotes if answering all screenshot questions is the goal.

### Gaps

- I have not independently read every original game bundle for this assessment. I verified the current claims against selected frozen exports and the paper's own scope statements. The substantive source checks supporting individual incidents remain those in the retained reviews, not new work by me.
- This assessment does not endorse the screenshots' paper lists, general claims about what the literature measures, or the bottom screenshot's suitability ratings. I treated the visible questions as the user's agenda. Some screenshot content is cut off or covered by the phone interface.
- There is no honest single numerical completion percentage for these eleven heterogeneous questions. A precise table of established results, unmeasured quantities and required designs is more defensible than an aggregate score.
