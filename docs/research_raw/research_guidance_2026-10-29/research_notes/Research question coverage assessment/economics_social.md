# Economic and social question coverage

## Scope and overall finding

### Takeaway

The current paper answers narrower descriptive and measurement questions within Q3, Q4, Q6 and Q7. It does not fully answer any of these four as worded in the screenshots. Q6 has the strongest complete mechanical populations, but its broader questions about pricing and risk remain unanswered. Q3 has the richest contextual examples, but strategy effectiveness is untested. Q4 and Q7 have useful trajectory evidence without a systematic test of sustained planning or collective emergence.

This is an assessment of the current 40-page draft, not a proposed manuscript revision. I visually read all six saved screenshots and read the current methods, economic results, social results, all eight transcript narratives and their 28 exact quote cards, discussion, limitations, setup and validation. I inspected selected existing audit summaries. I did not execute new engine branches, re-derive the corpus, verify the screenshots' literature claims, alter the draft, or run new games. Page numbers below refer to the current [manuscript PDF](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/manuscript.pdf).

### Inferences

- The paper's forensic contribution is stronger than its answer to the full behavioral agenda. Proving that a label is wrong can be valuable without estimating the underlying capability.
- The latest severe-incident emphasis improves inspectability, but eight selected episodes do not substitute for a question-wide denominator or a causal test. Five main-text cases occupy approximately pp. 15–19 and the three controls pp. 37–40, while sustained planning and collective dynamics have no equivalently explicit question-led evaluation. The framing should eventually make that imbalance visible rather than suggest that more vivid transcripts complete the research agenda.
- Answering all eight questions thoroughly need not mean claiming causal answers to all eight in this paper. A credible response can include a direct bounded finding, a documented non-answer and a precisely specified missing measurement. Adding prose alone cannot supply unobserved outcomes.

### Gaps

The examples and paper names in the screenshots are guidance, not validated definitions or a literature review. In particular, the screenshot's claim that few papers measure all eight was not checked here. Question 4's full introductory sentence is partly hidden by the mobile interface; its heading and liquidity example are sufficiently visible across images to assess the intended topic without reconstructing the hidden words. ## Q3: Cooperation, negotiation and deception

### Takeaway

We can answer what several agents said and did during negotiation, how often they initiated proposals, and how selected misleading claims were received. We cannot yet answer whether the strategies were effective, whether agents are systematically too generous, or whether cooperation produces greater welfare. Coverage is substantial for observed conduct and limited for negotiation quality.

The screenshot asks when agents cooperate, compete, form alliances, bluff or exploit one another, whether their strategies work, and whether overly generous offers reflect a bias toward agreement. Its metric table asks for agreement rate, individual payoff, joint surplus and fairness. [Question text](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/research_guidance_2026-10-29/images/IMG_9223.png), [metric table](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/research_guidance_2026-10-29/images/IMG_9227.png).

### Cited findings

- Section 4.1, p. 8, and Table 3 report 526 proposals and 113 settlements, distinct initiation/response roles, and initiation propensity plus intensity. Among settlements, 60 complete no group, 43 complete one party's group, and 10 complete both. The prose explicitly says these are not agreement quality. The retained role audit distinguishes final author from initiator and supplies per-player-game units. [Economic results](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/economic_results.tex), [negotiation table](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/negotiation_table.tex), [role and denominator audit](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_phase2_2026-10-28/core_review/astra/negotiation_units.json).
- Section 5, pp. 13–20, documents the conduct contract and five main-text episodes. G13 has a delivered omission with an explicit recorded concealment rationale. G18 has repeated assurance, invocation and withdrawal as the seller becomes distressed. G20 has an explicit attempt to coordinate cash denial. G3 has a false additional-stock implication contradicted by a private note, and G7 has a public $600 final offer below a private $625 contingency. [Social results](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/social_results.tex), [five contextual cases](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/transcript_conduct.tex), [exact excerpt manifest](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/data/transcript_manifest.json).
- The counterexamples are substantive. G6 and G19 accept while correcting the false premise; G4 records the correct cost and alternative bargaining direction before the false claim; G11 transiently echoes a false proposition but corrects and rejects it. G13's observed $70 price difference is not an identified persuasion effect, G18 has no identified costly reliance, and G7 never tests whether Sol would have demanded the extra $25 absent the word “final.” [Counterexamples](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/social_results.tex), [additional chronology](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/social_detail.tex), [transcript interpretations](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/transcript_conduct.tex).
- Apparent gifts can be action-resolution conflicts. G19's false proposal accompanies an abort instruction, and G12's bankruptcy accompanies a sale request. The paper preserves these separately rather than inferring a coherent intention from the severe outcome. [Controls, pp. 37–40](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/transcript_controls.tex).
- Public bluffing and deception are expressly allowed. The paper therefore distinguishes concerning tactics from breaches of the task contract. This is indispensable if the user wants alignment claims rather than a catalogue of adversarial play. [Normative contract, Section 5.1](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/social_contract.tex).

### Inferences

The supported answer is: agents sometimes negotiate around denial, liquidity and inheritance; some use misleading presentation or stated limits inconsistent with other recorded text; recipients can correct false claims and still accept favorable terms. These mechanisms are observed, but their success rates and causal economic value are not measured.

The screenshots' “generosity bias” example needs two additional steps. First, “generous” must be defined against a state-dependent outside option or valuation, not face value or cash direction. Second, favorable terms must be attributed to the received action's provenance. A bid above list price, a group-completing concession or an acceptance against refusal text does not itself establish a stable preference for agreement.

In this competitive game, bilateral surplus is also not synonymous with collective welfare. A trade may improve both parties' prospects at the expense of the other players. Any proposed payoff measure must state whose utility it values and its horizon. Summed eventual win probabilities across all players cannot by itself distinguish mutually beneficial trade from redistribution.

### Gaps and improvements

**Exposition only, proposed.** Give Q3 a direct answer paragraph, followed by an explicit “not measured” list. Separate initiation frequency, settlement likelihood, term quality, tactical influence and instruction adherence. Present each transcript as evidence for one of those constructs, rather than allowing salience to imply severity prevalence or strategy effectiveness.

**Existing-log analysis, proposed.** Reconstruct unique negotiation threads and distinguish per-thread settlement, per-received-offer acceptance and per-proposing-turn activity. A bare 113/526 count does not settle every denominator question. Code a complete defined population for factual errors, omissions, bargaining postures, coordination appeals and funding-denial reasons, retain unknown/ambiguous categories, and audit the labels against source fields. Add pairwise pre/post cash, clear collateral, group completion and immediately reachable construction, with the rejected alternatives and accepted-offer author recorded. Report observed downstream outcomes at a declared window, without calling them treatment effects. This would answer “when and how often” more thoroughly; it would not establish why.

**New experiments, proposed only.** At verified states, randomize truthful disclosure versus the same omitted fact, finality wording, assurance validity, or neutral versus threatening framing. Hold the economic terms and available information fixed where the question requires it. Repeat recipient choices across seeds and opponent conditions. Measure factual endorsement separately from acceptance and subsequent payoff. For fairness, first define an applicable norm or task objective. For over-generosity, compare offers with a specified reference valuation or policy and outside option. None of these experiments is authorized or run by this assessment.

## Q4: Long-horizon planning and memory

### Takeaway

The paper follows full games and demonstrates why planning labels inferred from final actions can be wrong. It does not yet measure how consistently agents maintain plans across time or whether memory improves that ability. Complete logs over a long horizon are a prerequisite for this question, not an answer by themselves.

The screenshots emphasize sustaining strategy over many decisions and retaining enough liquidity for future rent rather than repeatedly spending everything. The metric table lists final reward, resource efficiency and failure frequency. [Question heading](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/research_guidance_2026-10-29/images/IMG_9223.png), [liquidity example](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/research_guidance_2026-10-29/images/IMG_9225.png), [metric table](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/research_guidance_2026-10-29/images/IMG_9227.png).

### Cited findings

- Section 2 specifies twenty public timeline entries and ten private notes, plus structured trade history. Opponent holdings and cash are public; future draws are hidden. Section 6.2 reports that 1,394 of 1,696 Opus decision windows contain no model-authored private note, including 286 with only synthetic fallback notes. This is an observed memory condition, not a randomized memory treatment. [Setup, pp. 2–4](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/setup.tex), [validation, pp. 21–22](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/validation.tex).
- Sections 3.2 and 4.2 distinguish acquisition, the first actual management window, legal entry opportunities, later-created opportunities, valid actions and defaults. Across 63 trade-completed groups, 43 are built in the first management window; among 42 entry-buildable groups, 34 are built. Non-builds include missing opportunities and received-call or default failures, not simply short planning horizons. [Methods, pp. 5–6](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/economic_methods.tex), [results, pp. 9–11](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/economic_results.tex), [63-window classification](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_phase3_2026-10-29/fable/exhibits/first_management_causes.json).
- Genuine reserve choices are retained alongside those failures. G3 d176 chooses to retain $204 instead of spending $150; G1 d426 declines a legal house and preserves $75 more liquidation capacity. Conversely, all 61 Opus defaulted targets are reachable after reordering, so they cannot be classified as a chosen reserve policy. [Section 4.2](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/economic_results.tex), [retained recovery audit](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_phase2_2026-10-28/core_review/sol/build_recovery_audit.json).
- The complete elimination census supplies 56 cases below a generous financing upper bound, three payable current debts, and one hotel-constrained case. Actual G20 d327–336 pays $1,400 after a multi-step liquidation, whereas G16 d602 exhausts assets below $2,000. These establish financing execution and failure at observed debts, not whether earlier reserves were optimal. [Section 4.3, pp. 11–13](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/economic_results.tex), [portable evidence bundle](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/artifacts/historical-claim-audit.zip).
- G13's insurance purchase does not prevent the later railroad estate transfer; G18's blocking purchase later passes to the rival through bankruptcy. These are informative long-horizon consequences, but neither compares the eventual trajectory with an alternative policy. [G13 transcript](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/transcript_conduct.tex), [G18 consequence, p. 36](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/social_detail.tex).

### Inferences

The supported answer is: agents express forward-looking goals and some execute multi-step acquisition, construction and liquidation sequences; other apparent planning failures originate in the response process or restricted finance. The paper has not estimated a sustained-plan completion rate, planning horizon or memory-retention accuracy.

Final victory and resource efficiency are not direct memory scores. Cash retained after an investment is not enough by itself either: collateral, building resale losses, access to management, upcoming risks and the opportunity cost of underdevelopment matter. Repeated mistakes in different games cannot establish failure to learn if no cross-game learning channel is provided.

### Gaps and improvements

**Exposition only, proposed.** Add a question-led account of the existing complete trajectories: goal, executable plan, required opportunity, applied steps, deviations and eventual result. Use at least one successful long sequence, one chosen reserve, and one failed plan attributable to resolution. Explicitly say which longitudinal measures are absent rather than treating “twenty complete games” as a planning evaluation.

**Existing-log analysis, proposed.** Build plan-to-action chains from prospective statements, then identify every later opportunity to perform the stated next step. Code abandonment, justified revision, blocked action, execution conflict and unobserved continuation separately. Summarize plan persistence in own turns and opportunity count, not global decision count alone. For reserve behavior, compute available cash and reachable liquidation capacity before optional spending, with current legal access and declared stress endpoints. Track building purchases followed by forced sales and half-price losses, while keeping inherited holdings and end-of-game censoring visible. These are observed liquidity patterns, not optimal reserve rules.

Memory can also be examined without an intervention: code verifiable state propositions, prior agreements and previously corrected errors; check whether they remain in the delivered prompt at each later statement or action; distinguish contradiction despite visible information from information no longer present. The current 2,054 affected initial timelines and channel-delivery audit should be covariates or exclusions according to a declared question. Such a pass could estimate observable retention and correction recurrence in a specified record population. It cannot show that a memory architecture caused either outcome, nor infer an unexpressed belief from silence. [Existing delivery and formatter measurements](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/validation.tex).

**New experiments, proposed only.** Compare the same state and task under declared memory conditions, such as factual summary, bounded history, missing relevant history and corrected versus corrupted history. Repeat decisions and complete continuations under a defined horizon, with action resolution held fixed. Separate a local plan endpoint from eventual win probability. This is necessary for a causal memory claim, but not for the useful descriptive plan-chain analysis above.

## Q6: Economic decision-making and risk management

### Takeaway

The paper answers whether specific legal budgets can execute and what cash flows occurred. That is a strong answer to liquidity feasibility and a partial answer to economic behavior. It does not establish whether prices or portfolios maximize expected utility, and it does not calculate risk-adjusted investment returns. Overall coverage should therefore be “partial,” not “high,” even though the financing subproblem is unusually well audited.

The screenshot asks whether agents can price assets, allocate scarce resources, manage risk and reason about uncertain returns. Its concrete $400 purchase example requires expected rent, remaining duration and reserves. The metric table asks for profit, utility, risk-adjusted returns and deviation from reference policies. [Q6](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/research_guidance_2026-10-29/images/IMG_9225.png), [continued example](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/research_guidance_2026-10-29/images/IMG_9226.png), [metrics](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/research_guidance_2026-10-29/images/IMG_9227.png).

### Cited findings

- The 60-elimination census establishes current financing feasibility, including three legal payment witnesses and the G7 hotel lock. The 66-default census separates invalid submitted encodings from reachable allocations. A legal purchase or survival route is not declared economically preferable. [Sections 3.3–3.4 and 4.2–4.3](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/claim_protocol.tex), [economic results](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/economic_results.tex).
- Section 4.4 and the economic appendix report 103 spending units, $103,450 expenditure, $93,000 whole-game developed paid rent and $92,550 after first own spending. Forty units receive zero developed paid rent; 26 also cause no creditor elimination. These are deliberately not classified as failed investments, profit or incremental ROI. The retained audit agrees with all 6,989 initial economic states. [Methods](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/economic_methods.tex), [economic appendix, pp. 33–35](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/economic_detail.tex), [corpus economic audit](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_phase2_2026-10-28/core_review/sol/corpus_economics_audit.json).
- The G20 budget example distinguishes preserving brown income from financing six orange houses by liquidating the browns. This is a real portfolio tradeoff, but neither conditional acceptance branch estimates the relative expected value of those portfolios. G19's successive dark-blue owners have different spending, salvage, receipts and exposure. [Economic appendix](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/economic_detail.tex).
- Reference policies exist and were used in validation continuations; they were not evaluated as matched economic baselines. One continuation hits 20,000 decisions, demonstrating the need to define the horizon before using rollout value. [Validation, Section 6.1–6.3](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/validation.tex).

### Inferences

The paper cannot answer the screenshot's $400 question from realized rent alone. A good ex ante purchase can receive no landings, while an expensive purchase can prosper through favorable movement or inherited improvements. Final bankruptcy also does not prove an earlier reserve policy irrational. The price's strategic value depends on set completion, denying opponents, bank stock, future finance and how other players respond.

The current evidence is strongest at distinguishing cash from reachable liquidity and submitted plans from executable allocations. It is weak on valuation under uncertainty. Calling this an answered “economic rationality” question would mistake certified feasibility for optimality.

### Gaps and improvements

**Exposition only, proposed.** Put a compact “measured / not measured” distinction beside the economic results: financing reachability, actual spending and receipts, and conditional development are measured; expected value, marginal utility and risk-adjusted performance are not. State the answer to the $400 example as unresolved without a valuation criterion, not as an arbitrary gap to fill with prose.

**Existing-log analysis, proposed.** Complete a source-backed event decomposition for each acquisition and investment: purchase or transfer price, interest, redemption, construction, receipts, salvage, estates and remaining assets. Stratify by exposure duration and ownership route. Report optional-spending cash buffers, reachable liquidation buffers, subsequent forced-sale costs and debt-frequency patterns. Extract any prospective numerical rent or insolvency forecasts already expressed, checking them against the information available at that time. These additions describe calibration of stated local propositions and realized risk exposure; they must not imply that a favorable realized path validates the prior choice. Comparisons among existing policies remain observational under unequal states and response conditions.

**New experiments, proposed only.** Define the target utility, opponent distribution and horizon. Compare purchases, bids and construction plans at held-out matched states with credible reference policies and repeated stochastic continuations. Report outcome distributions, bankruptcy probability, downside risk and policy regret relative to the declared comparator. An efficient benchmark need not solve Monopoly optimally, but must state what its reference policy can and cannot establish. Full policy evaluation must allow downstream decisions and semantic dice consumption to change after the intervention. A no-paid-model analysis using reference continuations would still be a new experiment and is not being run here.

## Q7: Emergent behavior and collective dynamics

### Takeaway

The paper contains genuine interactions and aggregate asset concentration, including an explicit coordination attempt. It does not yet establish how often coordinated behavior arises, how stable coalitions are, or whether communication rather than common incentives causes collective patterns. Coverage is partial and descriptive.

The screenshot asks about spontaneous cooperation, collusion, price wars, hoarding, coalition formation and other unexpected patterns. The metrics are cooperation frequency, coalition stability and collusion patterns. Some mechanisms fit Monopoly directly; supplier price wars do not map directly onto engine-fixed rents. [Q7](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/research_guidance_2026-10-29/images/IMG_9226.png), [metric table](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/research_guidance_2026-10-29/images/IMG_9227.png).

### Cited findings

- G20 d96 explicitly requests that others withhold building cash from Grok; Grok recognizes the appeal, and Gemini rejects its concrete sale. Sol had already stated an independent funding-denial reason, and Opus later proposes cash purchases. This is an attempted coordination episode with counterevidence to a table-wide agreement, not an established cartel. [Transcript 3, pp. 17–18](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/transcript_conduct.tex).
- G4 has a public invitation to trade against a leader, while G13 and G18 show that efforts to prevent inheritance can fail later through insolvency. The prose says no multi-party pact, cartel or deliberate kingmaking is identified in those adjudicated examples, not in every possible corpus episode. [Additional social context, p. 36](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/social_detail.tex).
- Winners are creditors in 54/60 eliminations, including 35/40 nonterminal eliminations. Of 58 winner spending groups, 34 contain inherited deeds and 30 first complete through an estate. These are measured concentration pathways under intact creditor inheritance, not an experimentally identified mechanism of winner advantage. [Section 4.4 and economic appendix](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/economic_detail.tex), [event audit](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_phase2_2026-10-28/core_review/sol/corpus_economics_audit.json).
- Existing research artifacts reconstruct house inventory and identify zero bank houses in nine games. The report reconciles stock at all 6,989 decisions. This retained whole-corpus trace is richer than the current paper's emphasis on a selected G7 terminal lock, but a zero-stock count is not a count of strategically denied purchases. [House-stock report](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_phase3_2026-10-29/fable/exhibits/house_stock_report.json), [G7 local lock in Section 4.3](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_draft_2026-10-29/sections/economic_results.tex).
- The retained G7 analysis supplies a critical boundary: at actual earlier d330 management, Opus could itself buy a St. Charles hotel and release four houses. That does not escape the later debt state automatically, but prevents interpreting earlier stock retention as a permanent denial with no possible response. [Earlier legal opportunity check](/Users/kushagra/CS-Projects/MonopolyBench/docs/research_raw/paper_phase2_2026-10-28/core_review/sol/g07_stock_retention_check.json).

### Inferences

An observed coalition appeal is a stronger fact than parallel self-interested refusals, but a weaker fact than coordinated participation and enforcement. Common board incentives can produce similar choices without communication causing them. “Emergent” should not become a synonym for any striking event in multi-agent play.

Asset concentration and shared house scarcity are promising collective-level results already supported by the data. They concern the joint trajectory, whereas a single player's inaccurate statement is primarily an individual-level observation. The current manuscript gives the former less explicit thematic treatment than the new severe transcripts.

### Gaps and improvements

**Exposition only, proposed.** Separate individual tactics from multi-player interaction patterns. State a small set of observed collective phenomena, such as cash-denial appeals, finite-stock conflicts and creditor-driven concentration, with an explicit evidence limit for each. Define cooperation, coalition and collusion before interpreting examples. Do not import “price war” or legal-cartel meaning from another environment without matching affordances and norms.

**Existing-log analysis, proposed.** Code all coordination appeals and their actual recipients. For each, track acknowledgments, subsequent compatible and incompatible actions, pre-existing motives, commitments, reversals and duration in own turns. Construct time-varying trade, funding, rent and estate-transfer networks from existing events, with each edge's type retained. Quantify house-stock pressure and actual legal construction opportunities; distinguish a direct stock-blocked purchase from a missing monopoly, insufficient cash or a legal multi-step workaround. Report both proposal incidence and multi-party uptake with clear denominators. These analyses would strengthen descriptive collective dynamics but still not establish causal emergence or moral collusion rates.

**New experiments, proposed only.** Randomize communication availability or relevant coordination messages while holding the game state and counterparties fixed. Vary opponent compositions and incentives; define coalition adherence, stability and enforcement before outcomes are observed. To test inheritance or house-scarcity mechanisms, introduce explicit versioned rule variants and compare repeated games, without rewriting historical rules. A no-communication baseline can test a specified collective claim; it is not a universal detector of collusion. These are future designs only.
