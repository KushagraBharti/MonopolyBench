# Astra: complete four-paper cross-review

This review follows the independent full-paper draft and the initial positions in this directory. I read all four whole manuscripts. I did not read the three concurrent peer reviews before writing it. Frozen drafts and canonical sources are unchanged.

## Judgment and central argument

The four papers now contain a scientific argument about agents acting in a competitive economy. The best version combines **rival-aware bargaining, resource-dependent negotiation and successful local financing**. It should explain the relations among these behaviors, with construction and inheritance supplying their economic consequences. It should not become a paper about the research team's corrections, nor four disconnected papers about prices, defaults, bankruptcies and estates.

Fable's new liquidity analysis materially improves my earlier three-paper proposal. Holders with less than $100 reject 17 of 69 completion requests, compared with 120 of 175 at $300 or more. The difference appears in all five seed blocks and within opening and later offers. The low-cash response is usually a counter: 44 of 69, versus 48 of 175 among high-cash holders. Only 8 of 69 low-cash requests are accepted outright. The right headline is that bargaining becomes more open when holders have little cash, not that low cash makes them sell. Low cash, strategic need and insolvency are different quantities.

The within-pair trajectories give this association useful context. Thirteen of fourteen one-sided completing exchanges that followed an earlier refusal or counter by the same holder occurred with less holder cash than at the first such response. The medians fall from $571.50 to $82. This is not a controlled same-offer comparison: nine of fourteen exchanges change the reciprocal deed bundle. It conditions on eventual agreement, and six ending holder balances still exceed $100. It supports a trajectory account, not a cash threshold or causal rule. That distinction can be stated in two sentences beside the result, without repeatedly interrupting the paper.

Opus contributes the better rival-completion comparator and a new concession denominator. Among opening offers, author-only completion is rejected 138/187, compared with 152/238 when neither completes. The 9.9-point difference has the same direction in five blocks and three standard-response systems; Opus reverses. Among repeated offers with unchanged noncash terms but improved cash for the recipient, acceptance is 9/58 for author-only completion and 24/86 for neither. These are 38 and 43 threads, not independent interventions. The complete stage-by-completion table remains essential, while one concise result paragraph explains what these particular cuts add.

The strongest positive execution result is now independently supported by every author: 188 debt episodes, 128 payments, and three inherited cases with a legal payment route despite elimination. Sixty-one payments use multiple applied actions. The systems execute these local financing tasks through the legal menus; this is more concrete than an assertion that they possess or lack general strategic reasoning. I withdraw my own phrase "executed multistep reasoning" in favor of "successful multistep financing."

The integrated paper can therefore make a substantive claim: the recorded systems consider the consequences of trades for rivals, bargaining varies with the holder's own resources, and they usually execute feasible immediate debt payments, while development of acquired groups varies markedly across their trajectories. The study explains observed behavior and its economic setting. It does not assign optimal values to offers or infer a general model ranking.

## Material judgments

### Bargaining and prices

The cash association deserves a lead figure with the entire accept/counter/reject distribution, numerators and denominators. That shows the actual behavioral change. The completion/no-completion comparison and concessions can share a second panel or compact table. Do not show rejection alone and call the complement a release rate.

The cash contrast is not shared uniformly across configurations. Gemini rejects 10 of 11 low-cash completion requests, and Opus almost never rejects. The pooled association survives the author's stated exclusions and stage splits, but should not become a claim that every model follows the same cash policy. Nor does the higher share of completion requests directed at poor holders by itself show deliberate targeting: availability of counterparties, repeated offers and game progression change the denominator. G7 provides a particularly good explicit example of timing a bid to cash; the population count describes where requests occur.

The price results are valuable precisely because the closer comparisons are mixed. Higher seller asks occur in many seller/mortgage and stage strata. Seven same-game, seller and deed overlaps give four higher, two lower and one equal median ask for a completing buyer. First asks instead give five, one and one, because G4 Boardwalk changes during negotiation. Neither estimator holds the whole state fixed. We should show the seven rows and use the median as the declared descriptive summary, with the first-ask sensitivity in a note. Buyer ordering differs by configuration. Do not summarize it as "bids do not rise" or identify a general completion premium.

Opus's proposed mechanism, attention without calibration, remains unestablished. Its nominal post-trade cash bands have 100/134, 32/42 and 6/11 opening refusals. This is not an equivalence test, and available cash divided by house cost omits mortgages, redemption, reserves and group quality. Keep the exploratory table only if it serves a specific discussion. The agents' explanations can name building capacity while the present observations fail to determine whether their responses track it accurately. That is a measurement limit, not proof of a categorical heuristic.

### Sale trajectories and development

Fable's 43 "one-sided sales" are exchanges completing a group for exactly one side. They include barter. In G11 d145 the holder Opus pays $275 and receives New York while giving the completing brown deed; in G16 d133 Grok pays $250 and receives Park Place while completing Gemini's light blues. These are not proceeds-raising sales. Every subsequent-build count in the 43 rows reproduces from canonical events, but the paper must describe what was counted accurately.

Restricting to the 41 exchanges with positive net cash to the holder gives nine winning sellers among thirteen exchanges followed by construction within eight turns, versus one among twenty-eight without it. In the unreduced 43, the reported nine of fourteen and one of twenty-nine are arithmetically correct. The player-game groups overlap: G17 Sol appears in both. Its win is the sole winning observation in the no-build category and one in the build category. Thus the presentation must not imply disjoint cohorts of thirteen and twenty-one players. I prefer a supporting trajectory table rather than an abstract headline, regardless of filter.

Subsequent construction also depends on existing complete groups, survival, remaining opportunities and the seller's whole balance sheet. Cash is fungible. A later building event does not identify the sale proceeds as its cause. Fable's G7 story supports a more precise claim: the seller's note explicitly prices a deed by what it plans to build, and recorded construction follows. The individual plan/action connection is informative without claiming a general causal benefit of selling to rivals.

The development exposure analyses improve the old total-rent finding. Similar group durations and aggregate landing rates make a simple "more time held" explanation less persuasive. They do not remove differences in group quality, landing timing, opponent count, mortgages or construction levels. Replace "the difference does not come from exposure" with the actual duration/rate comparisons. State the per-landing figures as realized receipts, not a risk-adjusted return or treatment effect. A lifecycle figure can show much more than another rent-total bar chart.

### Financing and estates

The debt census is a positive result rather than merely a catalogue of premature bankruptcy. All four derived episode sets agree on identities, debt and paid-versus-eliminated outcomes. My earlier comparison additionally matched all three other authors' available action sequences and closing events where represented. The 128 payments use 180 mortgage actions and 89 sale actions, with no engine fallback. One successful Grok episode contains a conflicting content/call action; the successful system-level payment remains a fact. No new engine execution was performed.

The 131 denominator means demonstrated payment capacity: 128 recorded successful payments plus three previously checked alternative payment sequences. The menu-restricted G7 case is excluded. Explain that denominator once. Avoid using 97.7% as a model-wide competence estimate, while still stating the result plainly.

Estates are consequential even when the creditor was already strong. Fable's first-debt-menu state and Astra/Sol's pre-final-action state can differ from Opus's immediately pre-estate state during liquidation. Book assets, previous rent, cash and building levels measure different aspects of prior position. Select a clearly defined primary measure and give the others as context. Four wins among five initially nonleading creditors do not isolate the effect of inheritance, and fifteen prior leaders do not make it irrelevant. G17's transfer of developed dark blues to a low-cash eventual winner is an informative trajectory, not a counterfactual result.

The 58 estate completions among 85 unsettled directed pairs reuse completion events across pair records and include 37 terminal completions. They are not 58 separate bargaining disputes causally resolved by inheritance. If retained, distinguish 21 continuing-play from 37 terminal pair outcomes and keep this in the appendix. The main estate result should remain the economic pathway, with a concise population table and selected trajectories.

### Communication, LLM reasoning and questions

Eight incidents remain justified. I prefer G2, G15, G7, G19, G20, G12, G13 and G18. Fable's G1 is a useful appendix example; it should not displace G19's clear correction of a false premise followed by acceptance. The main cards should total roughly twenty rather than forty-four. G2 needs the intermediary-resale observation; G7 needs the bid-timing and subsequent construction; G13 needs the omission and buyer response; G18 needs assurance, invocation and withdrawal. Give the state, exact fields, outcome and interpretation without a repetitive mini-audit after each.

The G7 extension matters scientifically. Sol sells a noncompleting deed while rich, later sells a completing deed at a higher price when cash-poor with its own buildings to fund, and both parties build. It defeats a simplistic association of low cash with a cheap or strategically pointless concession. Its settled bluff can remain in two short excerpts. This is stronger economic context than choosing incidents for apparent evil alone.

The text can discuss LLM reasoning through recorded state-dependent considerations and successful or failed execution. It cannot recover an internal reasoning mechanism from a note, measure theory of mind from public-state calculations, or infer learning from changed offers. Keep all eight questions and A–C in a readable map; those without a measured outcome remain explicitly unanswered. The map is coverage, not eleven claimed contributions. Low aggregate acceptance also does not by itself refute a psychological agreement bias; it establishes that most offers were countered or rejected.

### Literature and presentation

The economics should define the strategic object, not decorate the paper. Externalities provide an appropriate reason that a seller cares who obtains a completing deed. They do not show that these agents follow an equilibrium policy. The fire-sale analogy needs more restraint. The AEA article defines a specific constrained-buyer mechanism and a price below appropriate value. Our below-list-price cut is neither of these. Selling above Monopoly list price does not contradict fire-sale theory; selling below it does not demonstrate that theory. I read the primary AEA abstract directly and recommend one conceptual citation or omission, not a claimed theory test.

Cosentino et al. already distinguish counterparty modeling from beneficial use in controlled bargaining. Our attention/execution distinction is an application to multi-party trajectories, not a new conceptual discovery. Fable's claim that FinBoardBench is complemented by "our records add a liquidation menu" risks implying a feature it already has is absent. Describe the actual difference in analysis rather than feature absence unless supported by a versioned implementation comparison.

Fable has the clearest prose flow, but it is the longest main text. Opus also spends too many pages on cards. Astra and Sol are more compact. Use that compactness with the new findings rather than concatenating four manuscripts. Two or three analytical figures, a short pipeline/prompt illustration, four or five main tables and eight compact incidents are sufficient. Detailed prompts, prices, event provenance, response conditions and the full question map can remain in the appendix. A human byline cannot be invented; keep the ownership decision visible in the release notes, with LLM assistance disclosed accurately.

## Own-draft corrections and dissent

I withdraw "executed multistep reasoning" as a population inference and favor successful financing. I add the cash-pressure and concession findings rather than defending my frozen draft's narrower opening. My earlier proposal gave the seller-price contrast too much priority compared with the now-verified cash response distribution. The final discussion should not repeatedly frame findings as "X need not imply Y."

I dissent from Fable's deterministic title, its general cash-clearing account, and the interpretation of post-sale construction as the separation of trajectories. I dissent from Opus's categorical-versus-calibrated mechanism. I disagree with reducing the main incidents below eight solely for a conference-length target the user has not selected. I also disagree with presenting all raw research and reviewer process details in the main paper. They belong in the evidence package.

My proposed title is **Bargaining under Cash Pressure: LLM Strategy and Financial Execution in Monopoly**. A second acceptable title is **Rival-Aware Bargaining and Financial Execution by LLM Agents in Monopoly**. The first makes the new empirical relationship visible; its abstract must immediately identify the study as observational.

## Verification and remaining ceiling

`check_delivered.py` and `check_fable.py` record exact source hashes and independent joins. The latter checks all 920 fresh Fable offer rows against the older ledger, reconstructs all 148 directed pair histories, reproduces the fourteen refusal-to-sale sequences, checks all forty-three subsequent-construction rows against canonical events, and matches all 188 debt episodes to Astra's table. Its initial assertion stopped because one file says `bankrupt` and another `eliminated`; normalization preserves the common meaning and no debt amount differed. The checks add no new semantic labels or game outcomes.

All 39 Fable pages were viewed in contact sheets, as were all 24 Sol and 32 Opus pages in the earlier pass. This is complete layout coverage, not full-resolution checking of every small cell. Source prose, table definitions and quoted passages were read. The authors' complete quotation-hash checks are inherited for most cards in this pass; I do not claim to have independently revalidated all original strings again. No material layout failure was visible.

The study remains small, with one roster, cyclic seat order, configuration-specific response conditions, post hoc analyses and model-assisted semantic coding. No baseline, optimal value reference, causal persuasion or learning result, unfamiliar-opponent transfer, or held-out method validation is supplied by this rewrite. Those limits constrain the scientific object. They do not require burying the positive observations that the records support.
