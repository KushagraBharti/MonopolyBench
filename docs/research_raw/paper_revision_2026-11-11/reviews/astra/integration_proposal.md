# Proposed complete integrated paper

This is a proposal after all four independent papers, pending the three concurrent whole-paper reviews. It is not an instruction to preserve Astra's frozen structure or to concatenate the drafts.

## Identity and central claim

**Working title:** Bargaining under Cash Pressure: LLM Strategy and Financial Execution in Monopoly.

**Central claim:** In the observed twenty-game economy, agents explicitly bargain over what a transaction enables for rivals; low-cash holders more often continue bargaining than reject completing-deed requests; and the systems usually execute feasible immediate debt payments. Following these interactions into development and inheritance shows what their strategic language becomes in play, without assigning an optimal value to each move.

This is an observational LLM-behavior paper. The contribution is the linked evidence about behavior across a complete economic trajectory. Provenance supplies its method. Existing game rules explain why construction and estates matter, while the records show the agents' responses to those incentives.

## Whole-paper architecture

1. **Introduction, about 650 words.** Start with a seller who must weigh funding its own position against enabling a rival. State the observation, not an ambition to measure eleven capabilities. Three contributions: response and pricing census; cash-pressure and trajectory analyses; successful financing linked to contextual conduct. Introduce the eight motivating questions as the broader agenda, with coverage in discussion. Identify the observational scope once.
2. **Environment, systems and evidence, about 900 words.** One diagram from delivered state and legal menu through proposal/counter/acceptance and later events. Short prompt excerpt with permission to deceive. Name all four configurations. Essential Opus and Grok conditions and history issue in a compact subsection before results; complete prompts and timing in appendix. Define offer, directed pair, group episode and debt episode. Declare post hoc cash/window choices and the status of LLM-assisted coding.
3. **Bargaining over a rival's opportunity, about 1,150 words plus two incidents.** Complete opening/later response table; author-only versus neither comparison; same-bundle concessions; asks versus bids with mixed seven-pair overlap. Explain 61 agreed rival-reason codes as textual evidence. G2 intermediary resale and G15 repeated refusal make the future opportunity concrete.
4. **Cash pressure and continued negotiation, about 900 words plus two incidents.** Full accept/counter/reject distribution below $100, $100–299, and $300+, with five-block and stage sensitivity. Fourteen within-pair trajectories, thirteen cash declines, varied final terms. G7 shows cash-timed bidding, sale at a high price and building by both sides. G13 shows a solvency pitch and omitted mortgage route. G1 is a supplementary cheap-sale contrast.
5. **Executing the resulting position, about 1,100 words plus two incidents.** The full debt census first: 128/131 demonstrated payable episodes paid, sixty-one multi-action paths, $1,400 example. G12 say/do contrast and G19 competent correction are short controls. Development timings, durations and realized rent per landing follow, with group-specific availability and configuration conditions stated locally. A small post-exchange construction table can remain exploratory; do not treat seller wins as an effect.
6. **Financing competitors and inheriting estates, about 650 words plus two incidents.** G20 financial-denial appeal and G18 withdrawal show opposite incentives to transfer cash. Pre-estate positions and 35/40 continuing-play creditors establish the background. Explain that rent and estates are two possible outcomes of an investment. Avoid winner-conditioned causality and terminal-completion headlines.
7. **Discussion and related work, about 800 words.** What is learned about attention, externalities, resource constraints and local execution; relation to controlled negotiation and economic-agent studies. One compact research-question coverage summary, with full Q1–8/A–C appendix table. Limits in one dedicated paragraph/subsection. A short conclusion states the observations and their scope positively.

Target about 6,500–7,200 prose words plus concise cards, with roughly 18–22 main pages in the current readable one-column drafting format. Do not pretend that this is a nine-page conference submission. A venue-specific condensation can be a later artifact once the scientific version is stable.

## Adjudications proposed now

- Accept Fable's cash-pressure counts and trajectories, after naming countering and selection correctly.
- Accept Opus's opening/no-completion and unchanged-bundle concession counts, with dependent units.
- Accept all four debt censuses and preserve observed success prominently.
- Use median same-deed comparisons (4/2/1), show first-ask sensitivity (5/1/1), and do not call either a premium estimate.
- Remove a categorical cognitive mechanism and any broad absence-of-calibration claim.
- Define completion exchanges separately from net-cash sales. If using proceeds language, use a positive-net-cash subset and report the altered denominator.
- Describe the post-sale construction association as selected trajectories; disclose G17 Sol's overlap between the player-game groups.
- Replace causal or exhaustive exposure language with the observed rates and durations.
- Explain response conditions accurately once; keep the exact refusal message and prompt diff in the appendix.
- Preserve Q1–8 and A–C without pretending that unmeasured transfer, learning, welfare or optimality has been answered.
