# Astra: provisional whole-paper comparison of delivered manuscripts

Status: Astra, Sol and Opus have been read in full. Fable 5.2's independent draft has not yet been delivered and has not been opened. This is not the final four-paper review or an integration decision. The initial position preceded peer-draft reading. Frozen manuscripts remain unchanged.

## Scientific judgment

All three papers now study agents' economic behavior rather than treating an audit as the scientific object. That is the right correction. The strongest argument is that agents explicitly bargain over the future opportunities a transaction creates for competitors, with local financial execution furnishing a separate, observable success criterion. Completion, construction, liquidity and inheritance connect those opportunities across a game. They should not become four competing headline discoveries.

Opus contributes the strongest new population comparison: opening offers completing only the author versus opening offers completing nobody, alongside cash concessions on an unchanged bundle. I independently reproduced both from the older offer ledger. These improve the empirical support beyond the original author-only versus recipient-only contrast. The latter should remain in the complete table, but need not lead the prose. All comparisons are exploratory descriptions of different transactions. Choosing the new comparison after inspecting these games should not be described as a prespecified test.

Sol gives the clearest conceptual account of attention to rival construction, intermediary resale and prospective estates. Its explanation of what the observations establish is more defensible than Opus's categorical-rule mechanism. It also has the best economic figure concept: ownership histories with acquisition, construction and exposure. The exact visual encoding can be simplified during integration.

My own paper gives the successful debt population the most prominence. That positive result should survive integration: 128 payments, 61 requiring multiple actions, and a concrete $1,400 payment sequence. But my wording "executed multi-step reasoning" must become "successful multistep financing" unless tied to a particular checked explanation. A sequence of valid actions proves execution, not an internal reasoning algorithm. Legal menus and automatic payment are part of the task.

## Material findings to resolve

1. **The capacity analysis does not demonstrate lack of calibration.** Opus reports opening rejections of 100/134, 32/42 and 6/11 across nominal cash bands. Similarity of the first two shares does not establish insensitivity, and the third differs. The bins omit redemption requirements, alternative collateral, reserves, group quality and other positions. The code compares cash with house cost, not executable construction. Keep it as an exploratory appendix table if useful; do not headline "responses do not change", "attention without calibration", or a categorical decision rule.

2. **Same-deed disagreement is an estimand difference, now reconciled.** Seven single-deed overlaps give 5 higher/1 lower/1 tie for first asks, but 4/2/1 for within-category median asks. G4 Opus Boardwalk alone changes direction: its first completing ask is $450, median $175, against the other ask of $280. Mortgage status and time also differ. List the rows and declare the median summary; a first-ask sensitivity can be retained. Neither summary is a controlled premium estimate.

3. **The buyer generalization is too strong.** Opus's own table has higher completion bids for Sol and Opus but lower bids for Grok, and no Gemini noncompletion comparison. The heading "Asking prices rise, bids do not" and the market-clearing explanation flatten this heterogeneity. Sol's formulation—buyer ordering changes across configurations and cuts—is the accurate synthesis.

4. **Keep the concession finding, with its unit visible.** Independent reconstruction reproduces all 199 qualifying offers: 58 author-only, 38 recipient-only, 17 both, 86 neither. Acceptance is 9/58 versus 24/86 for author-only versus neither. Those two cells contain 38 and 43 distinct threads, respectively. Repeated concessions are not independent cases or causal tests of adding cash. G15 illustrates a sequence without turning it into a general mechanism.

5. **Separate observed low cash from financial need.** Opus's 14/22 versus 7/31 restricts to cash-for-deed-only settled sales. Sol's 21/48 includes more complex cash settlements. These are different populations, not contradictory counts. Cash below $100 is an exploratory threshold, not insolvency or inability to finance elsewhere. Use "cash-poor sellers in the selected sales" and show the filter. Do not claim the market clears only when holders need money.

6. **Successful financing is robust and should be prominent.** Astra, Sol and Opus agree on every one of the 188 episode identities, debt amounts, applied action sequences and outcomes. Astra/Sol additionally match actual closing payment events. The 128 paid episodes use 180 mortgage actions and 89 building-sale actions, without engine fallback. One successful Grok episode, G1 d471 onward, includes a content/call disagreement; success remains an observed system outcome. This does not undermine the positive census, but prevents assigning every action to a coherent textual plan. The 131 payable denominator combines 128 observed payments with three inherited alternative-payment witnesses and excludes the actual-menu lock.

7. **Pre-estate summaries measure different notions of prior strength.** All three agree on ownership, buildings and prior rent. Astra/Sol measure the prompt before the final eliminating action; Opus measures immediately before the estate. G18 and G19 include a final mortgage, so debtor cash differs by $80 and $120. This is timing, not disagreement about inheritance. A prior-rent/building account is easier to interpret than a new book-value index. Never infer that an estate failed to change the winner merely because its creditor was already ahead.

8. **Retain exposure as context, not a third headline.** The $359/$57 in Sol compares first-window classes, while Opus's $374/$20 compares the development state at each landing. The numerators answer different questions. Both are descriptive. The lifecycle figure earns its space by showing timing and unbuilt exposure; a rent multiplier alone largely reflects the rules. One figure and one short paragraph are sufficient in the main paper.

9. **The common-code concentration is 31, not 34.** Direct joining of the retained common-positive keys gives 21 in G1 and 10 in G15. Sol's text says 34 of 61. This is a small reporting correction, not a changed conclusion. The 61/74 and 27/38 recipient-only counts remain correct. Kappa describes reader agreement, not human validation or a cognitive measurement.

10. **Expand G2 strategically, not indiscriminately.** The original Sol resale note and Grok's buyer-specific price reply are excellent evidence of a third-party option. Seven cards for one incident are unnecessary. Use two or three short fields and a concise account of the changed asks; keep the rest in the evidence supplement. Eight mechanisms can remain with about 18–22 main cards. The number involving Grok should be disclosed as selection context, not used to delete a useful exchange.

11. **Do not inherit new unscoped probability claims.** Opus adds "about 12% with doubles" to G13 without making the state and event definition part of the statement. The scientific point is the omitted mortgage, not a dice derivation. Retain only the checked single-roll distinction in a note or omit the probability aside from the main narrative. Do not run a new evaluation to settle it.

12. **Correct the information table, then stop repeating the defect.** Opus's rule table says prices and rents appear in purchase and auction prompts; its surrounding prose correctly says auctions show group. Use the precise statement once in setup, full prompt in appendix. The same principle applies to refusal, conflicts, history and rule conventions: necessary conditions up front, local qualifications where needed, detail outside the narrative.

## Proposed integrated argument, pending Fable

Working title: **Bargaining over Rivals' Opportunities: LLM Strategy and Economic Execution in Monopoly**.

One question: how do LLM agents account for the competitive opportunities their transactions create, and how are the resulting positions executed? Opening refusals, continued bargaining after concessions, and explicit resale/finance/estate explanations establish observable strategic attention. Mixed same-deed prices constrain its valuation interpretation. Successful financing and construction trajectories show what the systems actually accomplish. Selected conduct shows how communication participates in those decisions. Winning remains a trajectory endpoint rather than the value label for each action.

Use the opening contrast and all-debt census as headline economic numbers. The concession result is supporting evidence. Keep asking-price strata, same-deed counterexamples, ownership exposure and pre-estate concentration visible but subordinate. The question map belongs in discussion with a compact appendix table. The main text should contain one consolidated limitations section, not an inference disclaimer after every number.

Suggested figures: (1) opening rejection and cash-concession response shares with all numerators and denominators; (2) completion-to-development histories with opponent landings; (3) optional compact debt-path panel if the successful financing table does not convey the sequence length. The seven same-deed pairs are useful as an appendix figure. Avoid bar charts of rule-induced rent totals as the lead figure.
