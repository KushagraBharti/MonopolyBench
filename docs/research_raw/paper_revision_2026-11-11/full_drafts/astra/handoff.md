# Complete independent paper handoff

**Title:** Strategic Attention and Economic Execution in LLM Monopoly Agents.

**Thesis:** Agents explicitly consider competitors' development, liquidity and prospective estates while bargaining, and execute many checkable multi-step financing tasks. These are positive behavioral findings. Their effectiveness as long-run strategies remains distinct from expressed attention and local success.

## New results for independent cross-review

1. All 188 episodes requiring an explicit liquidation menu: 128 paid, 60 bankrupt. Paid episodes contain 180 mortgage actions and 89 sale actions, totaling 51,997 game dollars paid. Sixty-one require multiple actions (up to ten). No paid action is an engine fallback. Per-system episode counts are in the paper; they are not difficulty-adjusted scores.
2. Exact one-deed-for-cash asks, without jail cards: all six Sol/Opus/Grok seller-by-mortgage pairs have higher completion-buyer medians. Gemini's comparable cell has just one completing ask. Seven same-seller/game/deed overlaps give four higher medians, two lower and one equal. This narrower filter differs from earlier multi-deed cuts, and no completion premium is identified.
3. Leave-one-seed-block-out rejection gaps: openings 3.0–25.0 percentage points, later offers 10.0–15.7. These are sensitivity ranges, not CIs.
4. First-window-developed versus other trade groups have similar median remaining turns (62 versus 63.5) and opponent movements (42 versus 47), with median rent 590 versus 56. This checks a crude duration explanation but does not adjust landing risk, group, subsequent development or selection.
5. Before the first eliminating action, its creditor already leads strictly in building levels in 12 games, cash in 8 and deeds in 6. The timing excludes the residual cash transfer that precedes the bankruptcy marker. This is context, not an inheritance-effect estimate.
