# Fable 5.2: findings, limits and objections

11 November 2026. Written after the tabulations and the manuscript. It records what the records support, what they do not, where I disagree with the contract or with earlier interpretations, and how my initial position changed. Numbers are from `data/` and are regenerated by `build.sh`.

## Evidence labels used here

- **Fresh.** Rebuilt in this draft from `events.jsonl`, `decisions.jsonl`, `players.json` and `contracts/data/board.json`. No engine import, no game, no model call.
- **Inherited.** Taken from earlier work and not rerun: engine-executed payment sequences, the G7 restricted search, executed build plans, full replay, the first-management-turn classification, and two model-assisted semantic readings.
- **Read.** Verified by reading a named source field, source file or web page in this session.

## The central discovery I defend

**Attention to rivals is common to all four systems. Liquidity is what separates their trajectories.**

The claim has three parts, each with a population result.

1. **Every system treats a completing deed as a rival's claim on a monopoly (fresh, plus inherited codes).**
   - Holders with $300 or more reject 120 of 175 completion requests (69%). By system: Gemini 46 of 52, Grok 46 of 52, Sol 25 of 45, Opus 3 of 26 (Opus counters).
   - Asks to a buyer the deed would complete are higher in 6 of 6 seller by mortgage cells, 4 of 4 stage by mortgage cells, 6 of 7 seller-cash by mortgage cells, 4 of 5 seed blocks, and 11 of 14 deed by mortgage cells.
   - Asks rise with the buyer's proximity to a complete group: 1.61, 1.80 and 2.32 times list for unmortgaged single deeds. Bids move little: 1.45, 1.41, 1.59.
   - Inherited: both earlier readings found a stated rival consequence in 61 of 74 refusals of the recipient's own completion.
2. **The deed moves when its holder is short of cash (fresh).**
   - Holders with under $100 reject 17 of 69 completion requests (25%), against 69% at $300 or more. Same sign in all five seed blocks and in 13 of 16 games with both strata. Leave-one-block-out range 39 to 49 points.
   - The gap survives within opening offers (50% against 80%), within later offers (9% against 30%), without Opus as holder (35% against 79%), without Opus on either side (54% against 79%) and without offers that follow a conflicting Grok call (23% against 69%).
   - Of 43 one-sided completion sales, 20 come from a holder with under $100.
   - In 14 one-sided sales the same holder had earlier rejected or countered the same buyer's request. The holder's cash is lower at the sale in 13 of the 14, with medians of $571.50 and $82. Without Opus holders it is 11 of 11.
3. **What the cash becomes separates the trajectories (fresh, plus one inherited classification).**
   - Sellers in one-sided sales who built within eight turns won in 9 of 14 sales (8 of 13 player-games). Sellers who did not won in 1 of 29 (1 of 21 player-games). Among sellers with under $100 the split is 6 of 8 against 0 of 12.
   - After completing a group by trade, Sol and Gemini built in the first management turn on 33 of 35 groups, Opus and Grok on 10 of 28 (inherited classification). Ever built: 17, 17, 12 and 8 (fresh).
   - Exposure is similar: 4.1, 3.9, 3.6 and 4.8 opponent landings per 100 turns of ownership. Rent per landing is $347, $383, $169 and $91. Sol and Gemini exceed Opus and Grok in all five blocks.

A fourth result bounds the claim: **forced finance is reliable in every system (fresh, with three inherited witnesses).** There are 188 debt episodes. Players paid 128. Of 131 episodes with a payment route, 128 were paid. Sol 30 of 31, Gemini 37 of 38, Opus 41 of 41, Grok 20 of 21.

## Alternatives I examined and what happened to them

| Candidate from `initial_position.md` | Outcome |
|---|---|
| A seller premium for completing buyers | Direction is stable in coarse cuts. Not identified. The seven same game, seller and deed cases are 4 higher, 2 lower, 1 equal. Block 5 reverses. I report direction and no magnitude. |
| Liquidity releases blockers | Supported at the offer level and within pairs. It became the second leg of the central claim. Gemini is an exception as a holder (rejects 10 of 11 even under $100). |
| Refusing one's own completion to deny a rival | Present (38 of 140 recipient-only offers rejected outright) and the inherited codes support the stated reason. Too concentrated to headline: 34 of the 74 coded refusals come from G1 and G15. |
| Development gap as a planning finding | Partly supported. Exposure does not explain the rent gap. Opus's gap is partly its response condition and Grok's partly conflicting calls, so I do not call it a planning result. |
| First estate decides games | Mostly no. The first creditor already led in assets in 15 of 20 games. In the 5 others the creditor won 4. I report both and claim no effect. |
| Debt handling as competence | Supported. 128 of 131 payable debts were paid. |

One result was not in my initial position. Splitting sales by what the seller did next (build or not) separates winners from losers far more sharply than seller cash does. I added it after seeing that six of the twenty low-cash sellers won.

## How my position changed

- I expected "liquidity decides when blockers move" to be the weakest clause. It is the best supported.
- I expected the seller-side price pattern to need heavy hedging. It does. I kept it as evidence of attention and not as a price finding.
- I did not expect estates to be so often a marker of prior strength. That lowered their place in the paper.
- I did not expect the first version of my own pair analysis to be wrong. A pair's first offer is sometimes written by the holder (G1: Opus offered Boardwalk in a swap seven times). I now measure from the holder's first refusal and report both versions.

## Null, mixed and unfavourable results that the paper reports

- Same game, seller and deed asks: 4 higher, 2 lower, 1 equal.
- Bids for one's own completion: higher in 3 seller by mortgage cells, lower in 3.
- Unmortgaged asks in seed block 5: lower to completing buyers (1.62 against 2.14).
- Counters reprice as often when no one completes a group (81 of 110) as when the author would (79 of 111).
- Low cash also softens refusals of deeds that complete nothing (23% against 49%), so part of the liquidity gap is general.
- Gemini does not show the holder-cash pattern.
- Distress prices: Opus sold below list in 7 of 8 low-cash cash-only sales. The other systems sold above list in 5 of 6. The fire-sale price pattern is an Opus pattern under action-only recovery.
- First creditor already led in 15 of 20 games.

## Corrections to earlier interpretations

1. **The action-only prompt keeps the permission to deceive (read).** The previous paper said it "omits that sentence". The stored prompts show five changes. All concern the private-thought field and reasoning. "Deception in public chat is allowed" and "bluff, deceive ... intimidate" both stay. One standard prompt (6,989 uses) and one action-only prompt (1,604 uses) exist in the corpus.
2. **The refusal change is not at the commit boundary (fresh).** In G1 to G4, 68 of 160 first attempts were refused up to each game's last accepted standard response and 146 of 146 after. Last accepted responses fall between 16:14 and 16:38 UTC on 25 October. All 1,390 first attempts in G5 to G20 were refused.
3. **One refusal message, 1,605 times (fresh).** Finish reason `content_filter`, native `refusal`. Action-only requests were refused 0 times in 1,604.
4. **The provider documentation matches (read).** Anthropic's Opus 5.5 page says requests that push the model to reproduce internal reasoning in the response text may be declined under `reasoning_extraction`. The saved responses carry no category field. I state the match as plausible and unconfirmed.
5. **"$53,810 of $57,378" is not an exposure artefact (fresh).** Groups built in the first management turn and groups not built then have median durations of 62 and 63.5 turns and landing rates of 4.0 and 4.3 per 100 turns. The gap is $359 against $57 per landing. The outside referee assumed early builders had more exposure. They did not. The figure still does not belong in the abstract, because it is a rent-schedule fact.
6. **"Bankrupt players were insolvent" needed its denominator (fresh).** 56 of 60 eliminations are above the bound. The informative number is 128 of 131 payable debts paid.
7. **"Thirty-four records come from G1 and G15" was ambiguous.** 34 of the 74 coded rows and 31 of the 61 agreed rival labels come from those games.
8. **Sol's reasoning-token field is present in all 1,991 attempts and zero in 1,296 (fresh).** The Opus assessment's "null in 1,292" does not reproduce.
9. **Cost (fresh).** Gateway charge $76.04, upstream inference cost $141.71, BYOK-aware single-field total $141.39. The referee's $218 adds overlapping fields.
10. **G7 hotel lock (read).** The menu offered no building sale at all. The group held a hotel and eight houses, the bank had no houses, and the engine lists only the most-built deeds and lists a hotel only with four bank houses available.
11. **Redemption overcharge (fresh arithmetic).** One dollar at mortgage values $50, $90, $100, $110 and $200.

## Objections to the contract

1. **Word target against eight incidents.** My counter gives about 8,200 words of main prose. About 1,880 are incident context, outcome and reading. About 760 are limitations, related work and conclusion. The argument outside the incidents is about 5,600. I could not reach 7,500 with eight contextual incidents without cutting definitions or robustness that the reviews asked for. If the integrated paper must be shorter, cut related work and trim incident readings before cutting results.
2. **"No synthetic tests or replays" leaves three claims inherited.** The payment routes for the three payable eliminations, the G7 search and the executed build plans cannot be rechecked under this rule. I label them inherited in the text, the figure caption and the tables. I checked what I could by reading: the three bankrupt players' notes and holdings, and the G7 menu.
3. **"Pre-estate positions" needs a valuation, and any valuation is a choice.** I use cash plus deeds at list (half if mortgaged) plus buildings at cost, and I give rent received as a second measure. Both give 15 of 20.
4. **"No naive significance" also means no intervals.** I report block counts and leave-one-block-out ranges. A reader who wants a p-value will not find one. I think that is right for five seed blocks, and I say so once.
5. **"Keep eight incidents if they earn their place."** I kept eight and changed the set. G19 moved to the appendix as a control. G1 (a distress sale at a quarter of list) replaced it. G7 now centres on a liquidity-timed bid and a financing sale, with the settled bluff kept as its first half.
6. **The contract asks to prioritise "LLM fundamentals" over bookkeeping.** The records support claims about what these systems said and did. They do not support claims about cognition. My headline is behavioural and concrete for that reason.

## Limits a reader must keep

- Five seed blocks, one roster, one cyclic seating order. Wins are 8, 7, 3, 2 by system and 7, 7, 3, 3 by seat.
- No value reference. No decision is scored as right or wrong.
- Opus played 1,604 of 1,696 decisions through action-only recovery, without private notes and without a corrective retry. Its opponents played against that Opus.
- Grok's applied call contradicts its text in 139 of 1,068 decisions where the text names an action.
- The agents were not told the rules. Prices and rents appear only in purchase prompts.
- The $100 and $300 thresholds, the eight-turn window and the asset valuation were chosen after earlier work on the same records. Sensitivity is in the appendix.
- The seller split (built or did not build) is partly a proxy for already holding a group.
- The semantic codes are two model-assisted readings from one model family with no human check.
- The eight incidents were chosen by me after reading the records. Grok appears in seven.
