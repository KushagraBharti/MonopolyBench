# 2. The most serious remaining problem: what does the bargaining pattern mean?

## 2.1 The comparison does not isolate concern about a rival’s next move

This is the biggest acceptance barrier.

A proposer-only-completing swap gives the proposer a monopoly while giving the recipient some other deeds, perhaps with cash. A cash bid gives the recipient a resource it can immediately use for construction, debt, redemption or another purchase.

Those transactions can differ in:

- The recipient’s immediate benefit.
- The quality and number of incoming deeds.
- Mortgage liabilities.
- Net cash transferred.
- Existing portfolio complementarities.
- The recipient’s alternatives.
- The identity and behavior of the proposer.
- The stage of the wider game.

A simple policy such as “reject exchanges that give me unattractive assets for useful ones” could produce a substantial completion-associated swap gap without evaluating the rival’s future construction.

That is an **alternative explanation**, not something I am claiming your data prove.

The question your paper needs to confront is:

> **What evidence shows that recipients respond to the rival’s newly enabled actions, beyond responding to the value of what they themselves receive?**

The current census does not answer that.

The within-recipient consistency is valuable. But holding the recipient’s model fixed does not hold its cash, holdings, incoming compensation, alternatives or counterpart fixed.

**What to improve:** print one clear joint comparison of **recipient × opening form × completion**, accompanied by a few economically meaningful summaries of the offers. In particular, show the cash-bid breakdown alongside the swap breakdown. Then stop slicing the same small corpus and test the proposed interpretation directly.

A large regression with dozens of controls would not automatically solve this problem. Sparse, endogenous observations do not become a clean experiment because they enter a model.

## 2.2 Your semantic evidence comes from a different population

This is a particularly consequential issue on **page 5**.

The headline comparison concerns:

> **Proposer-only completion versus neither completion**, among opening swaps and cash bids.

The **61/74** coded reasons concern:

> Rejected offers completing **the recipient’s group**, meaning recipient-only or both-party completion.

Those completion categories are disjoint.

Consequently, this sentence is too strong:

> “The agents’ stated reasons connect that pattern to economic consequences.”

The reason analysis shows that agents sometimes mention rival consequences in other negotiations. It does **not** establish why the headline swap/cash contrast occurs.

Your discussion on page 16 acknowledges this. The first presentation needs to be equally precise.

Replace the transition with something like:

> “A separate population of rejected recipient-completing offers contains explicit references to rival consequences.”

If explaining the headline pattern is central, examine the explanations associated with the actual headline cells, including **acceptances and counters**, rather than only rejected offers elsewhere.

This is not a cosmetic wording issue. It is the connection between your statistical result and your proposed interpretation.

## 2.3 “Strategic attention” remains too elastic

The manuscript uses the phrase to cover several achievements:

1. Mentioning a rival’s possible future action.
2. Correctly calculating whether that action is feasible.
3. Producing a decision consistent with that consideration.
4. Changing the decision when the opportunity changes.
5. Making a strategically advantageous decision.

These require different evidence.

At present, a strategically plausible sentence can receive considerable interpretive weight even when its calculation is wrong. The nine-house example is an obvious warning.

Define the construct explicitly. I would distinguish:

| Level | Observable evidence |
|---|---|
| Expressed concern | The response explicitly mentions a consequence for another player. |
| Accurate assessment | The stated consequence is consistent with the actual state and rules. |
| Action consistency | The applied decision is compatible with the stated consideration. |
| Demonstrated sensitivity | The decision changes when the relevant opportunity changes under a suitable comparison. |
| Strategic effectiveness | The behavior improves a defined outcome relative to a reference. |

You have evidence for the first three at selected points, with qualifications. The fourth needs a stronger design. The fifth is explicitly outside the present study.

**Define which level each result supports.** Otherwise “attention” becomes a conveniently broad phrase that readers can interpret more strongly than the measurements warrant.

## 2.4 The discovery is retrospective

Page 17 correctly states that the offer-form split emerged during cross-review.

That disclosure is good. Exploratory research is legitimate.

But independently recomputing a selected contrast is **validation of the computation**, not independent confirmation of the behavioral regularity. The five seed blocks were already part of the corpus that generated the finding.

The next step should be to freeze the definitions and test the result on:

- A fresh cohort; or
- A prospectively specified set of legal negotiation states.

Do not retrospectively designate already-inspected games as a holdout.

A paper can publish an exploratory finding without a causal experiment. The problem is whether this particular exploratory finding carries enough significance for the venue you want. Right now, I think that case remains insufficient.

## 2.5 The Grok sensitivity analysis is reassuring and materially consequential

Removing conflicting Grok initiations changes the swap comparison to:

\[
93/112 = 83.04\%
\quad\text{versus}\quad
26/43 = 60.47\%.

The gap falls from **30.51 to 22.57 percentage points**.

The direction survives. That is a strength.

However, the removed non-completing swaps include **13 of the original 17 accepted comparator swaps**, or **76.47%**. Those interactions substantially affect the composition of proposals and acceptances in the comparison. (p. 20.)

Put both versions beside the headline result. Calling the exclusion a sensitivity analysis is appropriate; deleting those records does not reconstruct what the other agents would have encountered in a game without the conflicting actions.

# 3. The finance section is now stronger, but its difficulty is uncalibrated

## 3.1 The 97.71% result is useful within its stated scope

The denominator is good. The risk is what readers infer from it.

These are episodes beginning with an **explicit liquidation menu**. Ordinary payments from sufficient cash are excluded. The statistic measures successful payment on the encountered, demonstrated-payable liquidation states.

It does not measure:

- Overall solvency.
- Long-term financial judgment.
- Optimal choice of assets to liquidate.
- General planning ability.
- An intrinsic ranking of the four underlying models.

Your manuscript mostly respects these limits. Keep the complete partition immediately next to the conditional success rate so the number cannot be detached from its meaning.

## 3.2 Ten actions do not establish ten steps of lookahead

The highlighted sequence contains:

- Two building-sale calls.
- Eight mortgage calls.

That is a genuine ten-action execution of an announced liquidation objective, with a correctly predicted final balance.

It need not involve ten distinct dependent planning decisions. Once development is sold, several mortgages may be interchangeable. A compact policy such as “sell development, then mortgage until the debt is covered” could produce a long execution sequence.

Distinguish:

- **Execution length:** how many calls occurred.
- **Dependency depth:** how many prerequisites had to be anticipated.
- **Feedback support:** what the refreshed menus revealed.
- **Prospective specification:** what was stated before execution began.

The initial note documents an aggregate budget and broad liquidation policy. It does not necessarily document a fully specified ordered ten-action plan. (p. 13.)

Your existing caveat about updated menus is correct. Make the task description concrete enough that the reader can judge the difficulty.

## 3.3 A simple reactive baseline would be highly informative

For this specific result, a baseline is worth doing.

Restart from the stored debt-entry states and run a transparent policy using the same information and legal menus:

1. Choose a permitted cash-raising action under a stated rule.
2. Apply it.
3. Read the updated menu.
4. Repeat until payment or no legal progress.

Specify ordering and tie-breaking. Building-sale constraints can make apparently simple policies fail, so do not assume the outcome.

Measure:

- Payment success.
- Invalid attempts.
- Number of actions.
- Use of newly unlocked collateral.
- Assets and development liquidated.

This would answer whether the observed performance requires more than repeatedly taking available financing actions.

**You do not need a full Monopoly-playing baseline or a globally optimal liquidation solver to answer that local question.**

The purpose of the baseline is to calibrate difficulty. NeurIPS’s evaluation guidance does not require every empirical contribution to beat a baseline; this recommendation follows from the particular capability interpretation your paper invites. [nips.cc](https://nips.cc/Conferences/2026/EvaluationsDatasetsReviewerGuidelines?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 3.4 Nineteen unlocking episodes are valid evidence, with an obvious next question

You report nineteen successful uses of collateral absent from the first menu. That is a legitimate repeated-behavior count.

I would **not** criticize you for omitting a denominator from a success rate you never claim.

The stronger next analysis is:

> In which episodes was newly released collateral **necessary**, and in which was it merely one available way to pay?

That distinction would connect the successes more closely to the two coherent failures that omit newly released mortgages from their calculations.

## 3.5 The correct total contains an instructive error

Gemini predicts **$1,414**, but its components are:

- $675 from building sales.
- $690 from mortgages.
- $49 cash.

The reconstructed execution gives:

- $700 from sales.

You disclose this, which is good. But it matters to the interpretation: an accurate aggregate result can coexist with an inaccurate representation of the state.

The debtor’s prior total plus successful execution is stronger evidence than the other agent’s compensating arithmetic. A good paper should make that distinction as visible as the impressive endpoint.

# 7. What I would actually do next

## Step 1: Fix the evidence links before collecting more data

Make four immediate corrections:

1. Separate the 61/74 reason analysis from the headline offer population.
2. Correct or source the explicit inheritance motive.
3. Reconcile the replay modes.
4. Restore the missing interface configuration and artifact entry point.

Then publish the joint counts and precise pairing definitions.

These are bounded improvements to the existing work.

## Step 2: Build a matched-state test of rival opportunities

Use the primary question:

> **Does the recipient respond differently when the same offered terms enable a rival to take a strategically consequential action?**

Construct legally consistent state families in which:

- The focal recipient receives the same offered assets and cash.
- Its own portfolio and immediate options stay fixed where possible.
- The proposer’s complementary holdings change whether it completes a group.
- The relevant rules and numerical facts are fully supplied.

Then vary a specific consequence, such as whether the proposer can finance a legal building after the exchange.

Document unavoidable accompanying changes. Moving a complementary deed to another opponent, for example, may introduce another strategic difference.

Measure:

- Accept, counter and reject.
- Cash demanded in counteroffers.
- Correctness of post-trade cash and building calculations.
- Invalid responses and channel conflicts.

This gives you a direct test of response sensitivity. It does not automatically establish optimal bargaining or faithful internal reasoning.

## Step 3: Handle cash-versus-barter carefully

Do not replace a deed with its printed purchase price and declare the offers equivalent.

A deed has:

- Rental opportunities.
- Mortgage value.
- Complementarity with other holdings.
- Blocking value.
- Possible future trading value.

Cash has immediate liquidity.

Begin with **within-form** comparisons: fixed cash bids across rival-capacity conditions, and fixed swap bundles across the same conditions. Then compare the response patterns.

A claim about a pure framing effect requires equivalent consequences. Cash-versus-deed transactions generally do not provide that automatically.

## Step 4: Distinguish immediate opportunity from longer-term option value

Compare a completed group that can be developed immediately with one that cannot.

A response that changes with feasible construction would support a more specific interpretation.

However, **an unchanged response would not prove keyword dependence**. A completed group retains future option value even when immediate development is impossible.

To test sensitivity to the word “monopoly,” you would need a separate wording manipulation with the underlying state fixed.

This distinction matters because a poorly designed follow-up could reproduce the current problem under a more experimental-looking presentation.

## Step 5: Calibrate liquidation locally

Run the transparent reactive policy on the debt states.

If it matches or exceeds the observed success, emphasize reliable menu-supported execution and the specific failures exposed by the logs.

If it fails on meaningful states that the LLMs solve, those states become especially valuable evidence.

Either outcome can improve the paper. The test is useful because it changes the interpretation.

## Step 6: Confirm the discovery independently

Freeze the main contrast before new collection.

Choose the number of state families and repetitions based on endpoint variability and a stated precision goal. Repeated samples of one state estimate that state’s response distribution; diverse state families support broader conclusions.

If the new evaluation repairs several interface problems simultaneously, describe it as a new configured system. You cannot attribute its improvement to one repair without isolating that repair.

**I would prioritize these focused studies over another broad rewrite or a larger catalogue of incidents.**
