# Sol's independent whole-paper assessment

I read every line of `attached_review.txt` (649 lines) and `inline_review.md` (129 lines), then the current `integrated/manuscript.tex`, its preamble, every active section and included table, all quotation definitions, both printed prompts, and `evidence_map.md`. I also read the inactive inheritance section, but assessed the version actually included by the manuscript. I did not read another coauthor's response in this feedback folder.

My assessment is that the paper contains worthwhile observational science, but its main bargaining interpretation is less secure than its financial-execution evidence. The most useful revision would clarify what each result establishes and give the necessary-collateral cases more weight. Another large rewrite or catalogue of defects would not solve the scientific problem. The reviewers' ratings and venue predictions are judgments. I would not promise acceptance from these remedies.

I made bounded, read-only checks of existing offer ledgers, debt-entry records, applied actions, first-decision text, the G18 source field, and historical replay source/report files. I ran no engine, replay, branch, model call, synthetic test, baseline or game. I wrote only this assessment. I did not read the external 26-page reviewer report or independently read primary literature during this pass. Literature comparisons in the reviews therefore remain unverified by me.

## Seven issues, ranked by scientific importance

1. **The bargaining headline mixes differences in offers with differences in rival opportunity.** The attached review is right that recipients may reject poor compensation without calculating the proposer's future construction. The inline review adds a consequential, checkable reason to worry: the proposer composition differs sharply between the two swap populations. Grok supplies half the neither-completing swaps and fifteen of their seventeen acceptances. Recipient stratification does not address this. Retain the observed contrast, put proposer composition beside it, and describe rival-opportunity sensitivity as an interpretation the study has not isolated. Removing conflicting calls or all Grok proposals changes the description; it cannot recover the game that would have occurred without them.

2. **The semantic evidence does not explain the headline cells.** This is the attached review's most important correction. The 61/74 coding concerns recipient-only and both-party completion; the headline compares proposer-only with neither. Those are disjoint populations. The first presentation currently joins them too strongly, even though the discussion later qualifies the link. The codes support the presence of expressed rival concern elsewhere in bargaining. They do not explain the swap/cash difference. Define attention as an expressed consideration, then distinguish factual accuracy, consistency with the applied action, response sensitivity and effectiveness. A wrong construction calculation can still express concern, but cannot demonstrate accurate assessment.

3. **The strongest positive finance evidence is necessity plus prospective text plus payment.** The inline review is right to distinguish fourteen successes where released collateral was needed from nineteen successes where it was used. My record check reproduces that distinction below. This strengthens the paper's account of local planning. It does not prove a hidden planning algorithm, ten dependent steps of lookahead, optimal liquidation, or superiority to a reactive policy. The two coherent failures are identifiable omissions of the same financing resource. Calling them a general planning boundary is stronger than two cases warrant, especially when the same systems succeed elsewhere. Keep the complete 188-episode census as context and use the necessary-collateral subset to explain what was accomplished.

4. **Exploration and uncertainty need a clear scope, not a search for a favorable significance result.** The offer-form finding was selected after inspecting the corpus. Independent recomputation checks arithmetic, not replication. The paper should retain the exact finite-corpus counts and disclose selection once. The inline review's reported intervals and fitted models are not independently verified here, and cannot remove offer-quality confounding or retrospective selection. The observed cash-bid difference is negative; that does not establish a population null or absence of sensitivity. Twenty games are neither automatically sufficient nor automatically worthless. The relevant limits are the interacting roster, five seed blocks, unequal opportunities and selected contrasts.

5. **The objects being studied are configured interacting systems.** Opus recovery changes its available note and correction path; Grok's channels conflict; faulty historical summaries enter participants' context. These conditions matter to everyone who encounters their actions. The applied transactions remain genuine observations of this system. They are weaker evidence about a coherent intended policy or underlying model ability. Calling two arms broken compresses several different issues and implies a clean experiment the study never had. Keep the disclosures, restore the missing tool-choice configuration from retained settings, and avoid treating the three non-Opus configurations as having an identical interface. Zero refused recovery calls documents that response path; it does not identify the upstream refusal trigger.

6. **Two source claims need direct correction.** The G18 explanation does not explicitly state an inheritance motive. The current replay paragraph also describes field removal that the retained full-event checker does not perform. These are small edits with scientific consequences because they change what the evidence establishes. Neither implies fabricated transactions. Correct them from the existing sources, with the checked/inherited distinction below.

7. **The whole paper needs one contribution readers can carry away.** The useful contribution is an exploratory account of bargaining over persistent economic opportunities, paired with observed execution of financing dependencies. It is not the discovery that cash, monopolies or inheritance matter, nor a general account of LLM agency. Keep a few cases that establish different observations and use the others as supporting material. Asking-price medians, falling-cash histories, development receipts and estate concentration supply context, not separate demonstrations of causal mechanisms. Compression should make that hierarchy clearer. Restore a concrete artifact entry point and game mapping; endless caveats and revision history do not substitute for one.

### Necessary collateral versus collateral used

I read original debt-entry prompts for the 188 ledger episodes and their recorded applied actions, using `contracts/data/board.json` for monetary arithmetic. Let C be entry cash, S the generous salvage value of every building, and M0 the mortgage value of every deed already listed in the first menu. In sixteen episodes, C + S + M0 is below the debt while the generous bound including all unmortgaged deeds reaches it. Even selling every building and exhausting initial mortgages cannot cover those debts. Payment requires additional collateral. A sufficient bound alone still does not prove a legal route.

Fourteen of those episodes actually pay and use released collateral. Two end in bankruptcy, G12 Sol d190 and G17 Gemini d234; their positive legal payment witnesses remain inherited from earlier executed checks. Separately, nineteen actual payments use thirty-three mortgages absent from the initial menu. Thus fourteen necessary-collateral successes are contained within the nineteen uses. The other five uses do not satisfy this arithmetic necessity test. The two counts answer different questions and should both remain.

For traceability, the fourteen successful entry decisions are G1 d476; G2 d216; G4 d270 and d308; G5 d263; G6 d185; G8 d257; G11 d203; G13 d195; G17 d254; G18 d309 and d313; G20 d291 and d327. This grouping does not rank models.

I read all fourteen first resolved responses. Nine have applied private notes; five Opus cases have provider-returned explanations. All refer prospectively to mortgages after sales or to a full-liquidation budget that includes them. That supports the inline review's substantive point, with a qualification: not every text correctly enumerates the released amounts. G20 Opus d291 asks whether the cleared pink mortgages could close the gap rather than supplying a completed numerical calculation. G20 Sol d327 states an aggregate budget. This is my single-reader assessment of recorded statements, not human-validated coding or privileged cognition.

The manuscript's statement that actions alone cannot distinguish anticipation from menu following is correct. Those actions are not the only available evidence. Prospective text adds evidence of an announced dependency or budget, followed by observed execution. It still cannot show that the text caused the choice or faithfully exposes its computation. The inline claim that a loop taking any financing option pays 131/131 has not been established by my checks. I ran no such policy.

## Where the reviews disagree

The attached review accepts 128/131 as a valid conditional census, while the inline review calls it a ceiling effect. Both can be true in different senses: the denominator is valid, and most encountered tasks may be easy. Ease is a limitation on capability interpretation, not a reason to discard observed payments. Neither review supplies a verified executed baseline in the material I received.

The attached review asks whether released collateral was necessary; the inline review supplies a promising answer. My bounded check supports fourteen successful necessity cases and two failures with inherited feasibility witnesses. I favor bringing that evidence forward. I reject the stronger suggestion that notes settle the underlying cognitive mechanism.

The inline review is more dismissive of the swap contrast because of proposer composition. Its counts deserve incorporation, but calling the observations contaminated is too broad. They are valid counts of real applied offers from an unusual proposal population. The attached review's alternative-explanation account is the better scientific description. The current headline should become more modest even though the direction survives several descriptive breakdowns.

The inline review infers no human contribution from an unfinished byline. That inference is unsupported. The draft needs correct human authorship and responsibility before submission; the placeholder cannot establish who contributed. Likewise, a corrected per-game cost cannot by itself establish an adequate sample size or a valid expansion budget.

Both reviewers favor new controlled tests and baselines for stronger claims. Those proposals are outside this task. Their scientific usefulness does not override the prohibition on new evaluations, and a stronger claim must remain unresolved if it requires them. The inline suggestion to make a direct API call for refusal metadata is also outside this review; the received material does not establish that such a call would identify the historical trigger.

## What should stay, and what can change using existing records

Retain the full response census, the offer-form contrast with its proposer table, and the complete debt partition. Retain the nineteen collateral uses, the sharper fourteen-of-sixteen result with its feasibility qualification, and G20's announced budget and payment. Do not convert ten actions into ten-step lookahead; Gemini's offsetting component errors also matter when describing its correct aggregate.

Retain G15 as an expressed rival-development concern, G19 as factual correction alongside acceptance, and a compact selection of G7/G13/G20 showing how payments enter subsequent opportunities. G13 supports selective presentation, not an identified $70 persuasion effect. G20 supports an appeal and the speaker's follow-through, not successful collective coordination. Preserve their channel labels. Estate transfers and development receipts remain useful continuation evidence, with exposure and prior strength acknowledged.

Permitted remedies are descriptive re-tabulation, explicit population definitions, row-level source checking, source-based replay documentation, and bounded reading of existing explanations. A full offer-form/completion table and clearer matching rules for the fourteen bargaining histories would improve transparency. Existing-record uncertainty analysis is possible if justified, but should not be treated as confirmation of a selected hypothesis. External human coding would require actual human participation; do not relabel an LLM reading as that validation.

Forbidden remedies here include greedy-policy execution, fresh cohorts, matched or synthetic states, counterfactual rollouts, replay runs, engine branches, baseline-only games and provider probes. Calling these offline, free or inexpensive does not make them existing-log analysis. Nothing in this assessment recommends doing them now.

## Strongest dissent

I dissent most strongly from treating the absence of new evaluations as the absence of scientific evidence. The necessary-collateral cases combine a resource shortfall, prospective statements and successful dependent actions. That is meaningful positive behavioral evidence, even without proving difficult reasoning or outperforming a bot. At the same time, I would oppose retaining language that makes the form-dependent bargaining association sound like an established response to rival opportunity. More careful prose cannot supply that missing identification. The paper should be confident about the first kind of evidence and restrained about the second.

For root: keep the paper observational; put proposer composition beside the swap contrast; separate the 61/74 population; promote the checked necessary-collateral evidence; correct G18 and replay from sources. Preserve the useful trajectories. Do not commission new evaluations or promise a venue outcome.
