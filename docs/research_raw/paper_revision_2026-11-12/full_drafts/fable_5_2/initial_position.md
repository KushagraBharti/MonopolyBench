## What I think the paper is about

One question runs through the records: when an agent says what a move will make possible afterward, does its next action fit that statement, and what happens then?

The games give two places to ask it.

1. **The agent's own options.** A debtor that owes more than it can raise from the menu in front of it has one decision that matters, which is whether to keep going or quit. In sixteen debts the only way to pay ran through mortgages that the menu does not list until buildings are sold. Fourteen were paid. I expect the stored first-decision text to count the unlisted mortgages in thirteen, raise them without a total in one, and add up listed items only in both failures.
2. **A rival's options.** A holder asked for the last deed in someone's color group decides whether to hand over a monopoly. Recipients talk about the rival's set in nearly every such refusal. I expect the refusal to follow completion itself, in swaps far more than in cash bids, and I do not expect it to rise with how many houses the rival could afford afterward.

So the working thesis is: **these agents state consequences of a move before they act, for themselves and for rivals, and their actions agree with those statements. The records show accurate, action-consistent foresight about the agent's own financing in a small, rule-defined set of hard cases. For rivals they show stated concern attached to a salient event, with no measured response to the size of the rival's opportunity.**

I will use referee A's ladder once, in the methods: stated, accurate, consistent with the action, sensitive to the opportunity, effective. Each result gets one level. I will not repeat caveats after that.

## Where I expect to dissent

- **From the integrated paper, which carries my own earlier title.** "Bargaining over a rival's next move" names an interpretation the swap and cash split cannot carry. I proposed it and I withdraw it.
- **From making either result the sole headline.** Opus and I argued for leading with fourteen of sixteen. Astra objects to trading one overstated headline for another, and that objection is fair for a set of sixteen with two failures that a referee surfaced. I will lead with the question and give finance the first results section because necessity there is fixed by rule arithmetic, and the comparison in bargaining is an analyst's choice.
- **From referee B on "contaminated".** Grok's offers were real offers that recipients answered. The proposer table belongs beside the headline, and the size of the gap should be reported as a range across compositions.
- **From referee B on "a loop pays 131 of 131".** Nobody ran it and we may not. I will not print it. The three-tier partition by what the first menu could raise needs no baseline.
- **From referee A on the baseline as the only route to calibration.** The partition calibrates difficulty from stored menus. A loop cannot quit, so it would not face the decision where both failures occur.
- **From the earlier refusal to print any range.** Readers will compute intervals. I will print leave-one-seed-block-out ranges and resampling by seed block and by game, state the target as sensitivity of one fixed cohort, and print no p-values.
