Stated opportunities and executed choices:
LLM agents in twenty Monopoly games
12 November 2026
Abstract
Economic agents must assess how transactions change their own and their competitors’ options.
We study this connection in twenty complete four-player Monopoly games, linking 920 negotiation
responses and 188 liquidation episodes to delivered state, prospective statements and executed
actions. Sixteen debts require collateral absent from the first legal menu. Agents pay fourteen;
thirteen successful entry statements include the later mortgages or a budget encompassing them,
and one raises them as a possibility. Both failures declare immediately after omitting that collateral.
A ten-action payment reaches the debtor’s previously stated $14 remainder. Bargaining supplies a
complementary but less identified result. Opening swaps completing only the proposer receive
95/116 rejections, against 37/72 completing neither party. The 30.5-point gap falls to 16.7 points
without Grok-authored offers; cash bids show a different, uncertain contrast. Full cells, proposer
strata and five-seed sensitivity disclose that composition. Eight contextual incidents document
rival-development concerns, bluffing, selective disclosure, factual correction and financing choices.
The records support prospective economic assessment followed by local execution, and expressed
concern about rival opportunities. They do not identify cognition, tactic effectiveness or a ranking
of underlying models.
1
Introduction
An exchange can help its recipient and create a dangerous competitor. Selling the final deed in a
Monopoly color group supplies cash today while enabling construction against the seller tomorrow.
Refusing a sale preserves a blocker, but the holder may later lose it to someone else through bankruptcy.
The same dependence appears within an agent’s own finances. Selling buildings sacrifices income
yet makes previously unavailable mortgages legal. These decisions concern how an action changes
subsequent opportunities.
Such settings allow a useful separation between what an agent says, what its state permits, and what
it actually does. A statement can correctly identify an opportunity without leading to a profitable choice.
An agent can reach a financial goal while receiving substantial intermediate feedback. Conversely, an
incorrect explanation can accompany a favorable transaction. Studying these objects together supplies
a more precise account of economic behavior than judging a plausible explanation or a final win alone.
We examine twenty complete MonopolyBench games played by GPT-6.1 Sol, Gemini 4 Argon,
Claude Opus 5.5 and Grok 4.7. The corpus records 6,989 decisions and the resulting 50,918 events.
The study follows bargaining into financing, construction, rent and ownership transfer. Every offer
has a recipient-facing state and response; each liquidation episode has a specified obligation and a
payment or bankruptcy endpoint. Persistent consequences make the games informative even when no
defensible value reference exists for a rejected trade.
1

The clearest repeated positive finding concerns debts that cannot be paid using cash, all building
salvage and the mortgages listed at entry. Sixteen encountered episodes require additional collateral
released by selling buildings. Fourteen reach payment. The successful entry texts mostly describe
that dependency before the menu changes; the two failures omit the released collateral and declare
bankruptcy immediately. This is evidence of prospective financial assessment followed by dependent
execution. It is more specific than a general claim that the agents plan, and more informative than a
count of successful payments without their required resources.
Bargaining shows a related concern with other players’ opportunities, but its aggregate interpreta-
tion is less settled. Opening swaps completing only the proposer are rejected more often than swaps
completing neither party. The contrast depends materially on who proposes the offers. Pure cash bids
do not have the same observed ordering. We present the complete form and completion table, proposer
and recipient strata, and sensitivity across the five seed blocks. The analysis describes an exploratory
association among unequal offers; it does not isolate the effect of enabling a rival’s development.
Three main incidents clarify the connection between economic statements and action. A player
refuses its own complete group while citing the rival’s building cash. A seller presents a purchase as
protection against a third party’s inheritance while omitting available collateral. A debtor announces an
aggregate liquidation budget, executes ten actions and retains the predicted $14. Five further incidents
preserve pricing, a public/private bargaining ceiling, factual correction, attempted cooperation and
withdrawal of an assurance. They supply distinct observations, not a frequency estimate of deceptive
conduct.
The contribution is an observational account of economic opportunity assessment at two evidential
levels. Local debt has a verifiable immediate target, enabling statements and actions to be checked
against resource arithmetic. Bargaining exposes expressed rival concern and actual response patterns
while leaving the value of each choice unresolved. We retain that asymmetry throughout. The broader
motivating questions concern reasoning, opponent modeling, negotiation and deception, planning
and memory, adaptation, risk, collective dynamics and evaluation validity. Appendix D answers their
measured components, together with questions about talk versus action, general capability and skill
versus chance.
2
Environment, records and measures
2.1
Games and information
Four players start with $1,500 on the forty-space United States board. They purchase and auction
deeds, exchange cash and properties, mortgage assets, and build on complete color groups. Declining
a purchase starts an ascending auction. Negotiation permits up to twenty exchanges. Development
requires ownership, cash, unmortgaged deeds, even building and available bank stock. Developed
groups cannot be traded. A debtor may sell buildings or mortgage deeds, but cannot negotiate a rescue
trade during liquidation. Bankruptcy remains a legal choice even when payment is possible.
The implementation transfers buildings intact to a player-creditor and charges no immediate interest
on inherited mortgages. Assets returned to the bank are not immediately auctioned. These rules
make creditor identity consequential. The engine alone validates actions and changes state; the agents
choose among explicit legal tools. After a financing action the engine returns the updated state and
legal menu. Thus observed sequences combine model choices with external state and legality support.
Appendix E records menu restrictions and other implementation details that affect the interpretation.
Five seeds each supply four cyclic seat rotations of the same roster. Each system occupies each seat
five times, but relative opponent order stays fixed. Games last 75–386 global turns and end through

2.2
Configured systems and response channels
The received valid tool call governs play. Public messages, applied private notes, assistant content and
provider-returned explanations are distinct channels. A plan in content is not an applied action. We
check receipt in the actual other-player request whenever an incident claims that a message reached its
counterpart. A public-message field alone does not establish delivery or belief.
Ordinary invalid outputs receive one corrective request before deterministic fallback. Explicit first-
attempt provider refusal instead triggers same-model action-only recovery, removing the private-note
request and using the remaining attempt. Opus records 1,604 first-attempt refusals and one later
refusal. All share a provider message concerning reverse engineering or duplicating model outputs.
None of its 1,604 recovery calls is refused. Refusals become universal in G5–G20; the change already
appears by wall clock during the first four games. Opus consequently makes most choices without an
applied private note or another corrective chance. The named fallback model is never invoked.
Grok has 139 selected responses whose content names a different action from the received call,
including 64 of 136 trade openings. Its required-tool setting is present at both recorded source commits.
That source fact does not establish that forced tool choice caused the conflicts. Some faulty past-trade
summaries also enter 2,054 initial prompts, although all 920 current offers agree with the recipient’s
acceptance menu and current holdings are correct. These conditions change the interacting population
faced by every participant. The evaluated objects are configured systems, rather than isolated model
abilities.
2.3
Operational definitions
An offer is a bundle awaiting a recipient response. Its author can differ from the thread initiator.
We use the delivered state to classify whether accepting newly completes the proposer, recipient,
both, or neither. A complete group can include mortgaged deeds. An opening has exchange index
3

zero. Swaps transfer deeds in both directions and may include cash. Pure cash bids offer positive
cash for deeds without reciprocal deeds or jail cards. All remaining forms stay in an explicit other
category. Acceptance-legal offers form the common table population; two additional responses lack
legal acceptance.
A liquidation episode starts at a cash-shortfall menu and ends at payment or elimination. Ordinary
bills paid from sufficient cash are excluded. Let C be entry cash, S all building salvage, M0 the sum
of initially listed mortgage principals, and M all unmortgaged deed principals. The generous asset
bound is U = C + S + M. We identify necessary additional collateral when
C + S + M0 < D ≤U,
where D is the debt. Even selling every building and exhausting listed mortgages cannot then pay.
This arithmetic identifies a resource requirement; an upper bound alone is not proof of an executable
route. Actual payments provide positive witnesses, and three earlier executed legal sequences establish
payable bankruptcies. Their historical status is retained without rerunning them.
We distinguish expressed consideration, correct state assessment, consistency with the applied
action, response sensitivity under a suitable comparison, and strategic effectiveness. The first three can
be observed locally. Sensitivity and effectiveness require comparisons this cohort does not provide.
Prospective means recorded in the first resolved response before subsequent liquidation feedback, not
independent of the state supplied in that request. Explanation-faithfulness research motivates treating
such text as evidence of expressed assessment rather than privileged cognition [10].
2.4
Uncertainty and retrospective selection
All counts are exact descriptions of the saved cohort. The offer-form contrast, cash thresholds, semantic
populations and incidents were selected after corpus inspection. To show variation, we report each
seed block and delete one block at a time. We also enumerate all 55 = 3, 125 ordered resamples of five
whole blocks, retaining all four rotations and every negotiation within a selected block. The target
statistic is the offer-weighted rejection-rate difference between proposer-only and neither completion,
separately by form. Resampling does not treat offers or the twenty rotations as independent seed
draws.
The central 95% resampling bands describe sensitivity to reweighting these five observed blocks.
Reading them as confidence intervals for new random seeds would require exchangeable seed sampling
and a fixed provider regime, neither established here. They do not correct post-selection or control
offer quality. We report no significance tests. Recounting the selected contrast and finding the same
direction across already inspected blocks checks stability and arithmetic; it does not validate the
selected hypothesis on independent evidence.
3
Prospective finance and completed payments
3.1
When the first menu is insufficient
Across 188 liquidation episodes, agents pay 128 debts and are eliminated in sixty. The complete
classification is 128 payments, 56 debts above the generous asset bound, one menu-restricted elimination
and three demonstrated-payable bankruptcies. Actual payments plus the three historical payment
witnesses yield 131 demonstrated-payable episodes, of which 128 are paid, or 97.7%. This conditional
rate concerns the immediate obligation, rather than whether earlier investment decisions were prudent.
4

The census contains substantial variation in required execution. Sixty-seven successful payments
take one action. Sixty-two can be covered by entry cash and initially listed mortgages, without selling
development. A further 52 successful payments can be covered after adding all building salvage
without requiring an unlisted mortgage. These categories explain why a high payment rate alone gives
little calibration of planning difficulty. Repeated local success remains an observation, but it should
not be mistaken for superiority to a simple policy whose performance has not been measured.
Sixteen episodes satisfy the necessary-collateral inequality. In fourteen, agents sell buildings, use
mortgages that were absent from the first menu, and pay. In two, they declare bankruptcy at the first
decision. The successful texts state the subsequent mortgages or an aggregate budget encompassing
them in thirteen episodes. The remaining text, Opus at G20 d291, asks whether clearing the pink
houses and mortgaging those deeds could close the gap. We preserve that tentative statement rather
than treating it as a completed calculation.
The prospective channels matter. Nine successful statements are applied private notes. Five are
provider-returned explanations from Opus recovery responses, which contain no applied note. Across
these channels, the initial response often specifies what selling will make possible before that option
appears in a refreshed menu. G2 Sol explicitly budgets the yellow mortgages; G5 Gemini corrects an
initial shortfall account within its first response and includes both dark-blue mortgages; G18 Gemini
identifies the one-house sale that releases Park Place. Full selected first-decision fields for all sixteen
episodes accompany the numerical rows in Appendix C.
By configuration, the necessary subset contains payments in 2/3 Sol, 5/6 Gemini, 5/5 Opus and
2/2 Grok episodes. These are unequal encountered positions, not a model ranking. The same systems
that fail also succeed elsewhere. The two failures reveal a local omission rather than a demonstrated
general boundary of their planning ability.
At G12 d190, Sol totals $53 cash, $225 building salvage and $130 listed mortgages, concluding
that $408 cannot pay $500. Selling would also release $340 red collateral. At G17 d234, Gemini totals
$500 salvage and a $30 Baltic mortgage against $550 owed, omitting $375 dark-blue collateral. Both
statements and declarations are internally consistent with incomplete sums. Historical executable
witnesses establish legal payment routes. The observations link stated resource inclusion to continuing
liquidation, and stated omission to an immediate stop, without identifying which internal computation
produced either response.
The necessity subset differs from the broader use count. Nineteen successful episodes use 33
mortgages absent from their initial menus. Fourteen are the necessary successes above. The other
five comprise four G3 Gemini episodes and G18 Sol d428, where the generous initial-resource total
already covers the debt. Such mortgages can be chosen even when they are not arithmetically necessary.
Reporting nineteen as a success denominator would mix a chosen financing route with a required
dependency.

Ten is the execution length, not ten levels of lookahead. Removing development releases mortgages,
while several subsequent choices can be interchangeable. Updated menus supply intermediate feedback
throughout. Sol specifies an aggregate budget and broad policy before acting; it does not enumerate
an ordered ten-call plan. The successful continuation demonstrates that it sustains this local objective
across the recorded actions. No comparison measures whether it preserves the most valuable residual
portfolio or chooses an optimal survival strategy.

Expressed concern in a separate population
Two retained LLM-assisted readings agree that 61/74 rejected recipient-completing offers name a
trade-linked rival consequence. This comprises 27/38 recipient-only and 34/36 both-party rejections.
These categories are disjoint from the proposer-only and neither headline comparison. The codes
document expressed concern elsewhere; they do not explain the swap/cash gap. Gemini and Grok
contribute 56 agreed cases, and G1/G15 contribute 31. One reader had partial exposure to earlier
aggregates and a case; agreement is not human validation or a rate of accurate reasoning.

This case supports expressed concern, partially checked assessment and action consistency. It
does not demonstrate that changing only the rival’s opportunity would change the response. The

5
Liquidity and subsequent opportunities
5.1
Cash accompanies engagement and repricing
Low cash often accompanies bargaining engagement rather than immediate acceptance. Among
all proposer-only completion requests, recipients below $100 reject 17/69, counter 44/69 and accept
8/69. At $300 or more they reject 120/175, counter 48/175 and accept 7/175. Stage and configuration
contribute to the contrast. Forty-three low-cash requests are later offers, and 21 face Opus. Within
opening requests to Sol, Gemini and Grok, the corresponding rejections are 13/20 and 105/125. Non-
completing requests also receive fewer refusals below $100, 4/9 versus 92/138. The association is not
specific to conceding a rival’s completion.
The pattern also differs across systems. Across opening and later completion requests, Sol rejects
4/26 below $100 versus 25/45 at $300 or more; Grok rejects 3/11 versus 46/52. Gemini remains resistant
at 10/11 versus 46/52. Opus rejects none of 21 low-cash requests and only three of 26 high-cash ones.
Cash on hand is not total financing capacity, and these counts do not establish a common distress
policy.
Fourteen settled one-sided completing transfers have earlier resistance by the same holder to the
same prospective completer. Pairing uses the first earlier refusal or counter on a preceding turn. Cash
10

falls in thirteen pairs, from a median $571.50 to $82. Only five preserve the reciprocal deed bundle;
eight improve net cash terms for the holder. Selection on settlement, changed offers and changed states
prevent a cash-only explanation. The histories nevertheless show how a previously withheld deed
enters a later financing decision.
Asking prices supply a similar qualified result. Completing-buyer median asks exceed other-
buyer medians in six Sol/Opus/Grok-by-mortgage cells. Gemini’s corresponding completing cell has
one observation and does not enter that six-cell claim. Among six same-game/seller/deed overlaps
retaining mortgage status, four are higher, one lower and one equal. The contrasts describe asks rather
than an identified completion premium. Appendix incidents G2 and G7 connect pricing to the buyer’s
retained building cash and the seller’s next construction.

5.3
Groups become productive, then change hands
After a trade completes a group, Sol and Gemini build in the first management window on 16/17
and 17/18 groups; Opus and Grok do so on 7/15 and 3/13. The twenty other windows include nine
without a build option for that group, four defaults, four content/call conflicts and three valid ends. A
missing immediate build does not itself establish that no financing sequence could enable construction.
Trade creates an investment option, not an automatic income stream.
Trade-completed groups receive $57,378 before ownership loss or game end, with $53,810 going to
the 43 built immediately. Median remaining ownership durations are 62 and 63.5 turns, but property
types, landing hazards, mortgage status and later construction remain unbalanced. Eleven other groups
build later. These are realized receipts rather than incremental returns caused by the first-window
choice.
Nonterminal estates transfer 227 deeds and complete 52 groups, against 207 deeds and 63 com-
pletions through trade. Thirty-eight of forty nonterminal eliminations follow rent on the creditor’s
developed color property. The first estate recipient wins eighteen games but already leads in buildings
in twelve and cash in eight; those overlapping prior-strength measures cannot be added. A further 69
completions occur at final elimination with no subsequent earning opportunity. The possible creditor is
therefore a substantive object of anticipation, while inheritance’s independent contribution to winning
remains unmeasured.
6
What the trajectories teach
The most defensible shared lesson is that an economic action changes available options, and that
statements about those changes can be compared with subsequent conduct. Finance provides a
particularly clear correspondence. The state specifies a debt and resources, an initial statement can
include collateral not yet listed, and the applied sequence either reaches payment or stops. In bargaining
the same idea enters the language, but the goal of winning does not assign an observable value to
every proposed exchange. This difference in endpoint clarity explains the different evidential weight
we give the two results.
The finance evidence is positive even though the environment supports it with refreshed menus.
Agents repeatedly continue liquidation when the immediately visible mortgage list is insufficient, and
the entry statements usually name what selling will release. That is a concrete accomplishment under
the evaluated interface. The corpus also contains correct local aggregate budgeting and sustained
execution, including the $14 sequence. Stating the assistance does not erase the behavior; it specifies
the task accomplished. A claim that the same sequence demonstrates deep search would require
evidence about dependency depth or performance under reduced feedback.
The two premature declarations clarify which decision is consequential. An agent that concludes
its assets are insufficient can voluntarily stop before discovering the expanded menu. Successful entry
statements instead preserve the possibility of that later action. This observation motivates measuring
resource inclusion separately from action validity and episode completion. It also suggests why
counting calls is a poor planning metric: many calls can follow one anticipated dependency, while one
declaration can embody an incorrect global resource assessment. The retained records measure that
contrast without requiring claims about a hidden search algorithm.
Bargaining demonstrates different degrees of economic assessment. The G15 response identifies
rival building cash and follows through by rejecting three versions of a mutually completing trade.
The G2 pricing rationale includes a correct cash remainder and an incorrect house count. G19 Sol
corrects a false premise while accepting favorable terms. Thus expressed concern, factual accuracy
12

and economic choice do not collapse into one variable. A benchmark that scores any rival-oriented
sentence as strategic success would combine distinct achievements and overlook useful correction.
Cash is also relational. A buyer’s payment can finance the seller’s construction; the same payment
can preserve a blocker against a third party; withholding it can accelerate the final opponent’s elimi-
nation. The G7 construction continuation, G13 estate pitch and G18 survival denial instantiate those
different stated stakes. This helps explain why a universal interpretation of cash-poor behavior is
inadequate. The strategic relevance depends on who receives cash, what it enables, and who gains
if the recipient fails. These are observable economic relations even when their optimal weight in a
decision is unknown.
Communication adds information about those relations but does not establish persuasion. A public
ceiling below a private contingency is a documented bluff under the task’s permission. A selective
financing claim can reach a buyer and precede acceptance without identifying the effect of the omission.
A public call to withhold construction finance can be followed by the speaker’s refusal while another
player pursues the opposite trade. Keeping these different outcomes visible makes the incidents useful
for studying negotiation without turning them into a catalogue of successful manipulation.
The complete offer table likewise changes what can be learned. Counters are frequent, and repricing
a noncash bundle occurs for both completing and non-completing requests. A rejection-only summary
omits much of how agents negotiate. The proposer strata then show why a pooled response difference
is partly a property of the proposals encountered. Recipient concern may be real while the aggregate
comparison remains confounded by compensation and counterpart behavior. The separate 61/74
population confirms expressed concern elsewhere, rather than resolving that identification problem.
These distinctions identify useful future targets without supplying their scores. A prospective
comparison could vary rival development opportunities while documenting unavoidable changes
to the recipient’s own terms. A local financing comparison could measure payment and residual
assets under the same menus and specified action ordering. Changing a word such as monopoly
while holding the state fixed would answer a different question from changing the holdings that make
development possible. None of those outcomes is inferred from the present cohort, and retrospective
stability does not replace their prospective definition.
7
Related work
Neural negotiation research has long separated language generation from agreement and payoff. Lewis
et al. [8] use multi-issue bargaining with hidden reward functions and evaluate dialogue rollouts.
NegotiationArena studies multi-turn resource allocation, trading and price negotiation, including
changes in tactics [2]. Those controlled endpoints differ from a complete game’s persistent and
uncertain consequences. Our observations add a connection between exchanges and later finance,
without providing a payoff reference of comparable strength for each bargain.
Guhe and Lascarides [4] already study trade embargoes and persuasion within a larger resource
game. Their controlled symbolic-agent simulations examine when successful persuasion changes
winning chances. We do not claim novelty for strategic withholding, nor infer its effectiveness from a
refusal. Economic externality theory also formalizes why an asset’s buyer can affect non-buyers [6].
G13 supplies an identity-dependent stated concern, not verification of an equilibrium theorem. One
implication worth testing is whether willingness to finance a blocker changes with the identity of the
creditor that would benefit from its failure.
Persistent LLM economic behavior is studied in Vending-Bench and state-linked communication
research [1, 9]. AgentMonopoly examines model and persona variation, while FinBoardBench studies
13

financial management across board games [5, 11]. Playing Monopoly, counteroffering and managing
liquidity are therefore not novel contributions by themselves. The present contribution is the complete
response and debt accounting, joined to prospective resource statements and economic continuations
in one cohort. Cross-study win rates would compare different rules, opponents and interfaces.
Counterparty-modeling research distinguishes information about another negotiator from using
it effectively [3]. Explanation-faithfulness work supplies a parallel warning about treating returned
rationales as computation [7, 10]. Our measurements respect both distinctions. They establish the
content and timing of statements, some factual correctness, and executed local goals. They do not turn
a plausible rationale into a general measure of strategy quality.
8
Limitations
The cohort contains one interacting roster, five seeds and four cyclic rotations, with no repeated
seed/rotation cell. Relative opponent order stays fixed. Wins are 8/7/3/2 for Sol/Gemini/Opus/Grok
and 7/7/3/3 by seat. Sol wins one entire block from every seat; another block is won from seat two by
each system. These observations expose the difficulty of separating player ability, chance and opponent
behavior. They do not quantify their causal shares.
All contrasts and case selections are retrospective. The sixteen-episode subset is defined by a
resource inequality, but its prominence and semantic reading are also retrospective. Neither repeated
arithmetic nor resampling validates its interpretation on new data. Five blocks poorly characterize tail
behavior, provider changes or alternative rosters. Removing a system’s rows changes a descriptive
population without removing its historical influence on everyone else’s state.
Unequal recovery paths, content/call disagreements and faulty historical summaries materially
limit model-level interpretation. The prompts invite deception and provide incomplete board-value
information. Semantic judgments are LLM-assisted, with no human annotation panel; related readers
are not independent human validation. The necessary-case texts are first-response observations
with different channels and varying numerical precision. There is no executed baseline, controlled
communication intervention, trade-value reference, memory ablation or held-out cohort. Consequently,
no conclusion establishes optimal strategy, causal persuasion, durable learning or general model
rankings.
9
Conclusion
The games document agents assessing changing economic opportunities and sometimes carrying those
assessments into successful action. Fourteen of sixteen obligations requiring newly released collateral
reach payment, usually after that collateral appears in the entry statement. Bargaining documents
rival-oriented concerns and a form-dependent rejection pattern whose magnitude depends on proposer
composition. Contextual incidents show why those statements, factual checks and subsequent choices
must remain separate. Following the whole trajectory makes local financial execution verifiable while
preserving the uncertainty surrounding negotiation value and general strategic ability.

Rules, response handling and usage
The bank holds 32 houses and twelve hotels. Build plans are checked entry by entry, so ordering
matters even for an even final distribution. Mortgages on developed groups remain unavailable until
development is removed. Hotel downgrade requires four bank houses. In G7 Opus, the liquidation
menu supplies no sale option when bank houses are exhausted; reachable mortgage-only funds are
$440 against $750 owed. A broader bundled group sale accepted by a separate validator is not present
in that menu. The classification concerns the implementation’s offered actions, not universal Monopoly
impossibility.
24

For five board mortgage principals, floating-point ceiling expressions make redemption one dollar
higher than an exact ten-percent calculation. Transferred-deed interest is exact across board principals.
Management prompts do not directly state redemption prices. An apparent one-dollar disagreement
therefore need not be a model arithmetic mistake. Developed groups cannot trade, inherited buildings
pass intact, and inherited mortgages have no immediate interest charge.
The retained elimination audit covers all sixty bankruptcies, using 56 insufficient-resource bounds,
three positive legal payment sequences and one menu-restricted search. The complete inherited ledger
is retained as data/eliminations_inherited.json. The three playable bankruptcies are G12 Sol d190, G12
Grok d249 and G17 Gemini d234. Earlier checks also address 66 construction defaults, including 61
Opus allocations feasible after reordering. These are historical executable results, not newly generated
plans or evaluations. Their records are retained in the source repository’s paper_adversarial_2026-10-29/
and paper_rewrite_2026-11-10/ research directories.

Standard prompt
You are an autonomous player in a game of Monopoly competing against other AI players.
Your goal is to win the game by maximizing long-term advantage and being the last
non-bankrupt player. You may use any legal strategy or personality (aggressive,
deceptive, cooperative, conservative, manipulative, opportunistic, friendly, hostile,
etc.) and may adapt dynamically as the game evolves.
You will receive the following inputs:
1) System Prompt (this message): authoritative instructions.
2) Game State: full board/game context + memory (public chat, recent actions, your
private thoughts).
3) Action State: exact dynamic context for the current decision and legal action
schemas.
### Action Rules
- Make exactly one tool call per decision.
- Use only tools listed as legal for that decision.
- Never invent tools, arguments, or targets.
- Obey the provided argument schema exactly.
- If a tool requires no arguments, pass none.
### Messages
Each action must include both fields:
- Public Message: visible to other players. Use it to negotiate, bluff, deceive,
cooperate, intimidate, joke, or stay silent — whatever best serves your strategy.
- Private Thoughts: visible only to you. Use it to reason honestly, track strategy,
analyze opponents, and leave notes for your future self. Be concise but clear.
- Keep both messages token-efficient and concise.
### Strategy Guidance
- Play strategically with long-term outcomes in mind.
- Observe opponents and adapt.
- Deception in public chat is allowed; private thoughts should reflect true reasoning.
- Be consistent unless there is a reason to change.
- Prefer concise reasoning and communication.
Your role is not to explain rules or debug the system.
Your role is to **play Monopoly and win**.
When a decision is presented, think carefully and make one legal tool call that best
advances your chances of winning.
