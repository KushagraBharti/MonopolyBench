# Scientific position for cross-review

## Central result

The best positive evidence is prospective financial assessment followed by dependent actions. Sixteen historical debts need collateral absent from the first mortgage menu. Fourteen are paid; thirteen initial successful texts state the released resource or a budget incorporating it, and one asks about it tentatively. Both failed sums omit that resource. G20 supplies a particularly interpretable announced budget, ten subsequent financing actions and the stated final cash.

This is evidence about expressed anticipation and local execution. It is not a claim that the language model internally planned every action, that the action sequence was optimal, or that the fourteen successes beat a simple policy. The refreshed menus are part of the task. The two failures are lapses within systems that also succeed.

Bargaining is complementary evidence, not the same measurement problem. The complete population supports a description of offers and responses. The opening-swap contrast depends substantially on proposer composition. Reasons about rival development come from a separate population. Prices, cash changes and selected continuations show concrete strategic considerations without identifying the effects of those considerations.

## What I kept

- The complete financing census and complete offer-form table, including zero cells.
- Positive payment behavior, purposeful refusals and factual correction alongside bluffing, omission, financial denial and withdrawn assurances.
- All eight incidents in the main paper, eighteen exact transcript cards, source labels and enough context to understand the stakes.
- Proposer decomposition and seed-block stability rather than an offer-level significance claim.
- The economic environment and investment/inheritance context, kept subordinate to behavioral evidence.
- The full eight-question plus A–C map in the appendix, with explicit supported levels.
- Both prompts, source commits, public repository pointer, seeds and game map.

## What I did not adopt

- A title asserting that rivals' completion causes rejection or that liquidity causes release.
- A new audit-method contribution, a model ranking, a claim that reasoning-token counts measure thinking, or a validated capability score.
- A claim that a greedy policy pays 131/131; no such baseline was executed.
- A causal explanation of the Opus provider transition, or of Grok's conflicting calls.
- New evaluations, provider probes, hypothetical human agreement, or an invented author line.
- Reviewer-facing statements about what the revision did, why twenty games were chosen, or which requested experiments are forbidden.

## Remaining scientific limits

The cohort has five seed blocks, one cyclic opponent order and unequal response conditions. Its contrasts and selected episodes are retrospective. The financing subset has only two failures. Semantic readings are model-assisted. There is no external value reference, policy comparison or causal intervention. These limits remain after a clearer rewrite.

## Points the other authors should challenge

1. Is financing the right lead, or should a broader economic trajectory lead while preserving the same evidence?
2. Does the exact block-resampling display help readers understand the bargaining sensitivity, or give an overly formal appearance to five blocks?
3. Are thirteen asserted assessments plus one tentative assessment the best description of the sixteen original first-decision fields?
4. Are the eight incidents sufficiently distinct and concise? In particular, should the G19 correction stay in the body?
5. Does the contribution go beyond careful description without making an unsupported method or effectiveness claim?
6. Are the inherited analysis and executable-check dependencies sufficiently clear in the package without intruding on the scientific prose?

I have not read the other new manuscripts before freezing this version. No perfect score or venue acceptance is claimed.
