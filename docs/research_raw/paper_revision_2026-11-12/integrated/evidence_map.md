This paper describes the historical twenty-game cohort. No new games, engine execution, replay execution, synthetic cases, baseline runs, counterfactual rollouts or provider queries were performed.

The four assistants are language models. Their agreement is not human validation, and Sol/Opus research assistants overlap evaluated model families or identifiers. The paper does not use their review count as a scientific sample size.

Integration adds direct first-action checks for all 188 episode entries, the liquidation instruction in 338 initial decision payloads, eight above-bound menus with only declaration, and seven conflicting named-action responses among Grok's 28 acceptances. These are regenerated by tools/integrate_checks.py from original decisions. All sixteen initial fields are re-read into data/initial_text_manifest.json with hashes and pointers; the appendix prints them in full. Review judgments and newly adopted details are mapped in adjudication.json. No reviewer working folder is a runtime build dependency.

Final-review corrections are individually resolved in FINAL_REVIEW_DECISIONS.md. tools/final_checks.py checks reasoning-field presence and metadata across 6,989 final responses, 24 relevant original fields, the Opus cash-bid population, seven price cells and the stored payment sequences. It writes data/final_checks.json with source hashes. The first-action paragraph now uses the entry-defined 132/56 generous-bound partition. data/conflicting_openings.json is a pinned build input. The official Hasbro citation supports the familiar mortgage-release rule; literature_notes.md records the source and access depth.

Sol, Opus and Fable 5.2 each independently read the entire final review candidate. Opus and Fable independently reconstructed the principal offer and debt counts, and all three read the sixteen texts. Their new arithmetic checks corroborate the retained ledgers but do not turn the semantic judgments into human validation. No new engine-executed result is claimed.
