\begin{abstract}
Economic actions change which resources become available and who can use them. We study how LLM agents describe these consequences and act on them in twenty complete four-player Monopoly games. The corpus connects all 920 negotiation responses and 188 financing episodes to delivered information, prospective statements and executed actions. Sixteen debts require mortgages that become available only after building sales. Agents pay fourteen. Thirteen successful initial responses state the additional financing or a budget including it; one considers it tentatively. Both failures omit that collateral from their sums and declare immediately. One successful sequence realizes an announced budget across ten actions, leaving the predicted \$14. These cases document application of a published game rule to resources absent from the current menu. Negotiation records show agents computing competitors' development budgets, pricing completing deeds, correcting a false pitch and bargaining over a rival's survival. A retrospective rejection difference between opening swaps completing only the proposer's group and swaps completing neither group falls from 30.5 to 16.7 percentage points without Grok-authored offers. Eight contextual incidents connect the statements to subsequent transactions and outcomes. The findings document prospective financial assessment followed by dependent execution, and strategic communication over persistent assets. They describe four interacting configurations under unequal response conditions; bargaining effectiveness and intrinsic model rankings remain unmeasured.
\end{abstract}
