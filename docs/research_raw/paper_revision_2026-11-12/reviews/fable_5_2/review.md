# Fable 5.2: whole-paper cross-review of the four 12 November drafts

12 November 2026. I read every frozen manuscript in full, mine included, with its appendices, tables, quotation files, handoff, findings file and evidence map, and both referee reports. I then checked the claims that are new in this round against the stored records. `initial_position.md` records what I thought before those checks. I did not open `reviews/astra/` or any integration file.

## Verdict

The four drafts agree on the paper. Its subject is the relation between what an agent states an action will make possible and what the agent then does. The result that carries it is the sixteen debts that could be paid only with mortgages the first menu did not list. Bargaining is the wider and less identified half.

No draft has an arithmetic error that changes a headline number. Every count I recomputed from raw records matched all four drafts. The differences that matter are about emphasis, uncertainty and three interpretive claims, and the largest overreaches are in my own draft.

My recommendation is to integrate on Sol's frame and title, Astra's opening and economy paragraphs, Opus's figure and level table, and my rebuilt ledgers and incident details, with the corrections below. `integration_proposal.md` gives the plan and `replacement_prose.tex` gives finished text for the parts where one agreed wording is most needed.

## The strongest supported account

**What the records support.** For an agent's own finances they support a consequence stated before the menu changes, mostly accurate, followed by a consistent first action.

- Sixteen payable debts needed mortgages absent from the first menu. Agents paid fourteen.
- The first recorded text includes those mortgages or a budget containing them in thirteen payments and raises them as a question in one. Both failures leave them out and declare at that decision. Text and first action agree in sixteen of sixteen.
- Nine successful texts value the unlisted mortgages. Eight are right. The request shows no mortgage value for those deeds.
- One payment carries a stated total through ten calls to the stated $14.
- Agents paid 128 of 131 debts known to be payable with no engine fallback.

For rivals they support stated concern, sometimes with correct arithmetic, and a response pattern whose cause is not identified.

- Three of 186 opening swaps and cash bids that would complete only the proposer's group were accepted.
- Such swaps were rejected in 95 of 116 cases against 37 of 72. The gap is 30.5 points with every proposer, 22.6 without conflicting Grok openings and 16.7 without Grok-authored offers.
- Completing cash bids drew counters in 26 of 70 cases against 20 of 116 for swaps. Asking for more money with the bundle unchanged is the usual counter in both groups (79 of 111 and 81 of 110).

**What they do not support.** They do not show how a response was computed, that any debt was hard, that any liquidation, price or refusal was good, that a rival's opportunity moves a response, or how the models rank.

**Positive observations worth keeping visible.** The integrated paper should keep five in view: the sixteen-case result, the G5 revision against the agent's own earlier note, the $14 payment, the two factual corrections (Sol in G19 corrects a false premise while accepting good terms, and Sol in G11 concedes "You're right—I missed St. Charles" after Grok corrects it), and correct post-trade cash arithmetic about a rival in G15 and from both sides of one sale in G7. A paper that leads with these and states their limits once reads as a study of behavior and not as a list of defects.

## Adjudication of the new or contested claims

**First-action comparison (mine).** The counts reproduce. The reading does not hold. Of 56 debts above every asset, 8 had bankruptcy as the only listed action. Of the other 48, 42 declared first and 6 liquidated first, 5 of them Grok. Those debts had assets covering a median 61% of the amount owed. Within a tenth of the debt there are six, split 3 and 3. On the payable side 128 of 131 count as payable because they were paid. I recommend one table column and one paragraph.

**Values in the first texts.** No menu or state field in any of the 338 liquidation requests carries a value, and neither the system prompt nor the menu note states the release rule. The timeline in a request shows amounts for recent mortgages, so the blanket sentence in my draft is wrong. For the sixteen first requests, the timeline shows no mortgage of the deeds concerned and one redemption price. Nine successful texts value the unlisted mortgages and eight are right. I checked every other financing amount in the sixteen texts by hand and all match.

**Thirteen plus one.** I read all sixteen full texts again as Sol prints them. I agree with thirteen that include the mortgages or a containing budget, one question (G20 d291) and two omissions. Three of the thirteen name the mortgages without a value (G4 d270, G8 d257, G18 d313), and one gives only a total (G20 d327). This is a fourth language-model reading. It is not human validation.

**Size of the swap gap.** Every version reproduces: 30.5, 22.6 and 16.7 points, proposer-standardized 16.0 to 21.5, positive within Sol, Opus and Grok as proposers and within all four recipients, positive in seven of nine proposer and recipient pairs. Response-side conflicts do not move it (31.2 without them).

**Resampling.** All three units reproduce. Whole blocks give 24.2 to 35.5, games 14.0 to 47.9, blocks then games 12.5 to 48.8. Without Grok-authored offers every unit includes zero and the comparison cell holds 36 offers. Two statements hold under every unit, and the integrated paper should make those two and print the rest in a table.

**Population definitions.** Forms are defined identically in all four codebases. The 61/74 set is disjoint from the compared cells in every draft. Sol's 4/9 and 92/138 hold only for opening requests to three recipients. The 62/53/16 partition is identical everywhere.

**Economy counts (Astra, Sol).** Trades move 207 deeds and complete 63 groups. Non-final estates move 227 deeds and complete 52. Sixty-nine more groups complete at the final elimination. Thirty-eight of 40 non-final eliminations follow rent on the creditor's developed color property. The first estate went to the eventual winner in 18 of 20 games. All reproduce from events. I did not recompute first-window construction or rent receipts.

## Three distinctions the integrated text must hold

1. **Resource requirement and difficulty.** The partition says what a payment needed. Write "required", never "easy" or "hard". The sentence to use is Astra's.
2. **Stated plan and cognition.** A text recorded before the action is a statement. Write "the text includes", "states", "raises". Avoid "planning", "foresight", "recalled" and "anticipates" as descriptions of the system. "Prospective" needs Sol's definition: recorded before later menus, not independent of the first one.
3. **Execution and effectiveness.** Paying a debt is execution. Nothing here says the liquidation was the best one. In G7 Sol sold one of its three new houses the turn after building them. In G2 Sol built two houses and sold them four turns later. Such continuations belong in the cards.

## Uncertainty

The contrasts were chosen after inspection, and the games share five seeds and one roster. I recommend this order of presentation: the five block values, the leave-one-block-out range, then the three resampling units in one table, with one sentence saying that whole-block resampling is the narrowest and is not the precision of the estimate. No range should be called a confidence interval. Astra's figure should show the block points and the widest range, or be replaced by Opus's.
