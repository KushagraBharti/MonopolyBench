## What the four drafts agree on

The four drafts were written independently and reached the same paper. Each one says the subject is the relation between what an agent states an action will make possible and what the agent then does. Each one leads with debts and treats bargaining as broader and less identified. Each one reports the same core numbers:

- 188 debt episodes, 128 paid, 131 payable, 56 above every own asset, one menu-restricted.
- Sixteen debts that needed mortgages absent from the first menu. Fourteen paid. Thirteen first texts include the released mortgages or a budget containing them, one raises them as a question, and both failures leave them out and declare at once.
- Opening swaps rejected 95/116 against 37/72, cash bids 42/70 against 67/103, and the swap gap falling from 30.5 to 16.7 points without Grok-authored offers.
- The 61/74 reason codes belong to a disjoint population.
- Eight incidents, the corrected G18 motive, both replay comparisons, Grok's required tool choice.

This agreement is weaker evidence than it looks. All four of us read the same two referee reports and the same four assessments first, and the sixteen-case result was proposed by a referee. Four language models converging after shared input is not replication. It does mean the integration is mostly about emphasis, uncertainty and which new claims survive, and not about choosing a thesis.

## The finding I think matters most

The sixteen-case result. A debt whose payment needs collateral the menu does not yet list is defined by arithmetic on the delivered state, so the set does not depend on what any agent said or did. Inside that set the first recorded text and the first action line up in all sixteen cases: the released mortgages are in the text and the agent continues, or they are missing and the agent declares bankruptcy. The stated amounts are mostly right. That is a statement made before the menu changes, checkable against the state, followed by the matching action.

It supports three things: an expressed anticipation, a mostly accurate one, and an action consistent with it. It does not show how the response was computed, it does not show the debts were hard, and it does not show the liquidations were good.

## The narrative I expect to recommend

- **Frame.** Sol's two evidential levels: a debt has an endpoint the records can check, and a trade has none. Opus's own-options and rival-options pairing says the same thing in fewer words. Astra's opening paragraph, the $1,400 debt against the smaller debt that ends in bankruptcy, is the best first paragraph of the four.
- **Evidence ladder.** Opus and I both adopted referee A's five levels. Opus's single table of results by level is the best use of it. Tagging every paragraph with a level, as Opus's prose and mine do, reads mechanically.
- **Bargaining.** Report the full table, the proposer table and the composition versions together, then state plainly that the cause is not identified. Sol's bargaining section does this most carefully.
- **Economic populations.** Astra and Sol keep construction after trades, rent receipts, estates and holder cash. Opus and I cut them. The brief asks for the full economic populations, so I expect to side with Astra and Sol against my own draft.

## Where I expect each draft to be wrong or too strong

I list my own first.

**Fable 5.2.**
1. The quit-or-continue comparison (50 of 56 against 3 of 131). On the payable side it restates the payment rate, because 128 of the 131 are payable only because they were paid. On the unpayable side it means something only if those menus offered an alternative to bankruptcy. I did not check that before freezing. I will check it now.
2. My handoff says the first-menu partition "calibrates difficulty", and the paper calls one row "the hard set" and the others "easy". The partition states what resource a payment needed. Nothing measured difficulty.
3. "Each one is a board fact the system recalled." My fifteen checked figures are one per text and of mixed kinds. Some are shortfalls the prompt may supply directly. The claim needs the prompt read again and the figures split by whether the request contained them.
4. "Refusals follow the event of completion and not the size of the rival's opportunity." This is a null result on a coarse measure in cells of 8 to 48 offers, chosen after the fact, and I put it in the abstract and the conclusion. The sentence about the word "monopoly" driving refusals is speculation.
5. Refusal styles by system rest on regular expressions I wrote after reading examples.

**Opus.** "Most debts were easy" and "the decision that needs foresight is whether to stop" make the same difficulty claim I made. "Where channels agree, a stated plan is a good guide to the action that follows" generalizes from sixteen debts and eight chosen incidents. One quoted span in G13 may not support the sentence that introduces it.

**Astra.** The main figure draws the whole-block resampling range as the uncertainty bar. With five blocks that range is narrow, 24.2 to 35.5, and a reader will take it for precision. Opus's two-stage range for the same contrast is 12.3 to 48.8.

**Sol.** The main offer table folds sale offers into "other", which hides the 26 of 41 rejections of offers to sell a holder its own completing deed. Five incidents sit in the appendix, including the factual correction, which is one of the positive results the charter asks to keep visible.
