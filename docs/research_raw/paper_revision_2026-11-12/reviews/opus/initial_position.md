## What the four drafts agree on

The four drafts converge more than they differ. All four:

- lead or co-lead with the agent's own changing options: 16 debts whose payment needs mortgages released only by building sales, 14 paid, 13 first-decision texts that count the released collateral plus one that raises it as a question, and two bankruptcies whose stated sums omit it;
- report the full 188-episode partition (128 paid, 56 above the own-asset bound, 1 menu-restricted, 3 payable declarations) and say the 128/131 rate is not a difficulty measure;
- describe the opening swap/cash contrast (95/116 vs 37/72; 42/70 vs 67/103) as exploratory and composition-sensitive (30.5, 22.6 and 16.7 points), with the 61/74 reasons kept as a disjoint population;
- correct G18 (survival cash for the last opponent, no inheritance language), name both stored replay comparisons, restore Grok's required tool choice, the repository, commits, seeds and game map;
- keep eight incidents and the 8 + A–C question map.

So the integration problem is not which thesis to choose. It is which version of each shared claim is best supported and best written, and which of the claims that only one draft makes survive checking.

## My position before checking

1. **Thesis.** The best single account is the one my draft and Fable's share and Sol's title states most plainly: when these agents state, before acting, what a move will make possible for themselves or for a rival, the applied action agrees with the statement; the statement is often accurate about the agent's own finances and less often exact about rivals. The evidence ladder (stated, accurate, consistent, sensitive, effective) should organize the claims, once, and no result reaches the fourth or fifth rung.

2. **Finance leads, as a resource-requirement partition, not a difficulty scale.** Tiers A/B/N say what the first menu could raise. They are not calibrated difficulty. "Hard set" (Fable), "harder evaluative question" (Astra) and "most debts were easy" (my draft) all lean toward difficulty language; the integrated text should say "requires" rather than "is hard".

3. **Fable's quit-or-continue comparison (50/56 vs 3/131) is the most important new claim to check.** If it reproduces, it is a useful, positive, whole-population observation: the decision to stop agrees with payability at the first menu. But I expect it to be weaker than it reads. Most payable debts are trivially payable from listed items, most above-bound debts may be far above the bound, and one of the three payable declarations (G12 Grok) is a channel conflict in which the agent's own note and content say to pay. The informative comparison is near the boundary. I will check the margins before endorsing it, and I will not let it displace the sixteen.

4. **Fable's "the prompt supplies no mortgage value or release rule" claim needs a source check.** If the delivered liquidation prompt shows no mortgage values for unlisted deeds and the system prompt does not state the release rule, then the correct figures in thirteen texts are recalled board facts plus a rule inferred or recalled from outside the prompt. That would strengthen the finance result. If the state block carries values, the claim must go.

5. **Bargaining is a complete census with one robust simple fact and one fragile contrast.** I expect "3 of 186 completing openings accepted, against 21 of 175 non-completing" to be the most robust bargaining observation, because it does not depend on the swap/cash split. The swap/cash split stays exploratory. Fable's "within every recipient the cash-bid gap is 0 to 9 points, so the pooled −5 is composition" is a new Simpson-type claim and needs checking. Fable's "refusal does not follow the size of the rival's opportunity" and "attaches to the event of completion" go beyond the data: rejection is already 79–88% in every capacity bin, the measure ignores mortgage capacity, and a flat line near a ceiling is absence of evidence, not evidence of insensitivity. My own draft makes a milder version of the same claim and should be corrected too.

6. **Incidents.** Eight distinct incidents, exact quotes, named channels, checked receipt, supported continuations. I favor four full incidents in the main text and four in an appendix casebook with one-paragraph main-text summaries (Fable's structure), over eight full cards in the body (Astra and my draft), because the reviewers' strongest presentation complaint was length and dispersion. Fable's new context (Opus's G15 table appeal, Grok's earlier G2 bid) is useful if it checks out.

7. **Uncertainty.** Two drafts (Astra, Sol) use exact whole-block resampling only; mine uses two-stage block-then-game resampling; Fable prints block and game resampling side by side. With five blocks, block-only resampling has very few distinct outcomes and turned out narrower than game resampling. I think the integrated paper should print the per-block values and leave-one-block-out ranges as the primary display and give one resampling summary with its target and its retrospective status stated, without letting a narrow block-only band read as reassurance.

8. **What I expect to concede from my own draft.** Its eight full incident cards make the main text long; its capacity sentence ("no sign that refusals scale with the rival's building power") needs the ceiling caveat; and it under-uses the positive whole-population observations (opening acceptance, counter behavior) that Fable found.
