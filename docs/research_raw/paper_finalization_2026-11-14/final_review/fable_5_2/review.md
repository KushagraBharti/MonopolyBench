# Final whole-paper review

Fable 5.2, 15 November 2026. Candidate: `integrated/manuscript.pdf`, SHA-256 `e5c44beb…0dae`, 29 pages.

## Verdict

The paper is ready for release after one sentence is corrected and six small changes are made. I found no error in any count, table or proof. Every number I recomputed from the raw decision logs matches the manuscript.

One main-text sentence about the G13 incident is contradicted by the record. Seven further points are material: two wordings that claim more than the records show, one missing denominator, two supported results the paper needs and lacks, one literature sentence, and the release package. None needs a new evaluation, and none calls for restructuring. The changes add about 230 words to a main text that ends on page 7.

Exact passages and evidence are in `findings.json`. Paste-ready text is in `replacement_prose.tex`.

## What the paper establishes

The argument is sound and worth publishing in this form.

1. **Payment is weak evidence here, and the paper proves why.** In all 131 payable entry states, any uninterrupted run of valid cash-raising actions pays. I recomputed the premises from the raw records and read the five engine functions the proof depends on. The 108/12/9/2 partition is right. The two remaining G3 entries satisfy the stock argument, and the paragraph on bundled sales covers the case I expected to be missing. The paper states this as a conditional result from source and never as a measured baseline.

2. **The informative records are what agents count, keep and stop on.** The sixteen debts that need released collateral are read with a five-way rubric that I reproduce field by field. The two failures are false maxima with correct addition. The 28 of 30 building owners who pay without a sale are counted correctly.

3. **The same accounting appears in bargaining.** The G7 exchange is the best evidence of this in the corpus. Each side writes the other's development budget, and the figures are right. The response census, the composition of the swap contrast and the separate 61 of 74 population are reported at their actual strength.

4. **The scope is stated once and held.** Unequal response conditions, undisclosed rule differences, model-assisted reading and retrospective selection are all in the design section and the limitations. The paper claims no ranking, no causal effect and no priority.

## Factual correction

**F-01. G13 chronology.** The main text says the buyer "already considers that estate logic before the omission-framed reply." The record shows otherwise. Opus's $130 counter at d251 cites keeping Grok solvent against the railroad rent. Its weighing of who inherits the yellows is in the acceptance at d253, after Grok's reply at d252. ## Material findings

**M-01. G12 d249 is cleaner in the paper than in the record.** The main text says it "is not an omitted-resource case." The returned explanation first counts only the one listed sale target, three Virginia houses for $150, finds a $57 shortfall and writes "I will declare bankruptcy." It then revises to $393. The note inside the declare call opens with "Full even liquidation cannot cover the $250" and ends by calling bankruptcy a mistake. The paper reports only the end. The case is still a conflict between text and call, since the last assessment in every channel is a sufficient route. The paper should show the first half too. Its own rule is that channel selection must not hide a qualification.

**M-02. The stop decision has no population in the main text.** Section 3 names "decisions to stop" as informative and then reports six above-bound starts without saying out of how many. Fifty-six episodes have a full bound below the debt, eight menus force bankruptcy, and 42 of the other 48 first calls declare at once. One sentence gives the reader that. It does not make the table a headline.

**M-03. "Preference" in the 28 of 30 paragraph.** The menu lists the mortgage action before the sale action in every case that offers both. A sale is offered in 29 of the 30 first menus. The paper cites work showing that menu order can change behavior and says it cannot identify a menu effect. The paragraph should say "pattern" and state the order. The two Opus exceptions are worth a clause: only railroads were listed, and both returned explanations compare the railroad mortgage with the house.

**M-04 and M-05. See the next section.**

**M-06. The Arena comparison.** "With a human annotation check" overstates that study. Its labels come from model judges in three families, with a 50-message set labeled by one author. The contrast with this paper is population coverage, and both rely on model readers.

**M-07. Release package.** Details are under release usability below.

## The strongest positive result the paper leaves out

The abstract asks how agents assess resources that become usable after an action. The main text answers from sixteen payable debts. The same judgment is on record in 37 first responses.

Twenty-one of the 48 unpayable first responses face buildings that lock further mortgages, so their full bound differs from what the menu lists. I read all 21. Sixteen contain the full-bound figure inside an itemization that names the released mortgages. Two itemize them without a total. Three state the listed total as a maximum: Sol in G3 d426, Opus in G5 d309 and Grok in G8 d280. All 21 debts were unpayable either way.

With the sixteen payable debts, the population is 37 episodes in which the listed resources fall short of the debt and buildings lock more. In 32 the first response counts or raises the locked collateral. Five stop at the listed total, from all four configurations, and in three of the five it changed nothing.

A second observation concerns the same subtotal. Both failed totals equal the listed-only total to the dollar. Five successful fields state that same subtotal for their own portfolios and then add the released mortgages. The failures are the first step of the computation the successes complete.

These facts matter for three reasons. They more than double the evidence for its central question. They give the two failures a denominator. They show that a listed-only maximum is not peculiar to Sol and Gemini, which the sentence "the same two systems succeed elsewhere" otherwise suggests.

The supplement already holds the raw material. Its 43 of 48 count mixes 27 cases with nothing locked and the 21 where the question applies. The split is where the information is.

## Assessment of the integration decisions

I agree with most of the log. The adopted items are right: Sol's opening and portable build, Opus's 28 of 30 and G7 exchange, the bundled-sale paragraph, the selected-field rule, five incidents in the main text and eight in the appendix.

I also agree with three corrections to my own draft. A numeral match is not an accurate assessment, and G8 d287 and G17 d267 show why. "Facing unlisted collateral" was the wrong label for 37 episodes when 121 have some. The rules do not always agree on what can be raised, because the hotel-sale difference matters.

I dissent from two logged decisions.

- **Declining the 37-episode result outright.** The log says 37 "is not an exhaustive rate of missing collateral across all eligible entries" and also that the reading "may describe its explicitly selected subset." The subset has an exact definition, U0 below the debt with M* above M0. In the other 84 entries the listed resources already cover the debt, so no maximum is needed. The fix is to name the population correctly. Dropping the result loses the paper's broadest evidence on its own question.
- **Leaving the first-action counts out of the main text.** I agree they are not a headline or an accuracy score. The main text still needs to say that the six are six of 48.

Two items were dropped without a recorded reason: the listed-only total as a waypoint in successful texts, from Opus's draft, and the fact that the two Opus house sales were explicit comparisons. Each takes one sentence to restore.

## Release usability

The package builds. I copied the static sources into my own folder and compiled them to 29 pages with text identical to the pinned PDF and no LaTeX warnings. I ran the five recheck scripts on a second copy. All five pass, and 85 of 86 output files are byte-identical to the shipped ones.

Five things need attention before the archive is final.

1. Eleven unused section files ship. Two contain wording the 14 November review withdrew, including "before choosing whether to proceed" and "negative only when pooled".
2. `refresh_records.py` iterates a Python set, so a rerun changes the key order in `continuation_premises.json`. The content is equal and the hash is not. A user who follows the README then fails `package_release.py --verify`.
3. The entry-point path exists at no commit yet. The log already tracks this.
4. `qa/portability.json` describes an earlier archive with 28 pages. A newer `qa/portability_candidate.json`, written by the integration while I was reviewing, agrees with my build. The stale file should go, and the README still says finalization is in progress.
5. A rebuild in another directory gives the same text and a different PDF hash. The README should say so. The PDF also carries no title or author metadata.

## Dissent

My three points of dissent are these.

1. The 37-episode result belongs in the paper, correctly named.
2. G12 d249 should not be called "not an omitted-resource case."
3. The main text needs the denominator for the six above-bound starts.

I hold no dissent on the thesis, the structure, the incident selection or the scope statements.
