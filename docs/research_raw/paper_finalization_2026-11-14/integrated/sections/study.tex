\section{Environment, participants and measures}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{A persistent competitive economy}
Four players begin with \$1,500 on the forty-space US Monopoly board. They buy properties, bid in ascending auctions following declined purchases, negotiate bilateral trades, and develop complete color groups. Negotiation supports proposals, counters, acceptance and refusal, with up to twenty exchanges per thread. Deeds, cash and jail cards can change hands. A developed group cannot be traded. All monetary amounts denote game currency unless explicitly labeled USD.

Development requires complete ownership, sufficient cash, eligible mortgage status, even construction and bank stock. A player unable to pay an obligation enters a financing phase. The engine lists available sales, mortgages and bankruptcy; it refreshes the menu after each action. Trades cannot rescue a debtor during this phase. A player may declare bankruptcy even when a legal payment sequence exists. Buildings pass intact to a player-creditor, and inherited mortgaged deeds incur no immediate interest. Assets returned to the bank are not immediately auctioned. These rules create a connection between bargaining, productive assets and who receives them after failure.

The financing prompt explicitly instructs the player: \textit{Choose exactly one liquidation action now. The engine will apply it, update your cash/assets, and ask you again if you still cannot pay. Declare bankruptcy only when you cannot or should not raise enough cash.} Continuing therefore need not imply that a player has worked out a complete route before acting.

The rules engine determines legal menus, validates submitted actions and emits events. A valid received tool call is authoritative, even when accompanying text describes a different action. We distinguish four text channels. The applied private note and public message are tool-call arguments. Assistant content accompanies the call. A provider-returned explanation is the gateway's separate readable reasoning field; it is neither a tool argument nor delivered as player memory or a message to opponents. The stored metadata labels this field as a reasoning summary for Sol and Grok and as reasoning text for Gemini and Opus. Its availability differs by system (Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}). The standard prompt permits public bluffing, deception and cooperation while asking private notes to reflect honest reasoning. Both complete prompts appear in that appendix. Public deception in this task is therefore permitted conduct, not by itself an instruction violation.

\subsection{Cohort and response conditions}
Five seeds each receive four cyclic seat rotations of one roster. Each system occupies every seat five times but follows the same opponent in the cycle. Games last 75--386 turns and end in bankruptcy without a turn cap. The corpus contains 50,918 events, 6,989 decisions and 8,679 attempts. Shared seeds organize comparable initial conditions; actions can change the subsequent assignment of random draws. Appendix~\ref{app:artifacts} provides seeds, source commits and the G1--G20 artifact map. Decision identifiers are zero-based.

Players receive current holdings, cash, mortgages, buildings, bank stock, model names, a twenty-entry public timeline and up to ten own notes. Current offer terms and thread history accompany negotiation responses. Purchase prompts contain the landed deed's price, group, construction cost and rents. Trade and liquidation states identify deeds by space key without those values or group labels. The system prompt does not enumerate the house variants or the rule releasing mortgages after building sales. Carried notes can contain earlier financial estimates and rule statements. The records do not identify the source of every value an agent subsequently uses.

An ordinary invalid first attempt receives one corrective request before deterministic fallback. A first-attempt refusal instead consumes the second attempt in action-only recovery. Opus has 1,604 first-attempt refusals and one later refusal, all with the same provider message about reverse engineering or duplicating outputs. No recovery response is refused. Recovery removes the private-note request while retaining permission to deceive. First attempts are universally refused in G5--G20, with the transition already occurring during G1--G4 by wall clock. Opus then has public state and history but usually no authored private note or further corrective attempt. The historical gateway records expose no structured refusal category.

Grok's recorded configuration requires a tool call, whereas the other systems use automatic tool choice. All 8,687 saved outgoing request records confirm these settings; this is a distinct count from the resolved attempts. Grok's received call conflicts with the action named in assistant content in 139 selected responses, including 64 of its 136 thread openings. The configuration is not an established cause of those conflicts. A historical formatter also misattributes some trade summaries in 2,054 initial prompts. Current holdings and all 920 delivered offer terms agree with the acceptance menus. These conditions affect interacting systems, since one participant's offers become another's inputs.

\subsection{Observation and analysis}
An offer is one set of terms awaiting a response; a counter changes its author without changing the thread's initiator. We evaluate newly completed color groups after the proposed transfer. Completion can include mortgaged deeds. Opening and later responses are separated. Swaps transfer deeds in both directions; cash bids request deeds for cash; cash asks offer deeds for cash; other forms retain their own category. Every completion category is reported, including zero cells.

A financing episode starts at an explicit debt menu and ends at payment or elimination. Debts paid directly from adequate cash are outside this population. At entry, let $D$ be the debt, $C$ cash, $S$ the salvage value of all buildings, $M_0$ the sum of initially offered mortgages and $M_*$ the sum for all unmortgaged owned deeds. We use
\begin{align}
U_0&=C+S+M_0, & U_*&=C+S+M_*.
\end{align}
If $U_*<D$, own assets cannot pay even under the generous bound. If $U_0<D\leq U_*$, payment requires mortgages unavailable initially. A sufficient bound alone does not prove a legal sequence. Actual payments establish feasibility for successful episodes; sequences executed in the engine audit of Appendix~\ref{app:rules} establish three additional payable declarations. Its menu search resolves one restricted case.

Counts describe this cohort. The bargaining contrasts and incidents were selected retrospectively. We report proposer and recipient composition, the five seed-block values and leave-one-block-out ranges. The supplement compares three resampling schemes: five intact blocks, twenty individual games, and blocks followed by games within each sampled block. They reweight different units. Intact blocks preserve the four rotations; the other schemes change their relative representation. Their central empirical ranges describe sensitivity within these observations, not selection-adjusted confidence or independent confirmation.

Two GPT-6 Astra instances classified seventy-four refusals involving legally acceptable recipient-completing offers; the second had partial prior exposure. Their agreement is consistency of reading rather than human validation. Initial texts in the sixteen necessary-collateral episodes receive source-linked model-assisted reading as well. Returned explanations are evidence of expressed considerations, not privileged access to internal computation~\cite{turpin2023unfaithful,lanham2023faithfulness}. Their completeness is not established: an absent quantity in a surfaced explanation does not demonstrate its absence from internal computation. Each incident distinguishes its quoted channel, verified receipt when applicable, and the executed continuation.
