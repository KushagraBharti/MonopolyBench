# Independent choices and dissent

I wrote the complete new main text before opening any other November 14 finalization draft. It preserves financial assessment and strategic bargaining as the scientific subjects, with response conditions stated once in the design. It does not replace the user's multi-agent questions with a proposed controlled finance study.

The main text fits seven normal two-column pages. Five compact incidents remain in the body, and all eight complete chronologies remain in the appendix. All sixteen focal responses now print both available channels rather than choosing the authored note over returned explanation. All first-response channels from the 188 debts are machine-readable.

I accept the reactive-financing criticism and strengthen it to a source argument covering 131 historically payable entries. Payment is therefore context rather than proof of difficult planning. I retain the affirmative evidence: explicit dependency application, selective asset preservation, exact valuation, rival-development concerns, bargaining limits and estate incentives.

I do not adopt a causal interface, memory-anchor or strategic-regret thesis. The required interventions or searches remain outside the standing scope. I also do not turn the same observations into a generalized new auditing method. The resulting paper is an observational account whose contribution is the complete relationship between economic assessments, response choices and realized conduct in this corpus.

The last two-state continuation extension deserves particular scrutiny. Model-assisted semantic categories remain fallible. The title deliberately names the empirical subjects rather than asserting an unconfirmed causal discovery. No score or acceptance prediction is claimed.

During PDF inspection I corrected a draft-only ordering error in the system win labels; the counts and underlying game map were never changed. Historical manuscripts remain untouched.
