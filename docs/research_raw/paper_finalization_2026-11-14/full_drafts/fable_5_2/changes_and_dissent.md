# Changes and dissent

Fable 5.2, 14 November 2026. Written after the draft was complete. I did not open the other authors' new drafts.

## 1. The question I chose

**What do these agents count as raisable money, their own when a debt arrives and a rival's when a trade is proposed, how accurate is the count, and how does the applied action relate to it?**

I chose it for three reasons.

- It is what the records can answer exactly. Every mortgage and resale value is fixed by the board, so a stated total is right or wrong to the dollar.
- It covers the positive evidence and the errors with one measure. An exact bound and a listed-only total are the same kind of object.
- It joins finance and bargaining without a forced bridge. The notes in G15, G2, G7, G13 and G20 are budgets written about another player.

The 12 November manuscript asked whether agents anticipate what their actions make available and whether behavior realizes the assessment. Payment was half of its evidence. Section 3 of this draft shows that payment follows from not stopping, so that question no longer has a second half.

## 3. Independent judgments

1. **Lead with exact valuation.** The strongest positive finding in the corpus is that agents state the full asset bound to the dollar with no price list. It holds across systems and across 48 cases, and it is checkable by arithmetic. I put it ahead of the sixteen.

2. **The sixteen are a text result.** Their value is in what the first responses say. I give the tiers a table and the figure, and I give payment one sentence.

3. **The listed-only total is a description, and I state its denominator.** I do not call it a mechanism. I list four candidate explanations without ranking them.

4. **The stop decision mixes two things.** One is what can be raised, which has an exact answer. The other is what the creditor receives, where the engine departs from the rulebook without saying so. The paper says this in the setting section, where it changes how a reader takes everything after it.

5. **Grok's conflicts are reported as channel disagreements.** I do not say which channel shows the intended action. I do say that five of the six financing starts on unpayable debts carry text that favors stopping, because the first-action table is misread without it.

6. **No accuracy rate.** Section 5 prints the two-by-two table and then says why it is not a score. The abstract and introduction give no rate for first actions.

7. **Incidents are chosen for the question.** Each bargaining incident in the main text contains a budget written about another player. The three in the appendix are about a correction, an appeal and an assurance. They are accurate and less tied to the question.

8. **No p-value, no interval on 14 of 16, no model on the bargaining contrast.** The percentile block bootstrap is named as such, reported once in the main text for the one contrast where it matters, and described as sensitivity to block weights.

## 4. Dissent

### Against the team synthesis

**4.1 The synthesis proposes a question about "what financial resources agents represent" and "where assessments agree or disagree with actions". I narrowed it to what is counted and whether the count is right.** "Represent" claims more than text can show. "Count" is what a note does.

**4.2 The synthesis keeps payment as "a useful recorded outcome".** I agree it is recorded. I give it no analytic role beyond the census, because in 131 of 131 payable states it is implied by the first-menu choice and by not declaring later. All three payable declarations happened at the first menu.

**4.3 The synthesis treats the 179-of-188 figure as something to reject.** I agree it must not be a headline or be called accuracy, and the draft does neither. I disagree that the table should be hidden. A reader needs the full two-by-two table to see that the nine departures are nine. The draft prints the table, says what it is not, and spends its words on the nine.

**4.4 Main-text incidents.** Astra and Sol preferred a broader casebook in the main text, and I preferred three on 14 November. The brief asks for at least five. I have six, by adding one finance incident and by cutting each to the facts that bear on the question. I still think G19, the G20 appeal and G18 belong in the appendix of a paper with this question.

**4.5 The conditional-inventory account.** Astra's recommendations called it the strongest candidate. I list it as one of four and add the two facts that weaken any memory account: both failed sums equal the listed-only total, which the menu alone yields, and a carried conditional inventory precedes four payments and two declarations.

### Against the referee feedback

**4.6 The planted-number thesis** is not supported by the records and is not in the paper.

**4.7 "The task needs no anticipation."** Routing needs none. Stating the bound does. The draft separates the two, and the second is where the positive evidence is.

**4.8 A regret metric** is not computed. Section 4.4 describes routes and says no search was run.

**4.9 I did not add a reference quota.** The bibliography has 24 entries, each used for a distinction.

### Against my own earlier work

**4.10** On 12 November I contributed the first-action count and presented six Grok channel conflicts as decisions to finance. Section 5 corrects that.

**4.11** On 14 November I wrote that the note "is written after the action is chosen". I withdrew that in cross-review, and the paper says only that note and action are parts of one response.

**4.12** On 14 November I made no claim about the 23 hotel states among the payable debts. I then covered 21 of them. Astra closed the last two. I checked that argument before using it (section 5 below).

**4.13** My earlier drafts treated the engine's settlement rules as background. They are now Table 1.

## 5. Check of Astra's extension from 129 to 131

The brief asks for a critical check. I did the following.

- Read `_resolve_pending_payment`, `_mortgageable_space_indices`, `_sellable_building_space_indices`, `_validate_sell_plan`, `_apply_sell_plan` and `_handle_bankruptcy` in the engine.
- Confirmed by blob hash that the engine file is identical at both run commits and in the working tree.
- Recomputed the premises for G3 d456 and G3 d461 from the raw records: one hotel in light blue, one in dark blue, houses on red, bank houses 0 and 6, houses in hotel-free groups 8 and 2.
- Re-derived the argument for the general case of a valid action, which may be a bundled sale plan that the menu does not list item by item. Astra's note covers bundles in one sentence. Appendix B states why each unit operation inside a bundle leaves R unchanged or raises it.

Result: the argument holds. Two points of wording differ from Astra's note.

- Astra's condition is stated for the two entries. I state it as a general sufficient condition: at most one hotel per hotel-bearing group, and bank houses plus houses in hotel-free groups at least four. With two hotels in one group the first exchange can lower R, which is why the condition needs the first clause.
- I say "valid financing actions that each raise cash". An empty or zero-proceeds plan would not shrink the measure.

I agree with Astra on what the argument is not. It is not a baseline that was run, and it does not cover invalid calls, call limits or other states. G7 is outside the 131.
