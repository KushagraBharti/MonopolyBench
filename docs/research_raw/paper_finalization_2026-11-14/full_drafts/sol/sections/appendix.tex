\section{Source argument and historical feasibility evidence}
\label{app:proof}

The engine source is identical at the two collection commits for the payment, mortgage, and building-sale routines considered here. The relevant file is \path{python/packages/engine/src/monopoly_engine/engine.py}. Functions \texttt{\_resolve\_pending\_payment}, \texttt{\_mortgageable\_space\_indices}, \texttt{\_sellable\_building\_space\_indices}, and \texttt{\_validate\_sell\_plan} specify the rule conditions used below. The active fallback in \path{python/packages/arena/src/monopoly_arena/decision_resolver.py} prefers an available mortgage, otherwise one listed building sale, and declares without either. The source proof concerns valid cash-raising continuations, not an evaluation of that implementation's runtime behavior.

Before payment, let $Q$ be cash plus remaining nominal salvage plus unmortgaged principal. A mortgage exchanges remaining principal for cash. A house sale exchanges salvage for cash. A hotel downgrade exchanges one building-equivalent unit for cash and leaves four house levels. Therefore $Q$ is invariant. The sum of remaining building-equivalent levels and unmortgaged deeds decreases by at least one under every strictly cash-raising action, including valid bundles. The sequence is finite. To establish eventual payment it remains to exclude an unpaid state in which assets exist but the menu offers no financing.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrL{0.60\linewidth}@{}}\toprule

\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Recorded holdings satisfy complete ownership and evenness of every developed group in all 131 entries, and every initial $Q$ covers the current debt. The final two are G3 d456 and d461. Both have one light-blue hotel and one dark-blue hotel, plus red houses. The first has zero bank houses and eight houses in hotel-free groups; the second has six and two respectively. While hotels remain, define $R=B+H$, where $B$ is bank houses and $H$ houses in hotel-free groups. A sale from such a group moves a unit from $H$ to $B$. Mortgaging leaves $R$ unchanged. Downgrading a group's sole hotel spends four bank houses but places them, and any previously locked houses of that group, into $H$. Thus $R$ cannot decrease. Initially $R=8$. If $B\geq4$, a remaining hotel can be downgraded; otherwise $H>0$ supplies a highest-house sale. After all hotels are removed the hotel-free argument applies. Valid bundles preserve the same accounting.

No continuing sequence under those premises can exhaust offered financing unpaid. If all resources were exhausted, cash would equal initial $Q\geq D$, and automatic payment would already have occurred. The argument is for the current obligation and excludes invalid actions, no-proceeds plans, interruptions, voluntary declaration, and subsequent debts. The extension from 129 to 131 was first supplied by Astra in the historical review; this draft independently checked its entry arithmetic and the invariant argument. That is a further model source review, not human verification or an executed baseline.

The middle 53-case classification requires a different argument: a no-released-mortgage route exists. Forty-seven observed payment routes use only initially offered mortgages; G12 d249 has a retained five-house sale witness. The five remaining actual payments use released collateral despite $U_0\geq D$. G3 d228 and d356 have houses only. G3 d482 has seventeen bank houses and one hotel; G18 d428 has twenty-nine and three hotels. Selling their development is feasible without new mortgages. G3 d461 can downgrade either single hotel with six bank houses, clear that now hotel-free group to replenish stock, then downgrade the other. In each case initial cash, all realizable salvage, and initially offered mortgages cover the debt. These are constructive source arguments, not newly executed action sequences.

Historical evidence for the three payable declarations is retained in the earlier executable audit. G12 d190 sells three red houses and mortgages Baltic, Illinois, and Indiana, then pays \$500 and leaves \$38. G17 d234 sells five dark-blue houses and mortgages Baltic and Boardwalk, pays \$550, and leaves \$180. G12 d249 sells five pink houses, pays \$250, and leaves \$43. The G7 restricted-menu search starts after a mortgage with \$390 and can raise only another \$50. It has no offered sale because bank stock is zero and the hotel is the group's highest development. These witnesses and search predate the present revision; they were not rerun.

The menu and sale validator are distinct. Highest-building targets guide individual sales, while a bundle is checked for final evenness and net bank stock. G18 d309 actually sells St. Charles houses in a bundle even though only States and Virginia appear as highest targets. Conversely, the G7 sale action is unavailable altogether. Reachability claims must specify the actual menu. Historical replay passing under this implementation does not prove fidelity to all published Monopoly rules.
