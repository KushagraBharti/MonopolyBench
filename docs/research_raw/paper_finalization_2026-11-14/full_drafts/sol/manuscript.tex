\begin{abstract}
Financial decisions depend on resources an action can make available and on the opportunities it creates for competitors. We examine these assessments in twenty complete four-player Monopoly games, with 6,989 decisions, a census of 188 financing episodes, and 920 negotiation responses. Sixteen payable debts require collateral absent from the initial mortgage menu. Their first responses contain five explicit dependency statements, three ordered routes, five inclusive budgets, one tentative resource assessment, and two incomplete inventories. Successful examples include an exact \$1,414 valuation and selective financing that preserves other deeds. Both incomplete inventories add correctly but omit mortgages released by sales, then declare bankruptcy. A source-based argument establishes that uninterrupted valid cash raising suffices for all 131 demonstrated-payable entries; payment itself therefore does not establish difficult route planning. Exploratory bargaining records show different countering and rejecting styles, buyer-specific pricing, factual correction, bluffing, and negotiation over a rival's survival. We connect these observations to applied calls and subsequent economic events, preserving contrary response channels and actual information conditions. The findings characterize expressed financial competence and strategic conduct in one interacting roster, without inferring causal mechanisms or a model ranking.
\end{abstract}
