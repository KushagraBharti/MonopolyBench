# MonopolyBench paper readiness assessment

Astra, 2026-10-27. This records my independent assessment and the reconciled findings from the overlapping Sol, Opus, Fable, and Astra research round. The other memos preserve their authors' positions. This document identifies superseded numbers rather than silently treating every peer statement as established fact.

We have enough evidence to start the full integrated paper now. The environment, current corpus, economic trajectories, and substantive validation are ahead of the July manuscript. Submission still requires a versioned analysis release, validation of the new measures, explicit treatment descriptions, and a rewritten argument and bibliography. Additional games should answer a specified research question. A larger tournament is not a prerequisite to drafting this bounded observational paper.

I read both parent discussions, the historical manuscript and research plans, engine and arena code, telemetry reducers, baseline policies, rules and recovery tests, cloud validation documentation, and primary comparison papers. I independently reconstructed cohort totals and selected decision histories in `astra_independent_checks.py`. Its JSON output contains source hashes, the event-linked first-builder results, two no-trade winners, management-access examples, and the game-1 raw-response inconsistency. Sol independently reconstructed ownership, cash, trade-created groups, and legal management windows. Opus and Fable independently checked core counts and response inconsistencies. Their broader interpretation is not a substitute for source verification.

The current cohort contains 20 games, five seed blocks with four cyclic rotations, 80 player-games, 50,918 events, 6,989 resolved decisions, 526 initial trade proposals, 394 counteroffers, and 113 completed trades. It is one four-model roster. Sol, Gemini, Opus, and Grok win 8, 7, 3, and 2 games respectively. The first builder wins 17 of 20. These are descriptive results with dependence within games and seed blocks. They do not establish a general model ranking or a causal effect of building first.

The economics already supplies an interesting argument. Negotiation volume differs from the consequences of negotiation. Gemini makes 18 initial proposals, participates in 36 completed trades, and receives $42,360 rent; Opus makes 240 proposals, participates in 70 trades, and receives $23,995. These totals also depend on survival and opportunities and must not be presented as intrinsic model traits. Sol and Gemini each have a win with no completed-trade participation, games 14 and 15. Trading is therefore one route to development, not a necessary condition for victory.

Across 113 completed trades, 60 complete no color group for either side, 43 complete a group for one side, and 10 complete groups for both sides. This is a set-completion classification, not a general trade-quality score. A trade can be economically useful without completing a set; completing a rival's set does not alone prove the grantor made a mistake. Subsequent development, liquidity, rent, and solvency should remain separate outcomes.

First-menu use measures conditional responsiveness. It conditions on earlier acquisition, financing, jail, and access choices. Calendar delay remains economically meaningful and must be reported separately. A player's next own turn can occur in jail without a management menu. Game 6 Opus and game 8 Gemini illustrate this distinction. The earlier 41/63 figure uses a different window from the reconciled 43/63. Neither is interchangeable with the 34/42 entry-eligible measure. Do not carry forward the preliminary 74% headline, call next-own-turn latency inherently wrong, or describe repeated group episodes as independent games.

The infrastructure merits a substantial contribution when the paper ties its capabilities to actual scientific uses. Saved legal menus support feasibility-conditioned analyses. Linked observations, raw responses, selected actions, and events expose interpretation errors. Recorded-action replay supports deterministic reconstruction, which is distinct from rerunning stochastic provider calls. Frozen campaign identities, durable execution, self-contained saved games, and release tooling support reuse by another team. The render-only UI is useful for inspection, but its existence alone is not the scientific advance.

I ran `bash scripts/verify.sh` successfully. The log records 591 Python tests passed, two skipped, and 36 frontend tests passed, plus contract checks, lint, typing, and the frontend build. The log is archived as `verification.log` in this directory. Rules scenarios, deliberate artifact corruption, recovery, and saved-response reuse tests support substantive integrity validation. Archived cloud validation supplies further operational evidence. None automatically validates the new opportunity measures or proves strategic calibration. The six reference policies are simple and initiate no meaningful trades. Finite hand-specified accounting and window scenarios are the immediate validation need; local reference-policy games can support a separately specified non-trade or policy-discrimination claim.

Two interface conditions belong in the main experimental design. Opus frequently uses refusal recovery with a different response requirement, and has only 152 private-thought events across 1,696 decisions. Private-rationale comparisons cannot treat that missingness as ordinary silence under an identical treatment. The refusal-attempt totals and decision-level recovery totals use different units and should be reconciled before reporting a single rate. Grok has 139 content/tool action-name mismatches across 1,663 decisions in Opus's and Fable's independent scans; Opus identifies 26 with identical arguments. I directly verified game-1 decision 112 in the raw stored response: content says reject, tool says accept, and the arguments match. The engine applied accept.

Keep the applied action authoritative and preserve the contradictory message. Flag the affected decision and the provenance of downstream episodes. The mismatch alone identifies neither hidden intent nor a model, provider, gateway, or forced-tool-choice cause. Removing trade rows does not recover a counterfactual game. A mismatch remains an observable communication/action inconsistency, but cannot by itself establish deliberate deception. Game 1 can illustrate Gemini's response to the actual deal it faced, with disclosure; I would choose a cleaner lead case after checking candidate histories.

Social evidence supports precise accounts of bargaining posture, assurances, and anti-leader appeals. Game 16 contains appeals to restrict trading with the leader alongside offers made to that leader. This does not by itself establish a binding coalition, promise breach, or cartel. The strongest historical assurance case remains a different configuration and must be labeled as such. The prompt permits deception, so deception cannot automatically be called instruction misalignment. I favor a bounded social section now; a strong commitment or alignment headline would need additional evidence matched to that claim.

The closest literature substantially overlaps with generic versions of our pitch. [AgentMonopoly](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29330v2) already studies Monopoly behavior with seeds, rotations, and public/private records. Its Table 3 currently lists 40 + 40 + 40 + 20 = 140 games. Fable's 160 correction is an arithmetic error in the memo, not an established source correction. [FinBoardBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27896v1) already discusses staged economic success and distinguishes proposer and recipient trade outcomes. [AgentBoard](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/877b40688e330a0e2a3fc24084208dfa-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html) motivates analysis beyond final success. [NegotiationArena](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/bianchi24a.html) is a precedent for an environment contribution accompanied by behavioral findings. Our defensible contribution combines exact longitudinal economic consequences for both trade sides, opportunity creation and use grounded in recorded legal choices, and a verified reusable research release. It is not novelty of Monopoly, process metrics, seeds, or logging individually. Do not infer that a comparator lacks an implementation feature just because its paper does not discuss it.

The July `monopolybench_ieee_draft_v0_1.tex` is a substantial eight-game audit manuscript with an outdated bibliography and narrative. `monopolybench.tex` is an IEEE sample template. The build manifest is historical; its listed final PDF and supplement are absent from the current output directory. Existing methods prose, architecture material, historical examples, and figures can be reused after checking them. The current paper requires a rewrite, not a final copyedit.

The next writing pass should produce a complete first draft while completing a finite evidence pass:

1. Freeze definitions, source manifests, units, and claim boundaries in one regenerable analysis package. Rebuild acquisition from transfer events so creditor inheritance is not counted as a direct purchase.
2. Validate accounting, entry legality, window boundaries, jail access, financing during a window, and censoring with hand-specified scenarios and audited corpus edge cases. Retain all-player acquisition denominators alongside conditional deployment.
3. Produce the block/rotation outcome figure, activity and outcome profiles, two-sided set-completion results, both deployment timing measures, and a checked economic episode. Link each to the code and source events.
4. Write design and infrastructure around the research capabilities, declared Monopoly variants, actual participant treatments, determinism boundary, and release procedure. Separate integrity evidence from measurement evidence.
5. Rewrite the introduction, related work, and discussion around the supported contribution. Keep social claims at the level earned by reviewed evidence. Treat stronger commitment or alignment experiments as claim-dependent work.

One integrated paper remains the strongest plan. The earlier 40% findings, 40% environment/infrastructure, and 20% validation allocation is a useful emphasis, not a rigid page quota. The abstract and introduction should lead with the economic question and findings; detailed results need a clear explanation of the experimental setup first. I would begin the manuscript now and resolve the listed measurement work during that draft, without reopening broad planning or adding experiments without a stated purpose.
