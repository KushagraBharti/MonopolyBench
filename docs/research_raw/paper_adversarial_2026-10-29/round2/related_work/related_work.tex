\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Economic trajectories and communication.}
Vending-Bench evaluates sustained operation of a simulated vending business, where inventory, prices and expenses connect decisions across a long trajectory~\cite{backlund2025vendingbench}. The Vending-Bench Arena communication study is a close methodological comparison~\cite{li2026arena}. It analyzes 2,583 emails from twenty year-long runs using language-model classification, deterministic checks of extracted claims against simulator records, and a separate reasoning-trace audit. It also checks transfer-promise follow-through. Its validation includes component audits and full-pipeline replication with two other judge families, alongside a fifty-email reference set labeled by one author. The single-annotator limitation therefore does not describe its entire validation procedure. Arena already establishes the value of checking communication against economic records. Our analysis asks a narrower question about particular decisions: which actions were legally reachable, what information reached a recipient, and what the recipient's response establishes. Logged facts, feasible actions and inferred beliefs require different evidence.

\paragraph{Monopoly behavior and response resolution.}
AgentMonopoly studies behavioral profiles, model size and persona interventions in the same underlying game~\cite{wu2026agentmonopoly}. It supplies allowed actions, validates responses, records invalid outputs, and compares language with actions. Its shared prompt permits bluffing while prohibiting public messages that contradict the chosen action. Cash-negative elimination omits our debt-liquidation phase, but its player-creditor inherits buildings intact, as ours does. Its described replay visualizes recorded sessions; the inspected runner and server save turn snapshots rather than expose an action-stream re-execution path~\cite{agentmonopoly2026repo}. These are different uses of replay. In the pinned implementation, malformed JSON or invalid property targets become \texttt{pass} without corrective feedback. Failure diagnostics, request retries and a configurable empty-answer retry are nevertheless present. The paper specifies a 150-step horizon with a net-worth winner at the cap; the cited runner defaults to 600 player-turns. We keep the reported experiment and repository default distinct. These differences motivate explicit resolution records, rather than a claim that only MonopolyBench preserves failures.

FinBoardBench evaluates financial decisions in Cashflow, Acquire and Monopoly~\cite{hu2026finboardbench}. It provides phase-specific legal actions and records state, events and actions in memory. Its fault-tolerance procedure validates decisions, returns errors for correction, and records forced fallback events after retry exhaustion. This is direct prior work on distinguishing agent decisions from system interventions. Its Monopoly rules require liquidation before bankruptcy and return buildings to the bank upon creditor transfer. Our intact-building inheritance changes subsequent rents, so rent receipts are not directly comparable investment-return scores. Our additional analysis connects a defaulted response to its attempted construction target and tests that target through permitted engine actions.

\paragraph{Complete configurations and alternative trajectories.}
DECE evaluates complete model--harness configurations across ten score-based Eurogames~\cite{zhang2026dece}. Its legal interfaces, persisted states and execution records support replay and resume. Matched studies vary the execution framework while holding the focal model, seed, seat and opponents fixed within a comparison. DECE is therefore a close comparator for the claim that orchestration belongs to the evaluated system. Its games have no dialogue or negotiation channel, while our social analysis depends on delivered messages. Conversely, our observational cohort does not identify the effect of changing a harness. A local action intervention tests whether an endpoint is reachable under stated rules; it does not estimate how a different agent configuration would perform.

NegotiationArena already stores serialized games for reloading, modification and rerunning counterfactuals, and experimentally varies bargaining instructions~\cite{bianchi2024negotiationarena}. Restore-and-branch capability is thus not itself a novelty claim. Its controlled contrasts also illustrate why an accepted transaction alone cannot establish a message's persuasive effect. Empty Commitments distinguishes promises unsupported by runtime capabilities from feasible promises lacking an enabling tool call, using explicit feasibility and anchoring rules~\cite{empty_commitments_v2}. This is related to separating an attainable action target from the response that was actually submitted. Our commitment chronologies use delivered memory and later actions, but do not supply a predefined commitment ontology or a general breach rate.

\paragraph{Scope of the present contribution.}
We combine recorded legal menus, attempt-resolution provenance, delivered memory and engine re-execution to audit specified historical claims. Section~\ref{sec:claim-protocol} fixes a prefix, actor, permitted intervention class and endpoint. A legal action sequence witnesses feasibility; an admissible bound or exhaustive search can certify impossibility within that class. Reproducing the original event stream alone does neither. The certificate suite validates the selected elimination and construction populations and their audited prefixes, rather than every possible prefix or economic proposition. Delivery records constrain claims about exposure, while recipient text can show explicit correction or endorsement; neither proves latent belief or identifies persuasion. The contribution is this bounded claim audit and its finite-corpus demonstration, without firstness claims for economic simulation, failure logging, state-based checking or counterfactual replay.
