% REPLACE: "Long-horizon agents must acquire resources ... economic events."
In long-horizon multi-agent play, an applied action is not always a chosen one. We study twenty complete four-player games of MonopolyBench, a deterministic environment that records, for every decision, the legal menu, each response attempt, the retry or fallback that resolved it, the memory delivered to the agent and the resulting economic events. Across 6,989 decisions and 8,679 attempts by four frontier configurations, we find that a measurable share of economically consequential actions were produced by response resolution rather than by the agent's stated plan: all 61 construction plans defaulted to an end-turn action were affordable and legal when the identical purchases were reordered; one of three payable bankruptcies was declared by a received tool call whose accompanying text requested a house sale; and three of eight unbuilt entry-buildable groups ended through a received action that conflicted with its content.

Our contributions are threefold. First, we provide an integrated account of complete economic and social trajectories that preserves acquisition routes, bilateral transaction consequences, financing access, response provenance and downstream outcomes. Because every attempt, retry and fallback is recorded, the account separates actions an agent chose from actions its configuration produced; no comparator we inspected reports attempt chains as a measured object. Second, we state an exact-prefix adjudication procedure (Section~\ref{sec:prefix-procedure}) that restores a recorded decision prefix in the engine, executes a specified sequence of the actor's own legal actions, and returns one of three certificates: a feasible route, an exhaustive local infeasibility, or a rule lock. We apply it to agents' claims and to our own research labels. Third, we validate the recorded system and identify concrete requirements for future norm-explicit evaluation and training environments. We do not claim to have introduced replay, multiplayer economics or factual checking of agent communication; the specific differences from the closest systems are stated in Section~\ref{sec:related} and Table~\ref{tab:capability-matrix}.

\subsubsection*{Exact-prefix adjudication procedure}
\label{sec:prefix-procedure}
\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.95\linewidth}\small
\textbf{Input.} A game $g$, a decision index $k$, a claim $\phi$ about the acting player $p$ at that decision (for example ``$p$ can pay its current debt $D$'', or ``$p$'s submitted purchase multiset $M$ is affordable and legal''), and a declared local endpoint $E$ (debt paid; multiset completed; menu exhausted).

\textbf{Step 1: restore.} Re-execute the recorded action stream of $g$ through decision $k-1$ in the engine from the saved seed and roster. Require that the regenerated events, the pending decision identifier and the rebuilt memory window equal the recorded ones; otherwise abort and report a replay failure.

\textbf{Step 2: search.} From the restored state, execute only actions legal for $p$ at each step, without consuming another player's decision. Diagnostic alterations (an opponent's acceptance, altered bank stock) must be declared as conditions and are reported separately.

\textbf{Step 3: certify.} Return one of: (a) \emph{feasible route}: an explicit legal sequence reaching $E$, with the terminal cash; (b) \emph{infeasible}: either the nominal bound $U<D$ (Equation~\ref{eq:sol-upper}) or an exhaustive enumeration of the permitted sale and mortgage orders that never reaches $E$; (c) \emph{rule lock}: $U\ge D$ but every permitted order is blocked by a stated rule (bank-house shortage, even-selling), with the blocking rule named.

\textbf{Output.} The certificate, the sequence or enumeration, the endpoint state, and the list of declared conditions. The procedure never asserts what the agent or any opponent would have done after $E$.
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}
In this paper the procedure is applied to sixty eliminations (fifty-six infeasible by bound, three feasible routes, one rule lock), to sixty-one defaulted construction plans (all feasible after reordering), and to the specific reserve, budget and restructuring cases named in Section~\ref{sec:sol-economic-results}. It is also applied to our own labels: two writing-stage summaries and one analyst financing restriction were corrected by it (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\paragraph{Long-horizon economic agents.}
Vending-Bench evaluates autonomous operation of a simulated business and motivates economic capability as a safety-relevant property~\cite{backlund2025vendingbench}. Vending-Bench~2 extends the author-reported environment to a simulated year with note-taking tools and adversarial suppliers~\cite{andon2026vb2}. The multiplayer Arena communication study is the closest comparison for our social results: it analyzes 2,583 emails from twenty year-long runs of thirteen frontier models, combining a language-model judge, deterministic checks of extracted factual claims against simulator logs, and reasoning-summary analysis, and it measures the enactment of 525 verifiable transfer promises~\cite{li2026arena}. Its agents receive no conduct instruction, its label validation is a single-author consistency check on fifty emails, and its environment is not publicly redistributed, so our comparison is to its reported method rather than to re-runnable data. Its deterministic verification applies to claims that the simulator's pricing, transfer and inventory logs can resolve, and it returns an insufficient-data verdict otherwise. In MonopolyBench every proposition about ownership, cash, charges and development is decidable from the complete event log, and every feasibility claim is decidable by engine execution from the recorded prefix. We do not claim that multiplayer economics, state-checked statements or longitudinal communication analysis are new. Our cases add exact historical action menus, engine-executed financing sequences, per-attempt resolution records and an explicit permission for public deception.

\paragraph{Monopoly and board-game economies.}
AgentMonopoly studies economic and social behavior, persona control and within-family model sizes in a closely related game~\cite{wu2026agentmonopoly}. Its paper and Apache-2.0 repository provide legal-action lists in the prompt, a deterministic seeded engine, per-turn session snapshots and a dashboard mode for playing back recorded sessions; the inspected code has no function that restores a recorded state or re-executes the engine from it, and prompts and raw model outputs are not stored as fields~\cite{agentmonopoly2026repo}. Its limitations caution against treating explanations as direct evidence of computation. Three protocol differences bound the comparison. Its bankruptcy rule eliminates a cash-negative player with no mortgage-and-sale liquidation phase, so our accessible-finance tests address a mechanic it does not have; in the inspected code a player-creditor nevertheless inherits buildings intact, as in our declared variant. Its trade is a single offer answered by acceptance or rejection, so the counteroffer attribution problem of Section~\ref{sec:sol-economic-results} cannot arise there. Its prompt encourages deceit and bluffing but states that the public message ``must match the reality of your action'', whereas ours imposes no such constraint. Invalid outputs there are forced to a pass action without a corrective retry and are not reported numerically; here every attempt, retry and fallback is a recorded object. Its eight open-weight models range from 8B to 32B parameters at temperature zero; a 150-step cap invokes a net-worth winner. Our four frontier configurations use provider-default sampling and the observed games end by uncapped bankruptcy. The inspected paper reports 140 games while the repository table totals 120, and the repository runner read on the same date applies a 600-turn default without the net-worth rule; we compare to the paper's stated protocol and retain the discrepancies rather than infer a shared count.

FinBoardBench evaluates one focal model against three fixed opponents across three board games, including Monopoly liquidity and investment summaries and judge-rated trade quality~\cite{hu2026finboardbench}. Its Monopoly trades are structured proposals with accept, reject and counter responses and a reason field. On bankruptcy to a player it returns buildings to the bank and passes the deeds to the creditor, whereas our creditor inherits buildings intact; this difference is central to why our rent receipts are not a comparable ROI score, and we evaluate all four players rather than a focal seat. DECE evaluates complete model--harness configurations across ten score-based Eurogames with matched harness studies, persisted state, legal interfaces and replay-and-resume~\cite{zhang2026dece}. We adopt its complete-system framing without claiming its controlled harness comparisons; its games have no inter-player dialogue or trading, and its primary results do not report invalid-action handling. Cattle Trade provides another auction and bargaining comparison in which heuristic code agents outperform most tested models~\cite{muller2026cattletrade}. The present cohort contains no corresponding policy-baseline evaluation. Table~\ref{tab:capability-matrix} summarizes the inspected capabilities.

The practical lesson of the economic record is that an applied action is a joint product of the agent's attempt and the harness's resolution of it, and that the two must be reported separately before an end-turn or a bankruptcy is read as a decision.
