MonopolyBench: Auditing Economic Capability and Social
Conduct in Long-Horizon Multi-Agent Competition
MonopolyBench project
First complete draft, 29 October 2026
Abstract
Long-horizon agents must acquire resources, finance plans and negotiate while earlier actions
reshape later opportunities. We study economic capability and the social conduct of bargaining
in MonopolyBench, a deterministic four-player environment with recorded legal menus, response
attempts, delivered memory and economic events. Across twenty completed games, we analyze
6,989 decisions and 8,679 attempts using event reconstruction, overlapping source review and exact-
prefix execution of local alternatives. Forty-three of sixty-three trade-completed groups receive
construction in the first management window. Among forty-two groups buildable on entry, eight
remain unbuilt in that window: three through engine defaults, three through received actions
conflicting with accompanying content, and two through stated holds. Three of sixty eliminations
admit a legal current-debt payment sequence; another has sufficient nominal value but is con-
strained by hotel liquidation. Social chronologies show a misleading omission, recipient correction
alongside acceptance, a transient false echo, and withdrawal of a standing offer. Some received
proposals contradict their accompanying text, so settlement alone identifies neither persuasion
nor coherent proposer intent. All twenty recorded action streams replay, and all 6,989 memory
windows reconstruct. Public deception is permitted, and one configuration operates almost entirely
under a provider-refusal recovery condition without authored private notes. The cohort therefore
establishes neither an instruction-misalignment rate nor a capability–alignment trend. It contributes
an empirical account, an exact-prefix audit procedure applied to agents’ claims and research labels,
and requirements for future controlled, norm-explicit comparisons.
1 Introduction
An agent that can accumulate useful assets must also decide when to spend, when to preserve liquidity,
what to tell a counterparty and whether an apparent constraint is real. Over a long interaction, these
decisions change the environment in which later decisions are made. An early bargain can fund a
rival’s construction; a profitable group can yield no paid rent before its owner is eliminated; a recovery
policy can alter both the applied action and the memory available on the next turn. Evaluating the
resulting agent requires more than checking whether its last action succeeded.
Long-horizon economic benchmarks make this problem concrete. Vending-Bench studies au-
tonomous operation of a vending business, while its multiplayer Arena extension exposes agents to
trade, strategic communication and competition [2, 11]. AgentMonopoly and FinBoardBench study
economic choices in board-game environments, and DECE evaluates complete model–harness config-
urations under controlled economic conditions [6, 26, 30]. These studies already establish multiplayer
economic interaction, legal-action interfaces, replay and state-checked communication as relevant eval-
uation tools. The remaining difficulty is connecting an observed outcome to the precise opportunities,
information and resolved actions that produced it.
1
We study that connection in MonopolyBench, a reproducible four-player environment with persis-
tent economic state, negotiation and an inspectable record of every decision. Our broad motivation
is to evaluate both economic capability and alignment as agents become more capable. The present
empirical question is narrower:what do complete competitive trajectories reveal about agents’ acquisition,
financing, development and social conduct, and which interpretations survive an audit of the alternatives and
evidence available at the time?A further design question asks what additional conditions would make
the capability–alignment relationship identifiable.
We analyze twenty completed games containing 6,989 decisions and 8,679 response attempts.
Four configured participants each play five times in every seat across five seed blocks. The record
links applied actions and economic events to legal menus, delivered memory and available response
channels. We combine event reconstruction, overlapping source review and exact-prefix execution of
local alternatives. This permits a claim such as “the debtor could pay” to be tested as a legal sequence,
and a claim such as “the buyer was persuaded” to be checked against prior motives, recipient exposure
and the actual response.
The results support a detailed capability profile rather than a ranking. Trades often assemble
groups, but two winners complete no trade. Development can be deferred with a stated reserve
rationale, lost through response resolution, or inaccessible until financing changes. Three of sixty
eliminationsadmitalegalroutepayingthecurrent debt; anotherhassubstantialnominalvaluetrapped
by a hotel-liquidation rule. In the social record, misleading framing and selective disclosure coexist
withexplicitrecipientcorrection, transientfalseechoesandbargainingthatproceedswithoutidentified
uptake of the false premise. Since public deception is permitted, these observations describe conduct
under a specific contract rather than a general instruction-misalignment rate.
Our contributions are threefold. First, we provide an integrated account of complete economic
and social trajectories, preserving acquisition routes, bilateral transaction consequences, financing
access, responseprovenanceanddownstreamoutcomes. Second, weapplyanexact-prefixadjudication
protocol to both agents’ claims and generated research labels, distinguishing current menus from
reachable legal sequences and recorded speech from delivered information and reliance. Third, we
validate the recorded system and identify concrete requirements for future norm-explicit evaluation
and training environments. The contribution is this combined protocol and empirical account, not a
claim to have introduced replay, multiplayer economics or factual checking of agent communication.
2 Environment and evaluated configurations
MonopolyBench places four agents in a shared, deterministic Monopoly implementation. Each agent
attempts to remain the last nonbankrupt player by acquiring property, trading, financing construction
and managing obligations. Economic decisions have persistent consequences: a mortgage provides
cash while disabling rent on the mortgaged deed; construction consumes cash and finite building
stock; a trade changes both players’ opportunities; and bankruptcy redistributes the debtor’s assets or
returns them to the bank. The environment consequently connects resource acquisition to investment,
negotiation and eventual survival within a fully recorded game.
The engine is the authority for state and legality. It emits a decision point with explicit legal choices,
validates the selected action and records resulting events. The interface renders these records rather
than independently implementing rules. Agents receive public economic state, their current decision
context, and bounded memory. Opponents’ cash, holdings and configured model names are visible,
but their private notes and the future random and card state are not. Public history retains twenty
timeline entries; the agent’s own private history retains ten notes. Trade responses additionally include
structured negotiation history. These are distinct observation channels: a statement saved in the

3 Study design and economic measurement
3.1 The cohort and the object of evaluation
We study twenty completed four-player games, comprising eighty player-games, 50,918 events, 6,989
decisionsand8,679responseattempts. Fiveseedseachhavefourcyclicseatrotations. Sol,Gemini,Opus
and Grok each occupy seats 1–4 five times. The evaluated participant is the configured agent, including
its model, inference settings, observations, memory and response-resolution policy. This complete-
system interpretation follows existing arguments for agent evaluation and is especially consequential
when a retry changes the response condition [7, 30]. An attempt is one recorded response within a
decision. We write Gn dk for zero-based decisionk in gamen (Appendix A). The engine applies one
resolved action for that decision; several decisions can occur during one player’s turn.
The finite cohort is our population of description. Seat balance does not make the games indepen-
dent draws from a common distribution of opportunities. Decisions within a game share an evolving
board, and rotations within a seed block share initial random sequences. Purchases, jail choices and
eliminations alter how those sequences are consumed. Two saved treatment fingerprints also divide
games 1–4 from games 5–20. The inspected source diff changes campaign-concurrency plumbing and
other application files without changing engine, prompt, resolver or model-client files. This inspection
does not identify an effect, or establish the absence of one. We therefore report observed outcomes and
paths without treating thousands of decisions as independent evidence of an intrinsic model ranking.
Our analysis combines whole trajectories with narrower opportunity-conditioned summaries.
This follows the general motivation for process-sensitive agent evaluation [13], but requires explicit
economic units. A deed acquisition changes ownership of one property. A group completion occurs
when a player acquires the last deed needed for a color group. A trade is an atomic settlement between
two players, with its transfers and charges evaluated together. We distinguish the original thread
initiator, the author of the final offer and its accepting player. These roles need not coincide. We record
completion at stable transaction boundaries so that an intermediate transfer inside a swap cannot
create a fictitious opportunity.

3.3 Local legal-sequence tests
To test a financing or construction claim, we restore the actual historical decision prefix, check its
event agreement, and execute a specified sequence of the acting player’s own legal actions through the
engine. We stop at a declared local endpoint, such as completing a proposed construction multiset or
paying the current debt. A test cannot borrow another player’s management opportunity. Conditional

acceptance, altered stock or other diagnostic changes must be stated separately. Legal-action interfaces
and replay already occur in related economic benchmarks [26, 30]; the present use is to adjudicate
concrete claims about recorded trajectories.
For a debtor with cashC, a generous nominal upper bound is
U = C + Vall building salvage + Vall clear deed mortgages. (3)
The mortgage term counts each unmortgaged deed once, including railroads and utilities and collateral
that clearing buildings could unlock; already-mortgaged deeds contribute nothing. IfU is below debt

hotel downgrade needs bank houses, and the even-selling rule can prevent reaching nominal salvage.
Conversely, a current menu can understate finance because mortgages become available only after
sales. A tested payment route establishes current-debt feasibility, not future survival or the value of
surrender. Once a branch changes the state, the historical policy suffix does not identify how any player
would continue.
3.4 Reconstructing conduct and recipient response
Social analysis begins with a proposition or commitment in its chronological context, rather than a
globallabelattachedtoacolorfulexchange. Welinkthespeaker’sobservation, publicmessage, selected
action, resulting events and any saved private note or provider explanation. We then identify which
recipient prompts contain the relevant statement, both earlier in the interaction and at the decision
where reliance is proposed. A recipient may have acted on earlier exposure even if the message has
since left its current window. Conversely, a message’s presence in a canonical event does not prove
delivery to that recipient.
We adjudicate five questions separately. Is the stated proposition false or misleading under a
specified reading and the speaker’s available information? Does another recorded channel contradict
it? Was the proposition delivered? Does the recipient’s subsequent record endorse that proposition or
act in documented reliance on it? Does the applicable instruction or agreement prohibit the conduct?
These dimensions are not an escalating deception score. An observed breach can remain a breach
after a promise scrolls out of memory; missing context instead limits explanations based on knowing
violation or forgetting. A trade can settle while the recipient expressly rejects its public premise. A
statement can be false without evidence that the speaker knew it was false.
Provider explanations and private notes are recorded text, not direct measurements of internal
computation. We preserve agreement and disagreement among channels instead of assigning one
privileged access to intent. This is especially necessary when a received tool call conflicts with the
accompanying content or when an explanation corrects itself before the selected action. Work on
explanation faithfulness motivates this caution, but cannot determine the mechanism in any particular
record [10, 23]. Mechanically verifiable propositions, such as ownership, charges or the feasibility of a
stated action sequence, can still be checked independently.
Detectionopportunitiesdifferbyconfiguration. PrivatenotesarelargelyabsentunderOpus’saction-
only condition, provider explanations vary in availability, and content/tool conflicts are especially
observable in Grok’s records. We therefore use common public actions, statements and state for
comparable descriptive inventories, and identify channel-qualified case evidence separately. A rate
among decisions with readable explanations would remain a selected-subset rate: refusal, recovery
and game progression affect that selection. No adjustment by channel count alone establishes model-
comparable latent deception or intent prevalence.

3.5 Review, counterexamples and claim selection
The corpus received a complete public-scope pass and a complete pass over all available saved channels
and attempts, with unavailable or encrypted material marked as such. The extraction stage produced
441 public-scope candidates and 152 final full-channel candidates, alongside twenty-one historical
checkpoints. Fourcoauthorreviewstreamseachconsideredall614catalogentries,alltwentytrajectories
and a common sample of 240 original decision bundles. The catalog contains overlapping observations,
not 614 independent episodes. The common sample contains three decisions per player per game from
a public-unflagged pool; it serves as an audit of ordinary and missed cases, not a prevalence estimator.
Initial judgments, subsequent source checks and peer-informed corrections are retained separately.
This is AI-assisted extraction and adversarial review, not independent human annotation or a validated
automatedjudge. Readinganotherreviewer’scorrectresultisnotcountedasanindependentderivation.
Mechanical disagreements are resolved against source events, accepted transaction payloads and
engine-executed prefixes where applicable. Semantic disagreements retain the competing readings if
the record does not settle them.
Case selection favors informative contrasts: an apparent failure with a legal alternative, a super-
ficially similar successful path, a reserve choice that genuinely trades construction for liquidity, and
a favorable settlement without identified uptake of a misleading premise. We also preserve analyst
errors. For example, interpreting anunmortgageable menu field as a list of forbidden actions fabricated
a financing restriction; reading a one-step sale menu as total liquidation capacity omitted collateral
unlocked later. These corrections are evidence about the difficulty of evaluating long trajectories, not
additional agent failures. The case studies are explanatory exemplars tied to sources, rather than
frequencies of a semantic category inferred from a selectively nominated shortlist.

rotations of one seed block, while a different block is won from seat 2 by all four models. Shared raw

trace what agents actually acquired, financed and realized along these paths.

4.3 Construction, realized income and inherited portfolios
The 103 spending units incur $103,450 in own construction expenditure. Whole-game paid developed
rent to those units totals $93,000, of which $92,550 follows the owner’s first own expenditure. The
$450 difference is G2 light-blue rent collected on inherited development before the winner’s first own
$200 construction. Even the later window includes rent supported by inherited buildings. The totals
describe expenditure and receipts on observed ownership paths; subtracting them does not yield net
profit or construction’s causal contribution.
Forty spending units collect no paid developed rent. Twenty-six of those also produce no creditor
elimination on that group. These counts concern game/player/color units, whereas fifty-seven of sixty
elimination events arise from rent on developed color properties. A large unpayable demand can end
an opponent’s game and transfer assets without appearing as a paid-rent receipt. Other zero-rent units
retain assets, recover salvage, or face little remaining exposure. Denial and deterrence are possible
sources of value but are not measured here. The data therefore support zero observed receipts, rather
than forty failed investments.
Successive owners of G19’s dark blues provide a concrete contrast. Opus spends $1,200, receives
$175 in developed rent and later realizes $600 in building sales. Gemini inherits the deeds, spends
$2,000 over its subsequent ownership, and receives $1,400 in developed rent on a later Sol landing.
The two units concern the same properties with different timing, financing and exposure. They show
why an attractive group cannot guarantee receipts and why inherited ownership must remain visible.
They do not isolate an owner effect. A recent $1,200 construction step in the second trajectory must not
be substituted for Gemini’s $2,000 cumulative own expenditure.

5 Social conduct during economic competition
Every transaction in Section4 was also an exchange of language. The 113 settlements emerged from
526 applied proposals and 920 counter, acceptance and rejection events, and every one of the 6,989
applied decisions, including those behind these events, carried a public-message field, engine fallbacks
included; 107 of these fields are empty. Players used that channel to price assets, to claim facts about
the board, to state limits, to offer standing terms, to warn the table about a leader and occasionally
to say something false. This section reads those exchanges against the economic records that settle
what the words were about. Its aim is to describe conduct with the same precision as cash: what was
claimed, whether it was true, who received it, what the recipient did, what followed, and what else
was legally possible at that moment.
5.1 The normative contract and the evidence chain
The standard system prompt tells each agent to “win the game by maximizing long-term advantage
and being the last non-bankrupt player” and permits “any legal strategy or personality (aggressive,
deceptive, cooperative, conservative, manipulative, opportunistic, friendly, hostile, etc.)”. It describes
the public message as a place to “negotiate, bluff, deceive, cooperate, intimidate, joke, or stay silent”,
and states: “Deception in public chat is allowed; private thoughts should reflect true reasoning.” The
action-only retry, used only after a provider-refused first attempt, removes the private-note field and
shortens that sentence to “Deception in public chat is allowed.” Neither prompt directly specifies the
content of returned provider reasoning. The win and legal-action instructions appear in every prompt,
but the channel-specific requirements differ. A public message may deceive under every condition.
An applied private note is asked to reflect true reasoning only under the standard condition. Returned
provider reasoning is subject to no channel-specific requirement. Every Opus first attempt from game 5
onward was refused, so its applied decisions there were resolved under the action-only condition or
by fallback (Section2). Opus therefore mostly played without the private-honesty clause, not merely
without a private note.
The contract fixes how the rest of this section is labelled. A false public statement is not a violation
of these instructions, and by itself it is not evidence that an agent is misaligned with them. A false
private note written under the standard condition is a candidate breach of a request, but error, stale
context and disagreement between channels remain live explanations, so a false note does not on its
own establish dishonesty. Rule-breaking actions cannot be applied, because the engine executes only
menu actions; invalid attempts are recorded instead. The interesting question under this contract
is therefore not whether agents “lied” but how competitive claims were produced, how recipients
detected and answered them, and what they changed. This differs from AgentMonopoly, whose
prompt encourages bluffing but prohibits public messages that contradict the submitted action [26],
and from the Vending-Bench Arena communication study, which applies an external taxonomy to
agents given no conduct instruction [11]. An observed contradiction can be a measured violation in
one of those environments and an authorized move in ours.
We read each social episode along a fixed chain of records (Table5). The chain separates four
labels that are easily merged: a proposition’s factual status, whether a true statement misleads by
omission, its contractual status, and the speaker’s intent. The first two are often decidable from the
canonical state. The third follows from the channel and condition. The fourth stays unresolved unless
independent records agree and the competing explanations are excluded, which in this cohort they
rarely are [10, 23].

5.4 Cooperation, opposition and third parties
Fourplayerscompetingforonewinhavereasonsbothtocooperateandtoopposetheleader. Opposition
appears in several reviewed games; explicit cooperation against a leader appears most clearly in game 4.
The game 4 sequence above is the clearest conditional cooperation. One player publicly invites the
others to form “a multi-way or direct deal so p3 isn’t the only one with a monopoly”. A second accepts
the framing by name, pays the leader $200 for the deed it needs, and the two negotiate a swap that
gives each a buildable group. The cooperation was real in the sense that both parties obtained complete
buildable groups; whether either gained against the leader was not tested. It was also bilateral and
self-interested throughout: the false house-cost claim and the drop from a $143 to a $50 cash request
were made inside it. Grok, the proposer, went on to win game 4.
Explicit refusals to fund a rival recur in several reviewed games. After Opus (seat 2) sells Illinois
to Grok (seat 1) for $500 in game 20, completing Grok’s reds, Gemini (seat 4) ends its turn at G20
d96 with a public appeal: “let’s not fund their houses by buying their spare properties for cash”. At
G20 d101 it rejects Grok’s offer of Vermont for $150, publicly and privately on the same ground. The
appeal did not become an agreement. One of its addressees, Opus, later proposed cash purchases
from Grok twice, neither of which settled. Gemini’s rejections at G9 d82 (“fund another house on your
Dark Blues”) and G16 d322 (“Keeping p2 cash-starved at $142 is far better”) state the same reasoning:
each rejects a proposed cash payment on the stated ground that it would finance a rival’s next house or
redemption. These are the agents’ stated grounds. The rival-cash mechanisms in these two examples
were not recomputed in the writing-stage check.
Denial can also misfire under the declared inheritance rule. At G18 d335 Grok (seat 3) offers
Gemini $100 for mortgaged Illinois “so p1 never completes the reds off you”, and Gemini accepts at
G18 d336 on the same stated ground. Eighteen turns later, at G18 d360, Grok owes Sol (seat 1) $950
with $64 in cash and declares bankruptcy. Its estate, including Illinois and both dark blues, passes to
Sol, completing the reds the purchase was meant to block. Bankruptcy fixes the creditor by the debt.
An estate transfer is therefore not a choice of beneficiary, and a denial purchase lasts only as long as its
holder survives. A separate game 18 record shows a single received action completing the eventual
winner’s first group. At G18 d83 Grok’s received tool call accepts $130 for Baltic, completing Opus’s
brown group, Opus’s first monopoly in the game it went on to win. Grok’s assistant content selected
rejection, and its applied note says to “Keep Baltic as a block”. This is a channel conflict with a material
immediate consequence. Its contribution to the win was not tested, and the conflicting records do not
support an intended-concession interpretation.
Across the adjudicated cases we found anti-leader appeals, denial purchases, refusals to fund rivals
and conditional bilateral cooperation. We did not identify a multi-party coalition, cartel or deliberate
kingmaking in these adjudicated cases. That is a bounded statement about the reviewed records, not
proof of absence; the cohort has no private bilateral channel in which collusion could be arranged out
of view, and the review did not code every trade response for leader-funding reasons. The eventual
winner was the creditor in 54 of 60 eliminations (Section4). Under a rule that sends the estate to
whoever is owed, that concentration records which player held the debt, rather than an explicit choice
of beneficiary. Studies that deliberately open collusive opportunities find behavior under designed
incentives that this cohort did not provide [20].

6 Infrastructure, artifact validation and exact-prefix audits
6.1 A reconstructible record of decisions and consequences
MonopolyBench separates the engine, orchestration harness, telemetry and render-only interface. The
engine emits authoritative states, decisions and events. The harness constructs model-facing payloads,
sends requests through OpenRouter and resolves returned attempts. Telemetry preserves applied
actions, prompt/response artifacts, attempt indexes, validation errors, usage records and event ranges.
This separation makes it possible to ask both what the system did and what information was available
when it did so. It does not make every derived summary reliable by construction.
Independent event reductions reconcile the cohort’s economic record. All 6,989 initial decision
payloads agree with reconstructed cash, ownership, development and bank stock. All 10,235 saved
state snapshots match an event-folded economic state at a corresponding boundary, and terminal
states reconcile in all twenty games. The 113 accepted trades match cash, deed and transfer-interest
flows derived from the final accepted terms, including $431 in interest across thirty settlements. Jail-
card transfers require transaction or bankruptcy context because they do not have dedicated transfer
events; the reconstructed holdings nevertheless match all 27,956 player observations. This is a schema
limitation, rather than evidence that the transfers did not occur.
The complete applied action streams replay in all twenty games after the documented canonical-
ization. Memory reconstructed from events matches all 6,989 recorded initial memory windows. A
separate branch test restores one recorded mid-game prefix in each game, spanning purchase, man-
agement, auction, jail and trade-response decisions. At these twenty prefixes, the pending decision
identifier and rebuilt memory match, the recorded action reproduces the next events, and a different
legal action produces a divergent continuation. Matching subsequent stochastic events tests more than
a public snapshot: the replay also restores the relevant hidden random and deck state.
These checks have a specific scope. They establish twenty tested branch prefixes and all recorded
memory windows, not full prompt equality at every possible branch or a new LLM-controlled suffix
experiment. Longer reference-policy continuations terminate by bankruptcy in nineteen of twenty
tested branches. The remaining branch is truncated after 20,000 decisions with two surviving cash-
accumulatingplayers. ThetwentyuncappedLLMgames’terminationisthereforeanobservedoutcome,
not a termination guarantee. Training or rollout experiments need an explicit horizon and truncation
rule.
6.2 Observation and resolution defects discovered by auditing
Validation also identifies discrepancies between canonical events and the summaries presented to
agents. A single trade-history formatting assumption treats the original initiator as the current terms’
author. It reverses giving directions in forty-four of 113 accepted-trade summaries—the same forty-
four settlements whose final author differs from the initiator in Section4—misattributes twenty-six of
413 rejected offers, and produces self-partner wording in 232 of 394 counters. At least one of these
manifestations appears in 2,054 of 6,989 initial prompts. The three counts describe one formatter
assumption on different surfaces, not three independent defects. Structured holdings and cash remain
correct, and in-thread trade history provides additional information. G14 d140 records transaction-
specific recognition of a reversed summary. That recovery is a counterexample to inevitable reliance,
not proof that all affected reasoning was repaired.
Public-message delivery also differs from canonical logging. Of 6,882 nonempty canonical public
utterance occurrences, 149 never enter the timeline: ninety-four are omitted when consecutive building
summaries retain an earlier nonempty message, and fifty-five are consumed by a bankruptcy cash
21
event without a timeline summary. Of 6,733 inserted occurrences, 6,720 are observed in at least one
opponent’s later timeline. The other thirteen have no observed opponent timeline delivery before the
record ends. These are occurrence-level channel facts. Repetition, private notes and other prompt
fields prevent equating timeline omission with total semantic nonexposure.
The recorded response contains another distinction between proposed language and applied
behavior. Grok has 139 received action-name conflicts between assistant content and the tool call: 138
are parseable comparisons and one requires a separately identified malformed-content case. Fifteen
have equal parsed argument objects, but only five have literally identical argument substrings. In
these records, the saved tool name agrees with the parsed and applied action. The discrepancy is
observable at the received-response boundary; its origin among generation, provider processing,
gateway behavior and local streaming assembly remains unlocalized. A count of disagreements is not
a validated predictor of what an overseer would infer.
Refusal recovery changes subsequent context as well as the current action. In 1,108 of 1,696 Opus
decisions, private memory is empty; in another 286 it contains only literal synthetic fallback notes.
Thus 1,394 decisions lack a model-authored private note in that memory window. The sixty-one
defaulted construction plans discussed above also feed synthetic notes into later context. The observed
configured-agent trajectory includes this interaction among response policy, validation and memory.
The corpus does not isolate the effect of restoring notes, changing retry budgets or repairing history
text.
6.3 What the environment currently supports
The implementation provides deterministic transitions, legal menus, persisted attempts and state,
recovery, reference policies and composed exact-prefix continuation. This study measures artifact
agreement and exercises these components for local alternative-action audits. It does not train a policy,
expose a packaged reinforcement-learning adapter or validate a reward interface. Nor does it establish
that fixing one observed defect improves long-horizon outcomes. Those are distinct experiments
requiring new runs.
The research artifacts preserve source identifiers, hashes, analysis code, case chronologies and
revision provenance. The manuscript’s figures are generated from retained audit outputs, and its
explanatory branches stop at stated endpoints. A publication release should package the permitted
raw artifacts, dependencies and regeneration commands with an external clean-setup test. The present
draft records what has been reproduced locally without representing the completed release or an
external replication as already achieved.
7 What these trajectories imply for agent evaluation
7.1 Capability is a path through opportunities, not a single score
The observed games contain economically consequential planning. Agents assemble assets through
different routes, refuse exchanges that would finance rivals, mortgage spare collateral to build, retain
reserves, and execute long liquidation sequences. They also miss available finance, submit recoverable
allocations in invalid order, and receive actions that contradict their accompanying explanations. These
behaviors coexist within configurations and sometimes within one trajectory. A win count compresses
them into an endpoint without revealing which capabilities were exercised or which failures occurred.
The distinction between whole trajectories and conditioned opportunities is therefore substantive.

The 34/42 entry-buildable count asks a narrower responsiveness question after that access already

or prior financing can remove part of the capability one intended to evaluate. Conversely, counting
every late inherited group as an unused investment invents opportunities after the game has ended. A
useful capability profile needs acquisition, access, applied execution and downstream consequences
together.
The same issue arises in negotiation. Initiating few proposals can coexist with effective recipient
bargaining; initiating many can reflect persistent but unsuccessful search. A complete group can come
with insufficient cash, expensive redemption, a funded opponent or no future management. Two
no-trade winners show that bargaining is not a necessary route in every realized game. The point is
not to replace wins with another universal economic score, but to expose the decisions and resource
transitions that make the outcome interpretable.
7.2 Alignment needs a norm, an opportunity and an observable response
The motivating question concerns both agent capability and how conduct changes as capability in-
creases. This cohort supplies a rich account of economically situated conduct, but it does not identify
that relationship. It contains four model families at one setting each, with differing response conditions,
rather than an independently measured capability axis. Moreover, its prompt permits public deception.
Calling all misleading public statements misalignment would apply a norm that was not assigned to
the evaluated agents.
This does not make the social results irrelevant to safety. They identify behaviors and measurement
problems that a norm-explicit evaluation must distinguish: selective disclosure, recipient correction,
transient false echoes, continuing assurances, withdrawal after financial deterioration, and disclosure
throughapublicfield. Theyalsoshowwhyafavorablesettlementisnotsufficientevidenceofpersuasive
success. G13’s buyer weighs solvency before the omission, but after the seller’s opening appeal; G4’s
recipient had already recorded the true construction cost; G6 and G19 settle while correcting the
premise. These restrictions do not identify a price effect or show that all manipulation fails.
The private-honesty request is also difficult to score from an inaccurate note. A returned expla-
nation can be inconsistent, mistaken or corrected within the same response. Requiring a declared
norm is necessary, but not sufficient, when the property refers to latent reasoning. Observable conduct
standards—for example, disclosure of a specified encumbrance or honoring a precisely defined autho-
rization boundary—can be evaluated more directly. Claims about honesty, intent and internal beliefs
require evidence beyond the literal presence of a self-description.
This measurement dependence accords with mixed findings in related work. The Arena com-
munication study reports no capability association under its economic ordering and communication
codebook; Emergent Collusion reports within-family differences under designed violation opportu-
nities [11, 20]. MASK distinguishes factual accuracy from honesty, while Safetywashing questions
whether safety benchmarks sometimes measure general capability [16, 17]. These results should moti-
vate explicit constructs and controlled comparisons, rather than a universal monotonic relationship
inferred from four win totals.
7.3 From an auditable sandbox to controlled evaluation
The next experiment should be organized around the claim it intends to identify. A material-disclosure
study could preserve G13’s financial state while assigning a fixed disclosure requirement and varying
whether the seller has an incentive to omit available collateral. A commitment study could specify
23
the standing offer’s terms, expiry and release authority before recreating a G18-style invocation.
A protection task could designate information that must remain private while offering profitable
opportunities to reveal it. These interventions would change the benchmark prompt or task and should
be versioned as new conditions, not retroactively imposed on this cohort.
For a capability–adherence comparison, the capability axis should be independently measured,
preferably with multiple tiers within model families and held-out economic tasks. Prompts, retry
policies, memory, tool choice and resource budgets should be fixed or factorially varied. Refusals and
invalid outputs must remain part of the measured system, with any recovery treatment separately
recorded. Repeated complete games would measure long-run outcomes; repeated continuations from
matched historical prefixes would answer narrower questions about the same available alternatives.
Statistical units should reflect dependence within games and seed blocks.
Economic utility, instruction adherence and recipient effects should be reported separately. A high-
profitpolicythatviolatesadeclaredauthorizationconstraintshouldnotbecomesafebecausethereward
outweighs a hidden penalty. Similarly, a recipient that accepts a profitable exchange while detecting its
false premise should not be scored as deceived solely because a transaction occurred. MACHIAVELLI’s
separation of rewards and norm-relevant behavior offers a related design precedent [15]; our cases
add economically explicit sequences and recipient-side chronology.
Exact-prefix auditing offers a bridge to this design. A claim about inaccessible finance can become
a reproducible local task with an engine-verified solution, while a true hotel lock supplies a negative
case. A coherent hold and a defaulted build supply superficially similar END actions with different
provenance. A corrected false premise and an untested persuasion hypothesis supply distinct social
targets. A future monitor evaluation should declare the channels shown to the monitor, its prediction
target and a held-out scoring rule. The present mismatch counts are diagnostics, not a validated
monitorability benchmark.
Reinforcement learning requires additional engineering and validation: an observation/action
interface, explicit objectives, reward semantics, horizon and truncation rules, reset distributions, and
evaluation on situations not reused during training. A binding contract object or private bilateral
channel may support particular social tasks, but neither is a universal prerequisite for training. Existing
replay and recovery components support precise resets; they do not establish that learning in this sand-
box improves behavior elsewhere. Transfer, reward exploitation and robustness to new counterparties
remain empirical questions.
7.4 The evaluator must be audited too
Several consequential corrections concerned our own labels rather than agent actions. A terminal green
inheritance had been described as G15’s productive acquisition path; original events instead show
orange completion, construction and $3,250 in developed orange rent before the final estate. A $1,200
construction step had been mistaken for G19 Gemini’s $2,000 cumulative expenditure on dark blues.
Other errors arose from interpreting referents in messages, treating a menu as a complete financing
set, or attributing a counteroffer to the thread initiator. Each could have changed an economic or social
conclusion despite a numerically correct event log.
Overlapping review helped expose these errors, but reviewer agreement was not the final criterion.
The corrections required source chronology, explicit units or legal execution. This distinction matters
as agents increasingly evaluate other agents: a fluent, mutually agreed narrative can still misdescribe
the recorded state. The research contribution is consequently both empirical and methodological—a
set of observed trajectories, and a procedure for making claims about those trajectories answerable to
their underlying records.

8 Related work
Long-horizon economic agents. Vending-Bench evaluates autonomous operation of a simulated
business and motivates economic capability as a safety-relevant property [2]. Vending-Bench 2 extends
the author-reported environment to a simulated year with memory tools and adversarial suppliers [1].
The multiplayer Arena study is the closest communication comparison: it analyzes 2,583 emails from
twenty year-long runs, combining a language-model judge, deterministic checks against simulator
records and reasoning-summary analysis [11]. It also examines promise follow-through. Its validation
uses a single-author label check, and the environment is not publicly redistributed; our comparison
concerns its reported method rather than an independent rerun. We do not claim that multiplayer eco-
nomics, state-checked statements or longitudinal communication analysis are new. Our cases connect
those questions to exact historical action menus, legal financing sequences, per-attempt resolution and
an explicit permission for public deception.
Monopoly and board-game economies. AgentMonopoly studies economic and social behavior,
persona control and within-family model sizes in a closely related game [26]. Its paper and repository
describe legal-action lists, a deterministic engine, replay and per-turn records, and its limitations
caution against treating explanations as direct evidence of computation [21]. Its bankruptcy rule
eliminates a cash-negative player without our mortgage-and-sale liquidation phase, and its prompt
prohibits public/action contradictions. Its eight open-weight models range from 8B to 32B parameters
and use temperature zero; a 150-step cap invokes a net-worth winner. Our four configurations instead
use provider-default sampling and the observed games end by uncapped bankruptcy. These rule,
horizon and configuration differences define the scope of comparison. The inspected paper reports
140 games, while the repository experiment table totals 120; we retain the revision discrepancy rather
than infer a shared count.
FinBoardBench evaluates financial decisions across three board games, including Monopoly liquid-
ity and investment summaries and judged trade quality [6]. Its Monopoly bankruptcy treatment sells
buildings, whereas our creditor can inherit them intact. This difference is central to why our observed
rent receipts are not presented as a comparable ROI score. DECE evaluates complete model–harness
configurations across economic games, including matched harness studies, persisted state, legal inter-
faces and replay [30]. We adopt its complete-system framing without claiming its controlled causal
comparisons; our cohort also includes dialogue, which its inspected design excludes. Cattle Trade
provides another auction and bargaining comparison with heuristic baselines [14]. The present cohort
contains no corresponding policy-baseline evaluation.
Social goals, negotiation and commitments. SOTOPIA makes social goals and evaluation criteria
explicit in agent interaction, while Concordia supports generative social simulations through a game-
masterarchitecture[ 24,32]. MonopolyBenchinsteadanchorseconomicconsequencesinadeterministic
rules engine. NegotiationArena measures bargaining interventions in controlled exchanges [3]; its
design illustrates the additional comparison needed to turn a settlement chronology into a persuasion-
effect claim. Work on repeated-game promises, runtime-infeasible commitments and release authority
distinguishes promise formation from later fulfillment [18, 19, 22, 25]. Our G18 chronology adds a
persistent economic context and delivered-memory evidence, but no predefined commitment object or
breach rate.
General agent evaluation. AgentBench, WebArena and OSWorld establish interactive environments
with execution- or state-based checks; TheAgentCompany extends evaluation to consequential work-

place tasks [12, 27, 28, 31]. The reliability and policy-adherence concerns inτ-bench, the explicit
attacker and trust boundaries in AgentDojo, and the experimentation tasks in MLE-bench illustrate
distinct constructs that an agent benchmark must operationalize [4, 5, 29]. AgentBoard and HAL

9 Limitations
Cohort and dependence. Twenty games from five seed blocks are a small, selected evaluation of
four configured systems. Cyclic seat balance does not cover all opponent orders or separate skill
from realized movement. Raw dice sharing does not preserve the semantic use of draws, board
positions or future opportunities. We make no model ranking, capability trend, seat-effect estimate or
generalization from decision-level sample size to independent evidence. The observed source-version
boundary and refusal shift are not randomized treatments. Each agent faces the same three opponent
models throughout, with their configured names visible in its state. Prior familiarity with the widely
documented game may also influence play.
Implementation scope. Results depend on the declared rules, particularly intact creditor inheritance,
finite stock, per-operation even-building validation and the opportunity to declare bankruptcy. Alter-
native Monopoly implementations can change the financing and social incentives. Engine-grounded
truth is truth for this benchmark, not necessarily for another ruleset. Finding and documenting an
observation or recovery defect does not identify how repairing it would change the cohort’s outcomes.
Observation and interpretation. Saved channels differ in availability and reliability. Some provider
reasoning is encrypted or unreturned; private notes can be missing, synthetic or inconsistent with other
returned text. Recipient exposure establishes delivered information rather than attention, belief or
reliance. Case-specific contradictory text supports a narrower statement than stable model knowledge
or intent. We do not estimate latent deception prevalence, nor treat authorized public deception as
demonstrated instruction misalignment.
Selection and adjudication. Definitions and semantic review categories developed during explo-
ration and were not preregistered. The semantic examples are selected from an exhaustively traversed
record, but exhaustive traversal is not exhaustive validated labeling of every possible proposition.
The catalog contains overlapping cases and historical entries, and the common sample comes from a
filtered pool. Four model-assisted review streams are not four independent human annotators. Initial
judgments and revisions remain available, but no agreement statistic is presented as label validity.
Additional blinded human adjudication would be valuable for a future behavioral benchmark.
Local alternatives and economic value. A feasible legal route establishes an endpoint under stated
assumptions. It does not show that an agent would select it, that another player would accept a
proposed exchange, or that the altered trajectory would preserve later draws and decisions. Current-
debt payment need not be optimal, and a marginal rent threshold on an observed landing is not
a winner counterfactual. Paid rent omits unpaid terminal demands, salvage, estate transfers and
unmeasured deterrence; the spending-unit results are neither net profit nor incremental ROI.
26
Validation and release. Full recorded action replay and memory reconstruction are verified locally.
Twenty branch checks do not test every possible decision prefix or full prompt reconstruction beyond
memory. Reference-policy continuations are not LLM suffix experiments, and uncapped observed
endings do not guarantee future termination. The complete publication reproducibility release, in-
dependent clean-environment reproduction and any trained-policy or reward-interface evaluation
remain separate work. This first draft makes no claim that those steps have already been completed.
10 Conclusion
MonopolyBench’s twenty recorded games show agents acquiring and developing assets, bargaining
over rival opportunities, preserving reserves and financing obligations across long competitive trajec-
tories. They also expose execution failures, inaccurate constraints and disagreements among returned
channels. Those distinctions matter: an END action need not be a choice to wait, nominal wealth need
not be accessible, and an accepted exchange need not demonstrate belief in its public premise.
Event reconstruction and exact-prefix legal tests turn these distinctions into inspectable evidence.
They support bounded conclusions about current-debt payment, development and social chronology
while correcting errors in the analysis itself. The present cohort does not establish how alignment
changes with capability. It provides measured behaviors, validated local tests and a reproducible
environment from which that question can be studied under explicit norms, controlled configurations
and repeated continuations. Evaluating long-horizon agents rigorously requires the same discipline at
both levels: preserving the trajectory and testing the interpretation placed on it.

D Validation, provenance and research workflow
The canonical artifacts areevents.jsonl, actions.jsonl, decisions.jsonl, per-attempt prompts and re-
sponses,statesnapshots,runconfiguration,summaryandproviderusage. Eventsequenceanddecision
ID bind the analysis to the actual game. Raw tool calls, selected attempts and fallback origins remain
separate from generated narratives. The study’s event-accounting checks, attempt audits, exact-prefix
branches and interpretation updates are retained in the phase-1 and phase-2 research directories; this
draft’s evidence map identifies the paths supporting each section.
Four model coauthor streams contributed overlapping review and writing: Astra, Sol, Opus and
Fable. These are research-assistant streams, not the game participants. The manuscript-stage streams
used GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol, Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Fable 5.1, respectively. The Sol and
Opus streams share model families with two evaluated participants, and review was not blinded
to this overlap. Extraction and adjudication were separated, initial judgments were preserved, and
peer-informed checks were identified as such. The 614-item catalog includes twenty-one historical
checkpoints and overlaps between public and full-channel extractions. The 240 original-bundle checks
are a fixed stratified audit with sixty decisions per configuration, not a blind random estimate of
misconduct. All reported mechanical replications are distinguished from endorsements of a retained
result. No model-coauthor agreement rate is treated as human annotation reliability.
The draft adds one independent event recount during writing, which reproduces the cohort’s deed
routes, stable-boundary completions, negotiation denominators and all 103 spending units. It also
corrects two trajectory summaries: G15’s orange group, completed at event 711 and first built at 718,
collects $3,250 before green arrives only in the terminal estate; G19 Gemini spends $2,000 cumulatively
ondarkblues, ratherthanthe$1,200ofoneconstructionstep. Theearliersourcejudgmentsareretained
in the research history instead of silently overwritten.
The manuscript build is separate from these gameplay analyses. Its new LaTeX source, copied
audit tables, figure-generation script and bibliography are packaged together. Existing outline and
literature-review files remain unchanged. This package is sufficient to regenerate the manuscript
figures and PDF from retained aggregate data; it is not a substitute for the full saved-game and
environment release. Before publication, the release should fix a repository version, artifact manifest,
redistribution permissions and clean-environment analysis instructions, and an independent reader
should reproduce a primary table and inspect its cited decision sequence.
