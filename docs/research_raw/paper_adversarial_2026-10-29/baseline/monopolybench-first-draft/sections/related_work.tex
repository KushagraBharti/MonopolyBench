\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Long-horizon economic agents.}
Vending-Bench evaluates autonomous operation of a simulated business and motivates economic capability as a safety-relevant property~\cite{backlund2025vendingbench}. Vending-Bench~2 extends the author-reported environment to a simulated year with memory tools and adversarial suppliers~\cite{andon2026vb2}. The multiplayer Arena study is the closest communication comparison: it analyzes 2,583 emails from twenty year-long runs, combining a language-model judge, deterministic checks against simulator records and reasoning-summary analysis~\cite{li2026arena}. It also examines promise follow-through. Its validation uses a single-author label check, and the environment is not publicly redistributed; our comparison concerns its reported method rather than an independent rerun. We do not claim that multiplayer economics, state-checked statements or longitudinal communication analysis are new. Our cases connect those questions to exact historical action menus, legal financing sequences, per-attempt resolution and an explicit permission for public deception.

\paragraph{Monopoly and board-game economies.}
AgentMonopoly studies economic and social behavior, persona control and within-family model sizes in a closely related game~\cite{wu2026agentmonopoly}. Its paper and repository describe legal-action lists, a deterministic engine, replay and per-turn records, and its limitations caution against treating explanations as direct evidence of computation~\cite{agentmonopoly2026repo}. Its bankruptcy rule eliminates a cash-negative player without our mortgage-and-sale liquidation phase, and its prompt prohibits public/action contradictions. Its eight open-weight models range from 8B to 32B parameters and use temperature zero; a 150-step cap invokes a net-worth winner. Our four configurations instead use provider-default sampling and the observed games end by uncapped bankruptcy. These rule, horizon and configuration differences define the scope of comparison. The inspected paper reports 140 games, while the repository experiment table totals 120; we retain the revision discrepancy rather than infer a shared count.

FinBoardBench evaluates financial decisions across three board games, including Monopoly liquidity and investment summaries and judged trade quality~\cite{hu2026finboardbench}. Its Monopoly bankruptcy treatment sells buildings, whereas our creditor can inherit them intact. This difference is central to why our observed rent receipts are not presented as a comparable ROI score. DECE evaluates complete model--harness configurations across economic games, including matched harness studies, persisted state, legal interfaces and replay~\cite{zhang2026dece}. We adopt its complete-system framing without claiming its controlled causal comparisons; our cohort also includes dialogue, which its inspected design excludes. Cattle Trade provides another auction and bargaining comparison with heuristic baselines~\cite{muller2026cattletrade}. The present cohort contains no corresponding policy-baseline evaluation.

\paragraph{Social goals, negotiation and commitments.}
SOTOPIA makes social goals and evaluation criteria explicit in agent interaction, while Concordia supports generative social simulations through a game-master architecture~\cite{zhou2024sotopia,vezhnevets2023concordia}. MonopolyBench instead anchors economic consequences in a deterministic rules engine. NegotiationArena measures bargaining interventions in controlled exchanges~\cite{bianchi2024negotiationarena}; its design illustrates the additional comparison needed to turn a settlement chronology into a persuasion-effect claim. Work on repeated-game promises, runtime-infeasible commitments and release authority distinguishes promise formation from later fulfillment~\cite{shi2026cheaptalk,shi2026agentslie,empty_commitments_v2,commitment_standing}. Our G18 chronology adds a persistent economic context and delivered-memory evidence, but no predefined commitment object or breach rate.

\paragraph{General agent evaluation.}
AgentBench, WebArena and OSWorld establish interactive environments with execution- or state-based checks; TheAgentCompany extends evaluation to consequential workplace tasks~\cite{liu2024agentbench,zhou2024webarena,xie2024osworld,xu2025theagentcompany}. The reliability and policy-adherence concerns in $\tau$-bench, the explicit attacker and trust boundaries in AgentDojo, and the experimentation tasks in MLE-bench illustrate distinct constructs that an agent benchmark must operationalize~\cite{yao2025taubench,debenedetti2024agentdojo,chan2025mlebench}. AgentBoard and HAL motivate process-sensitive analysis and inspection of complete systems~\cite{ma2024agentboard,kapoor2026hal}. METR's time horizon measures task duration calibrated to human work~\cite{kwa2025timehorizons}; our game turns, decisions, attempts and tokens are different horizon measures and are not converted into that construct.
