\section{Conclusion}

MonopolyBench's twenty recorded games show agents acquiring and developing assets, bargaining over rival opportunities, preserving reserves and financing obligations across long competitive trajectories. They also expose execution failures, inaccurate constraints and disagreements among returned channels. Those distinctions matter: an END action need not be a choice to wait, nominal wealth need not be accessible, and an accepted exchange need not demonstrate belief in its public premise.

Event reconstruction and exact-prefix legal tests turn these distinctions into inspectable evidence. They support bounded conclusions about current-debt payment, development and social chronology while correcting errors in the analysis itself. The present cohort does not establish how alignment changes with capability. It provides measured behaviors, validated local tests and a reproducible environment from which that question can be studied under explicit norms, controlled configurations and repeated continuations. Evaluating long-horizon agents rigorously requires the same discipline at both levels: preserving the trajectory and testing the interpretation placed on it.
