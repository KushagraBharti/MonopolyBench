\section{Conclusion}

Agent trajectories support different claims at different levels of evidence. In MonopolyBench, an END can follow a rejected construction attempt, a bankrupt player can still have a legal payment route, and an accepted proposal can coexist with explicit correction of its false premise. These distinctions change the interpretation without erasing the applied outcome.

The twenty-game audit pairs attempt and delivery records with specified historical interventions. Its portable suite reproduces 50,918 events, checks all 66 construction defaults and 60 eliminations, and retains witnesses, bounds and a narrowly exhaustive hotel-lock certificate. Selected social chronologies establish exposure, corrections, omissions and withdrawals while leaving causal persuasion and latent intent unresolved. The paper's claim is a bounded method and finite-corpus demonstration: preserving a trajectory is necessary, and verifying the behavioral interpretation requires additional, claim-specific evidence.
