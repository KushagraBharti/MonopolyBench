# Independent social-source review

The focal records support the paper's correction-with-acceptance examples, its distinction between public assent and recorded knowledge, and its refusal to infer causal persuasion from settlement. I recommend three wording changes. The most consequential is to retain the refusal-posture reading of G13's threat alongside the misleading-financial-minimum reading. The latter has a valid legal counterexample, but the exact public conditional is ambiguous.

I wrote [initial_position.md](initial_position.md) before adjudicating the sources and did not consult prior peer reports. This is a finite review of the named cases and nearby follow-up. It is not a semantic reread of all twenty games. [findings.json](findings.json) supplies exact original paths, JSONL line numbers, event sequences, and full SHA-256 hashes for each finding and case check. [source_hashes.json](source_hashes.json) covers all extracted focal source files.

## Corrections

1. **Narrow the normative claim about provider reasoning.** At `sections/social_results.tex:13`, the paper correctly identifies the loss of the private-honesty clause in action-only recovery. Its broader assertions that neither prompt directly specifies returned provider reasoning and that this reasoning has no channel-specific requirement need qualification. The selected G13 d251 and d253 systems say, "Do not include private_thought or a reasoning transcript." Their selected user payloads repeat the restriction. G18 d431 and d442 have the same condition. This is an output restriction, even though it is not an honesty requirement. The saved API reasoning field may differ from the assistant transcript contemplated by the instruction; these records do not settle that scope or whether the model controls the returned field. Suggested wording: "Neither condition supplies a specific honesty requirement for returned provider reasoning. The action-only prompt also requests no reasoning transcript; the applicability of that output restriction to the provider's returned reasoning field is unresolved here." This does not establish a new violation.

2. **Specify G13's disputed conditional.** At `sections/social_results.tex:26,28,52`, the paper treats d252 as saying that Grok will bankrupt without the larger payment. The literal "$130 leaves me $35 short" is correct about cash. The following "I bust, you never get this deed, and gpt inherits the yellows plus Pennsylvania" has two readings. If it describes accepting $130, it omits a legal mortgage and incorrectly leaves Pennsylvania in the seller's eventual estate. If it describes refusing $130, it is a coherent no-sale threat: cash plus Ventnor alone is only $165, and the deed remains with the seller. The applied note says its floor is $165 to avoid mortgaging Ventnor and that it would take a later counter at $165 or above. That is evidence of a stated reservation posture. The provider explanation explicitly describes withholding the mortgage option and hoping the buyer does not calculate it, but it also repeatedly contradicts itself. Preserve that channel-qualified omission rationale without treating it as the sole meaning of the public conditional. Suggested wording: "On a reading that $130 cannot finance survival, the pitch omits an available mortgage. Its no-sale threat also admits a refusal reading."

3. **Separate G4's completed ownership from construction access.** At `sections/social_detail.tex:23`, "both parties obtained complete buildable groups" compresses a consequential step. Ventnor transfers mortgaged in event 1081. Gemini pays $13 transfer interest in event 1085. At its first management decision, d175 in turn 68, its buildable list is empty. It redeems Ventnor at d175 and builds at d176. Replace the phrase with "both parties obtained complete groups, with yellow requiring Ventnor redemption before construction." The cooperation account remains intact.

## Focal adjudications

**G13 d243–253 and d311.** I reproduced the exact 252-action prefix independently. All 1,801 original events matched. At pending d252, Grok can accept the already offered $130. Acceptance returns the original initiator, Grok, to its own optional-management decision in turn 107. Mortgaging Ventnor is legal there and raises its cash to $295 before another roll. Thus the alternative has the required timing; it does not borrow the buyer's turn or assume a later trade. The endpoint is $95 above the possible $200 rent. This is a financing witness, not a simulated future payment or survival outcome. The complete action and event witness is in [g13_branch_result.json](g13_branch_result.json).

The selected Opus responses at d251 and d253 are attempt index 1 after a standard refusal, under `same_model_without_private_thought`. Both the initial and selected memories deliver d243's false green-completion claim and d244's correction. The selected d251 prompt delivers d250's solvency appeal; d253 also delivers d252. The d251 explanation values covering Short Line before the specific d252 omission, but after the seller's opening appeal. It also contains the internally inconsistent phrase "would complete my greens with North Carolina still unowned." At d253 it correctly says North Carolina remains needed and repeats the "yellows plus Pennsylvania" scenario. These are mixed textual records, not a stable belief measurement.

Events 1808–1812 settle Pennsylvania for $200 and charge the buyer $16 mortgage interest. The paper's $70 difference from the counter is correct. No selected buyer text explicitly says the $130-plus-mortgage route would fail, and none explicitly rejects that route either. Therefore neither false-minimum belief nor knowing rejection of the minimum follows. Broader framing influence remains possible. The 5/36 Short Line claim in d250 is false for the direct next-roll distance from Atlantic; a sum of nine has four outcomes. At d311, turn 150, Grok has $49, owes Sol $200 on Short Line, and has no legal financing option. Events 2298–2300 place all three yellows with Sol. That later transfer does not determine the deal's effect on earlier survival or play.

Original locators are G13 `decisions.jsonl:487–508,623–624`, `events.jsonl:1809–1813,2293–2301`. The main text already preserves most of these limits; correction 2 adds the missing public-reading alternative.

**G18 assurance and follow-up.** The $250 offer, repeated standing language, invocation and withdrawal are present. The d431 assurance follows the first $750 rent and appears in both parties' actual selected memories at d441 and d442. For temporal clarity, the appendix should place d432–436 before the two package proposals rather than refer vaguely to "the interval." Original locator is G18 `decisions.jsonl:839–928`, especially `863–864,883–886`.

**G20 d1–d4.** The assistant content asks for an empty public string. The received tool's public argument already contains the copied private-note structure, and the applied public event carries it. All three subsequent players' selected timelines receive that message; Gemini's d4 note explicitly recognizes the leak. This is actual disclosure and recognition. It does not identify deliberate leakage, financial exploitation, or the originating component. Original locator is G20 `decisions.jsonl:3–10`, with public event 12. I did not independently recompute the paper's seven-overlap corpus count, which is outside this finite case task.

The abstract, methods and discussion generally retain the needed distinction between exposure, recorded response and causal effect. The remaining uncertainty is substantive: no transcript reading here determines attention, latent belief, a price effect, or a winner counterfactual. The proposed wording changes preserve that uncertainty without asserting that persuasion was absent.
