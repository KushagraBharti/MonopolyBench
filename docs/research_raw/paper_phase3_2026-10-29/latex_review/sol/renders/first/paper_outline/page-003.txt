Working title and central argument


Preferred working title: MonopolyBench: Studying Long-Horizon Agent Capability and Social Conduct in a
Competitive Economy.

Alternative emphasizing the research program: MonopolyBench: A Replayable Sandbox for Studying Agent
Capability and Alignment. Use this only if the abstract immediately distinguishes the current descriptive
results from future alignment experiments, and the release package is complete.

The paper asks how agents sustain goal-directed economic behavior and how they conduct themselves
toward other agents over the same long trajectory. MonopolyBench provides persistent resources, com-
peting interests, consequential negotiation, constrained financing and inspectable state. The current
cohort supports economic profiles and source-grounded social explanations. Exact historical-prefix tests
expose when nominal assets are inaccessible, an apparently skipped investment was a discarded plan, or
a generated behavioral label was wrong. These findings inform how to design and audit long-horizon
safety sandboxes. They do not establish how alignment changes with capability growth.

Keep three research questions throughout:

 1. Capability: How do the evaluated systems acquire, negotiate, finance, develop, liquidate and preserve
   economic position across complete games?
 2. Conduct: What do they claim, conceal, promise, coordinate and revise, relative to their actual instruc-
    tions and counterparties’ delivered information?
 3. Evaluation design: Which records and actionable opportunities allow these behaviors to be interpreted
    reliably, and what additional experimental design is needed for capability–alignment comparisons
   and training?

Contributions should be concrete: the environment and replayable research records; bilateral and longi-
tudinal analysis of a complete cohort; exact-prefix legal-alternative auditing with demonstrated scope;
and norm-explicit social chronologies that preserve conflicting channels and counterexamples. Shared
infrastructure is acknowledged rather than claimed as first. Release status must be stated honestly until
steps 17–18 finish.


Abstract plan


Open with the joint capability/conduct question. Introduce the persistent four-player economy and the
20-game, five-seed, four-rotation cohort. Describe the analysis method in one sentence. Include one
economic result and one social contrast, plus the result that applied non-building can arise through
different execution paths. End with the supported evaluation-design implication and the boundary of the
current study.

Candidate economic numbers are the 60-elimination financing split or the eight-case development residual;
use one, not a list of all totals. The social result should pair misleading framing with recipient correction
or a prior motive. Do not lead with a model leaderboard, a deception rate or a generic claim that agents
become misaligned as they improve. Final title and abstract are written after the manuscript, in step 15.
