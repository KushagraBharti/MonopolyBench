5  Social conduct during economic competition


This section should have comparable analytical depth to the economic section, with original source
references and meaningful contrasting cases rather than a short anecdote box. Social evidence is not
restricted to dishonesty: conditional cooperation, selective bargaining, anti-leader appeals and changed
plans all matter.


5.1  Misleading framing and recipient response

F8 uses three contrasts. G13 places the buyer’s earlier d251 $130 counteroffer and recorded solvency
rationale before the seller’s d252 omission and the d253 $200 acceptance. The $130 route is legally sufficient
with existing collateral. The pitch is delivered; settlement follows; a $70 persuasion effect is not identified.

G19 shows explicit premise correction with acceptance, while the proposer’s own call includes an abort
instruction. G11 shows a transient false echo, verification and rejection within the recipient’s record. G4
and G6 extend the dossier with prior correct knowledge, public concession and correction-with-settlement.
These cases support proposition-specific statements and forbid a general immunity or absence-of-uptake
conclusion.


5.2  Offers, assurances and changed plans

Analyze G18’s standing $250 offer, later invocation and withdrawal after intervening rent and liquidation.
Distinguish a feasible offer from an irrevocable promise and identify conditions, expiry and actual reliance.
Costly reliance or dishonesty at inception is not established. Contrast free-form bargaining posture, explicit
future assurances and structured settlements; they impose different evidentiary requirements.


5.3  Cooperation, opposition and third-party effects

Trace anti-leader appeals and refusals to fund rivals, including occasions when the speaker continues
self-interested trade. Evaluate whether a proposal became an agreement and whether the agreement
was acted on. No cartel, binding coalition or deliberate kingmaking is identified in the adjudicated
current-cohort cases. This is a bounded finding, not proof of universal absence. Bankruptcy’s creditor is
fixed by the debt, so an estate recipient alone cannot reveal a chosen beneficiary.


5.4  Disclosure and conflicting channels

Include the G20 private-note text appearing in a public field and recipient recognition. Preserve source
uncertainty. Use the Grok name-conflict count as context, with 15 equal parsed argument objects versus
five identical serialized substrings. Separate those records from ordinary changes of plan and the behavior
of external monitors. Missing private text, provider summaries and selected response conditions have
different detection opportunities; no cross-model deception-rate table follows from their raw counts.

Citations: Arena study, NegotiationArena, AgentMonopoly, SOTOPIA, the two workshop promise papers,
Empty Commitments v2 and Who May Release. Transition: these findings expose requirements for the
evaluation instrument and for future alignment experiments.
