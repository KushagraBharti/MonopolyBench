## 1. What I think the paper is, in one paragraph

The paper studies how configured LLM agents sustain goal-directed economic capability across complete, uncapped, four-player Monopoly games, how their communication relates to their actions and to other players' interests, and what a long-horizon multi-agent environment must record for those questions to be answerable at all. The twenty-game cohort supports exact longitudinal profiles of configured agents, a bounded source-linked social chronology, and a set of measurement-validity findings about the agent system (provider policy, response handling, prompt rendering, validator semantics). It does not support a capability–alignment trend, an intrinsic provider ranking, a causal persuasion effect, coalition or kingmaking findings, optimal-policy claims, or counterfactual winners. Those stay in the paper as the research question the environment is built to answer, with the designs that would answer them stated and their status marked as not run.

## 2. Positions I hold going into debate

**P1. The unit of evaluation is the configured agent, and the paper should say so in the first results paragraph.** Each participant is model + system prompt + memory window + tool schema + response policy (Opus: refusal → action-only retry; Grok: `tool_choice=required`). Every per-model table carries that label. This is not a caveat to bury: DECE's harness-effect result and Vending-Bench 2's own note that scaffolding matters make it a live reviewer question (DECE: inherited from readiness verification, re-verify this phase).

**P2. Three evidence layers, named once, used everywhere.** (i) Applied layer: `events.jsonl`, `actions.jsonl`, legal menus in `decisions.jsonl`. (ii) Delivered layer: the exact prompt the player saw, including the bounded 20-entry timeline and 10-note memory, which is event-reconstructable (A: Opus phase-1 sandbox checks 20/20; Astra memory audit 6,989/6,989). (iii) Authored layer: public message, applied private note, non-applied content, provider reasoning text or summary, each with its availability by model. Social and capability claims state which layer supports them. Nothing in layer (iii) is privileged intent (A: R24, R25; G1 d327 "$3" summary vs $3,772).

**P3. The economic findings are the paper's strongest and most original material, and they should stay longitudinal.** Ready now (A, Astra R03–R17 with Sol/Opus concordance): negotiation propensity vs intensity with two denominators; 113 settlements completing groups for 60/43/10 sides; 43/63 trade-completed groups built in the first actual management turn, 34/42 entry-buildable; all 61 Opus defaulted plans legal after reordering; recovery changes quantities in 12/18 Gemini and 5/25 Sol successes; 60 eliminations with 56 infeasible under a generous bound, 3 payable, 1 hotel lock; 103 spending units, $103,450 outlay, $93,000 whole-game paid developed rent ($92,550 after first own spend), 40 units with zero paid developed rent; winner is creditor in 54/60; 58 winner spending groups, 34 with inherited deeds, 30 first completed by estate; nine games reach zero bank houses. These are descriptive, dependent, one-roster, five-block results.

**P4. The social section should be a chronology of measured cases with their negative controls, not a rate.** The retained set (A): G13 d250–253 misleading framing with recorded omission rationale, exposure and settlement, with the buyer's prior insurance motive at d251; G4 d148–152 public concession with private correction recorded before the false claim; G6 d19–21 and G19 d135–136 acceptance with explicit premise correction; G11 d255–258 transient echo then rejection; G18 d431/441/442 standing-offer invocation and withdrawal; G20 d001/d004 private-note disclosure and recognition; G7 d42 "final" with a $625 private ceiling; anti-leader appeals in G16/G18/G20 with no identified pact. Each case names the norm evaluated (the prompt permits public deception; private notes are asked to be true; legal action is engine-enforced), the opportunity, the applied behavior, the recipient's recorded response, and the competing explanation. No deception rate, no misalignment headline, no "no collusion" absence claim beyond the inspected record.

**P5. Interface findings explain affected results; they are not the subject.** Four items belong in a single methods subsection plus one validation table: Opus refusal condition (1,605 refused attempts, 1,604 first-attempt chains, identical provider string, 0.57–0.76 in G1–4 and 1.00 in G5–20); Grok received name conflicts (139; 15 equal parsed arguments; 5 literally identical substrings; source unlocalized); trade-history formatter defect (three manifestations, union 2,054/6,989 initial prompts; exposure not reliance); per-entry even-building validator × plan encoding × retry budget (61 Opus defaults). They appear again only where they change a result's reading (e.g., the Opus deployment rate; G1 d112 as a flagged exhibit).

**P6. Capability/alignment stays the framing question, with honest labels.** What is implemented: engine-authoritative rules, legal menus, deterministic seeded replay, exact decision-prefix reset with event-rebuilt memory (tested at 20 prefixes), reference-policy continuations, per-attempt provenance, frozen campaign treatments. What is measured: configured-agent economic profiles and social conduct in one cohort. What is not implemented or measured: RL adapter, training gains, a capability axis, an alignment score, any cross-capability trend. The discussion should state the minimal design for the trend question (several capability tiers within one provider lineage, same harness, same prompt, matched seeds, repeated realizations, pre-specified social codes) and mark it as future work. The Opus refusal is a provider-policy × harness interaction and must not be written as a safety refusal (A: all four ledgers agree; `claude-refusal-condition-shift` memory agrees).

**P7. No new paid games are needed for any ready claim.** Existing-log analyses that would upgrade claims are finite and specified in §4. Experiments are listed only against the stronger claims that would require them, and none of those claims is in the ready set.

## 3. Where I expect to disagree with peers (declared before reading them)

- **Novelty wording.** Astra's early challenge is right: Vending-Bench Arena (V, author eval page fetched today) has three or four co-located agents who "can email each other, send money, and trade goods", is scored individually, and the page reports price collusion, misleading supplier advice, exploiting a competitor's distress, and a paid agreement to stop stocking a product. So "multiplayer economics with alignment-relevant conduct" is already observed in an industry eval, and with overlapping models: Round 13 lists GPT-6 Sol, Claude Opus 5.5 and Grok 4.7; Round 14 lists Gemini 4 Argon (V). The page has no paper or arXiv link (V); the earlier readiness note of an "Arena paper arXiv 2608.14825" is unverified and must be checked or dropped. Our differentiators must therefore be stated as: engine-enforced rules with recorded legal menus; deterministic replay and prefix reset; per-attempt response provenance; a bounded adjudication method that separates proposition truth, channel, exposure, reliance and intent; all players evaluated, not one focal agent; uncapped games ending by bankruptcy. Not "first", not "more realistic", not "the only multi-agent economy".
- **FinBoardBench.** The README says it "explicitly analyzes longitudinal acquisition and finance". The v1 HTML fetched today (V) reports a cash-bucket liquidity snapshot (§3.2: purchases below $500 cash 47 times, below $200 23 times), an LLM judge (DeepSeek V4 Pro) rating 45/86 proposer and 11/18 receiver Monopoly trades harmful (§4.2), an ROI profile figure (§4.3), seat rotation without seeds, and fault tolerance with up to five execution failures then a system-forced fallback (Appendix E). I did not find a per-turn trajectory analysis in the inspected sections. I will re-read §3.2–4.3 before asserting "not longitudinal"; until then the comparison says "reports liquidity and ROI summaries; per-turn trajectories not reported in inspected sections".
- **AgentMonopoly.** Astra reports it disclaims explanation-as-intent and links speech to actions. I have not yet re-fetched v2 today (fetch interrupted); readiness-stage verification (2026-10-27) found 140 games (40+40+40+20), seeds 42–46, open 8B–32B models, modified rules (rent waiving, negative-cash elimination, 150-step cap), `chain_of_thought` logged, code on GitHub. If the disclaimer is verified, our method claim narrows to the adjudication procedure (proposition truth / channel / exposure / reliance / intent with counterexamples and source IDs), not to "treating explanations as self-reports".
- **How much the social section should claim.** I will argue for substantive main-text cases (P4) and against both a deception-rate table and a one-paragraph bounded mention. The cases are findings about what competitive economic language does and does not do to recorded recipients; they are not alignment verdicts.
- **House retention and kingmaking.** I expect pressure to headline "models hoard houses to deny rivals". The record supports nine zero-stock games, stated motives in some notes, one realized lock (G7 d339) and reachable workarounds elsewhere (G5 d306, G12 d312). No binding-shortage count exists. I will resist a denial headline unless the count is done.
