# Detailed venue-neutral outline (Fable, phase 3, step 14)

Working title candidates: "MonopolyBench: Sustained Economic Capability and Social Conduct of Configured LLM Agents in a Replayable Competitive Economy"; shorter: "What a Long-Horizon Multi-Agent Economy Must Record". No venue tailoring. Each section lists: argument, evidence (claim IDs from Astra's phase-2 ledger R01–R25 or named files), exhibits (F/T/A from `figure_table_plan.md`), citations (keys from `references.bib`), alternative explanations the text must carry, and the transition sentence to the next section. Word targets are proportions, not a page budget.

## Abstract (≈200 words)
Argument. Twenty uncapped four-player Monopoly games among four configured frontier agents, with every decision's legal menu, every response attempt and every event recorded and replayable. Agents assemble sets by purchase, auction, trade and inheritance, finance and deploy them unevenly, and lose through debts that mostly exceed any legal financing. Public bargaining includes misleading framing that recipients often correct; no coalition or kingmaking is identified. Provider policy, forced tool choice, prompt rendering and validator semantics change what an agent system does over a long horizon and must be declared as part of the evaluated object. The data support configured-agent profiles and a case-level social chronology, not rankings, persuasion effects or a capability–alignment trend; the environment's recorded affordances are what a designed test of that trend would use.
Numbers allowed in the abstract: 20 games, 6,989 decisions, 60 eliminations (56 infeasible, 3 payable, 1 lock), 103 spending units, 61 reordered-legal defaults. No win counts in the abstract.

## 1. Introduction (≈10%)
Argument. (a) Long-horizon autonomy is now evaluated mostly in single-agent tasks scored by outcome; competitive economies add interdependence: one agent's deal changes another's future menus. (b) Two questions coexist on the same trajectory: how well an agent pursues a long-horizon economic objective, and how it conducts itself toward other agents while doing so. (c) The capability–alignment relationship across model generations is the motivating question; this cohort cannot trace it, and the paper says so in the first section. (d) Contributions, phrased as measured capabilities: an engine-authoritative, replayable environment with recorded legal menus and per-attempt provenance; a measurement method that audits legal alternatives at exact historical prefixes; a descriptive economic account of 20 complete games; a norm-explicit social chronology; and measurement-validity findings about configured agents. (e) Explicit non-claims: no intrinsic ranking, no causal persuasion, no coalition finding, no instruction-misalignment result (public deception is permitted), no trend.
Evidence. R01, R02 for scope; prompt text (`player_config.py` SYSTEM_PROMPT_V1) for the norm.
Exhibits. F1 (grid) as the opening figure; T4 affordance ledger referenced forward.
Citations. backlund2025vendingbench (capital acquisition motivation), li2026emergentmisaligned and andonlabs2026vendingbencharena (competitive economy with misaligned communication already observed), wu2026agentmonopoly, hu2026finboardbench (closest Monopoly work), kwa2025timehorizons (to say our units are not human-time horizons), zhang2026dece and kapoor2026hal (harness matters).
Alternatives to carry. That winning reflects seat and dice; that explanations reveal intent; that interface effects are the whole story. Each is answered later, not here.
Transition. "We first place the design among existing evaluations, then describe what the environment records and why that determines which questions are answerable."

## 3. Environment and evaluated object (≈15%)
3.1 Rules variant. Declared departures from classic Monopoly (T1): creditor inherits developments intact with no transfer interest; bank returns are not auctioned; voluntary bankruptcy is surrender even if financing remains; management is the active player's post-turn phase and restricted in jail; finite 32 houses and 12 hotels with per-entry even building and no building auctions; 10% rounded-up interest to the receiver of a mortgaged deed in a trade; unmortgage charge ceil(1.1×). Evidence: `docs/benchmark_audit_fixes.md`, engine lines cited in Opus A3 and my A6. Alternative: results under classic bankruptcy (buildings sold back) could differ; state it.
3.2 Protocol objects and decision loop. Snapshot, event, decision (legal menu), action. Observation: public state for all players, bank inventory, 20-entry timeline, 10 own private notes, decision-specific context including full offer history. Memory is a pure function of events (reconstructed 6,989/6,989). Evidence: Astra memory audit; Opus sandbox checks.
3.3 The configured agents. Model, provider pin, medium effort (provider-native, not equal compute), system prompt with three norms (legal action enforced; public deception permitted; private notes "should reflect true reasoning"), tool schema with required public_message and private_thought, tool_choice (required for Grok only, `player_config.py:328–334`), response policy (two corrective attempts; Claude refusal recovery → action-only retry → never-used Opus 4.8 stage → deterministic fallback). Evidence: `docs/model_response_policy.md`, T2.
3.4 Replay, reset and provenance. Deterministic state and artifact replay (20/20); exact decision-prefix reset with event-rebuilt memory and divergent legal continuation (20 prefixes); per-attempt raw responses with refusal metadata; reference policies exist but are not used for findings. Affordance ledger T4 with the three-row split (environment implemented / analysis protocol performed / unimplemented: branching API, reward interface, RL adapter, training, contract objects, private bilateral channels, honesty-constrained treatments, oversight agents).
Transition. "Given these records, the study design determines what comparisons are fair."

## 4. Study design and adjudication method (≈10%)
4.1 Cohort. Five seeds × four cyclic rotations; each agent in each seat five times; shared raw dice stream within block diverging in use at draws 37–124 (A2); one realization per cell; games 1–4 at source 42f85183 and 5–20 at d6808534 with only concurrency changes in the diff; all games end by bankruptcy. Evidence: `game-index.csv`, `summary.json`, phase-1 reproductions. Alternative: block and source confounded with the Opus refusal share (0.57–0.76 vs 1.00).
4.2 Channel availability. T2. Statement: rates are undefined where a channel is empty by construction; Opus private notes 91 of 1,696; provider text types differ; Grok content beside tool calls in 1,648 decisions.
4.3 Review procedure. Complete extraction over 6,989 decisions and 8,679 attempts by model extractors (Terra; Astra-started/Sol-completed ranges; a public-only pass with 1,367 Sonnet and 5,622 Haiku decisions); four model coauthors adjudicated 614 catalog entries, all 20 trajectories and a fixed 240-decision missed-case sample with independent initial judgments preserved, debates logged, scripts for arithmetic and exact-prefix checks. Not human annotation, not preregistered, not independent replication; the cohort was explored before any codebook. Citations: kapoor2026hal (LLM-aided log inspection), li2026emergentmisaligned (single-labeler validation as a comparable limit), zhu2025agenticbenchmarkchecklist.
4.4 Units and dependence. Games within blocks; decisions, groups and eliminations are dependent; report counts with denominators and no decision-level significance tests (miller2024errorbars).
Transition. "We now follow the economic life cycle of a game: acquisition, bargaining, access, construction, rent, liquidation, inheritance."

## 6. Social conduct under the declared norms (≈15%)
6.1 Norm and method. Public deception permitted; private notes asked to be true; legal action enforced. Five channels with availability; the applied private note is an action payload; returned explanations are self-reports. Dimensions kept separate: proposition truth against the speaker's delivered observation, misleading implication, exposure at the recipient's specific decision, recorded recipient response, reliance, intent (stated / unresolved / contradicted). Citations: wu2026agentmonopoly (contradiction prohibited there), li2026emergentmisaligned (codebook approach), shi2026cheaptalk, shi2026whenagentslie.
6.2 Misleading framing with a recorded omission rationale: G13 d250–253, with the buyer's d251 solvency motive before the pitch and d253's inheritance-denial rationale (F6 mine / F8 Astra). What is supported: omission, exposure, settlement. What is not: belief change, a $70 effect, instruction violation.
6.3 Correction, concession and echo: G6 d19–21 and G19 d135–136 (acceptance with explicit premise correction); G4 d148–152 (public concession, prior private correction); G11 d255–258 (transient echo then rejection). Supported: proposition-specific non-uptake. Not: general immunity.
6.4 Bluffs, standing offers and disclosure: G7 d42 ("$600 final" with a $625 private ceiling); G15 d50/d54 and G17 d138 "limits" (no reliance); G19 "final number" that held; G18 d431/441/442 standing offer invoked and withdrawn; G20 d001/d004 private-note string in public, recognized.
6.5 Third parties: anti-leader appeals (G20 d96, G16, G18 d335–344) with self-interested exceptions; leader-funding denial as a stated rejection reason; no pact, cartel or kingmaking in the inspected record (bounded absence). Estate transfer cannot identify a chosen beneficiary because the creditor is fixed by the debt.
6.6 Channel conflicts as hazards, not conduct: G12 d249, G19 d135, G18 d083 labelled "tool_choice=required; source unlocalized"; phantom-build notes (G8 d177, G13 d160); summary errors (G1 d327 "$3"). No monitorability headline (agreed).
Alternatives carried throughout: error, stale context, changed plans under financing pressure, received-call conflicts, permitted bluffing.
Transition. "Several of the cases above turned on how the agent system, not only the model, produced its action. We now state those conditions and the results they touch."

## 7. Measurement validity: the agent system over a long horizon (≈8%)
7.1 Opus refusal condition (1,605 attempts; identical ToS string; 1,604 action-only chains; 1,108 empty and 286 synthetic-only memories; 61 defaults from a consumed retry). Provider policy × harness; not a safety refusal; trigger unidentified; confounded with block and source.
7.2 Grok received name conflicts (139; 15 equal parsed arguments; 5 literal substrings; only agent under forced tool choice; source unlocalized). Handling: applied path authoritative; flag; never infer intent.
7.3 Prompt rendering: trade-history formatter (three manifestations; 2,054/6,989 prompts; exposure, not reliance; G14 d140 recovery); timeline omissions (94 + 55).
7.4 Validator and engine semantics: per-entry even-building check; unmortgage ceiling; GOOJF card transfers without events; multi-creditor liquidation scenario (G19 d263).
7.5 Label error classes in our own pipeline (referent, tense, arithmetic, menu reading; 12–14 contradicted entries per coauthor) and what the exact-prefix checks caught.
Exhibits. T3, T2. Citations: zhang2026dece, kapoor2026hal, zhu2025agenticbenchmarkchecklist.
Transition. "With the conditions declared, we can say what the cohort establishes about capability and alignment, and what a designed study would need."

## 8. Capability and alignment: what is established, suggested, and untested (≈7%)
8.1 Established: configured-agent economic profiles; exact local diagnoses (discarded plans, inaccessible equity, payable debts); a bounded social chronology; interface effects on specific results.
8.2 Suggested but not identified: that recipient-side selectivity and term design matter more than proposal volume; that late development pays as lethal debt rather than rent; that house retention is sometimes intended and rarely binding.
8.3 Untested, with the design named: capability–alignment trend (within-lineage tiers, fixed norms and harness, matched seeds, repeated realizations, pre-specified codes); causal persuasion (message ablation from frozen snapshots); recovery-policy effects (non-refused retry or end-state validator); instruction misalignment (binding honesty/commitment norm with monitored opportunities); coalition (identifiable agreement object). None run.
8.4 What the sandbox already affords for those designs and what it lacks (T4). Citations: backlund2025vendingbench, pan2023machiavelli, li2026emergentmisaligned, korbak2025monitorability.
Transition. "These boundaries are also the paper's limitations."

## 9. Limitations (≈3%)
One roster; five blocks; one realization per cell; declared rule variant; medium effort not equal compute; two agents under non-standard response conditions; model-adjudicated review without human annotation or preregistration; social prevalence not coded; optional existing-log analyses listed in `experiment_decisions.md`; no RL or training evidence.

## 10. Conclusion (≈2%)
Restate the two questions and the recorded-affordance answer. End on the design requirement: an evaluation of long-horizon multi-agent conduct must record legal menus, every attempt, and the delivered prompt, and must declare the agent system, or it cannot tell competence, execution and instruction compliance apart.
