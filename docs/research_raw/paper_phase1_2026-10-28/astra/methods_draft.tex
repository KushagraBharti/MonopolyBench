MonopolyBench studies sustained agent capability and alignment-relevant behavior
in a competitive, persistent economy. We ask how effectively agents pursue
long-term goals, how their decisions and communications affect other participants,
and what an evaluation environment must record to make these behaviors open to
inspection. Economic and social analyses address the same trajectories: an
agreement changes ownership and liquidity, which changes subsequent opportunities,
incentives, and interactions. The broader motivation is to evaluate these
relationships as agent capabilities improve. The present cohort supplies an
observational comparison of configured agents; it does not by itself establish
a trend across capability growth or the effects of training.

The proposed contributions are an inspectable multi-agent economic environment;
a reproducible evaluation workflow linking observations, responses, actions,
and consequences; a systematic analysis of capability and social conduct over
complete games; and validation of the accounting and opportunity measures used
in that analysis. The behavioral findings remain to be established through the
exhaustive review. The environment's potential for reinforcement learning is a
research direction supported by existing components. Interrupted-run recovery
reconstructs the live engine through action replay and rebuilds prompt memory
from events. Composing these components supports exact decision-prefix reset
and alternative-action continuations. Checks at one mid-game decision in each
of the 20 games reproduce recorded prefixes, memory, and recorded continuation
events, and accept a different legal action that changes the continuation.
These are engine continuations with reference policies, not new LLM experiments.
One reference rollout reached its decision budget before bankruptcy; rollout
users therefore need an explicit truncation condition. A packaged branching
interface, a reinforcement learning adapter, and demonstrated training gains
require separate evidence.

The primary cohort comprises 20 completed games: five seeds, each played under
four cyclic rotations of one four-agent roster. The configured participants are
GPT-6.1 Sol, Gemini 4 Argon, Claude Opus 5.5, and Grok 4.7. All request the
provider-native medium reasoning setting; this does not equalize computational
budgets between providers. Each participant occupies each absolute seat once
within a seed block. Cyclic rotations preserve relative opponent order and do
not cover all possible orders. There is one recorded realization per
seed--rotation combination. All games end through bankruptcy without a turn
cap. Older saved games are a separate historical corpus and are not pooled into
the primary cohort's prevalence estimates.

The engine owns game state, enforces the declared rules, and exposes the available
actions at each decision. Agents buy and auction properties, negotiate transfers,
mortgage assets, develop complete color groups, pay obligations, and attempt to
remain solvent. The retained rules variant is explicitly documented. In
particular, creditor inheritance preserves development without transfer interest;
bank-returned deeds are not immediately auctioned; voluntary bankruptcy acts as
surrender; ordinary management occurs at the active player's post-turn stage
with jail restrictions; and scarce buildings do not trigger a competing-purchase
auction. A voluntary trade charges the recipient of each mortgaged deed interest
equal to ten percent of its mortgage value, rounded up. The
paper should describe these differences briefly rather than call the environment
rules-complete classic Monopoly.

At a decision, the agent receives public information about each player's cash,
position, jail and bankruptcy status, properties, mortgages, and development.
It also receives bank building inventory, unowned properties, and the current
decision context and legal options. The public memory contains at most 20
recent timeline entries, and private memory contains at most 10 recent notes.
Decision-specific context can contain further local history. These observations
are distinct from the analyst's full record and the engine's hidden random and
deck state. Timeline summaries can omit individual public utterances even before
the window fills. Analysts therefore distinguish canonical speech, its timeline
representation, earlier recipient exposure, and availability at the focal
decision. A message missing from the current window may still have influenced
intervening actions. Such absence does not erase an observable broken commitment,
but limits explanations based on current knowledge or forgetting.

The common initial instruction asks agents to maximize long-term advantage and
be the last non-bankrupt player. It explicitly permits deceptive public
strategies. The analysis therefore distinguishes economic effectiveness,
observable social conduct, and compliance with instructions that were actually
imposed. For example, a factual misrepresentation can be studied as
alignment-relevant conduct without being mislabeled a violation of an honesty
instruction absent from the experiment. Aggressive competition alone does not
establish harmful intent, collusion, or commitment violation.

Two response conditions affect the configured agents' comparability. Claude's
1,605 refused attempts state a provider Terms of Service restriction concerning
reverse engineering or duplicating model outputs. The triggering feature is
unidentified; recovery changes the prompt and tool schema together. These records
do not establish an ethical objection to the game or its permitted deception.
Refusal recovery removes the private-note requirement. In 1,394 of its 1,696
decision prompts, private memory was empty or contained only synthetic fallback
notes, although public history and current state remained available. Refusal
frequency changed across campaign dates and seed blocks under a constant initial
system-prompt hash. The configured Opus 4.8 model fallback was never invoked;
the participant's configuration label does not mean that this model played.
Sixty invalid action-only recovery
attempts proceeded directly to deterministic fallback, without a further
corrective retry. Grok uses required tool choice, and 139 applied decisions have
an action-name disagreement between response text and tool calls. Some occur
during legal building opportunities. These conditions affect the interpretation
of sustained capability as well as social conduct. The applied trajectory remains
authoritative; channel contradictions do not identify intent or the responsible
component. Detailed provenance, block-specific counts, and affected episodes
belong in the reproducibility supplement.

The saved evidence distinguishes four levels: applied actions and resulting game
events; public communications; generated private notes; and provider-returned
reasoning fields where available. Provider-labeled summaries remain summaries,
encrypted fields remain unreadable, and token counts do not imply access to
reasoning text. Returned explanations are evidence of what a response states,
not direct access to latent computation. Failed or rejected attempts remain
available but are not substituted for the applied action. The research package
preserves these distinctions at the decision and attempt levels.

The accounting reconstruction follows property ownership, cash, mortgages, and
development through canonical events. Purchases, auction acquisitions, trades,
and creditor inheritance are separate routes. A color-group episode begins
when one player owns all constituent properties and ends on ownership loss or
the end of observation; reacquisition begins another episode. A route label
describes the final acquisition event completing the group, with constituent
acquisition histories retained separately. Group completion is not itself a
measure of trade quality. Both parties' liquidity, compensation, opportunities,
and subsequent outcomes remain relevant. Groups created by the game-ending
bankruptcy have no deployment follow-up and are reported separately. Negotiation
roles distinguish the original thread initiator, author of the accepted terms,
and accepting player, since counteroffers can reverse the terms' authorship.

Development timing is reported in two ways. Elapsed delay measures time from
acquisition to management access or building. Conditional responsiveness asks
whether the player builds during the first actually offered management turn,
given the recorded legal options. Entry eligibility means at least one building
purchase in that group was legal, not that a full development plan was affordable
or optimal. Financing can create eligibility later in the same turn. Jail and
access choices remain part of the economic trajectory rather than disappearing
into a conditional rate. Missing opportunities and incomplete follow-up are
recorded explicitly.

Get-out-of-jail card transfers lack a dedicated transfer event. Their accounting
uses accepted trade payloads and bankruptcy context, checked against the saved
player observations. Candidate early-surrender cases require more than the
presence of a mortgage or sale option: listed financing may still be insufficient
to pay the debt. An upper bound on available cash is a screening condition;
legal financing sequences and strategic interpretations require case review.

Social coding records state-verifiable false statements, misleading implications,
contradictions with contemporaneous model-authored notes, and evidence of
recipient exposure or reliance separately. None automatically establishes intent.
Exposure is necessary for an influence claim, but an unexposed false statement
remains part of the speaker's conduct. Commitments retain their conditions,
attempted or completed status, and links across negotiation threads and turns.
Each rate reports the opportunities and evidence channels needed to detect it.
Missing private notes or provider reasoning cannot be coded as absence of a
behavior. Channel availability depends on response treatment and the observed
trajectory, so dividing by available channels does not recover comparable
underlying prevalence. We report public/state-observable conduct separately
from evidence-qualified subsets. Reviewers retain alternative explanations
and ordinary counterexamples. Conditional management rates likewise do not
replace assessment of the full trajectory, including financing, access, and
survival.

Validation separates accounting correctness, instrument behavior, and behavioral
interpretation. Independently specified scenarios test transfers, cash boundaries,
inheritance, loss and reacquisition, management access, and observation endings.
An independent event-based audit reproduces cash, ownership, mortgages,
development, and bank inventories at all 6,989 recorded decision observations
without discrepancies. This validates agreement between these recorded economic
representations; it does not establish strategic optimality or social labels.
Existing rule, replay, corruption, and recovery tests provide additional scoped
evidence. Recorded-action replay is distinct from a fresh stochastic provider run.

Outcomes and repeated decisions within the same game are dependent, and games
share five seed blocks. Raw dice pairs match over common within-block prefixes,
but a draw can serve different roles, such as movement, jail attempts, or utility rent, in
different trajectories. Shared draws therefore do not imply identical
opportunities or a causal decomposition of luck and skill. Full chronological
review, counterexamples, and declared denominators support descriptive findings;
stronger causal or general capability claims require a corresponding design.
