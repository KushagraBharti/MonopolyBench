MonopolyBench asks how agents sustain economic capability and social behavior
over extended competitive interactions, and how these relationships should be
evaluated as agent capabilities improve. An agent must assemble assets, preserve
liquidity, convert opportunities into income, negotiate with competitors, and
respond to changing incentives. The same trajectory also records assurances,
cooperation, misrepresentation, targeting, and departures from earlier plans or
commitments. These behaviors are joint subjects of the evaluation. The present
study compares configured agents in a fixed cohort. It does not identify a
capability-growth trend or establish that economic success measures alignment.

We evaluate alignment-relevant conduct against an explicit norm or instruction.
The retained instruction permits deceptive public strategies and asks for honest
private notes. State-verifiable false statements, misleading implications,
observable inconsistencies, commitment breaches, and evidence of recipient
reliance remain distinct dimensions. A false statement remains a finding about
speaker conduct when its recipient did not receive it. An observable breach
remains a breach when the earlier commitment is absent from the speaker's latest
prompt. Claims about deliberate deception, knowing breach, forgetting, or
successful influence require further evidence. Private notes and provider text
can corroborate or contradict an interpretation but do not reveal latent
computation. The study retains well-supported model-specific cases even when
other participants lack the same evidence channels.

Canonical public speech, its representation in shared timeline memory, and
delivery in a participant's actual prompt are separate records. In this cohort,
6,882 canonical public messages contain non-whitespace text. Of these, 94 do not
enter timeline memory because consecutive building summaries retain an earlier
nonempty message. Another 55 bankruptcy messages are consumed at an unsummarized
cash-transfer event before the bankruptcy marker. The remaining 6,733 enter
timeline memory; 6,720 have an observed subsequent delivery to at least one
opponent through that memory. These counts concern message occurrences, not
unique texts, social labels, or all possible routes of semantic exposure. The
instrument reproduces all 6,989 recorded initial memory windows. For an influence
or reliance claim, reviewers identify the relevant recipient, prompt, field,
and statement. They distinguish earlier delivery from availability at the later
action. They also check repeated statements, carried private notes, local
context, and intervening actions before inferring nonexposure or a mechanism.

The common public-action-state record supports comparisons of recorded conduct.
It does not supply identical strategic opportunities. Channel-qualified counts
use the evidence needed for each label and describe their selected populations.
Private-note availability varies with response recovery, seed block, and source
version, so channel-qualified rates do not recover comparable latent prevalence.
We retain raw counts, player-game trajectories, opportunity denominators, and
missing-evidence categories. Unknown cases are not negative labels. Where
eligibility itself is unobserved, even a numerical missing-case bound requires
additional assumptions and is not presented as an identified prevalence bound.

Economic capability is reported as a trajectory profile. It includes acquisition
and financing choices, elapsed access delay, deployment during actually offered
management, subsequent rents and liquidity, ownership loss, and terminal outcome.
Conditional deployment describes responsiveness among reached opportunities;
the full profile preserves player-games that never assemble a group, lack
access, or cease to be observed. A legal building purchase is an available
choice, not a certificate that building maximizes winning chances. Group episodes
created by terminal bankruptcy have no deployment follow-up. Previously developed
inherited groups differ from newly assembled groups, and final acquisition route
does not summarize the history of all constituent deeds. Realized receipts have
trajectory-dependent follow-up and do not estimate the causal value of trading.

Response handling can change the context in which later economic and social
decisions occur. The recorded provider block for Opus leads to a recovery
condition that removes the private-note requirement; later prompts often lack
model-authored notes, and invalid action-only recoveries can proceed directly
to fallback. This is an observed interaction between response handling and
long-horizon decision context. The provider states a Terms of Service reason
concerning reverse engineering or duplication of model outputs. The records do
not identify the triggering prompt feature or the effect of the changed context
on capability. Recovery changes instructions and tool schema together, and block
differences overlap with source and seed block. Grok's content/tool disagreements
likewise remain linked to their actual applied actions. Flagged and unflagged
episode descriptions are selected subsets, not corrected trajectories or bounds
on a hypothetical repaired agent.

Validation addresses separate claims. Event accounting checks whether transfers,
cash, ownership and development reconcile. Prompt and delivery audits check what
information the configured agent received. Behavioral review tests whether case
labels follow the evidence and their definitions. Agreement in one part does
not validate the others. The phase-two review covers all games, decisions and
attempts in chronological context, records unavailable evidence, and retains
ordinary decisions and counterexamples. Independent first-pass judgments precede
joint adjudication of strong labels. Four researchers jointly interpret and write
the economic and social findings. Reviewer agreement describes reproducibility
of the coding, not objective access to intent.

The environment already composes action-prefix replay, hidden engine state,
legal-action stepping, and event-reconstructed memory into reset and alternative
action continuations. Tests at 20 representative prefixes reproduce the recorded
prefix, memory and recorded next events, then execute a different legal action.
Reference-policy continuations reach bankruptcy in 19 cases; one reaches the
20,000-decision test budget. These results support implemented simulation and
branching affordances. A packaged reinforcement-learning adapter and training
benefits remain separate work. Future learning experiments must define agent
observations, actions, rewards, termination and truncation, opponent policies,
and training/evaluation separation. Analyst-only labels and hidden state must
not silently enter the acting policy's observations. Controlled LLM continuation
experiments additionally specify stochastic sampling and intervention conditions.
