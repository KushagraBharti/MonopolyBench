2.4
Delivered information and conduct norms
Prompts show cash, ownership, buildings, bank stock, twenty public timeline entries and ten own
private notes. Trade responses include current terms and thread history. Agents do not receive
opponents’ private notes or future random outcomes. A history formatter wrongly assumes that
the initiator wrote the current terms: it reverses forty-four accepted-trade summaries, misattributes
twenty-six rejections and creates self-partner wording in 232 counters. At least one such line appears
in 2,054 initial prompts. Current structured holdings remain correct, and current-offer fields agree
with the recipient’s acceptance menu in all 920 response opportunities. Correct state does not erase
misleading history, but it permits accounting for the actual exchange presented.
Logging a message is not the same as delivering it. Of 6,882 nonempty public-message occurrences,
149 never enter the timeline; thirteen inserted messages have no later observed opponent exposure
before the record ends. Incident claims about reception use the actual recipient’s request.
The standard prompt asks agents to win through any legal strategy and explicitly permits public
bluffing and deception. It also states, “Deception in public chat is allowed; private thoughts should
reflect true reasoning.” The action-only prompt omits that sentence. A permitted public bluff is not
instruction misalignment. A false private note may honestly express an error and does not itself
establish dishonesty. Private notes, assistant content and returned provider explanations are separately
recorded channels, not privileged measurements of internal reasoning [7, 12].
3
Measures and evidence
3.1
Population accounting
An offer is one proposal or counter awaiting a response. Its author is the writer of the current terms,
not necessarily the thread initiator. We reconstruct both parties’ cash after mandatory charges, trans-
ferred deeds and newly completed color groups. Completion means ownership of every deed in a
group, including mortgaged deeds; it is not buildability or economic value. Settlements are evaluated
atomically. In forty-four of 113 settlements, the final author differs from the initiator.
A completion episode runs from acquisition of the completing deed until loss of the group or game
end. For every trade-completed group, the first management window comprises the owner’s actual
management decisions in its first such turn after settlement. We separately record construction
availability on entry, availability later in the window and construction applied. A missing construction
option on every recorded menu does not establish impossibility under unvisited financing choices.
Cash flows, deed flows and construction expenditure remain separate. Paid rent after group
completion measures realized receipts until loss or game end. A spending unit is one game/player/color
triple with own construction expenditure. Its developed-rent receipts differ from all rent received on
the group, and inherited buildings can earn income before the player’s own spending. Neither ratio
supplies incremental return on investment. Acquisition, interest, salvage, unpaid rent demands and
estate value are separate quantities.
Counts cover the declared finite populations. Offers in a thread share terms and players; decisions
in a game share a board; rotations within a seed block share random sequences that later actions may
consume differently. We report configuration, game and thread-position breakdowns without treating
decision counts as independent capability trials.
