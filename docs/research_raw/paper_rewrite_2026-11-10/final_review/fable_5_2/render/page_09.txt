Transcript 2. Refusing one’s own complete group three times
At G15 turn 63, Sol has $730 and Grok $333. Sol offers its two greens for Grok’s Ventnor and $100.
Both would complete a group:
Sol · public message · G15 d168 · attempt 0 · full field
Grok: both my greens for Ventnor and $100. We each get a monopoly instead of letting the
developed oranges dictate the game.
Grok’s refusal focuses on the unequal cash left for construction:
Grok · applied private note · G15 d169 · attempt 0 · excerpt
Trade gives p4 Atlantic+Ventnor+Marvin and ~$830 plus mortgage capacity to drop 5+ yellow
houses ($150) immediately, then they roll again on doubles. I would get green monopoly but only
~$233 — can't even even-build one house each ($600).
The proposed balances, $830 for Sol and $233 for Grok, agree with the ledger. One house on each
green costs $600, as the note says. That is not the minimum legal construction: one green house costs
$200. The explanation concerns a full first layer and leaves other financing possibilities untested.
Sol removes its cash request and adds Kentucky, then makes a third offer:
Sol · public message · G15 d174 · attempt 0 · full field
Both greens, Kentucky, and $250 for Ventnor. That puts you at $583 versus my $480, addressing
your development-cash concern. We both get a way to compete with oranges.
Grok · public message · G15 d175 · attempt 0 · full field
No. $250 doesn't fix it. You'd still mortgage and drop houses on yellows before you roll again.
Ventnor stays mine.
What happened. No exchange settles. Eighteen turns later Grok is the first player eliminated, and its
yellow and green blockers pass to Gemini, which wins. Sol never completes either group.
What this shows. Both parties discuss relative ability to develop, and Sol’s later terms directly address
Grok’s stated cash objection. Grok still refuses its own completion. This is evidence of expressed
rival-sensitive bargaining, not proof of optimal refusal, accurate maximum-finance calculations or
hidden-belief inference.
G15 d168–175, standard first attempts. The quoted public messages are present in the counterparties’ next actual requests.
