Transcript 1. Pricing a rival’s completion, then buying the group instead
At G2 turn 45, Opus has two yellows and offers Sol $325 for Marvin Gardens. Sol asks $650:
Sol · public message · G2 d124 · attempt 0 · full field
Marvin completes your yellows. $650 buys it; $325 doesn't compensate for handing you that
monopoly.
Sol · applied private note · G2 d124 · attempt 0 · full field
At $325 p4 retains $520, enough for nine yellow houses immediately. $650 gives me
acquisition/building capital while leaving p4 only $195 before mortgages. Retain the blocker unless
paid a substantial monopoly premium.
The residual cash calculation is correct; the construction calculation is not. Nine yellow houses cost
$1,350, so $520 alone buys three. The note expressly considers a rival’s development but overstates its
cash-based scale. It does not establish the maximum available through collateral.
Opus eventually calls $450 final while its private note retains a $475 ceiling. Sol refuses. At turn 69,
the direction reverses: Sol has $435 and seeks Opus’s two yellows in return for two railroads. Opus
now has $17 and replies:
Opus · public message · G2 d197 · attempt 1 · full field
These two finish a monopoly for you, and that's worth more than the railroads. I'll do it if you add
$100.
What happened. Sol accepts the added $100 and completes yellow. Opus is eliminated three turns
later, owing Gemini $1,300, and its newly acquired railroads pass to Gemini. Sol spends $300 on
yellow houses, later receives $150 salvage, and collects no developed yellow rent. It does collect $44 of
undeveloped Atlantic rent at turn 77. Its later bankruptcy also transfers the yellows to Gemini, the
eventual winner.
What this shows. The parties explicitly negotiate over completion and building resources, then
exchange roles. The later trajectory connects acquisition, construction, liquidation and creditor receipt.
It does not establish a correct price, a bad trade or zero total return. The model’s erroneous nine-house
claim is preserved as evidence, not adopted as the analysis.
