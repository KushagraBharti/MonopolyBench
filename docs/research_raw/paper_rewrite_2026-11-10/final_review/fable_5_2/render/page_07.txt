Low acceptance does not establish absence of agreement bias. An agent could accept infrequently
yet concede on disadvantageous terms. The record provides neither an agreed utility function nor
a fair-price or optimal-policy reference. What it establishes is the distribution of received responses,
their economic consequences and their response conditions.
4.2
Counters reprice exchanges with and without completion
Counter contents show how disagreement changes a transaction. Of 111 counters to author-only
completion offers, ninety-two preserve the same original author’s newly complete group. Eighty-eight
still leave only that party completing. Eighty-five improve the counterer’s net cash, with a median
increase of $125. Within those eighty-eight, seventy-nine preserve the entire noncash bundle, including
deeds and jail cards, and all seventy-nine increase the counterer’s cash. These are cash repricings of
the same exchange; the other nine also alter noncash terms.
This preservation and repricing is also common without completion (Table 4). Among 110 counters
to non-completing offers whose authors request at least one deed, eighty-seven retain every requested
deed and eighty-one preserve the entire noncash bundle while increasing the counterer’s cash. These
records establish negotiation over terms in both populations. They do not establish a distinctive
response to completion or equivalence between the populations.
An exploratory comparison with board prices gives no consistent higher ratio for bids completing
the bidder’s group across the position and mortgage-status cuts. Seller asks are higher for recipient-
completing deeds in those cuts, but neither deed composition nor state is held fixed (Appendix A.1).
Board price is not a valuation standard.
Explicit explanations supply separate evidence that participants sometimes consider relative devel-
opment. Both LLM-assisted readings identify a stated trade-linked rival consequence in sixty-one of
seventy-four outright rejections that would complete a recipient group. Grok and Gemini each supply
thirty-one of the seventy-four records, Opus eight and Sol four; the agreed sixty-one are not itemized
by configuration. The population contains thirty-eight recipient-only and thirty-six both-completing
offers. A rival monopoly is therefore already an offered outcome in nearly half. The reasons also
concern building funds or survival. The coding is fallible, and thirty-four records come from two
games. It principally documents the two configurations producing most outright refusals, rather than
comparable attention across all four. The cases below show what such considerations mean, including
an arithmetic error inside an otherwise strategic explanation.
