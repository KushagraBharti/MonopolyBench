Table 3: All 920 responses, including two without a legal acceptance. No response is an engine default. Seven
of Grok’s acceptances, one counter and three rejections conflict with the action named in its content. Counts
describe received responses under these conditions.
Author-only completion offers are rejected in 159/289 cases (55.0%), compared with 38/140 (27.1%)
for recipient-only completion. Much of this pooled contrast changes when negotiation stage is separated.
Among opening offers, rejection counts are 138/187 (73.8%) versus 31/51 (60.8%); among later offers,
they are 21/102 (20.6%) versus 7/89 (7.9%). Author-only offers more often open a thread, where
rejection is common in both categories. The within-position differences are about thirteen percentage
points, compared with twenty-eight points pooled. These are conditional descriptions, not an identified
effect of completion.
The response contrast also varies by recipient. Sol, Gemini and Grok reject author-only offers
more often than recipient-only offers and accept them less often. Opus reverses both orderings,
rejecting 3/52 versus 6/42 and accepting 7/52 versus 5/42, while mostly countering both categories.
The pooled rejection contrast holds in all five seed blocks and seventeen of nineteen games containing
both categories; the acceptance contrast holds in four blocks and thirteen games. These checks do not
remove price, portfolio or response-condition differences.
Each configuration accepts twenty-six to thirty offers, but disagreement takes different forms
(Table 3). Sol and Opus mostly counter; Gemini and Grok mostly reject. Only thirty-two settlements
occur on an opening offer, with eighty-one following a counter. This is compatible with continued
bargaining in selected negotiable threads, not evidence that introducing a counter would improve an
otherwise identical negotiation.
Initiation differs more. Sol opens 132 threads, Gemini eighteen, Opus 240 and Grok 136. The
response condition changes the interpretation of the last count: sixty-four Grok threads begin with a
received proposal whose accompanying content names another action, and seventeen of those threads
settle. Seven of Grok’s twenty-eight acceptances similarly conflict with content; three complete Opus
groups. They remain actual proposals and settlements that other participants encounter. They are not
clean measures of Grok’s intention to negotiate or agree.
