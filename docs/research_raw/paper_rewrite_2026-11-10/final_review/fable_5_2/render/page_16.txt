6.2
Estates redistribute groups while the game continues
During continuing play, estates complete fifty-two groups, forty-five owned by eventual winners;
eighteen of sixty-three trade-completed groups belong to eventual winners. The contrast describes
concentration conditional on the same games’ outcomes. Across all stable acquisition boundaries there
are 209 episodes: sixteen through direct purchase, nine through auction, sixty-three through trade and
121 through inheritance. Sixty-nine inherited completions occur at the final elimination and provide
no further management opportunity (Table 5).
Trade transfers 207 deeds. Estates transfer 397, including 227 before the final elimination. Eventual
winners receive 202 of those 227 nonterminal deeds and are creditors in 35 of forty nonterminal
eliminations, compared with 54 of sixty overall. The eventual winner receives the first estate in
eighteen games and all three estates in sixteen. These figures show concentration under the creditor
rule. The creditor often already owns the developed property causing the debt, so they do not isolate
inheritance’s contribution to winning.
Routes also differ in income and timing. Fifty-one of sixty-three trade-completed groups collect rent,
totaling $57,378 after completion; directly purchased completions collect $23,070. Estate-completed
groups collect $11,472, with fourteen of fifty-two nonterminal episodes receiving any rent. Later
acquisition leaves less exposure, and rent on inherited buildings need not involve new spending. Of
fifty-eight winner spending units, thirty-four contain an inherited deed at first own expenditure, while
only thirty first become complete through an estate. Four mixed-route units close through a later
purchase or trade.
Two winners, Sol in G14 and Gemini in G15, settle no trade. Gemini’s G15 orange group completes
through a Tennessee purchase and receives its first build on turn 47. It later collects $3,250 in developed
orange rent. Oranges trigger the first two eliminations; inherited browns trigger the last. Greens arrive
only through the terminal estate. This is a concrete purchase-to-income-to-inheritance trajectory, not
evidence that avoiding trade is the best policy.
6.3
Negotiating over prospective estates
The creditor rule gives every player a stake in a distressed opponent’s fate. The next exchanges explicitly
refer to that stake. They also show why a later trajectory cannot establish the success or sincerity of a
