         1 Introduction
         Apropertythatcompletesarival’smonopolyisnotanordinaryiteminabilateralexchange. Sellingit
         mayfinancetheseller while giving the buyer a source of rent. Refusing may preserve a blocker while
         leaving both parties exposed to a third player. If either later becomes insolvent, the deed can pass to a
         creditor without another negotiation. An economic agent must therefore consider the exchange, the
         developmentitenablesandthepositionsthatpersistafterit.
           Monopolymakesthesedependenciesobservableoveracompletegame. Playersbuy,auctionand
         trade properties, build houses, mortgage collateral, pay rents and liquidate assets. In the implemen-
         tation studied here, a player-creditor inherits a bankrupt debtor’s properties with their buildings
         intact. The recipient of an estate can consequently matter to players who were not parties to the debt.
         This paper asks how LLM agents bargain over such dependencies, and what happens between an
         acquisition, an investment request and a realized economic outcome.

         Gemini, Opus and Grok. The corpus includes all 526 negotiation threads, 209 group-completion
         episodes and sixty eliminations. Legal menus, response attempts, delivered prompts and canonical
         events let us relate the terms of an exchange to the opportunities and actions that follow. The unit

              information. It is not an isolated language model.
                 Theresults follow an economic sequence. First, agents negotiate over completion and development
              capacity. Responses differ depending on whose group would complete, but the pattern varies across
              recipients and negotiation stages. Counteroffers often preserve and reprice exchanges, both with
              andwithoutcompletion. Explicit explanations supply evidence that some participants consider rival
              consequences; heterogeneous offer distributions alone cannot identify those preferences.
                 Second, ownership does not guarantee executed investment. Trade-completed groups encounter
             different cash and mortgage conditions, and submitted construction plans can fail despite requesting
              reachable allocations. The first-window record separates absent construction options, defaults, con-
              flicting received calls and valid ends. This distinction changes the interpretation of an unbuilt group
             withoutassigning a counterfactual value to the missed construction.
                 Third, liquidation and inheritance connect local choices to later positions. Most elimination debts
              exceedevenagenerousfinancingbound. Threeexceptionshavepaymentsequences,whileanother
              nominally wealthy position cannot liquidate enough because of the hotel rule. Eventual winners
              receive 202 of 227 deeds transferred through estates before the final elimination. Selected exchanges
              explicitly bargain over which rival might receive such assets.
                 Eight contextual incidents accompany these results. They include a refusal based on rival devel-
              opment,apublicbargaininglimitcontradictedbyaprivatecontingency,anomittedfinancingroute,
              an appeal to withhold a rival’s building cash, and withdrawal of a repeated offer after the seller’s
             deterioration. Correction followed by acceptance and a contradictory bankruptcy response provide
              contrasts. The task permitted public deception. These episodes can be economically and socially
              consequential without constituting instruction violations, and a settlement alone does not establish
              successful persuasion.
                 Prior work already studies sustained economic operation, state-grounded communication,
              Monopolybehaviorandcompletemodel-plus-executionconfigurations[1,5,8,13,15]. Ourcontri-
              bution is a connected observational account of bargaining, executable development, current-debt
              finance and creditor succession. Historical feasibility checks support specific interpretations of that
              record. They are not proposed as a generally validated auditing method. The paper places material
              information and response conditions before the results, credits their consequences, and distinguishes
              the broader research questions the corpus informs from those it cannot answer.

             2.4   Deliveredinformationandconductnorms
              Prompts show cash, ownership, buildings, bank stock, twenty public timeline entries and ten own
              private notes. Trade responses include current terms and thread history. Agents do not receive

              instruction misalignment. A false private note may honestly express an error and does not itself

              recorded channels, not privileged measurements of internal reasoning [7, 12].
              3   Measuresandevidence
              3.1  Populationaccounting
             Anoffer is one proposal or counter awaiting a response. Its author is the writer of the current terms,
              not necessarily the thread initiator. We reconstruct both parties’ cash after mandatory charges, trans-
              ferred deeds and newly completed color groups. Completion means ownership of every deed in a
              group, including mortgaged deeds; it is not buildability or economic value. Settlements are evaluated
              atomically. In forty-four of 113 settlements, the final author differs from the initiator.
                 Acompletion episode runs from acquisition of the completing deed until loss of the group or game
              end. For every trade-completed group, the first management window comprises the owner’s actual
              management decisions in its first such turn after settlement. We separately record construction
              availability on entry, availability later in the window and construction applied. A missing construction
              option on every recorded menu does not establish impossibility under unvisited financing choices.
                 Cash flows, deed flows and construction expenditure remain separate. Paid rent after group
              completionmeasuresrealizedreceiptsuntillossorgameend. Aspendingunitisonegame/player/color
              triple with own construction expenditure. Its developed-rent receipts differ from all rent received on
              the group, and inherited buildings can earn income before the player’s own spending. Neither ratio
              supplies incremental return on investment. Acquisition, interest, salvage, unpaid rent demands and
              estate value are separate quantities.
                 Countscoverthedeclaredfinite populations. Offers in a thread share terms and players; decisions
              in a game share a board; rotations within a seed block share random sequences that later actions may
              consumedifferently. We report configuration, game and thread-position breakdowns without treating
             decision counts as independent capability trials.

              8   Discussion
              8.1  Whattheparticipantsnegotiate
             The economic unit that makes these games interpretable is a sequence from acquisition through
              financing and execution to income or transfer. Trade completions are similar across configurations—
              seventeen, eighteen, fifteen and thirteen—while first-window construction differs sharply—sixteen,
              seventeen,sevenandthree. Therecorddistinguishesstatedreservechoices,unavailablecurrentoptions,
              failed encodings and conflicting received calls. Reporting these transitions alongside settlements
              explains what each configuration actually converts into development.
                 The recurring object of negotiation is a position that extends beyond the exchanged deed. Par-
              ticipants state concerns about a rival’s construction cash, a blocking property’s eventual owner and
             whetherpayingadistressedseller strengthens a third-party creditor. The complete offer census estab-
              lishes howfrequentlydifferentownershipconsequencesappearandhowrecipientsrespond. Counters
              preserve and reprice completing and non-completing exchanges. Contextual records identify the
              economicconsiderations agents expressly discuss.
                 These forms of evidence should reinforce one another without being collapsed. Cash direction
              alone is not a price premium, a group completion is not a gain in winning probability, and a reason
              about a rival can contain false arithmetic. G2 illustrates all three distinctions within one trajectory.
              G15 shows a recipient refusing its own completion after the proposer addresses a stated liquidity
              concern. The useful finding is the presence and content of such strategic considerations in observable
              bargaining, not a general verdict that these agents negotiate rationally.
                 Inheritance extends those considerations through time. A deed that is withheld, bought as a
              blocker or sold as insurance can later pass to a creditor. Eventual winners receive thirty-five of forty
              nonterminal estates, often after collecting rent on an already-developed group. The G13 and G18
              chronologies make creditor identity an explicit topic of negotiation. They do not identify how long a
              transaction preserved solvency or whether reneging improved a position. The distinction is between
              explaining the economic stakes and estimating a tactic’s effect.

                 8.2    Execution conditions belong in the behavioral result
                 Thesamerecordcontainsachievedtargets,statedreservechoicesandfailedconstruction requests. A
                 wincountoranunbuilt-groupcountalonecannotdistinguishthem. Reorderedwitnessesestablish
                 that all sixty-one defaulted Opus allocations were reachable at their historical states, while the original
                 encodings failed. Received Grok calls sometimes create negotiations, end construction or declare
                 bankruptcyagainstanotherchannel’srequestedaction. These are properties of the observed system,
                 withconcrete consequences for the game.
                     Calling the unit a complete configuration is necessary but does not remove confounding. Opus’s
                 provider recovery changes the prompt, retry budget and later memory. Grok’s channel conflict has
                 unresolved origin. Misleading histories can coexist with correct current holdings. Dropping affected
                 records wouldselect a different population rather than reconstruct ordinary play under a corrected
                 interface. We therefore retain actual transactions and carry their conditions into the relevant counts
                 andincidents.
                     Historical feasibility is most informative when the question is narrow. The fifty-six upper-bound
                 negatives settle own-asset payment at those debts. Three witnesses establish available payment routes.
                 Thehotellockseparatesreachablefinance from nominal assets. None settles whether prior spending
                 waswise,whetherpaymentwasoptimal,orwhichplayerwouldwinunderanotheraction. Thislocal
                 precision supports the behavioral account without making an unvalidated claim for a general auditor.

          11 Conclusion
          Thetwentygamesshowagentsbargainingoverrivaldevelopmentandprospectiveestates,followed
          by different paths from ownership to construction, debt payment and creditor transfer. Counters
          commonlypreserveandrepricetransactionswhetherornottheycompleteagroup. Recordeddevel-
          opmentdependsonbotheconomicaccessandresponseexecution. Mostcurrenteliminationdebts
          are unpayable, while distinct exceptions expose omitted collateral, a received-call conflict and a hotel
          lock. Contextual transcripts connect those mechanisms to blufÏng, correction, omission and changed
          assurances. Following the transactions through these stages explains behavior that deal counts and
          final outcomes alone leave unresolved. The result is a source-grounded account of strategic economic
          interaction in these configurations, with local evidence that remains distinct from causal persuasion,
          optimal investment and victory.
