\paragraph{OPF-03, finance\_inheritance.tex:16.}
G7 d339 is the reverse problem. Opus owes \$750, has \$390 and can mortgage Oriental for \$50. Its pinks hold four houses, four houses and a hotel, and the bank has no houses. The engine offers a building sale only on a group's highest-built deeds, and a hotel sale only when four bank houses can replace it. The liquidation menu therefore contains no sale action, only the Oriental mortgage and bankruptcy. The engine's plan validator checks evenness and bank stock only after a whole plan, so it would accept a sale that clears the group, but no offered action can submit one. The mortgage-only continuation reaches \$440 and automatic bankruptcy, despite \$1,310 in nominal resources. The lock is thus a property of the implemented liquidation menu. An earlier opportunity to free houses would concern another historical prefix, not an action available during this debt decision.
