# Independent all-four position

Written after reading the complete TeX manuscripts, included appendices, generated tables, transcript definitions and handoffs of Astra, Sol, Opus and Fable 5.2. I have not opened Astra's cross-review, comparison-in-progress, checks or render report. This is a fresh Sol review, not endorsement of my frozen draft. PDF extraction and contact-sheet production cover all 23, 25, 43 and 39 pages respectively; detailed visual inspection follows separately.

The best scientific identity is an observational account of economic interdependence in twenty games of four configured systems. Bargaining changes rivals' construction budgets and the estates their creditors may receive. Ownership, access, requested allocation, applied action and realized receipts are different measurements. Complete mechanical populations plus selected source-qualified incidents can establish that connected account. They cannot establish optimality, causal persuasion, learning, capability rank or an inheritance effect on winning.

Astra gives the most compact complete candidate. My draft preserves useful temporal and economic distinctions but repeats caveats and misses the new counter and conflict joins. Opus adds the strongest new narrative, the G2 reversal from refused completion to trade, construction, liquidation and inheritance. Its counteroffer contents improve the population account, but its language of pricing completion is stronger than the cash comparisons identify. Fable integrates cases effectively and adds thread-position, acquisition and conflict information. Several of its most memorable statements exceed its own denominators.

The main result should retain the 918-offer completion table, cash direction and the explicit configuration exception. Counters that retain a completing deed while asking for more cash establish continued bargaining over that bundle. They do not identify a completion premium, because ordinary counters also demand more and bundles can change. Explicit quoted reasons can establish that a speaker describes a completion premium without estimating one in the population.

Errors already visible without Astra's report:

- Opus's G2 quote says $520 buys nine yellow houses. At $150 per house it buys three. Preserve the quote and correct the analysis explicitly. The arithmetic error matters to claims of financial competence.
- Fable says completing deeds are released "mostly" by cash-poor holders, although 21/48 is below half. Its $100 threshold and selected settled population do not establish scarcity-caused release.
- Fable says no list price asks for a completion premium and that the systems price completion itself. Heterogeneous deed prices, bundle composition and cash direction cannot identify that premium.
- Fable's "protection bought lasted 43 turns" is a causal duration unsupported by an observed later bankruptcy. My own "payment purchases time" and Astra's similar formulation have the same problem.
- No construction offered in a recorded management window is not exhaustive impossibility under all financing sequences in that window. Seven mortgaged arrivals and two low cash balances describe settlement conditions, not a proof that acquisition terms explain each entire window.
- Opus calls an executable allocation "sound" and says one configuration lost most of its development. Feasibility and repeated defaults do not identify sound investment or a measured amount of foregone value.
- Opus says all text channels oppose bankruptcy at G12 although its public message announces bankruptcy. Its content and private note oppose the received declaration; the public message agrees with it.
- My draft's printed portable-artifact path needs checking; repository-relative dependencies must be resolved from the actual manuscript directory.

I propose eight main incidents, embedded in their economic sections: G2 price/refusal/reversal with its arithmetic correction; G15 four-player reciprocal completion refusal; G7 clean public/private bluff; G19 explicit correction with acceptance; G20 financial-denial appeal with contrary evidence; G12 payable bankruptcy conflicting with content and note; G13 omission/estate appeal with chronology and alternate reading; G18 withdrawal after seller deterioration. G11 belongs in an evidence appendix as a correction and two-player refusal control; G3 remains an appendix finance/stock control. G2 and G15 add different information, so neither should displace G19's explicit correction or G12's severe execution case. A shorter seven-case version can move G7 beside G2 as a compact appendix comparison, but should acknowledge losing the settled-bluff contrast.

The inheritance headline should be 52 nonterminal inherited completions versus 63 trade completions, with 69 terminal completions separated before displaying the total of 121. Deed-flow scale, 397 versus 207, answers another question and should not be equated with productive opportunities. Winner-conditioned 202/227 deeds and 35/40 estates describe concentration only.

Preserve the complete question map for Q1–Q8 plus A–C, but make it a scope table rather than eleven claimed contributions. Keep achieved targets and successful finance beside failures. Use a short related-work section with precise inspected-version claims; abstract-only checks cannot support implementation comparisons. Make LLM coding, family overlap, partial unblinding and inherited engine executions explicit.

Three likely rejection reasons remain even after a strong integration: a small confounded cohort with severe observation/recovery defects; an insufficiently distinct contribution relative to existing economic-agent studies; and retrospectively selected semantic evidence without an independent validity standard. Rewriting can clarify these limits and strengthen the finite-cohort findings, not eliminate them.
