# Opus initial all-four position

Written 2026-11-10, after reading all four complete drafts and before opening `reviews/astra/` (its all-four review, comparison, source checks and render review). I read every main section, appendix, generated table and quote file of Astra, Sol, Opus (my own) and Fable 5.2, plus each handoff, evidence map, figure note, literature note and initial position. Sol's `transcript_quotes.tex` and Astra's are byte-identical to the shared `sections/transcript_quotes.tex` (checked with `diff`). I ran four read-only scripts against retained ledgers, board data and canonical decision prompts (in `checks/`). No engine code, model call or game was run.

## The paper all four drafts are trying to write

Every draft lands on the same scientific object: four configured systems bargaining over color-group completion, turning completed groups into construction or failing to, financing or failing to finance debts, and passing estates intact to creditors. That convergence is real and useful. The drafts disagree on emphasis, on which new tabulations to trust, and on how hard to push interpretations. The disagreements below matter more than the shared skeleton.

## What survives, in order of strength

1. **Complete census facts with their conditions.** These are the 920/918 response census, 63 trade-completed groups and their first-window decomposition (43/9/4/4/3), the 663 END partition, 56/3/1 at elimination, and estate flows. These are mechanical and reproduced by several drafts.
2. **The joint-completion response table, with Fable's two stratifications.** These are thread position and recipient configuration. I reproduced Fable's position split: author-only versus recipient-only rejection is 138/187 versus 31/51 among first offers and 21/102 versus 7/89 among later offers. The raw 55% versus 27% contrast is partly composition. 65% of author-only offers are opening bids, against 36% of recipient-only offers, and opening bids are rejected more everywhere. The direction survives within position, and in Sol, Gemini and Grok as recipients. It fails for Opus, which rarely rejects. This is the honest headline. Astra's and Sol's pooled presentation, and my configuration-only presentation, are each incomplete.
3. **Execution, liquidity and inheritance as separate states.** Completion is not buildability, a listed tool is not an affordable target, nominal equity is not accessible liquidity, and terminal estates are not opportunities. All four drafts say this; it is the paper's most defensible economic contribution.
4. **Seven contextual incidents with exact channels.** G13, G18, G20, G19 and G12 are strong in every draft, with mostly correct chronology. G7 is the clean settled bluff.

## Claims I now think are wrong or overstated (checked, not inherited)

- **"The completing side pays" is not a completion premium (Fable; echoed by Astra, Sol and my own "although it pays the recipient \$200").** The completing side receives a deed in 429 of 429 one-sided offers, so cash direction is mostly the price of the deed. Against board list price, pure cash bids for a deed that completes the bidder's own group run at a median 1.32× list (n=134). Bids in non-completing purchases run at 1.38× (n=178). Sellers' asks for a deed that completes the buyer's group run at 1.63× list (n=93), against 1.08× for non-completing sales (n=117). So sellers mark up completing deeds and buyers do not bid more for them. Fable's "the systems price the completion itself, on both sides" is not supported. "Recipients do not respond to incoming cash alone" (Astra) and "although the median offer pays the recipient \$200" (Sol, mine) frame the deed's price as if it were a bonus. List price ignores mortgages and composition, so even the seller-side markup is descriptive, not an identified premium.
- **"Nine could not legally be built on … properties of the purchase" (Fable) is wrong as stated.** I computed this from the canonical first-window prompts and the engine's buildable and redemption rules (arithmetic, not an executed witness). Two windows had cash to redeem the mortgaged group deed and still buy a house: G14 Grok had \$602 against \$193 redemption plus a \$200 house, and G16 Grok had \$523 against \$111 plus \$100. Seven of nine reach redemption plus one house if the owner also mortgages its own deeds outside the group. Only G15 Opus and G20 Opus could not. Grok's own notes defer redemption. "Never legal in the window" is an observed-menu fact that includes owner choices not to redeem. It is not an inability and not a property of the purchase. Astra's abstract phrase "nine never become buildable" drops "in that window", and 11 of the 20 unbuilt groups are built later.
- **"Opus lost most of the value to the refusal/retry/format interaction" is false (Fable intro, development and conclusion; MY OWN abstract, introduction and conclusion make the same error).** Opus's 8 unbuilt first windows are 5 never-legal and 3 defaults (`first_management_windows.json`). Opus builds on 12 of 15 groups eventually. The 61 Opus defaults are real and the reordering result stands, but 55 of 66 defaults fall in G5 and G13. They do not account for most of Opus's first-window shortfall, and the rent gap cannot be assigned to them.
- **"Estates complete 121 groups against 63 by trade" / "Bankruptcy moves more property than trade" (Fable abstract, intro and heading).** Sixty-nine of the 121 arrive with the final elimination. During play the figure is 52 against 63. Fable's own body and conclusion say this, so the abstract contradicts the body. The headline population for inheritance should be nonterminal: 52 estate completions (45 owned by the eventual winner, 14 collect any rent, \$11,472), 35 of 40 nonterminal estates, and 202 of 227 deeds.
- **"Completing deeds change hands mainly when their holder is short of cash" (Fable).** I reproduced 21 of 48, which is not "mainly". Opus is the seller in 21 of the 48 completing sales and in 13 of the 21 cash-poor ones. This is a configuration-composition fact as much as a distress fact. The \$194 versus \$571 median contrast is real and worth one sentence.
- **Duration of purchased survival.** Fable writes "the protection bought lasted 43 turns". Sol says "purchases time" and I wrote "bought time". Grok could have mortgaged Ventnor without the sale, and the next roll was uncertain. The record shows a later Short Line bankruptcy, not a purchased survival duration. Astra's phrasing ("bought immediate liquidity without preventing the later event") is the right model.
- **Selection presented as a finding (MY OWN draft).** My abstract, my discussion paragraph "Conduct audits can start at pressure points" and my conclusion say the sharpest conduct in the selected transcripts appears near solvency and inheritance. That is true of a selection I chose partly for stakes. It is not a finding, and it should be cut.

## Other errors in my own draft

- Abstract and Q1: "recipients refuse or **price** a rival's completion" and "three configurations show the acceptance gradient". Counters raise the counterer's cash in every cell (135 of 164 in "neither"). The acceptance contrasts per configuration rest on 2/83 versus 6/30, 4/76 versus 6/31 and 6/78 versus 7/37. Fable's rejection-by-recipient and thread-position splits are the sturdier version. The defensible counter finding is narrower. Among the 111 counters to author-only offers, 92 keep the author's completion, and 85 of the 88 that give the counterer nothing ask for more cash (reproduced). Recipients unwilling to transfer the deed mostly reject rather than counter, so this is conditional on countering.
- G2 vignette: Sol's quoted note says \$520 is "enough for nine yellow houses immediately". At \$150 per house \$520 buys three. Even mortgaging every other Opus deed reaches six. My draft quoted it and did not flag the arithmetic, which the contract requires.
- G20 d97: I wrote that Grok's text described building. The retained conflict record names `mortgage_property` in content and `end_turn` received. Fable is correct.
- "In 15 the arguments are identical": fifteen parsed argument objects agree, and only five literal substrings do (Astra has this right).
- "Three eliminated players could legally have paid … only through building sales that the displayed mortgage options did not reveal", and the discussion line "displayed options understated finance". Building sales are on the menu. G12 d249's content even requests one. The correct statement is narrower: the mortgage options listed on the liquidation menu fall short in all three, and payment requires sales and, for two, the collateral those sales unlock.
- "Low acceptance … reflects, at least in part, recipients pricing a rival's gain" is causal. It should go.
- Length: 9,500 prose words, 43 pages and eight main incidents. That is over target.

## Recommendations for the integrated paper

- **Architecture:** Sol's order and prose discipline (conditions, then methods, bargaining, execution and finance, inheritance, conduct, discussion and limits), with incidents placed where they illustrate (Fable and mine) except for a compact conduct section holding the distress and inheritance cases.
- **Bargaining:** the joint table and the position split in the main text. Add one per-recipient rejection line showing the Opus exception, plus my counter-content table with its conditional reading and a list-price note that replaces every "despite \$200" sentence. Use Fable's join of Grok's conflicting calls to qualify initiation counts: 64 of Grok's 136 threads open from a call whose content named another action.
- **Execution:** the first-window table with the never-legal row explained as observed-menu inaccessibility that includes declined redemption (2 cash-feasible, 7 of 9 own-finance-feasible, arithmetic). Keep the 61/61 reachable multisets, without "lost most of the value".
- **Inheritance:** nonterminal population in the headline. State the winner conditioning once.
- **Incidents (7 main):** G15 swap refusal (if delivery and quotes check out; it is more informative than G11), G7 bluff, G19 correction, G20 appeal, G13 insurance, G18 withdrawal, G12 conflicting bankruptcy. G11, G2 (with the arithmetic flag), G3, G4, G6 and G20 d1 go to an evidence appendix.
- **Seed blocks for question C:** Fable's block-3 and block-4 observations (same early dark-blue/orange seat wins under every configuration; block 2 Sol wins from every seat). They are good descriptive material for C, worded without "the dice decided".
