## 2. What survives the observational design

I rank the positive findings by how little they depend on a counterfactual.

**First: execution decides what a negotiated asset is worth, and the record shows the mechanism decision by decision.** The four systems complete 17, 18, 15 and 13 groups by trade. They build in the first management window on 16, 17, 7 and 3. Groups built in the first window later collect \$53,810 of the \$57,378 paid on trade-completed groups (my re-tabulation confirms all of these). For the 66 defaults the causal path inside each decision is recorded: a build plan was submitted, rejected for an uneven intermediate step, and replaced by an engine END because no retry remained. That is a documented mechanism, which is rare in this corpus. The inherited reorder check says 63 of 70 targets were reachable. A reader learns something usable: a deal count and a win count would both misdescribe these systems, and the retry budget is part of the agent.

**Second: almost no one quits with a way to pay.** Fifty-six of sixty eliminations fall below a bound that needs no engine to check. Three were payable and one was stock-locked. The two payable declarations with notes share one specific omission, the mortgages that open up after buildings are sold. I read the delivered menus for all four: the sale action was on the menu in G12 d190 and G17 d234, and both notes count salvage. What they leave out is \$340 and \$375 of collateral that appears only after the sales.

**Third: responses differ by whose group an offer completes, for three of four systems.** This is real and it is weaker than three drafts make it look. See section 3.

**Fourth: estates follow rent.** The eventual winner is the creditor in 35 of 40 eliminations before the last. Estates complete 52 groups during play, 45 of them for the eventual winner, and those groups collect \$11,472 of \$95,278 paid on complete groups. Inheritance in this cohort consolidates a lead that trade- and purchase-completed groups built. That is a more accurate and more interesting sentence than "bankruptcy moves more property than trade", which is my own draft's heading.

**Fifth: seven games follow a seat, four follow a system.** In block 3, seat 2 buys both dark blues from the bank by turn 29 in all four rotations and four different systems win from it. In block 4, seat 1 completes orange by purchase on turn 47 in three rotations and wins all three. In block 2 Sol wins from every seat. I re-derived this from the episode ledger. It is the closest thing the cohort has to a designed contrast and it belongs in the main text. Astra and Opus report the outcome pattern without the mechanism. Sol omits it.

## 3. The bargaining result needs three corrections that no single draft has

**(a) Thread position carries about half of the headline contrast.** 187 of 289 author-only offers are opening offers. 89 of 140 recipient-only offers are later offers. Within position, rejection is 138/187 against 31/51 for first offers and 21/102 against 7/89 for later offers. The gap is about 13 points in each stratum against 28 pooled. Only my draft reports this. Astra, Sol and Opus lead with 55 percent against 27 percent and never mention position. An integrated paper that prints the pooled contrast without the split is printing a composition effect.

**(b) Opus's "the completing deed stays on the table" is not specific to completion.** I rebuilt the counter join. Opus's numbers reproduce exactly: 92 of 111, 85 of 88, median +\$125. Then I asked the same question where no completion is involved. Among counters to offers that asked for a deed, the counter still hands over every requested deed in 92 of 111 author-only cases and in 87 of 110 neither-cell cases. That is 83 percent against 79 percent. Deed-keeping counters raise cash in 87 of 92 and 81 of 87, at medians of +\$125 and +\$100. Counters in this corpus keep the deed and move the price whatever the deed does. Opus's own text half-concedes this for the cash premium and then claims specificity for the deed. The data do not support that step.

**(c) My "the completing side pays" is the buyer paying.** In the neither cell, the side that receives more deeds is the side paying cash in 309 of 313 offers. My 373 of 429 is a lower share than that baseline. The sentence "the systems price the completion itself" does not follow from cash direction. Two narrower statements do survive. In swaps of equal deed counts, the completing side adds cash in 94 of 116 one-sided offers, where non-completing equal swaps split 44 to 25. And in one-deed-for-cash offers on unmortgaged color deeds, buyers bid a median 1.59 times list for a completing deed against 1.41 for a non-completing one, and sellers ask 2.32 against 1.79. Accepted offers are too few to say anything (6, 12, 4 and 6 per cell, and one pair reverses). These are offered terms on unmatched deeds. No premium is identified.

**(d) The stated reasons come from two systems.** Of the 74 rejections, Grok and Gemini supply 62. Opus supplies 8 and Sol 4. Of the first reader's 63 rival-consequence codes, 35 sit in the both-completing cell, where the rival's completion is a ledger fact. The coded evidence shows that Grok and Gemini write about rival consequences when they refuse. It says little about Sol and nothing about Opus.

What I would print: the cell table, the position split directly under it, the per-recipient row with Opus as the exception, one sentence on counters as repricing in every cell, the thread-outcome fact (24 of 26 settled initiator-completing threads end with the initiator completing after a median \$97.50 raise), and the two-system scope of the reasons. No uniform rival effect, no premium, no agreement-bias verdict.

## 4. "Never legal" is not "could not"

Every draft treats the nine trade-completed groups with no construction menu in the first window as lack of access. Mine goes furthest: "nine could not legally be built on", "properties of the purchase", "not decisions to wait". I read the delivered menus and applied notes for all nine.

- Four are Grok's. Redemption of the mortgaged group deed is on the menu in all four. Each applied note gives a reserve argument for not redeeming or building. In two (G14 d207 with \$602, G16 d234 with \$523) cash alone covers redemption plus one house.
- Five are Opus's, all action-only with no note. Cash is \$7, \$31, \$158, \$268 and \$125. In one (G16 d248) Opus redeems the deed and stops at \$75. Redemption is not on the menu in three. One group is unmortgaged with cash below one house.

So four of the nine are stated holds with the enabling action on the menu, which is the same evidential level as the two "stated holds" every draft already reports. Five are low-cash windows with no text. My draft's "17 of 20 trace to the acquisition terms or the response path" is wrong, and so is the shared phrase "never buildable". The honest partition of the twenty is: 4 with redemption offered and a stated reserve rationale, 5 low-cash windows without notes, 4 defaults, 4 received-call conflicts, 3 valid END with construction listed. None of this is a financing impossibility result. I did arithmetic on cash and collateral and did not run the engine.

## 8. Three reasons the best integrated paper could still be rejected

1. Nothing is identified. The central bargaining contrast shrinks by half under one stratification, holds for three of four systems, and has no valuation behind it.
2. The harness was defective for one system in 95 percent of decisions and for all systems in 29 percent of prompts. A reviewer can say the execution finding is a bug report and ask for the rerun that is out of scope.
3. Seven of twenty games follow a seat. The paper's own best evidence on skill and luck says outcomes in this cohort are dominated by early dice, which undercuts any system-level reading of the rest, and the semantic layer was coded and selected by models that overlap with the players.

I do not think any of the three can be written away.
