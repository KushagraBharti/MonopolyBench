The paper's scientific object is interdependent economic decision-making. In Monopoly, helping another player survive or build can protect oneself from a third player's acquisition. An asset's value consequently depends on financing, who receives its future estate, and when an action is executable. This links the bargaining census, construction and liquidation results, inheritance flows, and contextual transcripts into one subject.

My independent draft will lead with that interdependence rather than a winner leaderboard or general auditing method. I will report observed offer-response differences positively while keeping offer selection and configuration composition explicit. The conduct section will preserve six or seven distinct incidents and the later consequences that make them economic interactions rather than isolated quotations.
