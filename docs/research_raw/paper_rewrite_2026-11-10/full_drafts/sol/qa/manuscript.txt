Bargaining for productive assets:
Economic agency in twenty Monopoly games
MonopolyBench project
Independent complete draft by Sol
10 November 2026
Abstract
Economic agents must negotiate over assets whose value depends on rivals, execute development
under resource constraints, and remain solvent long enough to benefit. We study these connected
behaviors in twenty complete four-player Monopoly games involving four configured LLM systems.
The record contains 526 negotiation threads, 920 responses and 113 settlements. Responses differ
with the development opportunities exchanged: among acceptance-legal offers completing only the
current author’s color group, 159 of 289 are rejected; among those completing only the recipient’s
group, 38 of 140 are rejected. Cash terms also differ, and these observational comparisons do not
identify a rival-completion effect. Recorded explanations and contextual exchanges document ex-
plicit attention to financing rivals, keeping blockers and denying creditor inheritance. Development
then depends on access and execution: 43 of 63 trade-completed groups are built in their first man-
agement window, while the remainder include unavailable construction, defaults and conflicting
response channels. Existing legal-sequence evidence distinguishes three currently payable elimi-
nations from 56 debts beyond a generous own-asset bound and one stock-locked estate. Eventual
winners receive 202 of 227 nonterminal inherited deeds. Seven contextual incidents show how these
economic dependencies enter bluffing, solvency appeals, withdrawal and factual correction. We
characterize the historical systems, including substantial observation and refusal-recovery defects.
Provenance and replay support the behavioral conclusions; the study neither validates a general
auditing method nor ranks underlying models.
1
Introduction
An economic agent’s acquisition is valuable only through what it can subsequently do with it. In
Monopoly, completing a color group permits construction but does not supply the construction budget.
Paying a rival for the missing deed can finance that rival’s next development. Keeping a blocker
can prevent a monopoly, yet bankruptcy can deliver the blocker to exactly the player it was meant
to constrain. These dependencies make negotiation, investment and solvency parts of one strategic
problem. They also give communication concrete content. An appeal to keep a weak player alive may
concern the productive estate a stronger creditor would otherwise inherit.
We examine this problem through twenty completed games by four configured LLM systems.
Our central question is how their bargaining conduct connects to executable economic decisions and
the subsequent distribution of assets. This is an observational behavioral study of a fixed historical
cohort. It brings complete negotiation accounting together with development opportunities, financing
evidence and contextual exchanges. The purpose is to explain recorded conduct at the level the
evidence supports, rather than use Monopoly as a single score for intelligence.
1


The negotiations are consequential and differentiated. The agents do not merely exchange deeds
at bank prices. They counter, retain blockers, discuss opponents’ building cash and sometimes buy
time for an endangered player. Among 918 offers with legal acceptance, responses differ according to
which party would gain a new color group. Offers that complete only their current author’s group are
rejected more often than offers completing only the recipient’s group. The latter also ask the recipient
for more cash on median. This combination is informative about the contested terms of development,
although it cannot identify the effect of changing a rival’s benefit while holding the rest of a trade fixed.
Source-qualified reasons and exchanges provide the corresponding evidence that rival consequences
enter some agents’ expressed deliberation.
Acquiring a group is only the next stage. Some owners build immediately, some cannot yet build,
and some submit construction targets that fail at execution. All 61 defaulted Opus purchase multisets in
the retained audit are feasible after legally reordering atomic purchases, but invalid in their submitted
encoding. Those failures remain failures of the configured system. Their economic interpretation
differs from a decision to preserve cash. Likewise, nominal property wealth sometimes exceeds legally
accessible liquidity. The elimination census contains both feasible debt payments that were not made
and a hotel-constrained estate whose nominal value exaggerates its accessible finance.
The final stage is redistribution. Creditor inheritance transfers 397 deeds over the cohort, including
repeated transfers of the same property. Eventual winners receive 202 of 227 deeds transferred before
the final elimination. Under the implemented rule, buildings may survive creditor transfer intact. This
connects a debtor’s development to another player’s productive portfolio. Our transcripts show agents
discussing precisely that dependency. These are realized trajectories, with movement, prior decisions
and rule choices jointly determining who becomes a creditor.
Recent studies already examine negotiation tactics, persistent economic agents and Monopoly
behavior [1, 3, 4, 9]. Our contribution is a connected account of bargaining for productive assets in
this cohort, supported by complete mechanical denominators and selected chronological incidents.
Attempt provenance, delivered observations and previously executed legal witnesses support that
account. We do not offer those tools as an independently validated general annotation method. The
evidence includes substantial configuration and information defects, described before the results
because they are conditions of the behavior being studied.

Environment, cohort and response conditions
2.1
An economic game with explicit action access
MonopolyBench implements a forty-space United States board. Four players begin with $1,500 each,
receive $200 for passing Go, acquire deeds and attempt to become the last nonbankrupt player. The
bank begins with 32 houses and 12 hotels. A player declining a direct purchase starts an ascending
auction with sequential bidding and withdrawal. A structured trade can exchange cash, deeds and jail
cards; threads permit counters up to a twenty-exchange limit. No deed in a developed color group
may be traded. These rules connect bargaining to development rather than allowing arbitrary disposal
of a developed portfolio.
The deterministic engine alone updates state, emits events and supplies legal actions. The orchestra-
tion layer constructs requests, validates responses and resolves failed attempts. The interface renders
server records. At each decision, the agent receives public cash and holdings, current legal choices,
public history and bounded private memory. Configured model names are visible. Opponents’ private
notes and future random and card state are hidden. Public memory contains twenty timeline entries,
while private memory contains ten own notes. Trade responses also include structured negotiation

Several rules materially shape the findings. Remaining in jail withholds that turn’s management
window. Mortgaging a developed color group requires removal of its buildings. A hotel downgrade
requires houses available in the bank. Build-plan validation checks evenness after each entry; building-
sale validation instead checks final evenness and net stock, so a legal bundled sale can differ from a
sequence of individually legal sales. Mortgaged deeds can be traded with a ten-percent mortgage-
transfer charge rounded upward. Liquidation allows own sales and mortgages but no new trade.
The interface permits bankruptcy declaration even if another own-action sequence could pay. Four
bankruptcies occur automatically after a final mortgage.
Player-creditors can inherit developed buildings intact, while bank-returned deeds are not immedi-
ately auctioned. We interpret economic behavior under these implemented rules. They differ from
other Monopoly variants and affect both collateral access and the value of keeping a rival solvent. The
preserved implementation also has a floating-point redemption overcharge, sometimes charging $111
on a $100 mortgage instead of $110. Reproducibility under that implementation does not establish
conformity with intended rules. This rewrite performs no boundary experiment or outcome sensitivity
test for that defect.
2.2
The twenty games
The cohort has five seeds and four cyclic seat rotations per seed. Each configured participant occupies
every absolute seat five times. All twenty games terminate through bankruptcy without a turn cap.
There are eighty player-games, 6,989 decisions, 8,679 attempts and 50,918 events. Decision indices
in the text are zero-based; G13 d252 denotes decision 252 of game 13. Multiple decisions may occur
during one global turn.
Cyclic rotations balance absolute seats but preserve relative order within a block and cover four of
the 24 labeled seating permutations. Every configuration faces the same three opponent configurations.
Shared seeds cease to be matched movement histories when actions change the use of future draws.
Two saved treatment fingerprints separate G1–G4 from G5–G20. Earlier source inspection found
application and concurrency changes without changes to the engine, prompt, resolver or model-client
files in that diff; this is inherited source evidence rather than an identified treatment effect. Win counts,
retained as cohort context, are Sol eight, Gemini seven, Opus three and Grok two.
2.3
Information and response conditions precede interpretation
All LLM requests use OpenRouter. Ordinary invalid outputs receive a configured corrective retry and
then deterministic fallback if needed. Grok additionally uses required tool choice. Opus’s refusal path
requests an action without a private note and consumes its remaining retry opportunity. Consequently,
an invalid construction on that second request cannot receive another ordinary correction. Its config-
3


ured fallback model is never used. No temperature or nucleus parameter is supplied. Provider token
semantics differ; neither a common effort label nor reported completion length represents a matched
resource budget.
Opus has 1,605 refused attempts affecting 1,605 of 1,696 decisions. The returned message cites
Terms of Service concerning reverse engineering or duplicating outputs. That is the provider’s stated
reason. The upstream trigger remains unresolved, and the changed refusal frequency across the source
boundary does not establish a provider-policy change. The action-only recovery alters the prompt,
schema, note field and later memory together. In 1,394 Opus decisions, the private-memory window
has no model-authored note; 286 of those contain only synthetic fallback notes, while 1,108 are empty.
These are central properties of the evaluated configuration.
The historical trade-history formatter also confuses the thread initiator with the current offer author.
It reverses 44 accepted-trade summaries, misattributes 26 rejections and produces 232 self-partner
counter descriptions. At least one known manifestation appears in 2,054 initial prompts. Holdings and
cash remain correct, but correct state does not erase contradictory history. We treat these records as
behavior under inconsistent information. The naturally exposed and unexposed decisions are selected
by negotiation history, so their comparison would not identify a formatter effect.
Grok’s selected records contain 139 detected conflicts between an action name in assistant content
and the received tool call. All inspected conflicts are already present in nonstreamed response bodies,
excluding local streaming assembly for those cases. Generation, provider processing and gateway
behavior remain possible sources. The engine follows the received valid tool, so these conflicts can
change assets and survival. Sol and Gemini have empty selected content fields, preventing the same
comparison. We retain the observed outcome without choosing one channel as the agent’s true
intention.
Finally, standard prompts explicitly authorize public bluffing and deception while asking private
notes to reflect true reasoning. Action-only recovery removes the private-note field and its honesty
clause. Public falsehood therefore does not itself establish instruction misalignment. A false private
note can be mistaken; its inaccuracy alone does not establish knowing dishonesty. This normative
condition applies to every incident below.
3
Methods and evidential support
3.1
Units, reconstruction and economic endpoints
We use the twenty-game cohort as the population of description. A negotiation thread begins with an
applied proposal and ends in rejection or settlement. Each counter creates a new response opportunity.
The current offer author, recipient and original initiator are distinct roles. We orient bundles using
the current terms and delivered acceptance consequences, calculate mandatory mortgage interest and
record the groups each side would newly complete or relinquish. Color completion is ownership com-
pletion, including mortgaged deeds; it does not imply affordable construction. Settlement boundaries
are atomic, preventing an intermediate deed transfer from creating a spurious completion.
A completed-group episode records acquisition of a final color deed. Its first management win-
dow begins at the owner’s first actual optional-management decision after settlement and includes
subsequent management decisions in that global turn. Construction legal on entry differs from con-
struction becoming legal after financing within the window. No opportunity is imputed after terminal
bankruptcy. A spending unit is a game, player and color group with positive own house or hotel
expenditure. Including hotels captures owners who inherit four-house deeds and only buy conversions.
4


For a spending unit we report cumulative own construction expenditure and actual paid developed
rent, with both whole-ownership and post-first-own-spending totals. We keep acquisition prices,
mortgage cash, redemption, building salvage and estates separate. A terminal unpaid demand is
economically consequential but is not a paid receipt. These accounts do not measure incremental
return on investment, since inherited buildings, later spending and remaining exposure differ across
units.
Previously executed local checks restore a historical prefix and restrict alternatives to a named actor,
admissible actions and endpoint. The financing endpoint is payment of the existing debt using own
sales and mortgages, without assistance, new trades or future random movement. A generous bound
is
U = C + S + M,
(1)
where C is cash, S is full nominal building salvage, and M is mortgage capacity on every currently
clear deed, including collateral unlocked after buildings are removed. Ignoring stock and even-selling
restrictions enlarges the budget. Thus U < D rules out self-payment of debt D; U ≥D does not prove
accessible liquidity. A legal witness establishes one payable sequence. Neither endpoint establishes
future survival or justifies the earlier decisions that produced distress.
3.2
Checked evidence and inherited validation
The retained study reports event reductions matching all 6,989 initial decision states and 10,235 saved
snapshots, accepted-transfer checks for all 113 settlements, memory-window reconstruction, and
replay of all twenty action streams. A portable audit reports 60 elimination and 66 default-construction
prefixes. Its construction intervention splits count-valued entries into atomic purchases and legally
reorders them while preserving each deed/kind count, cash and stock. These historical results are
supporting evidence, not new evaluations in this rewrite.
We independently re-tabulate retained offer, spending, elimination and first-management ledgers
for this draft. We also reload original fields for every retained quote, check file and field hashes and
offsets, and preserve the generated quote definitions unchanged. These checks establish consistency
and excerpt fidelity. We do not rerun the engine, legal witnesses, regression tests, reference policies
or games. The evidence map distinguishes new consistency work from earlier source checking and
engine execution. A clean document build is a separate check.
The older negative-search checker required correction when review found a legal bundled sale
missed by atomic enumeration. Its restricted negative graph result is retained only for a case with no
sale action available. This is why we distinguish the 56 budget-bound cases, three positive payment
witnesses and one restricted stock-lock result. Replaying events establishes implementation consistency;
it cannot independently prove all rules correct or establish a general verifier’s semantic accuracy.
3.3
Text, chronology and research assistance

4
Bargaining over rival development
4.1
A complete response population
The cohort contains 526 threads with 920 responses: 113 acceptances, 394 counters and 413 rejections.
Acceptance is legal in 918 responses; the other two would leave a party cash-negative after mandatory
charges. Every response resolves without deterministic default, although 168 use a retry. No thread
spans a new global turn. The median thread has one response, the longest contains eleven counters,
and 414 threads follow an earlier negotiation between the same pair. These are repeated encounters
within evolving games.
The contrast in Table 2 is economically substantive. Author-only completion accompanies 55.0%
rejection and 6.6% acceptance, despite a median $200 transfer toward the recipient. Recipient-only
completion accompanies 27.1% rejection and 17.1% acceptance, with a median $350 transfer away
from the recipient. The latter offers most often induce counters. Both sides completing a group is also
contested, with 35 counters and 36 rejections in 81 opportunities. The pattern describes bargaining
over development and cash together. It cannot separate cash, portfolio composition, timing, mortgages
and rival benefit into independent effects.
The text supports a narrower positive interpretation of that pattern. In the 74 recipient-completion
rejections, the shared semantic codes identify explicit attention to the other side’s consequences in
61 records. Some explanations concern funding rival construction or surrendering a blocking deed.
Others misstate the actor’s trading role, including G15 d106 and G17 d186, and are flagged rather
than interpreted as coherent prices. The combination of complete consequences and source-qualified

4.2
Initiating, countering and accepting are different behaviors
Sol initiates 132 threads, Opus 240, Gemini eighteen and Grok 136. Their shares of eligible own turns
containing proposals are 10.0%, 24.2%, 2.1% and 15.7%, respectively. Counting turns avoids treating
repeated management menus as new access. Gemini’s eleven settled initial threads out of eighteen
describe selective initiation in this cohort; Opus’s forty-four out of 240 describe much more frequent
proposing with lower conditional settlement. They do not establish an ordering of deal quality.
Responder styles differ too. Across all 920 responses, Sol accepts 30, counters 140 and rejects 65;
Opus records 29, 141 and nineteen; Gemini records 26, 36 and 180; Grok records 28, 77 and 149. Opus’s
162 action-only responses out of 189 limit comparison of private rationales. These configurations
encounter different offers because their own and others’ prior decisions differ. The response counts
characterize the encountered negotiations rather than agreement bias or its absence.
Counters are central to settlement. Eighty-one settlements follow at least one counter and 32 do
not. Of 138 threads containing a counter, 81 settle; of 388 without one, 32 settle. Continuing a thread
and changing its terms are endogenous decisions, so that association is not an effect of countering.
In 44 settlements, the final offer author is not the original initiator. Attribution to the initiator would
reverse the meaning of those final terms, which is also the source of the historical formatter defect.
The 113 settlements complete a group for neither party in sixty cases, one party in forty-three and
both in ten. Group completion is one immediate consequence among several. The traded bundles
contain 207 deed transfers and $28,425 across 107 directed cash flows. Settlements can move blockers,
consolidate incomplete portfolios or supply liquidity without completing a color group. Treating every
non-completing exchange as purposeless would miss those functions, just as treating every completing
exchange as mutually beneficial would impose an unsupported utility model.

5
From acquisition to productive and transferable assets
5.1
Actual management opportunities
The games contain 475 direct, 72 auction, 207 trade and 397 inherited deed acquisitions, counting
repeated acquisitions. At stable boundaries, 209 color-completion episodes arise through sixteen direct
purchases, nine auctions, 63 trades and 121 inheritances. Sixty-nine inherited completions occur at the
game-ending bankruptcy. They create no later decision to build. Trade is also unnecessary for some
successful trajectories. Sol in G14 and Gemini in G15 participate in no accepted trade. In G15, Gemini
purchases Tennessee to complete oranges, builds seven events later and collects $3,250 in developed
orange rent; oranges trigger the first two eliminations before inherited browns trigger the last.
Every trade-completed group has an observed first management window. Forty-three of 63 groups
receive construction during it. The twenty residuals have distinct origins in Table 3. Nine never become
buildable in that window, four encounter defaults, four have conflicting received END actions, and
three have other valid END records. Conditioning on the 42 groups buildable at entry yields 34 built
and eight unbuilt, the latter divided into three defaults, three conflicts and two stated holds. The
entry-conditioned measure asks about a narrower state already reached; the whole-window measure
retains earlier financing constraints.
This decomposition matters for understanding economic execution. An agent may acquire own-
ership without an immediate development budget, or may attempt development that its response
policy fails to execute. Neither is equivalent to selecting an undeveloped portfolio. Conversely, legal
construction access does not establish that using it is prudent. The corpus has explicit reserve pref-
erences. At G3 d176 Opus has $204, can buy a $150 house and gives a valid action-only END with a
reserve rationale. At G1 d426 Grok consistently ends with $488 rather than buy another Atlantic house,
preserving liquidation capacity otherwise reduced by the purchase-to-resale loss. These examples
prevent response-condition defects from becoming a blanket explanation for every hold.
5.2
Allocation and executable encoding
Across 1,225 management menus listing construction, 663 applied actions are END. Sixty-six are
deterministic defaults, 24 have detected received-name conflicts, and 573 have no detected name
conflict. The last category includes absent or unparseable content; it is not a census of coherent reserve
policies. Availability of a construction tool also does not certify affordability of every particular target.
The retained exact-prefix audit examines all 66 defaults. They contain seventy well-formed con-
struction targets across attempts, one malformed target and 61 attempts without a parseable target.
8


All seventy well-formed encodings are invalid as submitted. Sixty-three targets are reachable after
atomic expansion and legal reordering; seven exceed current cash. At decision level, 63 of 66 defaults
contain at least one reachable submitted target, including all 61 Opus defaults. No added cash, stock
or action slot is supplied by this transformation.
For sixty of the Opus chains, a refusal precedes the invalid action-only retry. The exceptional G2
d129 chain has the invalid action before the refusal. The configured resolution policy applies END
after attempts are exhausted. These outcomes show a consequential separation between selecting a
feasible allocation and encoding an executable sequence. The target does not become a valid submitted
action retrospectively, and successful compilation is not a measured improvement in play. Defaults
concentrate in seven games, with G5 and G13 supplying 55 of 66. Their frequency therefore documents
recurrent execution under particular conditions rather than broad diversity of failure mechanisms.
Ordinary correction sometimes preserves the original allocation. Across all decisions with the
specific uneven-building error, exact-multiset recovery is 20/29 for Sol, 6/19 for Gemini, 5/5 for Grok
and 0/61 for Opus under its exhausted retry condition. An applied legal build can also change the target.
G2 d243’s seven-house target is feasible and would leave $208, while the actual retry and following
decision buy six houses and leave $358. G14 d145–148 attains its original eight-house objective through
several same-turn decisions. These distinctions are useful behavioral endpoints: legal repair, allocation
preservation and eventual attainment answer different questions.
5.3
Liquidation depends on legally accessible finance
The sixty eliminations comprise 56 declarations and four automatic bankruptcies after mortgages. For
56 estates, the generous own-asset bound falls below current debt. Three other states have existing
legal payment witnesses. G7 d339 is the remaining case, with adequate nominal value but a restricted
stock lock. This complete census describes debt at elimination, without converting unpayability into
approval of prior purchases or investment.
At G7 d339, Opus has $390 cash and clear Oriental collateral worth $50. Its pink group has
four houses, four houses and one hotel. No bank house is available to downgrade the hotel, while
even-selling prevents reducing a lower sibling. There is no sale action available. The sole financing
action mortgages Oriental and triggers automatic bankruptcy at $440 against $750 debt. The recorded
exhaustive mortgage-only continuation establishes the lock despite $1,310 nominal resources. Earlier
ways to release stock are different historical states.
The payable cases differ in mechanism. Sol at G12 d190 owes $500 and has a $748 nominal bound; a
retained route sells houses and mortgages unlocked collateral, pays and leaves $38. Gemini at G17 d234
owes $550 against a $905 bound; a route leaves $180. Their recorded accounts omit collateral unlocked
by sales. Grok at G12 d249 owes $250 against a $613 bound, and five house sales can pay with $43 left.
Its response instead contains a declaration conflicting with a sale request and text opposing bankruptcy.
We examine that incident below rather than count all three as the same reasoning error. Alternative
retained witnesses can leave different remainders because they mortgage different collateral; witness
cash is not an accessible-maximum estimate.
The actual record also contains successful staged finance. In G20 d327–336, $49 cash, $700 salvage
and $665 mortgage proceeds pay $1,400 and leave $14. At G16 d602, extensive liquidation reaches
$1,393 against $2,000, and the note reports the correct total. These contrasts show that staged financing
is sometimes executed and sometimes unavailable or missed. They give positive economic evidence
without requiring an optimal policy or alternate winner.

5.4
Receipts and creditor concentration
The 103 spending units incur $103,450 in own construction expenditure. Whole-game paid developed
rent totals $93,000, of which $92,550 occurs after first own spending. The difference is rent earned
on inherited development before the winner’s first own construction in G2. Forty units collect no
developed rent, and 26 of those also trigger no creditor elimination on the group. This records zero
cash receipts, not forty worthless investments. A terminal rent demand can eliminate an opponent
without being paid; salvage, inheritance and observation time also differ.
Successive G19 dark-blue owners illustrate the accounting problem. Opus spends $1,200, receives
$175 developed rent and later recovers $600 through building sales. Gemini inherits the deeds, spends
$2,000 over its later ownership and receives $1,400 on a subsequent Sol landing. Those are different
exposures to the same properties. Neither the board color nor an aggregate receipt-to-spending ratio
isolates decision quality. Counting only house purchases would omit G10’s winner, which inherits
four-house light blues and buys only $150 of hotel conversions.
The wider transfer record contains 905 paid-rent events totaling $121,154, of which eventual winners
receive $78,737. Estate cash totals $11,830, including $16 to the bank. Eventual winners receive 372
of 397 inherited deeds, with 170 of those deeds arriving at terminal bankruptcy. Excluding that
mechanical terminal concentration, winners still receive 202 of 227 inherited deeds and $7,148 of $7,843
estate cash. They are creditors in 35 of forty nonterminal eliminations and 54 of sixty overall.
These flows describe realized concentration through debt and inheritance. They do not estimate
an inheritance effect on winning, since winner status conditions on the same trajectory that produces
creditor identity. They nevertheless explain the stakes of bargaining over another player’s survival. A
transferred estate can include built assets, and an attempted blocker can become a creditor’s completion.

Economic dependencies in contextual interaction

original quote manifest supplies field paths, hashes and exact spans. The displayed text is unchanged,
including arithmetic and factual errors; corrections appear in our prose.

dishonest. There is no binding standing-offer object, fixed expiry or release authority. Sol’s intervening
liquidation pays rent rather than an identified cost incurred in reliance on the assurance. The observable
commitment reversal is therefore stronger than a bare change in price, but weaker than demonstrated
costly reliance or breach of a defined enforceable contract.

In G20, Opus sells Illinois to Grok for $500, completing Grok’s reds. Gemini then addresses the

Grok’s next private note recognizes an attempted cash boycott. At d100 it asks Gemini for $150 for

Gemini · public message · G20 d101 · attempt 0 · excerpt
No thanks, p1. Oriental is still unowned, and I'm not handing you $150 to build more houses on the

The observed action does not establish a functioning cartel. Gemini already expresses the same

behavior because of the appeal. No coalition effect or stability rate is estimated. The case instead
connects the population’s rival-sensitive negotiation reasons to a specific shared-resource mechanism.
Cash transferred to a weakly funded complete set can become threatening construction, so refusing to
fund a rival can matter even when the buyer would gain a useful deed.

Plans, forecasts and revision across decisions
Economic strategy also requires maintaining or revising an objective as opportunities arrive. The
fixed 240-decision sample contains 59 records with forward-looking own-action passages, 36 with
prospective opponent capacity or outcome passages and 25 with explicit correction or feedback
references. Categories overlap. Fifty-three private fields are absent, all for Opus, and none of these
15


sampled actions is an engine default. These counts describe text in a filtered sample, not population
rates of planning or correction.
Eight sampled records state landing policies to buy named properties if encountered. Searching
subsequent actual purchase menus yields six without a later named-target opportunity. Grok’s G2
d161 policy is executed when it later buys Mediterranean at d256. Its G16 d103 policy to buy Virginia
on sight meets a real opportunity at d159 with $371 against the $160 list price; the note still favors
purchase, but the received action starts an auction. Gemini subsequently acquires Virginia for $380.
The six unexercised policies cannot be scored as failures, and the latter case cannot be treated as a
deliberate abandonment when note and action conflict.
The record also contains sustained achievement. Sol at G9 d64 targets three houses on each dark
blue as funds arrive, adds houses at d67 and d102 and reaches three each at d163, across global turns
29–60. The G14 eight-house objective is attained over several same-turn decisions. These are realized
multi-stage goals, without a measured causal contribution of remembering the initial note. A shorter
financing sequence shows why endpoint choice matters. Grok at G20 d99 says it will mortgage Vermont
next, first attempts a sale at d100, and mortgages after rejection at d102. It violates a literal next-action
prediction while reaching the same-turn cash endpoint recorded in the note.
Opponent assessments usually concern public economic capacity. Grok’s G2 d161 forecast that
withdrawal leaves New York to another player is realized after two cash-constrained rivals also drop.
Such deductions can be useful without requiring inference about private beliefs. Other claims lack a
realized test. G19 d220 predicts that rivals will not pay for its survival, but no offer is presented before
elimination. A G17 auction forecast correctly anticipates being outbid, while the rival’s subsequent jail
and liquidation path supplies no building menu to test an accompanying development forecast. The
study therefore records particular capacity deductions and action forecasts rather than a calibrated
theory-of-mind score.
Revisions are equally concrete. In a G20 auction, Grok’s stated $235 ceiling becomes $230, followed
by a $232 bid that explicitly acknowledges exceeding the newer limit; it later withdraws without
exceeding the original bound. In G17, ceilings rise as the note supplies new rival-resale arguments.
These are revisions of stated rules, with no reference valuation proving that the revision improves
expected outcomes. They show that agents update and rationalize commitments during interaction,
while leaving the value of those changes open.
The full construction-submission ledger gives a separate recurrence endpoint. Among 114 decisions
with an uneven-intermediate-operation error, 109 have a later construction submission by that player in
the same game, and 63 repeat the error at that next decision’s first submission. In 54 error decisions, a
later corrective request carries the exact error. Fifty have a subsequent submission, with nine repetitions.
Restricting to the first such request per player-game yields 31 episodes, 29 subsequent submissions
and two repetitions. Restricting to exposed decisions that end in an applied build yields 44 subsequent
submissions and four repetitions. These overlapping populations distinguish exposure to a request,
repair of the current response and later recurrence.
Sixty Opus errors follow a refusal that already exhausts its attempt budget, so the configuration
receives no later construction-error request there. Most recurrent errors in the all-episode population
belong to that condition. Lower recurrence among request-exposed cases cannot be interpreted as a
feedback effect because states, targets, response conditions and selection into later builds differ. The
positive result is operationally narrower: there are successful repairs and achieved targets, alongside
observable recurrence and revision. The evidence does not establish durable learning, retention after
delay or adaptation to unfamiliar opponents.

Related work and current context
NegotiationArena studies multi-turn allocation and price bargaining and experimentally changes
behavioral tactics [1]. Its controlled outcome comparisons establish a different evidential basis for
tactic effectiveness than our observed settlements. The 2026 MERIT study introduces AgoraBench
and utility-grounded bargaining measures with feedback and learning interventions [6]. Our cohort
instead follows exchanges whose consequences persist in a shared game. It does not import another
task’s utility or fairness score to value Monopoly portfolios.
AgentMonopoly, inspected as arXiv:2610.29330v2, directly studies model, roster and persona differ-
ences in Monopoly [9]. It measures actions as well as language and permits bluffing while prohibiting
public messages contradicting chosen actions. Monopoly and language/action inconsistency are there-
fore established study topics. Our behavioral account emphasizes repeated counteroffers, management
after completion, debt liquidation and creditor paths. FinBoardBench, inspected as arXiv:2605.27896v1,
evaluates Cashflow, Acquire and Monopoly and explicitly records forced interventions after response
failures [3]. We credit that prior distinction between agent and fallback actions. Its described creditor
rules sell buildings to the bank, whereas intact buildings in this cohort connect inheritance to later
productive portfolios.
The Vending-Bench Arena communication study, inspected as arXiv:2608.14825v3, analyzes 2,583
emails in twenty year-long simulations [4]. It combines model classification, simulator checks and
reasoning-trace review, with full-pipeline replication using two additional judge families. This is
substantial prior work on economically grounded communication analysis, not merely unvalidated text
labeling. Its external speech-act codebook and conduct setting differ from our explicit permission for
public deception. Our case interpretations center financial alternatives, delivered context and explicit
correction without estimating a cross-model misalignment rate.
DECE, inspected as arXiv:2610.35773v1, evaluates complete configurations across ten Eurogames
and includes matched harness comparisons [11]. It supports the importance of execution conditions
but does not supply a causal estimate for the response differences in our cohort. Agent Evaluation
Reliability, arXiv:2610.00651v1, formally distinguishes reliable rankings of complete systems from
rankings of underlying models [2]. That distinction is especially relevant when refusal recovery jointly
changes the tool schema, retry access and memory.
Process-sensitive evaluation is also established by AgentBoard’s progress measurements [5].
Measurement-First Auditing, arXiv:2610.05830v1, combines provenance, matched controls and scorer
validation [10]. We consequently avoid claiming that making a behavioral judgment inspectable
validates a general auditing method. Empty Commitments, arXiv:2610.01045v2, separates promises
impossible under runtime affordances from failures to enable feasible action [7]. Our landing-policy
and withdrawal chronologies concern observed opportunities and changed positions rather than its
validated ontology. Work on unfaithful explanations further cautions against treating private text as
the computation that caused an action [8]. None of these sources determines the upstream cause of a
specific response conflict here.
9
Discussion
9.1
The economic unit is a continuing competitive relationship
The connected finding is that development opportunities are negotiated in relation to other players’ abil-
ity to finance and inherit them. The offer census, expressed rival-sensitive reasons, first-management
outcomes and creditor transfers describe successive parts of that relationship. The social cases give
17


those dependencies explicit content: withholding development cash, preserving an income-producing
mortgage option, waiting for a distressed seller, and preventing another player’s estate gain. These are
concrete forms of strategic conduct observed in a game, even though the cohort does not identify their
expected-value optimality.
Three economic states must remain distinct. Ownership completion creates permission to develop.
Accessible liquidity and stock determine which plans can execute. Realized rent and estates determine
subsequent resources, conditional on movement and survival. Combining these into a single com-
pletion or investment-success score would erase the causal order that agents confront. It would also
count terminal inherited groups as unused opportunities and system defaults as expressed reserve
preferences. The present evidence supplies a descriptive trajectory through those states, with successes,
constraints and execution failures all retained.
This is useful beyond the observation that some attractive trades are rejected. The meaningful
result is a structured account of contested development: rejection is common where completion favors
the current author, bargaining continues where the recipient buys completion, some text expressly
weighs rival financing, and the resulting groups have heterogeneous paths into production. The
cash differences and changing game states are part of that account. They prevent an isolated causal
explanation while making the actual bargaining object more specific than a count of generic agreements.
9.2
Coverage of the eight research questions
The user’s motivating questions span more than this cohort measures. For strategic reasoning, Q1,
we establish selective responses to different exchanges and documented consideration of rival conse-
quences, together with particular legal financial successes and mistakes. We do not measure maximized
winning probability. For opponent modeling, Q2, there are checkable public-capacity forecasts and
attempts to anticipate responses, without calibrated hidden-belief prediction. For cooperation, negotia-
tion and deception, Q3, the evidence is strongest on complete response behavior and exact forms of
bluffing, omission, withdrawal and correction. The conduct is authorized under the public-message
instruction; causal persuasion and a fair-surplus optimum remain unmeasured.
For long-horizon planning and memory, Q4, the sustained dark-blue goal and opportunity-
conditioned landing policies show both follow-through and execution conflict. The bounded histories
and action-only memory condition prevent a general memory-benefit estimate. For adaptation and
learning, Q5, ceilings change, retries repair actions and some encoding errors recur. Improvement
against a controlled feedback contrast or unfamiliar opponent is not established. For economics and
risk, Q6, the study documents actual expenditures, accessible finance, liquidation and receipt exposure.
Those measurements do not answer whether $400 is an optimal price for a property under uncertain
continuation.
For emergent and collective behavior, Q7, typed transfers quantify asset concentration and the G20
incident documents attempted coordination over cash. Ordinary dense trading or creditor concen-
tration is not a coalition measure. For evaluation validity and generalization, Q8, the reconstruction
checks support specific historical facts, while response and observation defects limit model attribution.
The narrow roster and failed readiness comparators leave transfer to stronger or unfamiliar opponents
untested. Appendix B maps all eight questions to observable endpoints and the remaining evidence
gaps without declaring the broad agenda complete.
18


9.3
The three broader questions
The distinction between sounding strategic and executing strategy has a mixed, concrete answer
here. Some expressed plans become multi-stage development or staged debt payment. Other feasible
allocations fail serialization, a planned purchase becomes an auction, and a sale-oriented response
becomes bankruptcy. Strategic prose therefore supplies targets and reasons to inspect, not a substitute
for successful action. These outcomes belong to the evaluated system, including its orchestration
policy, and cannot all be assigned to a model’s economic preferences.
The relationship between general and multi-agent capability is unidentified. Four heterogeneous
configurations, no independently measured capability axis and unequal effective response conditions
cannot estimate that association. Their win totals do not demonstrate either transfer of general in-
telligence or its failure. The literature’s larger cross-game and matched studies address parts of this
broader question under their own configurations; they do not fill the missing design in this cohort.
Finally, the records reveal some routes through which mistakes, movement and inheritance enter
outcomes, but do not decompose wins into skill, luck and exploitation. A debt-payment witness
changes a local endpoint; it does not preserve the historical future after the intervention. An eventual
winner’s creditor receipts are selected by realized play. Shared seeds likewise do not maintain matched
subsequent landings. The observed error and transfer mechanisms are explanatory evidence about
specific trajectories, not an allocation of causal credit for victory.
10
Limitations
The study’s target is one finite historical cohort. Five seed blocks, four cyclic orders and one fixed
opponent roster constrain generalization. Decisions and repeated offers are dependent, and the
source/refusal boundary is confounded with the first block. We report finite-corpus counts rather
than attach decision-level sampling intervals that imply an independent population of plays. Exposure
conditioning also selects states already produced by prior actions; it cannot repair the design for a
model comparison.
Implementation choices and defects are substantive experimental conditions. Intact creditor in-
heritance, even-building rules, access to declaration, the history formatter, unequal retry paths and
received-response conflicts change what is played and observed. The redemption rounding defect
remains preserved rather than repaired or experimentally varied here. Historical replay and legal
witnesses share the implemented rules and cannot independently validate them. Finding a defect does
not identify the outcome of fixing it.
Semantic categories and incidents were selected retrospectively with LLM research assistance,
including family overlap and partial label exposure. The exhaustive mechanical offer population does
not make its reason codes objectively correct. The temporal sample is filtered, provider and private
channels are unevenly available, and an exact quote can still be a false explanation. We supply neither a
human gold standard nor a population deception, intent or planning rate. Recipient delivery establishes
opportunity to receive a message, while explicit correction or endorsement supplies narrower textual
evidence than latent belief.
Local alternatives use stated endpoints and own-action restrictions. The negative checker cannot
certify general bundled-sale impossibility. Current payment feasibility does not establish future
survival or optimal continuation. Realized receipts omit unpaid terminal demands and do not estimate
incremental returns. Existing reference policies and scenario fixtures do not supply validated economic
optima; their readiness defects are summarized separately in the appendix. No new controlled
19


evaluation, game, model call, reference-policy run or synthetic verifier test was performed for this
rewrite.
11
Conclusion
Across twenty complete games, these configured agents bargain over opportunities whose value
depends on rival financing, executable development and creditor succession. The complete offer
record shows differentiated responses to development exchanges; contextual transcripts reveal the
corresponding pressure, bluffing and corrections. Development outcomes and liquidation evidence
then explain why possession, attempted investment and accessible wealth are different economic states.
Inheritance concentrates the resulting portfolios in eventual creditors. This connected behavioral
account preserves both effective local conduct and consequential execution failures. It supports
conclusions about the historical systems and their recorded relationships, while leaving optimal
strategy, causal persuasion, durable learning and general capability comparisons for designs that
actually measure them.

Message delivery and channel disclosure
The historical delivery audit distinguishes emitted public messages from timeline exposure. Of 6,882
nonempty occurrences, 149 do not enter the timeline: 94 are omitted during consecutive building
summaries and 55 are consumed by bankruptcy cash events without a summary. Of the 6,733 inserted
occurrences, 6,720 appear in at least one later opponent timeline. The remaining thirteen have no
observed opponent delivery there before the record ends. These occurrence counts do not rule out
repetition or exposure in another field.

E
Reference-policy and fixture readiness
The older agenda work includes a separate twenty-game reference-policy pilot at a 1,000-decision
horizon. These are existing results, not additional LLM games or new evaluations for this rewrite.
Nineteen are censored at the horizon, and the policies initiate no trade and apply only 44 construction
actions across 19,494 decisions. Censoring describes the observation limit rather than proof of nonter-
mination. Sparse development and absent bargaining prevent this pilot from supplying a competent
strategic comparator for the historical LLM cohort.
The retained 130-scenario screen finds eleven illegal reference actions, 55 states failing at least one
static consistency condition and 68 passing both screens. State screens are not proofs of reachability or
optimal reference actions. Private-note keyword criteria contribute to 128 scenarios, while 41 induce
identical actions from all six reference policies. Those checks reveal reasons to adjudicate tasks and
separate expression from strategy before using the library as a capability battery. None of the fixtures,
policies or production engine is repaired or rerun here.
