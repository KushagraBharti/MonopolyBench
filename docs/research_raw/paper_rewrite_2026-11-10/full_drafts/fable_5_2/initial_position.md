## What I think the paper is

An observational study of how four configured LLM systems trade, build, go broke and inherit in twenty complete four-player games. The object is economic conduct over a whole trajectory. The audit machinery (legal menus, attempt provenance, exact-prefix witnesses, delivery reconstruction) is the method that lets the conduct be described correctly. It is not the contribution, and the paper should not ask to be judged as a validated auditing method.

The critique's sharpest sentence is the right test: what should a reader believe or do differently? My answer is a three-step account of where a multiplayer economic game is actually decided in these records.

1. **Completion is priced and rival-sensitive.** The offer ledger's stronger contrast is the joint one: who completes a color group if the offer is accepted. Offers completing only the writer's group are refused outright more than half the time and accepted rarely; offers completing only the recipient's group are mostly answered with a counter. Cash runs in the direction a priced monopoly predicts. The four systems accept at similar frequencies and differ in how they decline. This replaces the old headline ("own completion is insufficient for agreement"), which the critique correctly calls a refutation of a rule nobody holds.
2. **A completed group is not yet a developed one.** Between settlement and rent sit post-deal liquidity, a legal building opportunity, a valid action encoding and the response policy. The 20 unbuilt groups out of 63 are mostly illiquidity or execution, not stated restraint. Financial distress is real when it arrives: 56 of 60 eliminations cannot be paid even on a generous bound.
3. **Bankruptcy, under intact creditor inheritance, is the largest mover of assets.** More groups are completed by estate than by trade, and the eventual winner is the creditor in most eliminations. Agents talk about this: they sell "insurance" against a rival's inheritance, buy blockers, and call for cash boycotts. Those distress bargains are the natural home for the transcripts, and several are later undone by the very inheritance they were meant to prevent.

I expect to argue that step 3 is under-weighted in the contract's provisional order, where inheritance is a sub-item of economics. I want to check from the retained data whether it deserves to be a structural finding.

## Agreements with the contract

Behavioral identity; conditions before interpretation; no capability ranking; no intent, persuasion or optimality claims; public deception was authorized; refusal cause unresolved; no new evaluations of any kind; one consolidated limitations section; LLM-assisted coding labelled as such.

## Where I expect to dissent

- **Do not open with defects.** Conditions must precede interpretation, but a section of formatter and refusal defects before any finding repeats the old paper's posture. I will state the conditions compactly in setup, with one table, and attach the specific condition to the specific result it qualifies.
- **The eight questions are a coverage map, not the spine.** They belong in one discussion table with a supported level per row. The introduction should state one question and the three-step answer.
- **The reference-policy pilot and scenario audit leave the main text.** They are readiness QA for experiments this paper does not run. One appendix paragraph.
- **The 126-certificate suite is supporting evidence.** Report what it classified; do not present the count as breadth.
- **Temporal and adaptation material shrinks to what has an endpoint.** One realized plan, one plan that met its opportunity and was not executed, the recurrence table. No theory-of-mind framing.
- **Transcripts should serve the argument.** Choose incidents by the economic mechanism they show (rival-sensitive refusal, distress pricing, withdrawn terms, coordination appeal, failed denial, correction with acceptance, execution conflict), not by how alarming they read.

## What I will not claim

A causal effect of rival completion on refusal; that low acceptance shows no agreement bias; that any refusal, purchase or bankruptcy was optimal or financially justified by a later debt; that inheritance causes winning; that any message persuaded anyone; a ranking of the four systems; durable learning; theory of mind; transfer to other opponents.
