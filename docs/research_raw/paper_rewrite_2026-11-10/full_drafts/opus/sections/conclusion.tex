\section{Conclusion}

In twenty complete Monopoly games among four configured LLM systems, the economic decisions that matter are linked. Recipients refuse or charge for trades that would complete a rival's group, sometimes turning down their own completion to deny it. The groups that trades do complete become development only when the owner can execute a build, and one configuration lost most of its development to defaulted plans that were affordable as requested. Whether a debt ends a game depends on which assets the rules let a player reach, which differs from both the displayed options and nominal wealth. When a player fails, its assets pass intact to its creditor, usually the eventual winner, and the sharpest bargaining conduct in the selected transcripts appears where that transfer is near.

For evaluating economic agents, the practical lesson is to follow the chain. Score offers by whose position they change, separate requested from executed actions, measure liquidity as legally reachable money, and record where a failing agent's assets go. The questions this cohort cannot answer, about causal influence, learning, transfer and optimal play, need designs built for them.
