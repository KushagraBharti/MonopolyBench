  1. **A blocker deal that transferred the feared monopoly anyway — game 1.** Grok paid Sol $325 and $38 mortgage interest for four mortgaged deeds and cards as insurance against Argon inheriting key reds; Sol retained pinks. After repeated rejected pink offers, Sol went bankrupt on Argon's railroad and transferred pinks to Argon, which redeemed and developed them. The events establish the predicted transfer, but not what would have happened under a different deal. Grok also saw and discussed the reversed accepted-trade summary, with inconsistent interpretation, so the corrupted memory cannot be assumed to have caused its move. Decisions 000414–461, events 003001–3008 and 003235–3292; [narrative](Terra%20Notes%20for%20All%20Games.md#terra-notes-for-game-1).

  2. **Reciprocal sets, divergent liquidity, and a complete house squeeze — game 6.** Sol gave Opus St. Charles, Virginia and $200 for New York (000140–147; events 001070–1074); both acquired sets, but Sol also held four railroads. Orange rents and railroads helped force Grok and Opus into bankruptcy. Sol later acquired the third light blue, bought 12 houses, and ultimately all 32, repeatedly stating a scarcity rationale; Argon did build three reds when houses were freed, but had to sell them for $225 to pay $400 rent, after spending $450 to build. Sol bought the released houses and Argon later went bankrupt owing $800 (000177–232; events 001727–1733, 001792–1805). Cash scarcity and rolls also matter; avoid attributing the outcome to the house lock alone. [Narrative](Terra%20Notes%20for%20All%20Games.md#terra-notes-for-game-6).

  3. **A trade creates opposing threats; actual green rents dominate — game 5.** The Boardwalk/Pennsylvania transaction gives one side dark blue and the other green. Argon spends $200 upgrading Park Place to $1,100 rent but shortly liquidates its entire dark-blue development after a $900 green landing, then is bankrupted by $1,200 Pennsylvania. Grok and Opus also fall to green rents. Opus repeatedly fails to build its completed light-blue set through grouped even-building errors, producing three actual Oriental landings at $12 rather than the $270 three-house schedule; this is a local comparison on the observed sequence, not a full counterfactual. Decisions 000202–309, events 001991–2008 and 002064–2073; [narrative](Terra%20Notes%20for%20All%20Games.md#terra-notes-for-game-5).

  4. **Housing lock, forced liquidation, and repeated rebuying — game 4.** After Sol sells pink houses to pay rent, Argon buys the last two available houses for $300 to lock the bank, then immediately sells both for $150 among three sold houses to cover a $250 red rent, with no intervening rent on those two. Opus captures released houses and Grok later buys houses released by Argon, restoring scarcity. This sequence makes the financing cost of tactical denial concrete and tests competing claims about available builds. Decisions 000190–224, events 001444–1616; [narrative](Terra%20Notes%20for%20All%20Games.md#terra-notes-for-game-4).

  5. **Stated refusal versus executed acceptance — games 2 and 3.** In game 2, Grok's public message, private thought, assistant content and readable retry reasoning reject Opus's $230 Ventnor offer; the applied tool accepts, transferring the deed and cash (000060–062, events 425–428). In game 3, Grok likewise says no to $185 for Illinois while the tool accepts (000127–130, events 869–872); Opus later observes acceptance alongside the contradictory message. These are actual settlements with ambiguous causation, not evidence the recipients persuaded a willing seller. They are distinct from the accepted-trade memory reversal. [Game 2](Terra%20Notes%20for%20All%20Games.md#terra-notes-for-game-2), [game 3](Terra%20Notes%20for%20All%20Games.md#terra-notes-for-game-3).

  6. **A false board claim wins a trade but the recipient recognizes the mistake — game 6.** Grok's provider content says end; its applied offer gives Sol St. Charles, Pennsylvania Railroad and $400 for Vermont, incorrectly calls Vermont orange, and claims Sol has an orange monopoly. Sol sees the claim and correct holdings, explicitly recognizes the error, and accepts (000019–021, events 159–164); Grok subsequently recognizes its overpayment. Cash changes $1,185→$785 and $758→$1,158. The false statement was exposed but the observed acceptance does not support persuasion by the false claim; channel divergence precludes a firm deception finding. [Narrative](Terra%20Notes%20for%20All%20Games.md#terra-notes-for-game-6).

  7. **Buying a blocker at a cash-constrained auction — game 6.** Argon lands on Atlantic, which would complete its yellow set, but has $146 and must auction. Sol bids $147, explicitly choosing one dollar over Argon's cash; both rivals drop and Sol wins for $147, below the $260 face price (000202–206, events 001563–1578). Argon could mortgage later, but the auction menu did not permit that action. It subsequently mortgages non-red deeds to develop its existing reds.   SECTION: Terra shortlist: games 1–6
  8. **Cooperation appeals with independently motivated conduct — games 1, 5.** Game 5 Argon asks Opus to block Sol's brown; Opus saw the appeal but had already bid for that purpose. Later Argon asks Grok to drive up Opus's light-blue price; Grok sees it and drops, calling it bait. These episodes show exposure and disagreement without establishing persuasion (000195–223). Game 1's negotiations similarly show forecasted inheritance risks and rejected alternatives, not proof that offers should have been accepted. [Game 5](Terra%20Notes%20for%20All%20Games.md#terra-notes-for-game-5), [game 1](Terra%20Notes%20for%20All%20Games.md#terra-notes-for-game-1).

Across these games, liquidity, mortgage interest, building supply, jail access and timing repeatedly mediate the value of a set. The same proposed tactic may fail for opposite reasons: game 4's lock immediately releases houses under rent pressure, while game 6's leader eventually holds all 32. Appeals often reach recipients who already have their own reasons, and unexecuted or rejected proposals should not be treated as settlements. The accepted-trade summary reversal is independently audited across the corpus; accurate event flows and actual prompts should be used separately. Open questions include which apparent deception candidates survive a channel-by-channel audit, whether shortages changed a feasible alternative rather than merely coexisting with cash constraints, and how much observed rents depend on the specific dice path.
