# Game 1 source review

I read Terra's complete game narrative and all 28 public catalog entries, including the corrected offer-rejection labels. Selected original fields, event totals and terminal legal sequences were checked. The original corpus-wide and early phase-2 audits remain separate evidence. This review does not claim another full reading of all 486 original bundles.

Gemini wins without initiating a trade, but it counters and accepts two set-completing exchanges. Its initial light-blue construction costs 750 and earns 4550 paid developed rent. Later green development costs 3000 and receives 1100 paid rent plus the terminal creditor event; inherited pink development costs 1500 and receives no developed rent before the game ends. These are observed receipts with different exposure windows, not causal returns. Grok's yellow group costs 450, earns 780 and returns 225 through sales. Sol's pink group costs 700, earns 50 and returns 350. Those totals correct simplified descriptions of nonbuilding or no income.

The first consequential trade chain cannot be presented as agreement consistently expressed by both parties. Grok's selected accept at 112 contradicts its content and the same call's private rejection. The ensuing yellow/light-blue trade is real, and Gemini develops at turn 43 after receiving its set at turn 40. Both the executed assets and the inconsistency belong in the account. Grok's later slow yellow development mixes explicit liquidity holds with three content-build/tool-end cases. Applied nonbuilding alone does not identify restraint.

The narrative contains a useful limit on stated trading policies. Gemini repeatedly requires a monopoly in return, but the 293 offer already satisfies that condition by completing pink and is rejected at 294 to avoid funding rival red houses nearby. The condition is not sufficient, and the later successful Pacific/States swap cannot be explained by condition satisfaction alone. Renewed proposals matching Grok's earlier superseded offers are genuine changed choices, but no live-offer acceptance or separate assurance breach is established.

Chronology also changes the speech interpretation. Sol's two auction raises precede Opus's 25-dollar valuation statement and its recorded cap already exists. The next action is a drop, so neither persuasion nor failed persuasion follows. Many anti-leader appeals are delivered, but the final bankruptcy appeal does not appear in the next Sol or Grok timelines. Logged speech, delivery and later conduct remain different observations.

Terminal financing ceilings are 318 for Opus, 121 for Sol and 170 for Grok, all below debt. Opus pays 1748 rent to Gemini, of which 1700 is hotel rent; its final 550 debt instead transfers 118 estate cash. Sol's refusal to sell its entire portfolio precedes a genuine forced elimination, with a recorded recovery rationale rather than an intention to choose the winner. Late receipts in the catalog are misattributed: Pacific's 1100 is a four-house rent before hotel conversion, and Vermont's 550 comes from an existing light-blue hotel, not newly inherited pinks.
