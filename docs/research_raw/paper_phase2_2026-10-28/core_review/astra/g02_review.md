# Game 2: financing, recovery and recipient interpretation

Scope: complete Terra game narrative, Sonnet game section and all 15 public catalog entries read. Selected original actions, prompts, attempt outputs and event segments were checked; this is not a second full-original reading of every decision. Earlier peer findings were known. `check_g02_claims.py` records original paths and hashes and independently replays the quantity-changing build recovery and final liquidation bounds.

Gemini wins after purchasing Park Place from Opus and constructing the dark blues. Grok's earlier 500 purchase of St. Charles supplies Gemini with cash while its original explanation incorrectly treats that pink deed as an orange blocker. The Park Place settlement costs Gemini 625 plus Pennsylvania Railroad. It then spends 600 on initial construction and retains 11 cash. This is a realized low-buffer investment, not an identified optimal strategy. Boardwalk's initial 600 rent occurs at two houses. Later 1300 Park Place debts eliminate Opus and Sol; those debts must not be counted as rent paid.

The Ventnor transaction illustrates why recipient interpretation requires its own source check. Grok's action accepts 230 while its attached text refuses. Contrary to the public catalog's inference, Opus already recognizes execution at decision 62 in its applied note, assistant content and readable provider text. Its public arithmetic concession does not mean it thinks the sale was rejected. At 63 it states the inconsistency publicly. This is exposure and transaction-specific recognition, not proof of a globally accurate internal state.

Opus's undeveloped light blues are initially a recovery failure rather than deliberate noninvestment. Five affordable allocations are rejected for operation ordering and end in defaults. When Opus first builds, Gemini's dark blues have three houses each. Opus later spends 500 on light-blue construction but receives no developed light-blue rent; its only light-blue receipts, 6, 12 and 16, precede construction. Several moves occur before its final Park Place landing. The estate has 927 of accessible liquidation value against 1300, so the final debt is unavoidable at that state.

Sol completes yellows through the two-railroad exchange, pays transfer interest, mortgages Illinois and three other deeds, redeems the yellows and builds two houses. The group's construction costs 300, later sells for 150 and collects no developed rent. These are observed receipts and expenses, not an incremental return estimate. Sol's cash boundary in the source catalog skips the Illinois mortgage and mixes trade interest with later redemption. Gemini inherits this estate after Sol's later 1300 debt exceeds its remaining 77.

Grok's later pink exchange is consequential but internally inconsistent: its applied note claims a completed pink set, its tool proposes 400 plus St. Charles and States for Virginia, and its assistant content instead chooses end_turn. Gemini's acceptance explicitly recognizes the actual incomplete holdings. The later empty proposal similarly has a probing rationale in the applied note but end_turn in the content and provider account. I initially considered the probing interpretation after reading the attached note; reading the complete returned channels removed that support for a channel-consistent strategy. No initial ledger endorsement was overwritten.

An exact-prefix replay independently confirms Sol's quantity-recovery qualification. At Gemini decision 243, the original grouped seven-house plan costs 1050 and is affordable. Interleaving the same seven purchases succeeds, leaving 208 and yellow levels 2/2/3. The actual corrective attempt builds three houses, and the next decision builds three more, leaving 358 and 2/2/2. A successful corrective retry thus means some legal construction occurred, not that the original allocation was preserved. No subsequent branch or winner effect was tested.

For construction accounting, Gemini spends 1600 on dark blues and receives 1700 paid developed rent plus two estates. Its own 200 light-blue spend follows inheritance: 450 of the group's 850 paid developed rent arrives before Gemini's first own build, and 400 after. Its 900 orange spend receives no paid developed rent; its 900 yellow spend receives no paid developed rent but triggers the final elimination. These contrasts show why rent totals, estates, inherited capital and follow-up exposure need separate fields. Grok's final accessible 203 is below the 330 Atlantic debt.

No public coordination appeal here establishes a collective agreement or persuasive effect. Gemini's explicit funding-denial reasons and refusal of an offer that would complete its utilities counter a simple always-complete-your-own-set interpretation. The public-only absence of a collusion case is not evidence about every unobserved channel.
