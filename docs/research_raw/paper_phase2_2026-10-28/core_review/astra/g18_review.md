## Trajectory and representative evidence

Opus assembles browns and light blues through purchases and trades, then develops them. Its Baltic acquisition includes a Grok reject-content/accept-tool conflict. Sol completes oranges, finances eight houses, and immediately sells one to pay a railroad landing. Gemini and Grok exchange Boardwalk and Virginia with170 cash, completing pink and dark-blue sets respectively. Their subsequent realized income differs: Gemini spends1200 on pink construction and receives450 developed rent plus600 salvage; Grok spends1000 on dark blues, receives no developed rent, and recovers500 through sales. These figures describe the observed path; neither identifies incremental investment return.

Opus captures the bank's last two houses. Gemini's ensuing liquidation returns four, which Opus buys; a further Gemini liquidation returns eight. This is an auditable financial and inventory feedback sequence. Bank exhaustion is not a complete estimate of frustrated construction demand or proof of every asserted housing-denial motive. Hotel upgrades return houses under the existing event semantics, and all464 initial bank inventories reconcile.

The late Pacific negotiation is a useful social case. Opus repeatedly says its250 offer stands, including after the first750 pink hotel rent. After a second750 payment and orange liquidation, Sol invokes the standing offer explicitly. Opus withdraws it. The assurance is in both the seller's441 and buyer's442 recorded public memories, and Sol's attached note explicitly refers to taking the earlier offer. Thus invocation and withdrawal are supported across turns. We do not establish original deceptive intent, a binding contract, costly reliance before the withdrawal, or a causal effect on defeat. The creditor's board and the seller's available threat changed before invocation.

Gemini's final Virginia mortgage has a separate social intent/action distinction. Its note hopes to impose mortgage transfer interest on the creditor. The engine instead automatically eliminates Gemini after the last mortgage and passes382 cash to Opus with no such fee. The extra80 bank cash is realized creditor income. This is not evidence of a deliberate gift: the stated burden rationale predicts the opposite effect under the implemented rule.

Sol's final300 Oriental price remains300; Opus's changes and Sol's private275 fallback are separate. The buyer's Oriental cash cost includes transfer interest and redemption.
