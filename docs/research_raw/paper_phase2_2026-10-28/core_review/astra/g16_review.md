## Economic and social interpretation

The anti-leader exchange includes repeated appeals, statements about withholding set-completing assets, finite auction ceilings and later offers to the leader. These give useful evidence about changing bargaining positions and individual incentives. They do not establish a collusive agreement or isolate persuasion. Grok's d436 applied proposal even carries a private instruction not to send it, so its entire record cannot be presented as one coherent intention.

Several extracted falsehoods do not survive source review. Orange-monopoly references describe prospective completion, not existing ownership. Paying describes an ongoing liquidation sequence. Choosing a third jail roll is legal and the notes explicitly acknowledge the forced-fine risk. Gemini's190 comparison follows from half of Opus's380 claim. Other errors remain specific and observable: three hotel sets conflates three hotels with one group, and some price/list-price arithmetic is wrong. Each proposition needs its own referent, time and evidence channel.

House-retention rationales and contemplated downgrades are useful recorded strategies, but an unexecuted plan is not a supply blockade. No case here identifies what would have happened under a different bid, trade, hotel policy or dice path. Original checks, source hashes, retained delivery records and legal branch steps are saved in `g16_claim_checks.json`.
