## Social and reasoning boundaries

Auction price pressure has explicit individual incentives. Sol's401 Boardwalk bid seeks to make Opus pay for completion; Opus's response distinguishes a hypothetical half-set for Sol from its own completion. The extractor incorrectly labels both as errors about Sol's holdings. Grok's caution sometimes omits the acquired deed's mortgage capacity, which refutes a stated necessary-insolvency argument without proving buying would be optimal.

Several potential falsehood labels disappear on original reading. The670 offer is explicitly cash AND property value, with a correct1337 cash forecast. Paying in full is an ongoing funding plan. Loaded dark blues are actually developed when described, even though later sold. At the same time, Grok's false orange monopoly claim remains false—but the extractor's proposed complete-red counterexample is also false because Grok owns only Kentucky and Illinois.

Sol repeatedly retains houses with a stated denial rationale, while bank stock never falls below six. This is intended denial without an identified binding supply case. Later red hotels release houses and Sol changes its stated reason to cash retention. Positive stock does not prove every hypothetical large plan feasible; it does prevent substituting zero-stock rhetoric for observed state.
