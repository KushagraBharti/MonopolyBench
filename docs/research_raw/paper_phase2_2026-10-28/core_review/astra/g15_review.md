## Economic and social account

Gemini/p1 wins without participating in any of the game's three completed trades. It purchases orange, mortgages215 of non-group collateral, and initially develops to2/3/3 houses. The missing third St James house arrives later at d148. Sol/p4 instead acquires cash, railroads and cheap auctioned properties, but never assembles a color group. These trajectories challenge any single account in which deal activity, favorable cash prices or asset accumulation is sufficient for victory.

The Park Place negotiation is especially useful. Opus/p3 repeatedly raises its offer; Sol holds its later750-plus-Electric terms. Opus accepts, leaving158 cash. Its earlier statement that this price would leave insufficient immediate cash for a200 house is accurate. Acceptance exceeds its stated700 reservation limit; it does not falsify the affordability calculation. Later building, rent receipts, house sales and insolvency complete the economic story. Opus spends800 on four houses, receives300 from selling three, and retains one200-cost house when eliminated. The300 loss on those sold houses is not the net return of the whole development strategy.

Grok/p2's Marvin/Reading/350 exchange changes the asset distribution without completing its intended group in the attached note. The record also contains a content/tool conflict. The actual transfer, recipient's recognition, subsequent correction and immediate50 Reading rent are separately meaningful; they do not establish deliberate sacrifice. The later Illinois negotiation raises the seller's own ask after a counter and ends without settlement. Its explanation anticipates adaptive hotel construction and contains incorrect financial propositions. The observed no-sale continuation cannot reveal the outcome under an accepted bargain.

Gemini's later refusals are more informative than generic trade inactivity. At d227 it rejects a large bundle while explicitly prioritizing denial of Sol's first monopoly. At d269 it refuses another deal because the requested600 plus Vermont would fund an opponent's development. These are recorded strategic reasons and actual rejections. They do not prove a mathematically guaranteed win or that every offered asset premium was disadvantageous.

## Construction, access and realized consequences

At d219 Gemini changes from house retention to five hotel upgrades. It states that11 houses are already available and the remaining opponents cannot exploit additional supply sufficiently. The upgrades cost400 and release20 houses, bringing bank stock to31. This is a concrete reconsideration of supply denial, not a timeless rule that houses should always be retained.

The subsequent Opus debt is950 at St James, against an engine-replayed ceiling645. The prior four-house rent750 would also exceed645 at that state. The upgrade therefore is not necessary for this particular observed elimination, even though it changes realized future rents elsewhere. Dark-blue inheritance includes one Boardwalk house; Gemini adds six new houses for1200 during four construction decisions at turn92, ending Park3/Boardwalk4. Inheritance occurs at turn89, not four global decisions immediately before the completed build.

The three final liquidation ceilings are Grok761 against800, Opus645 against950, and Sol280 against450. All are attainable by legal liquidation sequences and below debt, with no hotel-access complication in those estates. These endpoint results do not make earlier choices inevitable. Grok's preceding20 Illinois receipt also changes its threshold between negotiation and elimination, so a prior741 bound cannot be reused at bankruptcy.

## Communication and provenance

The Baltic episode combines a garbled applied rejection, an unexecuted120 counter, a later conditional revisit statement, an explicit private tactical-delay note, immediate shopping to another player and eventual100 settlement. It warrants original recipient-prompt review before any uptake claim. The conditional language is not an unconditional purchase promise; later purchase challenges a claim that Grok never meant to buy. Likewise, Claude's public willingness to work against the leader does not create an executed agreement. Sol's proposed purchase of improved dark blues is rejected by validation and is not delivered to Claude as an offer.

The game also includes multiple opposite-direction action/content conflicts. Applied actions remain authoritative. Missing private notes following refusal recovery are unavailable evidence, not intentional secrecy. Statements about bids, group counts, rent schedules and future adaptation must be evaluated individually.
