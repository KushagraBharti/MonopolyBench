## Economic trajectory

Sol/p1 completes orange by purchase, finances construction with undeveloped collateral, and wins without participating in a completed trade. Later inheritances and the North Carolina purchase give it green development. This is a useful comparison to trade-led routes: portfolio assembly, development finance and rent receipts can occur without a completed bargain. It does not show that trading would have made this player worse off.

Gemini/p2 obtains brown through the two Grok exchanges. The cash direction matters: Grok first gives Baltic and200 for Mediterranean; Gemini later pays100 to recover Mediterranean. Gemini consequently retains Baltic and net100 cash. The first exchange has a content/tool conflict, so the outcome cannot simply be described as Grok intentionally subsidizing a rival. Gemini also completes yellow when Ventnor transfers at d161. Redemption at d162 clears the mortgage; it does not create ownership of the set. Total buyer outlay is240+13+143=396, including transfer interest and redemption.

Opus/p4 sells assets as orange rents force liquidity needs. Its later Park Place sale concerns an encumbered deed and a changed state, unlike the earlier failed offers. Grok's acquired dark blues remain undeveloped, but at d220 it can redeem Park Place and still afford a house: cash642−193=449. Bank stock is zero. The lack of construction at that point is an actual supply restriction, not merely a chosen reserve policy.

Sol's final green purchases exhaust house stock. After Gemini's bankruptcy it holds32 houses. This makes the physical supply story concrete, although the effect on an alternative game outcome remains unknown. At the observed final North Carolina landing, Grok's maximum accessible resources are907. Three-house rent900 would be payable at that exact state; the actual four-house rent1100 is not. That is a local schedule comparison, not evidence that a three-house policy would have changed the winner.

## Social and record interpretation

Blocking, repeated rejected offers and anti-leader appeals occur, but they do not themselves establish a pact, collusion or intentional kingmaking. A malformed300 Marvin proposal at d193 is an attempted action that never becomes a received offer. It must not be counted as a rejection by the intended recipient.

Grok d140 explicitly notices the reversed accepted-trade memory and reconstructs the particular Mediterranean sale from holdings and cash. That is a valuable recovery example. The same returned explanation misclassifies Virginia and briefly overstates the opponent's yellow ownership, so recovery is transaction-specific. There is no evidence of general immunity to the memory defect.

Jail choices also change with economic position: paid exit accompanies the earlier orange acquisition, while later rent collectors favor shelter. A retained jail card provides an alternative to the forced third-roll fine, but does not prove avoidance of the subsequent landing. Neither a valid shelter rationale nor the observed rent receipts identify an optimal jail policy.

## Corrections and limits

At d225 Sol owns Reading, Pennsylvania Railroad and Short Line: three railroads, with B&O still unowned. Pennsylvania already charged100 at d223 while Short Line remained mortgaged. The private claim that redeeming Short Line doubles the other rails' rent is therefore wrong under this engine. Redemption does make Short Line itself collectible, which is a separate benefit. The actual charge111 also differs from the commonly stated110 through the implementation's floating-point ceiling.

Other qualifications concern complete quotations, cash direction, completion versus redemption, and recipient exposure. Face value is not a complete valuation benchmark. Missing private notes under refusal recovery are not deliberate silence. A supply-denial statement is not proof that the opponent's choice was constrained at that moment; the d220 state supplies the stronger direct evidence here.
