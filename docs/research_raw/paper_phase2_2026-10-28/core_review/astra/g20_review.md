# Game 20 source review

The complete 355-decision extraction trajectory, ten full-channel shortlist entries and 24 public catalog entries were read. Selected original actions, prompts and attempt fields were checked. This is not a claim that Astra reread all 355 original bundles. The first-pass reviewer did that wider reading. Earlier initial judgments remain in the ledger; this review supplies source-checked updates where applicable.

Gemini wins through a traded green monopoly, timed financing and continued development. Five trades settle. Gemini's green construction costs 3200, with 100 salvage and 2940 paid developed rent. Its final 1275 debt is unpaid and produces 130 estate cash. Sol spends 1700 across an initial brown build, orange development and a brown rebuild. Those constructions have different consequences: the oranges receive no full paid developed rent but eliminate Opus and acquire its estate. Zero paid rent is not zero economic effect.

Opus's recovery trade supplies Sol's final orange deed and gives Opus the dark-blue set plus cash. The third New York house raises the observed charge from a payable 220 to 600, exceeding Opus's 550 maximum. That is a local rent threshold, not proof of which player would win under another trade or build. All three actual eliminations are unavoidable at their recorded states, with maxima 417, 550 and 340. The final Sol bound includes mortgages unlocked after house sales.

The contrast is Sol's successful 1400 payment at decisions 327–336. Replaying the recorded actions restores the prefix, validates both hotel downgrades and all later sales and mortgages, and leaves 14 cash. Resources are 49 cash, 700 salvage and 665 mortgages. Gemini's prior 1098 insolvency forecast is wrong; its later 1414 total is correct despite offsetting component errors. The paper can compare these local forecasts and observed legal sequences without a winner counterfactual.

The game also contains a concrete distinction between cash-only and financed development. Sol's decision 235 says a 400 purchase leaves a set it cannot develop. At that exact response, accepting the actually offered price is legal. After mandatory interest, four spare-deed mortgages and redemption, four orange houses are legal and leave 41. This does not fund its earlier six-house target or prove that the alternative is desirable. The separate claim that 250 funds five red houses is numerically false. Intent and recipient uptake are not established.

Several social labels need correction. The 400 Illinois advertisement is public at decision 77 and appears in Opus's decision-78 memory. A formal offer was not applied, but no hidden private exchange is needed. Gemini's rejection of giving away a brown monopoly refers to completing Sol's group, not to owning that group itself. Sol's funding-denial rationale precedes Gemini's public anti-funding appeal, and Opus later offers funding, so a coordinated boycott is unproved. The phrase about not shopping monopolies is context-dependent bargaining language, not an unambiguous ban on later acquiring a set.

Grok's private-note fragment enters the public log and Gemini's delivered prompt. Gemini explicitly notices it. This supports exposure and recognition, not financial exploitation. Other content/tool differences produce an auction, a build instead of a mortgage, an informal offer instead of a formal proposal, and an announcement of a second house that was never built. For the last case, the attached private note is also inconsistent with the actual holdings. No source identifies deliberate public fabrication or which component caused the disagreement.

Original prompts preserve both Indiana and Illinois mortgage markers throughout decisions 296–310; their disappearance is a simplified-rendering error. Hotel upgrade and downgrade events explain the house transitions. Finally, the wrong railroad and utility redemption-multiplier propositions at 340 and 342 belong to Gemini. They are decision-specific statements, not evidence of stable model knowledge.

The script and Ruff pass. No canonical artifact, engine or prompt changed, and no provider calls or new games were made.
