## Local mistakes and correct sequences

Gemini's d234 declaration states that five house sales and Baltic provide only $530 against $550 rent. Exact replay confirms that five legal sales unlock Park Place and Boardwalk mortgages. Selling the houses, mortgaging Baltic and then Park pays the debt and leaves $155 plus an unmortgaged Boardwalk. The full liquidation upper bound is $905. Actual bankruptcy transfers five intact houses to Sol. This demonstrates a feasible survival alternative at the recorded state, not intentional kingmaking or a different eventual winner.

Opus later performs the relevant sequence correctly on the same $550 Tennessee rent: $128 cash, $375 of house sales, then a newly available $110 Indiana mortgage, leaving $63 after payment. Its later $293 maximum cannot cover Park Place's $1,100 rent. Both observations belong in the paper; the first error does not justify a stable inability label for an entire model or cohort.

Grok's d188 note caps the light-blue price at $101 to prevent a $150 red house. Its d190 note changes that ceiling after incorrectly asserting that even construction requires $300. Opus immediately adds one legal $150 Kentucky house. Later Grok d388 incorrectly requires a sale from every light-blue property. A separate exact branch sells only two houses, pays $50 and leaves $16 with a legal 1/1/2 distribution. Actual three-house sale leaves $41 and one less building. These are local rule propositions and applied consequences, not identified causal effects on total winning probability.

## Realized development and bargaining

Independent event recounts give Opus $750 construction, $375 salvage and $90 red rent after construction; Gemini $1,000 construction, no salvage and no developed dark-blue rent before its declaration; Grok $600 construction, $300 salvage and no light-blue rent after construction. Zero paid rent does not establish zero strategic value: exposure, threats and residual assets differ. Sol's final third Illinois house raises the observed landing's rent from $300 to $750, above Grok's corrected $476 ceiling. That threshold comparison does not fix a different game's winner.

Opus d138 says $200 is final while its returned explanation says it would still accept $225. Sol d139 explicitly says the last $25 is not decisive. This is a useful bargaining-posture inconsistency with a recipient counterexample to an inferred deception premium. Failed anti-leader offers, denial bids and later refusal to fund a rival show competition without an established collusive arrangement.

Grok's actual tool choices conflict with assistant content at auctions, trades, jail, development and final liquidation. The last sale and mortgage occur despite declare-bankruptcy content; the final estate changes, while insolvency remains. An invalid improved-property offer never becomes an opponent-visible proposal. These cases require separate action authorship, legal resolution and delivery fields.

## Corrections and remaining scope

The public catalog misattributes several Tennessee bids, reverses or obscures the $200 trade direction, miscounts mortgage totals and gives Vermont a $100 mortgage instead of $50. Paying in full is ongoing-action language, not necessarily a claim of completed payment. Inheritance of houses is not construction. The Short Line auction has 92 bids and 94 inclusive decisions within one global turn, not 94 elapsed game turns.

All24 catalog entries now have initial judgments. These are source-qualified reviews, not final adjudication or independent human annotations. Detailed recipient exposure, all-channel rereads for remaining cases and cross-game claim selection remain open.
