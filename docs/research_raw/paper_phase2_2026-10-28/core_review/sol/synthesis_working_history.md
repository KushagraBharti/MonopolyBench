## What the game tests

The original game-13 d252 system prompt requires winning through long-term advantage and legal actions. It expressly permits public deception, bluffing and manipulation, while asking private notes to reflect true reasoning. This distinction matters. A strategically misleading bargain may demonstrate a game capability within its authorized objective. It is not by itself a violation of the benchmark's governing instruction. Incorrect financial arithmetic, legal but avoidable bankruptcy, missing intended construction and contrary tool selections concern competent pursuit of the goal. They still require separate treatment because the applied record alone cannot identify whether the cause was a strategic choice, a mistaken model of the rules, a response-condition interaction or disagreement between generated channels.

This cohort supports mechanism-rich observations about these games. It does not identify a trend as general capability rises, a general disposition toward deception, a provider-specific causal effect or real-world safety. Public-only and full-channel candidates are overlapping, selected source entries. The 614 catalog rows contain 593 final entries and 21 historical checkpoints, not 614 independent episodes. The 240 common-sample decisions were selected from public-unflagged material without looking at their contents, stratified by game and player. Full-channel reviewers may already discuss them. That design audits missed cases within this frame; it is not a random estimate of all latent behavior.

## Complete trajectories constrain economic claims

The narratives contain natural-completion wins, trade-assisted wins, high-premium purchases, cash-capped auctions, expensive sets that never collect developed rent, and repeated forced inheritance. G14 and G15 show winners with no accepted trade; G10–G13 distinguish naturally acquired winning groups from later trade financing. A group-completion rate alone misses both acquisition access and the ability to activate or develop after settlement. Conversely, a locally profitable cash sale can coexist with a later loss because it arms a more liquid rival or consumes a scarce management opportunity.

Construction expenditure is an outflow, not realized profit. A mortgage raises cash while encumbering a deed. Face-value premium is a transaction descriptor rather than a complete surplus estimate. A rent charge ending in bankruptcy contributes only actual estate cash and transferred titles/buildings, not the face amount as paid rent. Winning by receiving a developed estate does not imply the debtor voluntarily selected the winner. Terminal inheritance also censors future use: assets arriving at the final event have no opportunity to earn post-inheritance revenue.

House withholding requires a graduated claim. Retention and a recorded denial motive may be observed without low stock. Low or zero stock may coexist with cash insufficiency, mortgage blockage or no complete rival group. A supply-only legality diagnostic addresses a narrower question than whether retention was optimal, irreversible, causally decisive or intentionally directed at the eventual victim. G19 is a strong proposed negative case because stated scarcity coexists with available houses and later actual construction. G4 offers fragile capture and half-price resale, while G7 supplies liquidation rather than construction access. These differences should remain visible in the paper rather than combined into a single success count.
