Agents that act for a long time in a shared economy must sustain an objective and deal with other agents while doing so. We study both in MonopolyBench, a deterministic four-player Monopoly variant in which a rules engine emits the legal actions at every decision, every attempt and state change is recorded, and the instructions permit public deception while asking that private notes reflect true reasoning. Four frontier models, GPT-6.1 Sol, Gemini 4 Argon, Claude Opus 5.5 and Grok 4.7, each configured through one gateway at medium reasoning effort, played twenty complete games over five seeds and four seat rotations, producing 6,989 decisions and sixty eliminations. We follow acquisition, bargaining roles, construction, paid rent and inheritance for all four players, and we test claims about those records by restoring exact historical decision prefixes and executing specified legal alternatives. Fifty-six eliminations could not have paid the current debt from the debtor's own assets, three could, and one held sufficient nominal value locked behind an unsellable hotel. Applied non-building after a trade arose through three distinct paths: engine defaults following a provider-refusal recovery condition, received tool calls that contradicted their own text, and stated reserve choices. In the social record a seller's misleading financial pitch preceded a higher settlement, but the buyer had recorded its motive before the pitch; other recipients corrected false premises and still settled, or echoed and then rejected them. These findings show what an auditable economic sandbox can establish about capability and conduct under a declared norm. They do not rank the models, estimate a deception rate or relate alignment to capability growth; we specify the controlled design those questions require.
