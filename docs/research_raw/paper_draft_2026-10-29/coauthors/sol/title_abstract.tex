% MonopolyBench: Economic Capability and Social Conduct over Complete Agent Trajectories

Persistent competition tests how agents sustain an objective and how they behave toward other agents while doing so. We study these questions in MonopolyBench, an executable four-player economy with explicit legal decisions, consequential bargaining and event-linked records. We analyze twenty complete games from five seeds and four seat rotations using four configured agents. Our analysis joins bilateral transactions and complete ownership trajectories with tests that restore historical decision prefixes and execute specified legal alternatives. Economic outcomes follow several paths: two winners complete no trades, inherited development complicates attribution of construction receipts, and three of sixty applied eliminations admit a legal payment of the current debt. Attempt records also distinguish reserve choices from defaulted or contradictory received actions. Social chronologies connect financial claims, delivered information and recipient responses. A misleading omission precedes a higher-price settlement, but the buyer already records an economic motive to preserve the seller's solvency; other recipients correct false premises or reject the associated offer. Public deception is permitted under the evaluated instructions, so these cases describe competitive conduct rather than a deception-based misalignment rate. The study demonstrates how executable economic records can improve behavioral attribution and specifies further controls needed to compare norm adherence across capability levels.
