## One-paragraph verdict

As drafted, this is mostly a carefully hedged **benchmark postmortem**. It contains a real but **underdeveloped measurement-method contribution**. The paper sets out to study "economic capability and social conduct" and is motivated by a capability–alignment question. It then disclaims, quite correctly, almost every inference that framing invites: no ranking, no capability axis, no misalignment rate, no persuasion effect, no cross-model deception rate. What survives is a large set of precise, source-checked, local facts and a recurring observation: **the obvious reading of a log record is often wrong**. END does not mean "chose to hold", and nominal wealth is not accessible finance. A sent offer may not be the intended offer, a settlement does not show persuasion, and a logged message may never have been delivered. Even an agreed analyst narrative can be wrong. That recurring observation is the paper's actual scientific content. The draft treats it as a series of caveats when it should be the thesis. The abstract lists findings without saying what knowledge changes.

## Strongest defensible thesis (my first formulation)

> In long-horizon LLM-agent environments, the natural behavioral labels derived from logs fail in a structured, partly quantifiable way. These are "declined to invest", "could not pay", "proposed X", "was persuaded", "was told" and "the agent won/lost". The errors come from three layers between a model's text and the state change: the response-resolution layer (retries, fallbacks, forced tool choice and provider refusals), the accessibility gap between displayed or nominal state and reachable legal sequences, and the delivery gap between canonical speech and recipient context. Exact-prefix legal-sequence execution, together with delivery reconstruction, adjudicates claims about feasibility and exposure. It does not adjudicate claims about intent or persuasion. These require re-sampling the policy at a fixed prefix, which the same infrastructure makes possible.

If that thesis holds, what changes: anyone computing "investment rate", "bankruptcy avoidability", "deception rate" or "persuasion success" from agent transcripts should expect label error of the size shown here. They should also add specific instruments. That result generalizes beyond Monopoly. The win table, the model comparison and the social anecdotes do not generalize.

## Attacks on that thesis, written down now so I cannot forget them

1. **"You found bugs in your own harness."** The largest label-error sources are specific to this harness: all 61 Opus default builds, Grok's 139 content/tool conflicts, the 44/113 reversed trade summaries and the Opus action-only condition. A hostile reviewer would say the generalizable claim is "check your harness," not a new measurement science. To survive, the thesis must show that the *categories* recur in other published harnesses, even if the rates do not. Retry/fallback, forced tool choice, refusal recovery and summary formatting all exist in AgentMonopoly-style and Arena-style harnesses. The rates themselves are not transportable.
2. **Small residual denominators.** 8 entry-buildable non-builds, 4 non-trivially-insolvent eliminations, 5 social chronologies. "Label error rate" claims at these sizes are case studies with fractions attached.
3. **Conditioning is post-treatment.** "Entry-buildable" depends on earlier financing choices, so the 34/42 conditional is not a clean responsiveness measure. The paper says this. It still uses the decomposition rhetorically.
4. **The exact-prefix tool answers feasibility, not the social questions it is placed next to.** Exact-prefix engine branches can show that G13's seller had a $295 route. They cannot show that the buyer would have paid less without the omission. The paper's Table "evidence chain" says so, but the abstract and contributions advertise the audit as applying to "agents' claims" in general. For social claims, the right instrument is fixed-prefix *policy* re-sampling with the message ablated, a paid experiment. It is the obvious next step and arguably required for a social-conduct paper.
5. **The Opus condition may dominate.** 1,605/1,696 Opus decisions ran under an action-only recovery prompt with a different tool schema, no private note, a degraded memory and no corrective retry. "Opus" in every table is not the configured agent. Any cross-configuration reading, including negotiation propensity (Opus proposes most) and build recovery (0/61), is a harness × provider-policy effect. The paper discloses this, but disclosure does not rescue comparisons, and the comparison tables invite them.
6. **The alignment framing is unearned.** The title and introduction motivate a capability–alignment question that the design cannot address: four configurations, no capability axis, deception permitted, no norm violated. Keeping "alignment" in the motivation when it is disclaimed throughout looks like scope inflation.

## Specific suspicions to check in primary records

- **G13 d250–253 chronology.** (a) Did Opus's interest in Grok's solvency or in denying Sol the yellows appear in Opus records *before* d250? If so, "that motive is not independent of the seller's framing" overstates dependence. (b) Was Ventnor actually mortgageable at d252: unmortgaged, and its color group free of buildings? (c) Does d253's delivered prompt contain the d252 message? (d) Is Short Line rent a current debt or a hypothetical future landing? (e) What happened afterwards: did the $70 or Grok's solvency matter? (f) Is "omits the mortgage" a fair reading when the applied note acknowledges it, and does the returned reasoning "explicitly describe omitting that route"?
- **Grok content/tool conflicts.** If the received tool call can contradict the assistant content even with equal arguments (15 cases), a generation-side story is strained. A stream-assembly or gateway explanation would turn G19 d135, G6 d19, G18 d83 and G12 d249 from "conduct" into "instrument defects". I need to inspect raw responses to see whether the stored data can localize the cause.
- **Public-claim inventory.** The paper says public channels are common to all agents, yet it reports no mechanically checkable public-claim inventory with denominators, such as false ownership, completion or cost claims in trade messages. That analysis is feasible without new games and would turn the social section from anecdote into measurement.
- **Cross-section consistency** of the 34/42, 3/3/2, 61, 139, 44/113 and 54/60 numbers across abstract, body and tables.

## Initial architecture leaning

Option A (measurement-validity paper: "what agent logs do not say") over Option B (empirical economic–social behavior study with a case-study social section). I expect to choose A unless the primary records show that the label-error categories are too harness-idiosyncratic to generalize. If so, the fallback is a narrower Option B′: a capability-profile paper with an explicit "instrument" appendix.
