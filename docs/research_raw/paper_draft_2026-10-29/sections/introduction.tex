\section{Introduction}

An economic game asks an agent to do more than select a profitable move. It must anticipate rivals, negotiate exchanges, preserve resources for later decisions and act on information accumulated during play. Success can come from good decisions, favorable movement, another player's mistake or a combination of these. A convincing explanation does not resolve that ambiguity. Neither does the final score.

These distinctions motivate eight research questions: strategic decision-making, opponent modeling, negotiation and deception, sustained plans and memory, adaptation, economic risk, collective behavior, and evaluation validity. Three broader questions connect them. Does strategic language correspond to strategic execution? Does general capability predict multi-agent capability? Can skill be distinguished from luck and weak opposition? An environment can offer opportunities to study all of these without one experiment identifying all of them. Our approach is to specify an observable target for each question, evaluate the targets supported by the record, and expose the remaining experimental requirements.

Vending-Bench evaluates business operation, and its Arena communication study checks statements against simulator records and analyzes promise follow-through~\cite{backlund2025vendingbench,li2026arena}. AgentMonopoly and FinBoardBench study bargaining and resource use in board games; DECE compares complete model--harness configurations across Eurogames~\cite{wu2026agentmonopoly,hu2026finboardbench,zhang2026dece}. These studies already motivate process-sensitive evaluation and state-grounded communication analysis. We build on that approach by linking economic consequences to the exact legal opportunity, response-resolution process and information delivered at a decision.

MonopolyBench records legal menus, every response attempt, resolution conditions, delivered memory and economic events in a deterministic four-player environment. We analyze twenty completed games containing 6,989 decisions and 8,679 attempts. The study combines complete mechanical censuses with explicitly bounded semantic reviews. It reconstructs every negotiation, checks all eliminations and defaults, follows construction and asset transfers, and examines a fixed sample of decision text. A separate offline study tests whether the supplied reference policies and scenario library are ready to support broader comparisons. No new LLM queries are used in these extensions.

The economic and negotiation results reveal both strategic tradeoffs and limits on their interpretation. Of 221 offers that could legally complete a recipient's color group, 34 are accepted, 113 countered and 74 rejected. Own completion is therefore insufficient for agreement; it is not a measure of the deal's total value. Estate transfers concentrate holdings in eventual winners even before terminal elimination, but creditor identity and winning evolve jointly. At the decision level, three of sixty eliminated players can pay the current debt through legal own-asset sequences, while one has sufficient nominal equity but is blocked by the hotel-liquidation rule. These are statements about feasible endpoints, not optimal strategy or counterfactual winners.

Response provenance changes the behavioral account. Of sixty-three trade-completed groups, forty-three receive first-window construction. The twenty others include nine without a build opportunity, four ending in defaults, four in content/tool conflicts and three in valid END records. Calling all twenty investment restraint would combine incompatible explanations. Likewise, a corrected build and a later repetition of its encoding error are different observations. We connect error episodes to subsequent submitted builds while retaining whether feedback was actually present in a later request.

The social records support equally specific conclusions. A recipient who corrects a false premise and accepts the exchange is a counterexample to treating settlement as sufficient evidence of endorsement. Other cases remain unresolved: a buyer echoes inheritance framing, but the record does not establish belief in a false financial minimum or the price it would have paid without the message. Contextual transcripts retain the appeal, the recipient's available information, the response and the subsequent outcome. Public deception was permitted in this task, so observed misleading communication and violation of an assigned norm are separate questions.

Our contribution is an empirical account of execution and interaction with claim-specific validation. Legal witnesses and scoped impossibility certificates test local alternatives; population ledgers distinguish opportunity, response and realized consequence; transcript checks constrain claims about social influence. All original events replay, and a portable certificate suite checks every historical elimination and default. The new reference-policy pilot and fixture audit also identify why running available code is insufficient to create a valid capability comparison. The paper consequently offers answers about this cohort's behavior and a tested measurement procedure, while leaving causal learning, unfamiliar-opponent transfer and general-to-multi-agent capability relationships to separately designed evaluations.
