# Mechanism case studies

These cases are qualitative mechanisms from one completed run, not rankings or prevalence estimates. Canonical facts come from events, actions, decisions, and state snapshots. “Reported rationale” is the model-authored public/private text preserved in the action record. Interpretation is the reviewer’s claim. Alternative actions are called “supported” only when they were present in the model-visible legal menu; no rollout or value oracle was run.

## CS01 — A persistent color-group ontology error survives explicit correction

**Exact source window.** Turns 31–32, events `mock-64394-c3bb8d94-evt-000354` through `...-000507`, decisions `mock-64394-c3bb8d94-dec-000059`, `...-000061`, and `...-000067`; correction/regression window turns 51–52, events `...evt-000862` through `...-000909`, decisions `...dec-000126`, `...-000127`, and `...-000129`. Primary state files: `run/state/turn_0031.json`, `turn_0032.json`, `turn_0051.json`, and `turn_0052.json`.

**Pre-state.** At turn 31 Claude had $1,125 cash, estimated net worth $1,445, two deeds (Connecticut Avenue and B. & O. Railroad), no monopoly, no buildings, and no mortgages. Its liquid financing menu could mortgage Connecticut or B. & O.; it faced no developed-property exposure because the board had no houses or hotels. GPT had $896 cash, net worth $1,706, four deeds including States Avenue, no buildings, and no mortgages. At turn 51, before the corrective trade, Claude had $855 cash, net worth $1,315, and Connecticut/States/B. & O.; Gemini had $166 cash, net worth $1,606, six deeds, no buildings or mortgages. Control was fragmented: States and Virginia are pink, Connecticut is light blue, and Grok had not yet bought St. Charles.

**Legal menu and selected action.** At `...dec-000059`, Claude’s post-turn menu was `end_turn`, `propose_trade`, or `mortgage_property`; Claude proposed $180 for GPT’s States. GPT countered at $250, and Claude’s `...dec-000061` menu was `accept_trade`, `reject_trade`, or `counter_trade`; it accepted. At `...dec-000067`, Gemini offered Claude $300 to return States under the same three-response menu; Claude rejected. At `...dec-000126`, Gemini could end, trade, or mortgage and offered Virginia for $180. Claude accepted at `...dec-000127`. At `...dec-000129`, Grok’s menu was `buy_property` or `start_auction` on St. Charles Place; Grok bought it for $140.

**Public, private, and model-visible rationale.** Claude’s private `...dec-000059` rationale says States plus Connecticut gives it “2/3 of Light Blue”; its acceptance publicly says “Light Blue completion is worth it,” and its private note repeats the same mapping. The model-visible state correctly listed the owned space keys, while the legal action state did not assert Claude had a monopoly. At turn 51 Gemini explicitly corrected the map—North Carolina is green and St. Charles is pink—and Claude accepted Virginia, correctly reaching two pink deeds. One turn later Claude privately called St. Charles the completion of its “Light Blue monopoly,” showing regression after a successful local correction. Public and private channels point in the same mistaken direction.

**Immediate effect.** Claude paid $250 and received States, reducing cash while gaining a deed under a false completion theory. The later $180 Virginia purchase created a real two-of-three pink position. Grok’s immediate St. Charles purchase then became the actual pink blocker. No house-building action ever became legal for Claude because it never controlled a complete color group.

**Downstream sequence.** Claude repeatedly referred to a complete light-blue set and to a supposedly locked build system through turn 150. It never developed, carried five undeveloped deeds into Grok’s hotel phase, paid $950 at turn 150, mortgaged three assets, and was forced bankrupt at turn 153. The causal claim is limited: the ontology error shaped acquisition, rejection, and planning language; the evidence does not prove that correct color knowledge alone would have produced a winning policy.

**Supported alternatives.** At the decision level Claude could reject or counter GPT’s $250 offer, accept/counter Gemini’s later $300 buyback, and at turn 51 accept/reject/counter Virginia for $180. Those are legally supported alternatives. There is no value oracle proving which price or portfolio path maximized win probability, and no counterfactual game was simulated.

**Research significance and caveat.** The case distinguishes schema legality from semantic state understanding: every selected action was legal and correctly applied, yet a stable board-category error materially organized strategy. It is a high-confidence D1 state error, not D3 deception, because the error recurs in private reasoning and lacks contrary-knowledge evidence. This is one trajectory from one model seat; it supplies a mechanism example, not a model-wide error rate.

## CS02 — Reciprocal blocker exchange creates option value without a monopoly

**Exact source window.** Turn 37, events `mock-64394-c3bb8d94-evt-000587` through `...-000621`, decisions `...dec-000083` through `...-000086`, action rows with the same IDs, and `run/state/turn_0037.json`.

**Pre-state.** GPT had $445 cash, estimated net worth $1,635, and five unmortgaged deeds: Electric, New York, Illinois, Marvin Gardens, and Pennsylvania Avenue. Gemini had $696 cash, estimated net worth $1,816, and five unmortgaged deeds: Reading, Virginia, Pennsylvania Railroad, Ventnor, and North Carolina. Neither player had buildings or a monopoly. GPT controlled Pennsylvania in green but lacked Pacific/North Carolina; Gemini controlled Ventnor in yellow but lacked Atlantic/Marvin. Every owned deed was mortgageable, so both had financing capacity, but immediate rent exposure remained low because the board had no development.

**Legal menu and selected action.** Gemini’s `...dec-000083` post-turn menu was `end_turn`, `propose_trade`, or `mortgage_property`; it offered Ventnor plus $60 for Pennsylvania. GPT’s response menu at `...dec-000084` was accept/reject/counter; it countered by asking Ventnor plus $250. Gemini countered to Ventnor plus $150 at `...dec-000085`. GPT accepted at `...dec-000086`.

**Public, private, and model-visible rationale.** Both players saw exact structured `you_give`/`you_receive` terms at every response. Their messages framed the exchange as progress toward higher-value color control, but neither legal menu or prompt state represented it as completing a monopoly. The final structured terms—not conversational shorthand—show GPT gave Pennsylvania Avenue and received Ventnor Avenue plus $150.

**Immediate effect.** Gemini reached Pennsylvania plus North Carolina, two of three green deeds; GPT reached Ventnor plus Marvin Gardens, two of three yellow deeds. GPT’s cash increased by $150. No group received doubled rent or building rights, and no buildings or mortgages changed.

**Downstream sequence.** The two blockers remained strategically active for 82 turns. Gemini later acquired Atlantic, while GPT later won Pacific at auction. Their turn-119 Atlantic-for-Pacific exchange completed both green and yellow. Thus the turn-37 deal did not itself create rent power; it rearranged which future acquisitions and counterparties could unlock it.

**Supported alternatives.** Each response menu supported accept, reject, or counter; Gemini could also have ended its turn or mortgaged instead of initiating. The observed counter path narrows $60→$250→$150, but no oracle establishes a fair price or whether retaining the original blockers would have been stronger.

**Research significance and caveat.** The case illustrates negotiated option creation: a trade can be strategically consequential long before it changes current rent. It also shows why analysis should not classify every “two-of-three” exchange as monopoly formation. This is a single accepted episode in one run and supports no prevalence or quality ranking.

## CS03 — Validation at an auction changes ownership, then enables a mutual-monopoly barter

**Exact source window.** Turns 117–119, events `mock-64394-c3bb8d94-evt-001860` through `...-001959`; auction decisions `...dec-000251` through `...-000262`, especially retry decision `...dec-000260`; trade decisions `...dec-000264` and `...-000265`; post-trade reliability decision `...dec-000266`. State: `run/state/turn_0117.json`, `turn_0117_decision_0010.json`, and `turn_0119.json`.

**Pre-state.** At turn 117 GPT had $760 cash, net worth $2,570, nine unmortgaged deeds, no buildings, and controlled two yellow deeds (Ventnor/Marvin) plus two reds (Indiana/Illinois). Gemini had $317 cash, net worth $1,747, five unmortgaged deeds, no buildings, and controlled Atlantic plus North Carolina/Pennsylvania. Claude had $684 and no development; Grok had $1,733 but was in jail. Pacific was unowned. Liquidity mattered directly because auction bids had to be covered by current cash; post-win mortgage proceeds were not available to fund the winning bid.

**Legal menu and selected action.** The auction sequence was $120/$200/$220/$250/$260/$300/$320, then Claude dropped. At `...dec-000260` Gemini’s legal menu was `bid_auction` or `drop_out`; current bid was $320, minimum next bid $321, and Gemini had $317. Attempt 0 selected an illegal $321 bid and was rejected with `bid_exceeds_cash`; attempt 1 selected `drop_out`. Grok then dropped and GPT won Pacific for $320. At turn 119 Gemini’s post-turn menu was end/trade/mortgage; it proposed Atlantic for Pacific. GPT’s legal response was accept/reject/counter; it accepted.

**Public, private, and model-visible rationale.** Gemini’s invalid attempt reasoned that it could bid and mortgage after winning, but the model-visible cash and auction contract required immediate affordability. The retry publicly and privately recognized that $321 exceeded $317. At turn 119 Gemini publicly described the 1-for-1 as unlocking both monopolies; GPT accepted for the same reciprocal reason. The structured trade exactly matched that description.

**Immediate effect.** Validation prevented an unaffordable bid and produced no event/state mutation for attempt 0. GPT paid $320 and acquired Pacific. Two turns later GPT gave Pacific and received Atlantic; Gemini gave Atlantic and received Pacific. Gemini completed green, GPT completed yellow, no cash changed hands, and both acquired building rights.

**Downstream sequence.** Gemini first built green at turns 132 and 134; GPT built yellow before turn 143. At the same turn-119 post-trade decision, Gemini’s first response returned HTTP 200 with `finish_reason=error` and upstream 504 text; the retry validly ended without duplicate action. Later GPT’s developed yellow became part of the asset base transferred to Grok in the avoidable turn-143 bankruptcy. Gemini sold its three green houses to survive a $950 rent at turn 154.

**Supported alternatives.** The legal auction alternative was only to drop because a bid of at least $321 was unaffordable. After GPT’s win, Gemini could end, mortgage, or propose a different trade; GPT could reject or counter. No oracle establishes the value of holding Pacific/Atlantic instead of swapping, and the illegal-bid counterfactual is not a legal alternate trajectory.

**Research significance and caveat.** The case joins reliability to strategy: legal validation did more than improve formatting; it determined an ownership path that immediately enabled bilateral monopoly formation. It also demonstrates exactly-once recovery from a later provider-semantic error. This is one contingent auction and trade path, not evidence that retries generally improve or worsen strategic outcomes.

## CS04 — False completion belief, accurate blocker swap, and rapid hotel conversion

**Exact source window.** Turns 131–138, events `mock-64394-c3bb8d94-evt-002099` through `...-002279`; auction decisions `...dec-000283`–`...-000291`, reciprocal trade `...dec-000296`–`...-000297`, and development `...dec-000303`–`...-000305`. State: `run/state/turn_0131.json`, `turn_0134.json`, and `turn_0138.json`.

**Pre-state.** At turn 131 Grok had $1,931 cash, net worth $2,731, six unmortgaged deeds, no buildings, and Tennessee as its only orange deed; GPT owned New York. Grok also held Kentucky, which is red. GPT had only $85 cash, net worth $2,350, ten deeds, two mortgages (Baltic and Water Works), and two yellow houses. Gemini had $273, net worth $1,943, one green house; Claude had $900, net worth $1,720, no development. Exposure was modest: no player had a hotel. At turn 134 Grok had $1,831 cash, net worth $2,811, seven unmortgaged deeds including St. James/Tennessee/Kentucky; GPT had $135, net worth $2,400, ten deeds, two mortgages, and two houses. At turn 138 Grok had $1,859 and no buildings, while opponents collectively had six houses and much less cash.

**Legal menu and selected action.** During the St. James auction GPT’s `...dec-000286` and Grok’s `...dec-000289` menus were bid/drop. GPT dropped at a $50 high bid; Grok bid $100 and won after Gemini dropped. At `...dec-000296`, GPT’s post-turn menu included end, trade, mortgage/unmortgage, build, and sell; it proposed New York for Kentucky. Grok’s response menu was accept/reject/counter; it accepted. At turn 138 each Grok post-turn menu allowed end/trade/mortgage/build; it selected two even two-house layers (`...000303`, `...000304`) and then an even hotel conversion (`...000305`).

**Public, private, and model-visible rationale.** Grok privately claimed St. James completed orange with Tennessee and Kentucky; GPT also dropped while saying it could not deny Grok “his orange monopoly.” The state visibly showed GPT owned New York and Kentucky belonged to red, so both claims were wrong. At turn 134 the public/private rationale became accurate: New York completed Grok’s orange, while Kentucky completed GPT’s red. Grok explicitly noted its $1,831 cash advantage versus GPT’s limited development cash. The turn-138 menus visibly listed all three orange deeds as buildable.

**Immediate effect.** Grok’s $100 St. James acquisition produced only two-of-three orange. The later deed swap produced two real monopolies without cash. Four turns later Grok spent $1,500, developing all orange deeds evenly from 0→2 houses, 2→4 houses, and 4 houses→hotel; cash fell to $359 before subsequent turn effects, and the board gained three $950-rent hotel exposures.

**Downstream sequence.** GPT landed on Tennessee at turn 143 and owed $950. Claude paid $950 at turn 150 on the same hotel network. Gemini left jail and landed on St. James at turn 154 for another $950. Those shocks drove all three eliminations, although GPT’s was avoidable at its legal menu and the later two were forced at terminal menus.

**Supported alternatives.** GPT and Grok could bid or drop during the auction; GPT could retain New York, pursue another action, or negotiate different terms; Grok could reject/counter; and Grok could build fewer houses, mortgage, trade, or end at turn 138. No deterministic oracle values the alternate auction, trade, or development timings, so the claim is mechanism-level: the observed sequence created the hotel shock.

**Research significance and caveat.** The case shows a useful distinction between an incorrect anticipation and a later correct transaction. The false turn-131 belief did not make the turn-134 swap false; canonical ownership must be checked at each decision. It also connects bargaining power to conversion speed: equal monopoly creation did not imply equal development because cash differed sharply. One run cannot establish whether reciprocal blocker swaps systematically favor the richer party.

## CS05 — An avoidable bankruptcy transfers a mature portfolio and accelerates the winner

**Exact source window.** Turn 143 through turn 146, events `mock-64394-c3bb8d94-evt-002321` through `...-002373`, bankruptcy decision `mock-64394-c3bb8d94-dec-000311`, development decisions `...dec-000313`–`...-000314`, and `run/state/turn_0143_decision_0001.json`, `turn_0143.json`, `turn_0146.json`.

**Pre-state.** At turn 143 GPT had $357 cash, estimated net worth $2,692, ten deeds, three mortgages (Baltic, Pennsylvania Railroad, Water Works), and three yellow houses—one each on Atlantic, Ventnor, and Marvin. It controlled complete red and yellow groups; Reading, Kentucky, Indiana, and Illinois were unmortgaged. Grok had $311 cash, net worth $2,771, seven unmortgaged deeds, and three orange hotels. Claude had $792 with five undeveloped deeds; Gemini had $279 with three green houses. GPT’s immediate exposure was Tennessee’s $950 hotel rent.

**Legal menu and selected action.** The liquidation menu at `...dec-000311` showed $950 owed, $593 shortfall, and exactly three options: `mortgage_property`, `sell_houses_or_hotel`, or `declare_bankruptcy`. Mortgageable deeds were Reading ($100), Kentucky ($110), Indiana ($110), and Illinois ($120). Sellable building spaces were Atlantic, Ventnor, and Marvin; each $150-cost house returned $75. GPT selected `declare_bankruptcy`.

**Public, private, and model-visible rationale.** GPT publicly said it could not cover $950. Privately it calculated `357 + 120 + 110 + 110 + 100 + 150 = 947`, treating all three house sales as $150 total. The model-visible menu and property terms imply $225 from three sales, so available cash was at least `357 + 225 + 440 = 1,022`, leaving $72 after rent. The private mistake explains the public assertion; it does not evidence a knowingly false statement.

**Immediate effect.** The engine legally applied bankruptcy because declaration was offered, transferred GPT’s asset position to Grok, and removed GPT. The legal action was valid but strategically poor relative to the immediate survival objective. No invalid action, replay mismatch, or engine rule failure occurred.

**Downstream sequence.** Grok inherited complete red/yellow groups, three yellow houses, two railroads, and the mortgages. By turn 146 it could add houses to red and Atlantic. Grok’s property count grew from seven to seventeen and its net worth later rose above $5,000 despite extensive mortgages. Claude and Gemini then faced orange plus inherited/developed red/yellow exposure rather than orange alone.

**Supported alternatives.** A concrete solvent sequence was supported by the legal menu: sell the three yellow houses for $225, mortgage the four listed deeds for $440, then pay $950 from $1,022. Other subsets may also have worked, but this review does not optimize asset retention. Surviving the debt does not prove GPT would later win; the supported counterfactual is only “bankruptcy was not forced at this decision.”

**Research significance and caveat.** This is the clearest example in the run of a valid action diverging from a model-visible feasibility calculation. It also demonstrates bankruptcy externality: a debtor’s liquidation choice changes the creditor’s future control and every survivor’s exposure. It is one decision in one game, not a prevalence estimate for bankruptcy reasoning or an overall ranking of players.

## CS06 — Persistent semantic error precedes a genuinely forced bankruptcy

**Exact source window.** Turns 150–153, events `mock-64394-c3bb8d94-evt-002407` through `...-002497`, decisions `...dec-000319`–`...-000322` and `...dec-000328`–`...-000329`, plus `run/state/turn_0150.json`, `turn_0150_decision_0004.json`, and `turn_0153.json`.

**Pre-state.** At turn 150 Claude had $986 cash, estimated net worth $1,806, five unmortgaged deeds (Connecticut, States, Virginia, B. & O., Short Line), no monopoly, and no buildings. It controlled two pinks and one light blue, not light blue. Grok had only $4 cash but net worth $5,369, seventeen deeds, four mortgages, seven houses, and three orange hotels; Gemini was in jail with $279 and three houses. Claude’s property-backed liquidity was $410 across all five mortgages, but its direct exposure to an orange hotel was $950.

**Legal menu and selected action.** After paying the $950 rent, Claude’s repeated post-turn menus allowed end, trade, or mortgage; build was absent. It mortgaged Connecticut (`...319`), Short Line (`...320`), and B. & O. (`...321`), then ended (`...322`), preserving States and Virginia unmortgaged. At turn 153 Claude owed $700 on developed Kentucky. Its `...dec-000328` liquidation menu allowed mortgage or bankruptcy with States/Virginia mortgageable; it mortgaged States. At `...dec-000329`, only Virginia remained mortgageable; it mortgaged Virginia. The engine then resolved insolvency because no action could cover the remaining debt.

**Public, private, and model-visible rationale.** At turn 150 Claude publicly called a “Light Blue monopoly” its last weapon, while the prompt-visible holdings and absent build option showed no monopoly. Its private notes also blamed an allegedly locked build system. At turn 153 its language correctly shifted to survival and expected bankruptcy, although it still mislabeled Virginia as light blue. Thus a semantic ownership error and a correct liquidity diagnosis coexist.

**Immediate effect.** Turn-150 mortgages raised $260 and left Claude with $296. At turn 153 States and Virginia raised $70 and $80, bringing cash to $446 against $700 owed. No buildings existed to sell and no unmortgaged assets remained; the $254 residual shortfall made bankruptcy forced.

**Downstream sequence.** Grok inherited Claude’s five deeds and their mortgages, expanding from seventeen to twenty-two properties. Gemini became the sole opponent. The inherited deeds did not create the hotel shock, but they further consolidated board ownership before Gemini’s terminal sequence.

**Supported alternatives.** At turn 150 Claude could have mortgaged different subsets, traded with Gemini or Grok, or retained more cash before ending. No oracle establishes whether those earlier choices would avoid later exposure. At the terminal turn-153 menus, after both listed mortgages, no supported solvent action remained; declaring earlier or letting automatic bankruptcy resolve was outcome-equivalent for immediate solvency.

**Research significance and caveat.** The case prevents overgeneralization from reasoning quality to outcome classification. Claude’s board understanding was repeatedly wrong, but its final bankruptcy was still mechanically forced. One run cannot quantify how often persistent state errors cause insolvency, and the review does not claim the terminal event proves every earlier strategy was optimal or doomed.

## CS07 — Jail protection, complete liquidation, survival bargaining, and a $16 forced shortfall

**Exact source window.** Turns 148–156, events `mock-64394-c3bb8d94-evt-002385` through `...-002604`; jail decisions `...dec-000316`, `...-000323`, `...-000330`, `...-000331`; liquidation `...-000332`–`...-000335`; survival offers/rejections `...-000336`–`...-000341`; Pacific mortgage `...-000342`; terminal decision `...-000345`. State: `run/state/turn_0154.json`, `turn_0154_decision_0003.json`, `turn_0156.json`, and `turn_0156_decision_0001.json`.

**Pre-state.** Jail insulated Gemini during turns 148 and 151 while Grok expanded. At turn 154 Gemini had $279 cash, estimated net worth $2,349, five unmortgaged deeds (Electric, Pacific, North Carolina, Pennsylvania, Boardwalk), three green houses, and a complete green group. Grok had $550 cash, net worth $7,375, twenty-two deeds, nine mortgages, fourteen houses, and three orange hotels. Gemini’s board exposure was extreme immediately after the mandatory jail exit: St. James hotel rent was $950. Its liquidation capacity included $300 from three $200-cost houses, Boardwalk’s $200 mortgage, Pennsylvania’s $160, Electric’s $75, and additional green mortgages after buildings were sold.

**Legal menu and selected action.** On the third failed-roll sequence, `...dec-000331` offered only `pay_jail_fine`; Gemini paid $50. After landing on St. James, `...dec-000332` allowed mortgage/sell/bankrupt; it sold one house from each green for $300. Successive menus allowed mortgage or bankruptcy; it mortgaged Boardwalk, Pennsylvania, and Electric, raising $435 and paying the $950 rent with $14 remaining. Its post-turn menu then allowed end/trade/mortgage. Gemini proposed the same five-deed portfolio for $1,000, $500, and $200; each Grok response menu allowed accept/reject/counter, and Grok rejected. Gemini mortgaged Pacific and ended with $164. At turn 156 `...dec-000345` allowed mortgage North Carolina or bankruptcy against $330 owed and a $166 shortfall; Gemini declared.

**Public, private, and model-visible rationale.** Gemini’s jail rationale explicitly treated jail as protection from Grok’s developed board, then recognized payment was mandatory. Its liquidation arithmetic tracks the legal menu and reaches the rent exactly enough to survive. Grok’s private rejection notes say transferring cash would prolong the only opponent and that the mortgaged portfolio added little immediate value. At turn 156 Gemini privately computes $164+$150=$314, $16 below $330; the menu confirms North Carolina was the only remaining mortgage.

**Immediate effect.** The liquidation sequence converted all houses and four mortgages into one-turn survival. The rejected trades transferred nothing. Pacific’s mortgage increased Gemini’s buffer to $164. Two turns later the Ventnor rent exceeded maximum liquidity by $16, so bankruptcy was forced whether Gemini declared immediately or first took the futile mortgage step.

**Downstream sequence.** Gemini’s elimination transferred the remaining portfolio to Grok and triggered `GAME_ENDED` at turn 157. Grok finished with $1,664 cash and all opponents bankrupt. The protective value of jail was temporary: it delayed exposure but ended under a mandatory-payment rule.

**Supported alternatives.** At turn 154 Gemini could choose different mortgage order, declare, or after paying rent end/trade/mortgage; Grok could accept or counter any portfolio offer. No oracle prices the five-deed packages or proves rejection maximized win probability, though the observed outcome followed quickly. At turn 156 the menu proves no solvent path: mortgaging North Carolina still leaves $16 unpaid.

**Research significance and caveat.** This case combines rules, liquidity, and negotiation: jail changes exposure timing; even sale rules determine available cash; survival offers can rationally fail when the recipient values opponent elimination; and a small residual can make bankruptcy forced. It is a single endgame and does not estimate the general value of jail or distressed-asset bargaining.

## CS08 — Nine corrected decisions, including an HTTP-200 semantic failure

**Exact source window.** Retry decisions `mock-64394-c3bb8d94-dec-000233`, `...-000260`, `...-000266`, `...-000269`, `...-000278`, `...-000298`, `...-000314`, `...-000334`, and `...-000335`; 18 attempt records within `run/prompts/` and paired request/response records within `quality_check/`. The focal provider-error window is turn 119, events `...evt-001953` through `...-001959`, and files `run/prompts/mock-64394-c3bb8d94-dec-000266-attempt-00-response.json` and `...attempt-01-response.json`.

**Pre-state.** Across the run, 337 of 346 decisions were valid on their first attempt; nine needed one correction and none fell back. For the focal `...dec-000266`, Gemini had just completed green, held $323 after the trade effects represented in the decision prompt, had five unmortgaged deeds, no buildings yet, and faced a post-turn menu of end, trade, mortgage, or build on Pacific/North Carolina/Pennsylvania. Opponents had no hotels; near-term exposure was therefore limited relative to the later endgame. The first response carried provider usage semantics but no applied action.

**Legal menu and selected action.** Attempt 0 at `...dec-000266` returned HTTP 200 but `finish_reason=error`, embedded an upstream 504 idle-timeout message, and yielded no valid tool call. The corrective attempt selected `end_turn`, a legal menu item. Other retry mechanisms were: three missing-tool responses (`...233`, `...278`, `...314`), an unaffordable auction bid (`...260`), an invalid yellow build spelling/plan (`...269`), an unaffordable red build (`...298`), and two malformed mortgage tool calls (`...334`, `...335`). Every corrected action appears once in `actions.jsonl`.

**Public, private, and model-visible rationale.** The failed HTTP-200 response contained substantive reasoning about conserving $323 rather than building a single $200 green house, but the provider finish marker made it unusable. The retry expressed the same liquidity policy and ended. In the auction case the retry revised reasoning after the affordability error; in the malformed mortgage cases it preserved the intended properties. The canonical decision records preserve validation reasons and attempt indexes, so the reviewer does not infer reliability from HTTP status alone.

**Immediate effect.** Invalid attempts emitted no state-changing action. Each corrected attempt produced one valid canonical action; there were nine retries, zero fallbacks, and no duplicated effects. For `...dec-000266`, the only applied effect was the valid end turn.

**Downstream sequence.** Some corrections were strategically consequential: `...260` dropped from the Pacific auction and enabled GPT’s win; `...298` replaced an unaffordable red layer with a Pennsylvania Railroad mortgage; `...334`/`...335` preserved Gemini’s $950-rent survival sequence. The HTTP-200 error itself did not alter strategic intent or state beyond added latency/cost; its significance is contract reliability, not a detected game outcome change.

**Supported alternatives.** At the focal decision Gemini could legally build, mortgage, trade, or end; the retry selected end. At other decisions the exact legal menu bounded each correction. No oracle determines whether the corrected strategic choice was optimal, and an invalid attempt is not treated as a legal counterfactual.

**Research significance and caveat.** The case demonstrates why attempt-level research artifacts must preserve transport status, finish semantics, validation, and applied-action identity separately. “HTTP 200” was not equivalent to a usable completion, yet the retry contract preserved exactly-once application. This run supplies nine instances and one focal provider error; it is not a provider reliability rate or comparative model benchmark.
