# Bankruptcy windows

Exactly three windows are declared, one per eliminated player. The ranges are deliberately longer than the default ±5-decision template because each collapse had a delayed strategic setup. Facts below come from canonical events, applied actions, decision menus, prompt/response attempts, and snapshots. “Could have” statements are restricted to actions actually offered by the engine; no unrun branch is presented as a factual outcome.

## BW-OPENAI · OpenAI GPT 5.4 Mini · turns 102–109

**Strategic position and runway.** At the start of turn 102, OpenAI remained alive with $405 while Gemini was completing a four-house Pink rent engine; the authoritative checkpoint is `run/state/turn_0101.json` [EVIDENCE:EVD-STATE-TURN-0101]. OpenAI had already sold Vermont to Gemini on turn 62 and St. Charles on turn 66, the latter completing Gemini’s Pink monopoly (`mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-001319`) [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-001319]. Those transfers improved OpenAI’s liquidity at the time but also created the later rent exposure.

**Prehistory and capital allocation.** Turn 102 raised Gemini’s Pinks to four houses each for $300 (`mock-44910-42ec35c5-dec-000278`; events `001944`–`001947`) [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000278-RESOLVED] [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-001947]. OpenAI passed Go on turn 104 for $200, then entered turn 108 with $631 and landed on unowned Baltic Avenue. It bought Baltic for $60, unmortgaged Mediterranean for $33, spent $400 to put four houses on each Brown, spent another $100 to convert both to hotels, and mortgaged Pacific for $150 (`dec-000285` through `dec-000289`; events `002010`–`002037`) [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000285-RESOLVED] [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000289-RESOLVED] [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-002037]. This sequence ended with $188 and two Brown hotels at the turn-109 checkpoint [EVIDENCE:EVD-STATE-TURN-0109].

**Decision surface and alternatives.** Before the eight-house build, `dec-000287` offered `end_turn`, trade, mortgage, unmortgage, and build; before hotel conversion, `dec-000288` again offered `end_turn`, trade, mortgage, build, and sell [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000287-START] [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000288-START]. Thus retaining more cash was legally available. It is an accounting fact that not spending some or all of the $500 development outlay would have increased immediate liquidity. It is not established here that either alternative would have produced eventual survival or a better continuation value.

**Triggering obligation and liquidation.** On turn 109 OpenAI rolled 5+3 from Baltic to St. Charles, owing Gemini $625 on a four-house Pink (`mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-002044`–`002045`; liquidation menu `dec-000291`) [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-002044] [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000291-START]. The first liquidation sold both hotels for $50 total, converting them back to four houses per deed (`dec-000291`; events `002050`–`002052`) [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000291-RESOLVED] [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-002052]. The next decision’s first attempt incorrectly tried to sell hotels that no longer existed and received `No hotel to sell`; the corrective attempt sold all eight houses for $200 (`dec-000292`; events `002057`–`002059`) [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000292-RESOLVED] [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-002059]. This retry did not create a fallback and did not change the terminal inability to pay.

**Final legal bound, action, and transfer.** After liquidation, the menu showed a remaining $187 shortfall and only Mediterranean and Baltic as mortgageable, together worth $60; the legal actions were mortgage one property or declare bankruptcy [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000293-START]. OpenAI declared bankruptcy (`dec-000293`) [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000293-RESOLVED]. The engine transferred its $438 remaining cash to Gemini and marked bankruptcy at events `002064`–`002066`; Brown, Marvin Gardens, Pacific, and North Carolina then transferred to Gemini at zero price (`002067`–`002071`) [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-002064] [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-002066] [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-002071].

**Communication and causal interpretation.** OpenAI publicly called the Brown build “best immediate EV” and privately described post-hotel liquidity as “lean but acceptable” on turn 108; the next roll made that assessment fail on the realized path [EVIDENCE:EVD-ACT-000288]. The collapse is best described as an observed one-turn overdevelopment/liquidity failure interacting with a $625 rent shock. Selling St. Charles earlier was a necessary enabling condition for Gemini’s Pink monopoly, but its long-run value cannot be isolated without a branch.

**Avoidability and uncertainty.** At the final liquidation state bankruptcy was arithmetically unavoidable with the offered unilateral actions. Earlier, `end_turn` was explicitly legal before both development steps and would have preserved cash, so the window contains a strong *avoidable-risk candidate*. No branch replay was run, so the report does not assert a unique counterfactual survival path.

**Aftermath.** Gemini received cash and five deeds, including the Brown monopoly. OpenAI left first at turn 109, narrowing the game to Claude, Gemini, and Grok. Cross-links: `TR-102-104`, `TR-105-107`, `TR-108-110`, `EP-BW-OPENAI`.

## BW-CLAUDE · Claude Haiku 4.5 · turns 155–166

**Strategic position and runway.** Claude entered this window with $627, no developed monopoly, and New York Avenue as a deliberate blocker against Gemini’s Orange completion [EVIDENCE:EVD-STATE-TURN-0154]. The model’s repeated public and private position was to preserve liquidity and hold New York while Gemini and Grok attrited one another. Claude had placed no auction bids in any of the eight auctions and had built no houses; its capital allocation was intentionally defensive.

**Prehistory, liquidity, and claims.** Claude’s cash moved from $627 to $577 after a doctor’s fee, then recovered through passing Go, a $100 card, and an Oriental mortgage. On turn 163, a Chance card sent Claude to Gemini’s railroad and doubled the four-railroad rent to $400 (`mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-002781`–`002785`), leaving $527 [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-002781] [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-002785] [EVIDENCE:EVD-STATE-TURN-0164].

**Trade alternatives actually offered.** On turn 164 Gemini offered $500 for New York and, after rejection, $850. Both response menus allowed accept, reject, or counter (`dec-000387`, `dec-000389`) [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000387-START] [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000389-START]. Claude rejected both, publicly and privately reasoning that Orange completion would remove its primary defensive leverage [EVIDENCE:EVD-ACT-000387] [EVIDENCE:EVD-ACT-000389]. Acceptance of the $850 offer was therefore a real legal alternative, not an invented rescue. One-step accounting says it would have added $850 cash while transferring New York; that exceeds the next realized $750 obligation. Because ownership and subsequent choices would also change, this is not proof of a superior full-game continuation.

**Triggering obligation and liquidation.** On turn 166 Claude rolled 5+4 from Pennsylvania Railroad to Illinois Avenue, where Grok’s three-house Red rent was $750 (`evt-002840`–`002841`) [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-002840] [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-002841]. With $527, the liquidation menu recorded a $223 shortfall; New York was the only mortgageable deed, and no building sale existed [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000394-START]. Claude mortgaged New York for $100 (`evt-002846`–`002847`) [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000394-RESOLVED] [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-002847]. The logged private report estimated post-mortgage cash at “~$687,” but the canonical event arithmetic was $627; this is a D1-style private accounting error, not a public deception claim.

**Final action/event, creditor, and asset transfer.** Because $627 remained below $750 and no further liquidation action was available, the engine automatically paid the entire $627 to Grok and marked Claude bankrupt (`evt-002848`–`002850`) [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-002848] [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-002850]. Oriental, Vermont, Connecticut, and mortgaged New York transferred to Grok (`002851`–`002854`) [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-002854]. There was no separate `declare_bankruptcy` action because the last mortgage exhausted the unilateral liquidation surface.

**Causal interpretation.** The immediate cause was a $750 Red rent after a $400 railroad shock three turns earlier. The strategically important antecedent was rejecting $850 for the blocker two turns before bankruptcy. Claude’s blocking thesis was coherent—giving Gemini Orange could strengthen the leading rent engine—but the realized path punished the choice before the blocker produced further leverage. This is a reviewed case of defensive-board-control value losing to near-term solvency.

**Avoidability and uncertainty.** At `dec-000394`, mortgaging New York still left an unavoidable $123 deficit and declaring bankruptcy was the only other legal action. At `dec-000389`, accepting $850 was legally available and sufficient under one-step cash accounting to cover the later rent, making this an *earlier avoidable-liquidity candidate*. Whether acceptance improves survival or win probability after Gemini completes Orange is not established.

**Aftermath.** Grok received Claude’s remaining cash and deeds. On the next turn Grok traded New York to Gemini, enabling the Orange monopoly and a nine-house build; thus Claude’s blocker delayed but did not prevent the structural transition (`evt-002865`–`002921`) [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-002870] [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-002921]. Cross-links: `TR-153-155`, `TR-156-158`, `TR-159-161`, `TR-162-164`, `TR-165-167`, `EP-BW-CLAUDE`.

## BW-GROK · Grok 4.3 · turns 254–272

**Strategic position and runway.** At the start of the window Grok had $136, Gemini had $3,287, and the bank had zero houses [EVIDENCE:EVD-STATE-TURN-0253]. Grok still owned the Red and Dark Blue monopolies, but Gemini’s 32-house lock prevented new development and its Pink/Orange/railroad income had created a large liquidity lead. Grok’s repeated plan was to preserve the three-house Reds as the only meaningful comeback engine.

**Shock and liquidation sequence.** On turn 256 Grok mortgaged Boardwalk for $200 to pay $200 railroad rent (`dec-000514`; events `003884`–`003888`) [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000514-RESOLVED] [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-003888]. Passing Go and a card temporarily raised cash to $336. On turn 260 a $625 States Avenue rent created a $289 shortfall. Grok mortgaged Park Place for $175 and sold one house each from Kentucky and Indiana for $150, then paid the rent (`dec-000519`, `dec-000520`; `evt-003929`–`003940`) [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000519-RESOLVED] [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000520-RESOLVED] [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-003940].

Gemini immediately bought the two released houses on turn 261, restoring the zero-house bank (`dec-000522`; `evt-003953`–`003955`) [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000522-RESOLVED] [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-003955]. On turn 262 Grok sold two Illinois houses and one Kentucky house to pay $200 railroad rent (`dec-000524`, `dec-000525`; `evt-003968`–`003978`) [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000524-RESOLVED] [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000525-RESOLVED] [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-003978]. Turn 263 street repairs cost $160 and forced the sale of two Indiana houses (`dec-000527`; `evt-003987`–`003994`) [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000527-RESOLVED] [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-003994]. Turn 264 Water Works rent forced sale of another Kentucky house (`dec-000529`; `evt-004007`–`004011`) [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000529-RESOLVED] [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-004011].

**House-lock response and communications.** On turn 265 Gemini paid $625 in general repairs but still spent $300 to buy all six released houses on Brown, explicitly preserving the house lock (`dec-000531`; `evt-004020`–`004028`) [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000531-RESOLVED] [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-004028]. Grok publicly kept describing Red “attrition pressure,” while its private reports increasingly acknowledged that only one Illinois house and minimal cash remained. The optimism was a forecast/positioning statement, not a verifiable promise or supported deception.

**Final trigger and legal alternatives.** Grok passed Go on turn 269 but paid $160 Brown rent, leaving $56; Gemini later paid Grok $36 Red rent, leaving $92 (`evt-004062`–`004065`, `004082`–`004084`) [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-004065] [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-004084]. On turn 272 a 1+2 roll landed on Income Tax. The liquidation menu recorded a $200 obligation, a $108 shortfall, no mortgageable deeds, and only the last Illinois house as sellable [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000539-START]. Selling that house would raise $75, still leaving a $33 deficit. Grok declared bankruptcy [EVIDENCE:EVD-DEC-000539-RESOLVED].

**Final event, creditor, and assets.** The engine transferred Grok’s $92 as `BANKRUPTCY_CASH`, marked bankruptcy, and returned remaining assets to the bank (`evt-004097`–`004099`) [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-004097] [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-004098] [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-004099]. The creditor was the bank because the triggering obligation was Income Tax, not rent. `GAME_ENDED` then named Gemini the winner at terminal marker turn 273 [EVIDENCE:mock-44910-42ec35c5-evt-004101].

**Causal interpretation.** The terminal tax was the immediate trigger, but the runway was exhausted by repeated Pink/Orange/railroad payments and the finite-house strategy. Gemini’s rapid reacquisition of sold houses converted Grok’s required liquidation into continued development denial. The earlier turn-167 trade gave Grok a Dark Blue monopoly and $500 but also gave Gemini New York and the Orange monopoly; both sides received real structural value, yet the house shortage prevented Grok from monetizing Dark Blue while Gemini built immediately.

**Avoidability and uncertainty.** At turn 272 no unilateral offered sequence could raise the missing $108: the sole sale produced only $75. Bankruptcy was therefore unavoidable on the recorded final decision surface. Earlier alternatives—rejecting the New York trade, different mortgage order, or liquidating Reds sooner—require branch evaluation and are not claimed as factual rescue paths.

**Aftermath.** Gemini survived alone with $3,921 and an estimated summary net worth of $10,071. Cross-links: `TR-252-254` through `TR-270-272`, `EP-BW-GROK`.
